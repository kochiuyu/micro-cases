ACC-28 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Target costing · Value engineering · Contribution requirements

A market price. A design cost gap?

A cost target depends on what the customer receives

ACCA’s target-costing guidance starts with an acceptable selling price and works back to the allowable cost. It also treats design, customer-valued functions and cross-functional decisions as central to closing the gap. A cheaper part is not automatically a better design.

All prices, costs and requirements below are fictional. This is a contribution-based design screen, not a market forecast or a claim that contribution equals whole-product profit.

Your classroom decision

A Hong Kong maker is designing a compact desktop storage box for independent craft sellers. Production has not begun and no customer orders have been accepted. For this classroom decision, any net selling price from HK$95 to HK$100 is feasible for the specified product at the same planned volume. Prices outside that range are not feasible. There are no discounts, taxes or channel deductions to add. Treat that range as a supplied assumption, not evidence about a real market.

Management requires at least HK$30 contribution per unit: net selling price minus all variable costs. The proposed design costs HK$78 per unit, comprising materials, assembly, packing, delivery and expected variable warranty work. Fixed design and capacity costs are already separately funded and unchanged by either candidate; the contribution screen is not a full life-cycle profitability calculation. Testing either candidate has the same funded cost. No unpaid work, reduced safety or uncounted tooling is permitted.

Choose which redesign to examine first. Component C replaces an internal divider assembly and is estimated to save HK$10 per unit. Assembly A removes an assembly operation and is estimated to save HK$8 per unit. Neither saving is yet a release authorisation. The detailed functional review will be available before selecting a design. Calculate the allowable variable cost and gap across the feasible price range, then name the evidence you require before treating either saving as acceptable. The options are alternatives, not additive.

Round 2 · More information

The supplied customer requirement identifies repositionable internal dividers as essential: customers must alter compartment widths without replacing parts. The original design meets it. C uses a fixed divider moulding, so it removes that essential function. All other product characteristics remain acceptable, but the feasible price range applies only to products meeting the full requirement. There is no supplied price or demand evidence for a different fixed-divider product.

A instead eliminates a separate alignment-and-fastening operation through an integrated guide. The functional review confirms that it preserves repositionability, strength, safety, appearance, durability and all other specified requirements. The net HK$8 saving is validated after all variable consequences, including expected warranty work; no extra fixed or lifecycle burden is hidden. The same planned volume and capacity remain feasible. Choose a design and price for this product, or explain why you would not proceed. Neither the contribution requirement nor the essential feature can be waived in this exercise.

Your decision

Which design and feasible price meet both the essential requirement and the contribution target, and why is the largest nominal saving not the answer?

Work backward from price

At HK$95, allowable variable cost is 95 − 30 = HK$65 and the current cost gap is 78 − 65 = HK$13. At HK$100, allowable cost is HK$70 and the gap is HK$8. Across the range the allowable cost rises from HK$65 to HK$70; the gap falls from HK$13 to HK$8.

The first investigation is not a release decision

C is a reasonable first investigation because its larger saving could close the gap at a lower price: cost HK$68 needs a price of at least HK$98 for HK$30 contribution. A requires HK$100. Choosing A first to investigate process simplification is also defensible. Neither can be selected on arithmetic alone before checking functions, quality and all affected costs.

Preserve what the price assumes

C fails the essential repositionable-divider requirement. Its HK$68 cost would give HK$32 contribution at HK$100, but that calculation uses a selling-price assumption that does not apply to the changed product. Do not silently redefine the customer or call the lost function unnecessary. A different fixed-divider offer would need new requirements and market evidence.

The smaller saving closes the valid gap

Choose A at HK$100: variable cost HK$70 leaves the required HK$30 contribution and preserves every specified function. At HK$95, A leaves only HK$25, missing the target by HK$5. Within the supplied range, only HK$100 works for A. The current design yields at most HK$22 and fails throughout.

The validated assembly change removes avoidable work rather than customer-valued capability. No further cost reduction is needed for this screen at HK$100, but that does not establish total product profit or a real-world sales forecast. Preserve the requirement and cost evidence when approving the design.

Instructor note

Allow three minutes for the allowable-cost interval and a provisional investigation choice. Reveal the functional review, then require both a feasible price and an eligible design. Ask why C’s apparent HK$32 contribution is not enough: the market-price premise is conditional on the specification.

This is pre-production target costing, not overhead allocation or explanation of a material variance. The exercise deliberately uses an absolute contribution requirement rather than a percentage markup or net-profit margin. Fixed costs are outside this variable-cost screen, explicitly funded and unchanged. Public value-engineering materials from US procurement describe function-preserving analysis, not legal requirements for a Hong Kong maker.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All products, prices, requirements and costs are fictional. Price feasibility is stipulated only for the full specification. Contribution means net price less all variable costs, not net profit; fixed costs are funded separately and unchanged. Two FHWA pages are related guidance. US public-sector materials are analytical background, not Hong Kong legal requirements.

  1. Ken Garrett for ACCA, Target costing and life-cycle costing. Supports working back from market price, multidisciplinary design and preserving customer value. This case separately defines an absolute contribution target and its cost boundary.
  2. US Federal Highway Administration, The Value Engineering Process and Job Plan. Describes function analysis, alternatives and supported evaluation before implementation. Highway examples and regulatory obligations are not imported.
  3. US Federal Highway Administration, Value Engineering: Frequently Asked Questions. Explains that value analysis is not merely cost reduction and discusses early design review. This historical page is used for the analytical principle, not current legal thresholds.
  4. US Federal Acquisition Regulation 52.248-2, Value Engineering—Architect-Engineer. Primary clause requires consideration of essential functions, performance, quality and implementation/life-cycle consequences. It is background, not a governing contract here.
  5. US research and engineering office, Value Engineering. Primary programme guidance links functional review with required performance, reliability, quality and safety. No defence product, programme savings or legal rule is used in the fictional example.
