PPL-43 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Employee voice · Receiver behaviour · Closed-loop follow-through

You heard the concern. Did anyone check it?

Practice the response, not just the warning

In March 2025, the Hong Kong Hospital Authority described team simulation designed to build collaboration and a culture of speaking up. A separate simulation study found that what receivers do matters too. The fictional exercise below is a training-design decision, not a clinical protocol.

Your classroom decision

You coordinate training at a fictional Hong Kong training centre. Teams rehearse passing a demonstration folder to another desk; it contains no real records. One conflicting form version is planted in every rehearsal. No patient, treatment or real file transfer is involved.

Plan S spends the whole rehearsal practising how the person spotting the mismatch makes a clear request. Plan R keeps that practice but uses half the same paid training time to rehearse how the receiving lead restates the concern, names the person who will check, and confirms the result with the team. Neither plan replaces any real incident, escalation or verification procedure.

In this hypothetical pilot, independent observers staged forty equally timed runs per plan, with one seeded issue per run. Runs were assigned to the plans at random. The preliminary tally counts runs with a spoken concern, not repeated statements or individual performance.

Round 2 · More information

The same observers now link the subsequent steps to each run. In S, 20 of the 32 voiced concerns were explicitly acknowledged, and 12 of those 20 were checked and reported back before simulated handover. In R, 25 of the 28 voiced concerns were acknowledged, and 22 of those 25 were checked and reported back. These are nested counts, and the remaining runs reached no verified closure.

There is enough budget to run one further pilot. Neither plan has been used on live work. The facilitators can change the practice script and separately score concerns that never get voiced. They cannot use this exercise for individual employee assessment.

Your decision

Which plan would you extend now? Compare speaking, acknowledgment of spoken concerns, and verified closure across all runs. Specify what a receiving lead should do and what you would test next.

Debrief · Do not stop at the first signal

S has 32/40 voiced concerns (80%); R has 28/40 (70%). Among voiced concerns, acknowledgment is 20/32 (62.5%) versus 25/28 (89.3%). The denominators answer different questions. Repeated warnings in a single run are not new issue opportunities.

Debrief · Acknowledged is not verified

Overall closure is 12/40 (30%) for S and 22/40 (55%) for R, a 25-percentage-point advantage in this exercise. Even after acknowledgment, eight S runs and three R runs lack verified closure. Twelve R runs never voice the concern. Rehearse the receiving role while retaining speaking practice and investigate that initial loss.

Debrief · Make it possible to close the loop

Model a response such as: I heard that the version on the folder conflicts with the demonstration list. I will pause the mock handoff, ask the checker to verify the version against the training key, then tell you the result. Score whether someone actually checks and replies, not whether they recite a phrase. Repeat with more scenarios, order randomized, consistent scoring and an opt-out unconnected to appraisal.

Instructor note

Reveal the acknowledgment and closure counts only after students choose their first metric. Ask which small step belongs to the receiver and which to the speaker. A simulated gain does not establish workplace safety or clinical outcomes. Do not role-play a real emergency, change a safeguard or collect personal or health data.
