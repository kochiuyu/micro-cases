# Micro Cases: from 240 to 360
## Approved expansion plan · 15 November 2026

**Scope:** 120 additional micro cases, ten in each of the twelve areas, using **21–30** within each area. The completed collection would have **360 cases, thirty per area**. Existing cases 01–20, their links and their permanent identities remain unchanged.

**Status:** The owner approved production of all 120 cases on 15 November 2026. Approval is permission to carry out the plan, not evidence that a case is researched, completed or published. Track publication in the registry and coverage ledger. Titles and fictional settings can be refined after research. Briefs remain editorial slots until a completed case is assigned its permanent registry entry.

## What the next 120 should add

The third round should extend the range of decisions, not merely change the company in an old case. Students should encounter a different causal mechanism, unit of analysis, constraint or evidentiary problem. A new setting alone is insufficient. The intended audience remains a business classroom: unfamiliar technical vocabulary belongs in optional explanation, not in the first decision question.

Keep the proven short format: a concrete role and decision, a compact exhibit, a first vote, a meaningful reveal, a second vote and a concise debrief. A reveal may change the best choice, show why a choice is robust, or expose the evidence needed to choose; it should not always reward reversal. Do not make every resolution “pilot it,” “collect more data,” or an unfunded combination of both options.

The plan was developed against the actual 240-case registry and lesson material at repository revision `08416caf0147ccf21fb79b05df4c85e69c87fc83`, not just the earlier proposal titles. Nearby-case comparisons in each brief are editorial boundaries to enforce during drafting. The area is the primary teaching lens, not a claim that the decision belongs exclusively to one discipline.

**Editorial review:** All 120 proposed briefs received an independent conceptual and overlap review against the existing collection. Duplicate concepts were replaced and decision choices tightened. This does not certify research leads, factual claims, legal interpretations, reuse rights or final calculations; those remain production gates.

## Reading the briefs

Each brief names an opening decision, a possible second-round reveal, one main learning objective, a distinction from nearby existing material and research leads. **Every research lead below is unverified**. It is a place to look, not a citation, news hook, factual finding or permission to reuse data. A named body does not establish that it has a suitable accessible source. Verify substantive source text before using any lead. If it does not support the case, replace it.

Opening companies, exhibits and reveals are proposed **fictional teaching designs**. Their quantities and empirical effects must not be passed off as observed results. Source-grounded context and invented classroom assumptions must be visibly separated. There is no requirement to find a real scandal or real company's failure to make a case compelling.

## Non-negotiable production standards

- Keep every fact needed to answer the questions in the case itself. No external links in the student main text; references and deeper reading remain optional and separate.
- Highlight both decision questions on the case page and presentation with the established pale-gold student-prompt treatment. Preserve exact question parity across page, slides and handout. Place the question before its worked answer; the reveal must not leak through an earlier slide or illustration.
- Retain the warm watercolor-and-ink narrative style. Inspect actual existing illustrations as references, vary the opening scene, and avoid readable text, numbers, real logos and real executives. The optimized 760 × 389 image and card crop both need inspection. Exact evidence belongs in accessible tables or charts, not painted into an image.
- Give both choices their strongest reasonable case where the facts permit. Do not turn legal compliance, safety, consent or equal access into optional bargaining chips. Never manufacture false balance when an option violates a binding constraint.
- Include a separate instructor note, assumptions and a concise debrief. Recalculate every quantity independently; keep financial, legal and regulatory context jurisdiction- and date-specific.
- Review overlap both against cases 01–20 and across this new round. If a brief converges on an existing mechanism during research, replace it before assigning its permanent ID.

## Research and quality gates

1. Find a dated, relevant hook where useful, prioritising Hong Kong, the GBA and wider Asia without forcing an unsupported local connection. A transparent hypothetical may be preferable to weak reporting.
2. Read substantive sources. Aim for the collection's established breadth of evidence, without padding the reference list. Separate guidance from empirical outcomes, announcements from implementation, and descriptive association from causation. Check reuse rights and source accessibility.
3. Draft the role, feasible choices and exhibit. Specify relevant costs, timing, capacity and rights before the first vote. Ensure the reveal changes the analysis rather than simply introducing an obvious answer.
4. Obtain independent conceptual, source and arithmetic review. Explicitly flag any source that only one reviewer could inspect; do not silently call it independently verified.
5. Integrate illustration, page, editable source, slides, handout, registry, card, search and previous/next links. Test long tables and dense context slides at presentation size.
6. Run the repository checks and script validation, confirm the exact published revision, then check live art, reveal, questions, search, filters and navigation. Record mobile and print outcomes as passed, failed or not run. Previous desktop testing is not mobile or print certification.
7. At each twelve-case wave, review conceptual variety, reading load, geographic balance, reveal patterns and artwork. This is an internal editorial checkpoint unless actual instructor/student feedback is obtained.

## Proposed case briefs

## ACC · Accounting, audit and management control

### ACC-21 — Two products. Which cost changes?
- **Opening decision:** A small food processor must sell a joint-process by-product at the split-off point or refine it further; managers disagree because an allocated joint-cost report makes the refined product look unprofitable.
- **Reveal:** A new offer changes the selling price of the unrefined output, while the already-incurred joint process stays unchanged. Students reconsider further processing using the forgone sale and additional costs.
- **Learning objective:** Separate joint-cost allocation from incremental processing decisions.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-01 treats a sunk purchase and salvage; ACC-11 assigns overhead. Here the boundary is a common joint process and a decision after split-off, with sale-at-split-off opportunity cost.
- **Unverified research leads:** ACCA management-accounting materials on joint products; original processor disclosures for a dated hook. Do not copy textbook numerical examples.

### ACC-22 — More production. More profit?
- **Opening decision:** A factory controller must choose which supplied performance bridge to present to the board before it judges a profit rise despite unchanged sales: a production-volume bridge, a sales-margin bridge, or a reconciled combination.
- **Reveal:** Finished inventory increased and fixed production overhead was absorbed into unsold units; reconcile absorption and variable-costing views using supplied normal-capacity assumptions.
- **Learning objective:** Understand how production, sales and inventory change reported period profit under different costing views.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-15 bridges historical profit and cash; ACC-19 studies investment-centre incentives. This case isolates fixed-overhead timing through inventory, not liquidity or ROI.
- **Unverified research leads:** IFRS IAS 2 explanatory material; ACCA absorption/marginal costing guidance; manufacturers' inventory disclosures.

### ACC-23 — On the truck. In whose year-end stock?
- **Opening decision:** A wholesaler's controller chooses which shipments belong in the year-end inventory and sales schedules when physical stock counts exclude goods in transit.
- **Reveal:** The classroom contracts specify different transfer-of-control points and one shipment was returned before acceptance. Students revise the cutoff schedule, not merely add every missing carton.
- **Learning objective:** Apply a stated control/cutoff policy consistently across inventory and revenue.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-04 separates quantity from valuation; ACC-06 allocates prepaid service revenue over attendance. Here the focus is period-end transaction boundaries across sellers and buyers.
- **Unverified research leads:** IFRS 15/IAS 2 educational material, HKICPA audit cutoff guidance. Supply simplified contract rules explicitly, without claiming delivery terms alone settle real accounting.

### ACC-24 — Expected repairs. Already a liability?
- **Opening decision:** A retailer prepares a warranty estimate using a supplied accounting policy and several plausible repair-cost scenarios. Students select a defensible estimate and explanation, rather than choose whether to conceal the obligation.
- **Reveal:** New fault evidence changes the mix of likely claims, but not the number of units sold. Recompute the estimate and distinguish it from a discretionary cash reserve.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish an estimated obligation from an earmarked fund, and update an estimate from relevant evidence.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-03 estimates customer returns for a target; ACC-14 estimates collection losses. This concerns obligation recognition and measurement, explicitly separating a liability from cash funding.
- **Unverified research leads:** IAS 37 and HKAS 37 educational guidance; warranty disclosures of durable-goods businesses. Avoid inventing legal warranty duties.

### ACC-25 — Depreciated on schedule. Still worth carrying?
- **Opening decision:** A manager proposes no accounting adjustment to a dedicated machine because its scheduled depreciation is correct. The controller must decide what impairment review is needed under a supplied simplified policy.
- **Reveal:** The relevant asset group, rather than the machine alone, generates the cash receipts; a compact exhibit changes the recoverable amount comparison.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish systematic depreciation from impairment and identify the appropriate assessment boundary.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-08 chooses a depreciation usage basis; ACC-04 values damaged inventory. This tests recoverability of a long-lived asset group rather than annual allocation or inventory resale.
- **Unverified research leads:** IAS 36/IAS 16 materials; accessible listed-company impairment notes. Real assessment rules must be checked before drafting.

### ACC-26 — A group sale. A group profit?
- **Opening decision:** Two wholly owned companies report a profitable internal stock sale. Students prepare the group's inventory and profit bridge rather than sum the company accounts blindly.
- **Reveal:** Some inventory has since been sold outside the group. Eliminate only the remaining unrealised profit and reconcile entity versus group perspectives.
- **Learning objective:** Explain why consolidation removes internal transactions without denying their legal-entity records.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-20 negotiates internal transfer prices and divisional incentives. Here the transfer is complete and the task is consolidated reporting, not choosing the price or physical allocation.
- **Unverified research leads:** IFRS 10 educational guidance; ACCA consolidation materials; original group-account disclosures.

### ACC-27 — The result improved. Which warning was missed?
- **Opening decision:** A repair network selects a compact monthly management scorecard from a fixed reporting budget. Financial results and a process indicator tell different stories.
- **Reveal:** A measure initially treated as a leading indicator follows, rather than predicts, the service failure; another measure is available early enough for a specified intervention.
- **Learning objective:** Match leading/lagging measures and intervention timing to a causal business hypothesis.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-18 separates forecast from target; PPL reporting cases concern incentives or reporting culture. IS-09 concerns freshness and action windows; this case instead tests whether an apparently leading measure represents a plausible causal mechanism or merely a correlated symptom. Timely data are stipulated for all candidates.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original balanced-scorecard literature; public service-performance frameworks. Evidence must not be portrayed as proving causality merely because an indicator moves first.

### ACC-28 — A market price. A design cost gap?
- **Opening decision:** A maker has a stipulated feasible selling-price range and required contribution. Students decide which redesign to examine when a proposed product exceeds allowable cost.
- **Reveal:** A lower-cost component changes a feature that the supplied customer requirement treats as essential; a different design saves less per unit but removes an avoidable assembly step.
- **Learning objective:** Work backward from value and required margin to a target cost without treating all cost cuts as equivalent.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-11 allocates actual overhead; ACC-13 explains material cost variances. This is pre-production target costing and cross-functional design, not post-hoc cost attribution.
- **Unverified research leads:** CIMA/ACCA target-costing guidance; documented product value-engineering projects. Market-price feasibility is a classroom assumption, not a forecast.

### ACC-29 — One package price. Three promises?
- **Opening decision:** A business sells equipment, installation and optional support as a bundle. Under a supplied simplified allocation policy, students decide how to allocate the transaction price before recognising any part.
- **Reveal:** Before the initial allocation is finalised, corrected evidence of standalone selling prices changes the proposed allocation, while the bundle total does not. This is not reallocation for later market-price movements. Students distinguish allocation from when each promise is fulfilled.
- **Learning objective:** Separate transaction-price allocation across distinct promises from cash receipt and revenue timing.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-06 covers timing of identical prepaid sessions; ACC-10 covers principal versus agent. This adds multiple distinct performance obligations and relative standalone prices.
- **Unverified research leads:** IFRS 15 educational examples and software/equipment revenue disclosures. Do not borrow proprietary teaching exhibits or imply a complete compliance analysis.

### ACC-30 — A healthier ratio. A healthier business?
- **Opening decision:** A controller compares two lawful year-end payment timings and explains their effects on current assets, current liabilities and the current ratio, without altering the underlying obligations.
- **Reveal:** The opening ratio is above one in one subsidiary and below one in another; the same cash payment changes their ratios in opposite directions.
- **Learning objective:** Interpret a balance-sheet ratio through numerator/denominator changes rather than equate a higher ratio with improved economics.
- **Distinctness:** ACC-15 is a profit-to-cash bridge; FIN-20 is a discretionary distribution decision. This is a comparative ratio interpretation exercise, not a recommendation to manipulate disclosure or delay owed payments.
- **Unverified research leads:** Financial-statement analysis educational material; public liquidity disclosures. Both payment dates must be contractually permitted and fully disclosed in the fictional case.

## FIN · Finance, banking and insurance

### FIN-21 — Same yield. Same interest-rate risk?
- **Opening decision:** A treasurer compares two permitted fixed-income holdings for a reserve not needed before the specified date. They share a quoted yield but different cash-flow durations.
- **Reveal:** A scenario requires an early sale after a rate change. Supplied repriced cash flows show that matching initial yield does not match price sensitivity.
- **Learning objective:** Connect cash-flow timing to duration risk without confusing credit quality with interest-rate exposure.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-18 matches deposit maturities to dated payments; FIN-11 compares floating loan payments. Here the distinct mechanism is market-price sensitivity of fixed cash flows before maturity.
- **Unverified research leads:** HKMA/Investor and Financial Education Council bond education; primary bond terms. Fictional classroom holdings only, no investment recommendation.

### FIN-22 — Commit now. Pay for the option to wait?
- **Opening decision:** A firm can commit to a full equipment expansion now or pay a non-refundable fee for a time-limited right to expand after a demand signal. Students compare explicit decision trees.
- **Reveal:** The reservation limits capacity or expires before a delayed signal, changing the value of waiting rather than simply changing the project discount rate.
- **Learning objective:** Value a staged real option as a right, not an obligation, with costs and expiry.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-19 selects compatible projects under a budget; FIN-08 compares fixed cash-flow alternatives. This permits a later contingent decision and prices that flexibility explicitly.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original real-options research and accessible corporate investment disclosures. Probabilities are supplied fictional assumptions, not research estimates.

### FIN-23 — No price today. No dilution tomorrow?
- **Opening decision:** Founders compare an immediate priced equity round with a simplified convertible instrument, using fully specified classroom terms and a common funding amount.
- **Reveal:** The next-round valuation activates a conversion cap rather than the discount; calculate shares and ownership under both routes.
- **Learning objective:** Translate financing terms into contingent dilution instead of treating an unpriced round as ownership-free funding.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-10 compounds sequential 20% ordinary-share dilution. Here ownership percentages cannot yet be read from the agreement: students must first determine the conversion price under competing cap and discount clauses, then derive issued shares. The contingent instrument mechanism, not percentage chaining, is the teaching target.
- **Unverified research leads:** Public accelerator standard-form explanations and securities-regulator educational materials. Actual instrument/legal terms require verification; use a deliberately simplified model.

### FIN-24 — A lower premium. How much risk stays here?
- **Opening decision:** A small enterprise compares two insurance quotes with different deductibles and per-loss limits across supplied loss scenarios, with mandatory cover already satisfied.
- **Reveal:** Several small losses occur instead of one equally large total loss, and the deductible is explicitly per incident. Recompute retained loss plus premium.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish loss frequency, deductible structure and aggregate retained exposure.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-05 asks whether the insured trigger covers an event; FIN-01 studies correlated claims. ECO-10 concerns unobservable prevention incentives. Here prevention and loss probabilities are held fixed; both policies cover the event, and the mechanism is per-incident deductible application and frequency.
- **Unverified research leads:** Insurance Authority educational material and complete specimen policy wording, not sales summaries. Fictional comparison, no recommendation to buy or cancel cover.

### FIN-25 — Payment promised. Documents accepted?
- **Opening decision:** An exporter chooses between a documented trade-finance route and an established open-account arrangement, comparing financing cost and clearly stipulated payment conditions.
- **Reveal:** Shipment is compliant but one required document has a discrepancy. The classroom bank terms specify the available correction route, cost and deadline.
- **Learning objective:** Separate commercial performance from documentary conditions in trade payment risk.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-13 distinguishes invoice sale from credit-risk transfer. This concerns the conditions under which a bank's documentary undertaking becomes payable, not receivable recourse.
- **Unverified research leads:** ICC public trade-finance explanations; Hong Kong bank educational guidance; verified documentary-credit rules where accessible. Do not turn a simplified case into legal advice.

### FIN-26 — Cash flows continue. How much is the ending worth?
- **Opening decision:** A valuation team compares two plausible project appraisals with identical explicit-period forecasts but different terminal assumptions. Students identify the assumption driving the decision.
- **Reveal:** A required sustaining investment was omitted from one steady-state cash flow. Recompute the bounded perpetuity/exit comparison using a supplied consistent rate.
- **Learning objective:** Understand terminal-value sensitivity and sustainable cash flow rather than treat distant value as a plug.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-14 aligns inflation and discounting; FIN-15 selects a risk-appropriate hurdle. This keeps those choices fixed and examines the post-forecast horizon.
- **Unverified research leads:** Damodaran's public valuation teaching material; corporate valuation disclosures. Growth must remain below the stated discount rate in any perpetuity model.

### FIN-27 — A credit limit. How much can we draw?
- **Opening decision:** A distributor compares a fixed working-capital facility with a lower-cost asset-backed line, using supplied advance rates and eligible receivables/inventory.
- **Reveal:** A group of receivables ages beyond the eligibility rule although total book assets are unchanged. Recalculate available borrowing, not merely the advertised ceiling.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish a headline credit limit from a borrowing base and committed availability.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-17 tests a leverage covenant after borrowing; FIN-03 maps an order's funding gap. Here the amount accessible is dynamically determined by collateral eligibility before drawdown.
- **Unverified research leads:** Bank asset-based lending explanations and public loan disclosures. No real collateral registration or security grant; contractual rules supplied as fiction.

### FIN-28 — The hedge moves. Does it move with our cost?
- **Opening decision:** A processor considers a permitted classroom hedge referencing a commodity index while its supplier price includes a local component. Students compare scenario cash costs with and without it.
- **Reveal:** The local premium widens independently of the index. The hedge offsets one exposure but not the full delivered cost.
- **Learning objective:** Identify basis risk and distinguish partial risk reduction from a locked all-in price.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-04 nets matching foreign-currency receipts and payments. This isolates imperfect matching between a reference asset and the actual exposure, with no real derivatives transaction.
- **Unverified research leads:** Exchange educational material on basis risk, supplier pricing disclosures and regulator risk explanations. Use supplied payoffs rather than trading instructions.

### FIN-29 — The same ownership. The same exit proceeds?
- **Opening decision:** A startup compares two funding offers with equal cash and headline ownership but different simplified liquidation-preference terms. Students allocate proceeds at several exit values.
- **Reveal:** The preferred holder may elect conversion under one offer; identify the threshold at which the elected route changes the waterfall.
- **Learning objective:** Separate percentage ownership from contractual distribution rights.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-23 concerns conversion at a later financing round; this concerns priority and optional conversion at exit. Entrepreneurship exit cases discuss strategic buyer choice, not a financing waterfall.
- **Unverified research leads:** Public venture-finance term-sheet explainers and primary model terms. Exclude tax/legal conclusions and supply all definitions.

### FIN-30 — Affordable interest. A repayable principal?
- **Opening decision:** A business compares amortising and balloon debt with the same initial funding, drawing a complete debt-service timeline and a separately specified reserve plan.
- **Reveal:** The assumed refinancing offer is not committed. Students distinguish servicing debt from retiring it at maturity and choose a feasible contingency under a fixed cash constraint.
- **Learning objective:** Recognise refinancing risk and principal maturity concentration separately from interest affordability.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-06 compares total loan cost across payment patterns; FIN-17 concerns covenants. This fixes quoted cost comparisons and foregrounds the uncommitted source of a large maturity payment.
- **Unverified research leads:** HKMA liquidity-risk education and corporate debt maturity disclosures. No assumed guarantee that lenders refinance a profitable borrower.

## MKT · Marketing, retail and consumer behaviour

### MKT-21 — One customer. Which channel earns the sale?
- **Opening decision:** A retailer chooses how to credit sales staff when shoppers inspect in store but complete orders online, under a fixed commission budget and transparent rules.
- **Reveal:** A click-and-collect journey begins online but needs substantial store advice. Students revise the attribution rule without paying every channel full credit.
- **Learning objective:** Align channel incentives with an omnichannel customer journey while separating sales attribution from customer value.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-12 locates conversion friction; MKT-13 values relationships. Here the decision is internal channel credit and conflict, not finding a funnel leak or calculating lifetime value.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original omnichannel retail research; public retailer descriptions of integrated service. Fictional attribution rules are not employment terms for a real firm.

### MKT-22 — The last click. The whole contribution?
- **Opening decision:** A campaign manager allocates a fixed reporting credit across ads and search for known conversion paths, comparing last-touch and a supplied alternative model.
- **Reveal:** A controlled holdout shows some buyers would have converted without the final ad. Students separate an accounting attribution convention from a causal claim about incremental sales.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish multi-touch attribution from incrementality.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-02 asks whether a coupon campaign generated incremental contribution. Here all channels share the same path and the primary issue is interpreting channel-credit models, not re-running a promotion.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original marketing attribution research; official platform documentation stating model limitations. Do not imply path data alone identify causal effects.

### MKT-23 — Better taste. Or a different expectation?
- **Opening decision:** A drink maker selects a positioning claim after a blind taste comparison and a branded trial produce different rankings for the same products.
- **Reveal:** The samples used different participants and serving conditions. Students choose a justified claim and next comparison rather than conclude packaging caused the difference.
- **Learning objective:** Separate intrinsic product experience from extrinsic cues and the evidence needed to attribute an effect.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-08 concerns package recognition on shelf; MKT-11 signals service expertise. Here the question is experienced product evaluation under brand cues, not findability or competence evidence.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original sensory-marketing/blind-versus-branded research; documented food-product test methods. No health or nutritional claims.

### MKT-24 — Preferred when compared. Never on the shortlist?
- **Opening decision:** A specialist retailer chooses between improving its comparative feature message and making its product salient at a particular purchase occasion, with a fixed communication budget.
- **Reveal:** Supplied interviews distinguish buyers who considered and rejected the brand from buyers who never recalled it as an option. Students identify which problem each intervention addresses.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish awareness, consideration and conditional preference.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-03 studies attribution of collaborator attention; MKT-10 helps customers choose among items already present. This concerns entering the consumer's consideration set before comparison.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original consideration-set research and public brand-tracking methodology. Interview counts are diagnostic fiction, not projected market shares.

### MKT-25 — Advertise every week. Or be present at the moment?
- **Opening decision:** A seasonal service divides a fixed campaign budget between continuous advertising and concentrated bursts around known booking windows.
- **Reveal:** Purchase occasions are staggered across customer groups, and a compact reach-by-week exhibit changes the apparent coverage of each schedule.
- **Learning objective:** Match advertising timing to buying occasions and retention of awareness, not just total spend or impressions.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-09 measures incremental audience reach across outlets; MKT-18 manages permissioned reminder frequency. This is media scheduling across purchase cycles, not adding a channel or messaging customers more often.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original advertising carryover/pulsing research; public media-planning methodology. Supply any decay assumption explicitly; do not invent a universal optimum frequency.

### MKT-26 — A lower price. A clearer customer business case?
- **Opening decision:** A business-service supplier chooses how to present two fully compliant tender proposals: feature comparison or a quantified customer-use case, with no change in actual price.
- **Reveal:** The purchaser's operating constraint means an advertised time saving cannot be converted into cash at the claimed wage rate. Rebuild a defensible value proposition using provided benefits and limitations.
- **Learning objective:** Translate features into customer value without equating time saved with cash saved.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-11 provides observable evidence of expertise; MKT-17 elicits preferences among bundles. This is economic-value communication in B2B selling, not capability signaling or bundle selection.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original customer-value-proposition research; transparent public procurement evaluation guidance. All mandatory tender rules remain fixed.

### MKT-27 — A smaller price tag. A cheaper quantity?
- **Opening decision:** A retailer chooses a truthful shelf-label design for different pack sizes when customers must compare total spend and unit price quickly.
- **Reveal:** Customers shop for different required quantities and one pack creates unusable surplus. Students explain why neither lowest ticket price nor lowest unit price always gives the lowest relevant outlay.
- **Learning objective:** Make price/quantity comparisons meaningful for the customer's usage requirement.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-14 studies reference comparisons for the same price; MKT-16 examines cannibalisation from a new pack. This holds assortment fixed and focuses on transparent comparison and consumption quantity.
- **Unverified research leads:** Consumer Council unit-pricing reports; primary consumer research on pack-size comparison. No hidden fees or unlawful labels as legitimate options.

### MKT-28 — They understand the benefit. Can they picture doing it?
- **Opening decision:** A home-organisation brand chooses between a polished benefit advertisement and a short hands-on demonstration for a product unfamiliar to prospective buyers.
- **Reveal:** Some customers understand the promised outcome but doubt they can perform the setup; others face a genuine space constraint the demonstration cannot solve.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish customer self-efficacy from product comprehension and actual fit.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-11 signals seller expertise; MKT-12 finds journey friction. This focuses on the buyer's perceived ability to use a product, not seller trust or checkout design.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original self-efficacy/product-adoption research; documented demonstration programmes. A demonstration is not assumed to eliminate a real physical constraint or guarantee sales.

### MKT-29 — Resolve one complaint. Reassure which audience?
- **Opening decision:** A retailer has already offered a fair remedy privately. Students choose a concise, privacy-preserving public response to a visible complaint versus a generic acknowledgement.
- **Reveal:** The public complaint concerns a repeatable service issue affecting readers who have not purchased, while the complainant asks that personal details remain private.
- **Learning objective:** Separate individual recovery from public trust communication and audience inference.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-02 restores the affected guest's trip; GVR cases address disclosure duties. This holds remedy and legal privacy requirements fixed and concerns what prospective customers can reasonably learn from a response.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original online-review response research and regulator privacy guidance. Do not propose suppressing reviews, exposing customer data or guaranteeing reputation uplift.

### MKT-30 — One customer profile. Two shopping missions?
- **Opening decision:** A grocer designs two communications for the same adult customer group: one general demographic message or distinct messages for emergency top-up and planned weekly shops.
- **Reveal:** The same people switch missions across days; the supplied purchase-occasion records cannot be partitioned into permanent customer types.
- **Learning objective:** Use occasion-based segmentation without mistaking situations for fixed identities.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-07 separates user, payer and chooser; MKT-17 makes product trade-offs explicit. This holds people and roles fixed and varies the purpose of the purchase occasion.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original benefit/occasion segmentation research; transparent shopper-mission studies. No sensitive profiling or claim that a small diary study represents the population.

## OPS · Operations, supply chains and logistics

### OPS-21 — Enough components. Ready in the right week?
- **Opening decision:** An assembler releases purchase orders for a confirmed finished-product schedule using a small bill of materials and stated lead times.
- **Reveal:** A component is used in two subassemblies and one scheduled receipt is already committed. Net the requirements without double counting stock or ordering only at the final assembly date.
- **Learning objective:** Convert dependent demand into time-phased material requirements.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-11 sets a reorder trigger for independent demand; OPS-13 checks available-to-promise stock. This explodes a multilevel bill of materials and offsets component needs by lead time.
- **Unverified research leads:** ASCM public MRP education; manufacturing process disclosures. A compact fictional BOM must be fully solvable on the page.

### OPS-22 — Shorten the longest task. Finish the project sooner?
- **Opening decision:** A venue fit-out manager spends a fixed acceleration budget on one activity in a supplied dependency network.
- **Reveal:** Another path becomes critical after the first activity is shortened. Students choose the next feasible acceleration and identify its effect on completion time.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish activity duration, float and the critical path.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-03 addresses a serial production bottleneck; OPS-04 sequences repeat batches on one machine. This is a one-off project network with parallel dependencies, not throughput capacity.
- **Unverified research leads:** PMI public scheduling guidance; documented fit-out/project examples. Safety approvals and required inspections cannot be accelerated away.

### OPS-23 — Two queues. One shared line?
- **Opening decision:** A service centre considers pooling two identical appointment-free queues under the same staff capacity; a supplied set of arrival/service sequences makes waiting visible without advanced formulas.
- **Reveal:** One service requires specialised staff and cannot be served by everyone. Students distinguish full pooling from a feasible partial-pooling rule.
- **Learning objective:** Identify queue-pooling benefits and capability constraints rather than infer more capacity from a rearranged line.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-07 matches staffing to busy periods; HSP-19 manages uncertainty during waiting. This keeps staffing fixed and changes how arrivals are assigned to servers.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original queue-pooling research; public service-centre operational studies. Do not claim pooling always improves every customer's wait or bypass priority rights.

### OPS-24 — A bulk discount. A lower total stocking cost?
- **Opening decision:** A reseller chooses between regular small orders and a larger discounted lot using stated demand, ordering cost, holding cost and storage capacity.
- **Reveal:** The quoted discount is all-units in one contract but incremental in the corrected offer. Compare feasible total-cost schedules rather than applying the lowest unit price to every unit.
- **Learning objective:** Combine purchase, ordering and holding cost under explicit quantity-discount rules.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-07 prices early-payment discounts; OPS-11 sets replenishment timing. Here the trade-off is order quantity and inventory carrying cost, with demand held certain.
- **Unverified research leads:** ASCM inventory guidance; supplier quantity-discount schedules with verified terms. No invented demand uncertainty or storage beyond capacity.

### OPS-25 — The fastest item. The best shelf?
- **Opening decision:** A warehouse assigns limited near-dispatch locations using pick frequency and item handling requirements rather than unit sales alone.
- **Reveal:** A bulky item generates many sales but few full-case picks, while another creates many separate pick visits. Recalculate travel workload under a feasible slotting plan.
- **Learning objective:** Match slotting decisions to handling events and physical constraints.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-20 prioritises inventory counting; OPS-14 optimises vehicle routes. This concerns internal pick travel and storage assignment, not stock accuracy or delivery windows.
- **Unverified research leads:** Warehouse slotting research and public logistics case reports. Weight, segregation and safe-access rules remain non-negotiable.

### OPS-26 — Ninety-eight per cent at every stage. What leaves the line?
- **Opening decision:** A producer compares two process-improvement projects using the number of good finished units, with supplied stage-level pass/rework flows.
- **Reveal:** One stage's reported pass rate includes reworked units, while another reports first-pass yield. Reconstruct the flow on a common basis before selecting a project.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish stage pass rates, first-pass yield and end-to-end good output.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-10 reads patterns over time within specification; OPS-08 prevents a specific mistake. This is yield accounting through sequential stages and rework, not control-chart inference or equipment speed.
- **Unverified research leads:** ASQ public quality/yield guidance and documented process studies. Do not multiply incompatible denominators or assume independent failures without stating the model.

### OPS-27 — Short supply. Which allocation keeps promises?
- **Opening decision:** A distributor allocates a short incoming shipment among existing customers using either proportional allocation or a supplied priority rule, with contractual minimums protected.
- **Reveal:** Some clients have alternative usable stock while others need one missing component to finish a kit. Reallocate the discretionary remainder under a stated service objective.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish equal quantities, proportional fairness and downstream service impact in shortage rationing.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-02 chooses transport for an urgent incomplete order; OPS-13 checks promise feasibility before accepting sales. This concerns allocation across accepted customers after a common shortfall.
- **Unverified research leads:** Supply-chain shortage allocation research; verified distributor service policies. Do not legitimise breaking binding commitments or invent customer damages.

### OPS-28 — Reserve capacity. Commit to every unit?
- **Opening decision:** A buyer compares a fixed purchase commitment with a capacity-reservation contract that allows a bounded later quantity choice for an uncertain seasonal order.
- **Reveal:** The reservation fee is credited only against units exercised above a stated minimum, changing the cost of unused capacity in a low-demand scenario.
- **Learning objective:** Separate capacity flexibility from unit ownership and price its contractual limits.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-01 chooses a single newsvendor quantity; OPS-09 postpones product differentiation. Here a two-stage procurement contract changes when quantity becomes binding, not what design is printed.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original supply-contract/quantity-flexibility research and public procurement framework terms. Avoid overlap with FIN-22 by focusing on replenishment quantities and supplier capacity, not long-lived project investment.

### OPS-29 — Ninety-five per cent service. Ninety-five per cent of what?
- **Opening decision:** A distributor selects a replenishment policy after two teams report the same service percentage using different definitions.
- **Reveal:** One measures stockout-free cycles and the other units filled immediately. A compact order log permits both measures and exposes their different treatment of a large missed order.
- **Learning objective:** Define cycle service, unit fill rate and complete-order service before comparing operational policies.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-11 determines reorder timing; IS metric cases trace data provenance. This focuses on distinct service objectives and the operational consequences of denominator choice, using one reliable shared log.
- **Unverified research leads:** ASCM service-level definitions and primary inventory research. Do not claim an observed small-sample rate guarantees future performance.

### OPS-30 — One production lot. One recall boundary?
- **Opening decision:** A food packer chooses a compliant prospective lot-traceability design, balancing recordkeeping effort and the granularity of traceable batches; mandatory safety procedures are fixed.
- **Reveal:** A mock trace exercise finds that two lots share an undocumented ingredient transfer. Students revise the defensible containment boundary and the recording design before future production.
- **Learning objective:** Link lot genealogy and operational traceability to the scope of containment.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-20 counts physical inventory; IS-20 traces a dashboard number. This tracks physical transformation and batch links, not measurement provenance or financial reconciliation.
- **Unverified research leads:** Centre for Food Safety traceability guidance; GS1 traceability standards and documented recall exercises. Actual unsafe products are not sold, delayed or knowingly excluded from required action.


## PPL · People, leadership and organisational behaviour

### PPL-21 · Useful feedback. Useful to do what?
- **Opening decision:** A studio manager chooses between a detailed ranking report and a short task-focused coaching session after a designer misses a noncritical brief requirement.
- **Reveal:** The designer can correctly explain the standard but cannot identify the point in their workflow where it was missed. A small process rehearsal is possible within the same paid coaching time.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish evaluative feedback from feedback that supports diagnosis and a specific next action.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-11 evaluates a whole period fairly; PPL-04 concerns newcomer readiness. This studies how an established employee uses feedback, not rating accuracy or onboarding.
- **Unverified research leads:** Feedback Intervention Theory; CIPD evidence reviews on performance feedback; original studies distinguishing task and self-directed feedback.

### PPL-22 · A disagreement over pay. Or over the package?
- **Opening decision:** Two fictional project leads negotiate a voluntary internal secondment. One proposes dividing a fixed allowance equally; the other wants to negotiate timing and training access alongside money.
- **Reveal:** Their priorities differ: one values a later start, the other an early training place. Both packages preserve pay floors and existing entitlements.
- **Learning objective:** Find joint gains by trading across issues rather than treating every negotiation as one divisible amount.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-14 addresses internal pay comparisons; PPL-19 addresses a transfer date after appointment. This isolates integrative negotiation with an explicit preference table and no dominant package.
- **Unverified research leads:** Program on Negotiation material on logrolling; original integrative-bargaining experiments.

### PPL-23 · One team bonus. Whose contribution is visible?
- **Opening decision:** A team preparing an optional exhibition chooses a lightweight final-output bonus review or a responsibility-and-dependency log with short check-ins. The latter uses two hours of its fixed preparation allowance; pay is identical.
- **Reveal:** The final output hides an unclaimed coordination task; members cannot distinguish low effort from waiting for a dependency. Logs may clarify ownership but can crowd out rehearsal and reward visible work over quiet support.
- **Learning objective:** Explain how identifiability and task interdependence affect accountability in collective work, without equating visibility with productivity.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-02 concerns individual piece-rate quality distortion; PPL-17 concerns shared decision evidence. Here the issue is attribution of contribution to a joint output.
- **Unverified research leads:** Social loafing and collective-effort-model research; CIPD team-effectiveness evidence.

### PPL-24 · Reward the suggestion. Change the reason for suggesting?
- **Opening decision:** A workshop chooses an optional small cash prize for every accepted improvement idea or protected paid exploration time with informational feedback. Neither replaces normal wages.
- **Reveal:** Staff begin submitting easily countable minor edits, while an exploratory collaborative idea remains unfinished. The small observation set cannot establish causality.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish controlling rewards, competence feedback and intrinsic interest; specify evidence needed before generalising a reward effect.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-02 studies measurable output versus damage; PPL-10 studies learning goals. This targets motivational interpretation, not a claim that financial rewards always fail.
- **Unverified research leads:** Self-determination theory; original reward experiments and meta-analyses distinguishing task interest and reward contingency.

### PPL-25 · Two reasonable managers. One impossible instruction?
- **Opening decision:** A project worker receives conflicting priorities from a functional lead and a client-project lead. The director can ask the worker to negotiate each conflict or create an explicit priority-resolution rule.
- **Reveal:** Both leads are meeting their own legitimate targets, and neither sees the other’s commitments. Total paid capacity is sufficient only if priorities are sequenced.
- **Learning objective:** Diagnose role conflict and ambiguous accountability in a matrix rather than attributing delay to individual unwillingness.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-18 concerns span of control; PPL-09 repairs a work handoff. This concerns incompatible authority claims over the same task and escalation rights.
- **Unverified research leads:** Role-conflict and role-ambiguity research; matrix-organisation studies; organisational design teaching sources.

### PPL-26 · A slower colleague. Or a harder queue?
- **Opening decision:** A service supervisor chooses targeted skill coaching or a short review of assignment conditions after one adviser completes fewer cases than colleagues.
- **Reveal:** The adviser receives more exception cases, but matched routine-case evidence still shows a small unexplained difference. Neither pure blame nor complete exoneration is warranted.
- **Learning objective:** Separate situational explanations from dispositional judgments and identify an informative fair comparison.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-11 addresses recency in appraisal and IS-13 aggregation reversal. This centres managerial attribution and the response to ambiguous performance, without constructing a Simpson reversal.
- **Unverified research leads:** Fundamental attribution error and correspondence-bias research; contextual performance assessment literature.

### PPL-27 · Flexible for the business. Predictable for the team?
- **Opening decision:** A retailer can post a stable four-week roster with agreed swap options or a shorter rolling roster that better matches uncertain demand. All shifts are paid, lawful and voluntarily agreed under protected hours.
- **Reveal:** Staff’s availability changes when notice is short; some apparently flexible shifts become harder to fill. Demand estimates are unchanged.
- **Learning objective:** Treat schedule predictability as an employment-quality and coordination variable, distinct from the number of hours offered.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-06 coordinates shared hybrid time; OPS-17 plans aggregate production. This studies notice, worker planning and mutual adjustment in the employment relationship.
- **Unverified research leads:** Stable Scheduling Study; ILO working-time material; retail scheduling field studies.

### PPL-28 · A cheerful greeting. At what emotional cost?
- **Opening decision:** A visitor desk chooses a rigid enthusiasm script or a respectful service standard permitting varied, calm expression. Both protect courtesy, accessibility and complaint escalation.
- **Reveal:** Staff report that the same outward script requires different effort across encounters; guests mainly ask for clarity and attentiveness, not identical cheerfulness.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish display rules, surface acting and authentic professional behaviour without diagnosing workers or treating anecdotes as causal proof.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-14 studies discretion for guest occasions; PPL-20 studies copying role models. This studies emotional demands of work and the design of acceptable expression.
- **Unverified research leads:** Emotional labour research; display-rule studies; occupational organisational psychology reviews.

### PPL-29 · A project ends. What does the team learn?
- **Opening decision:** After an on-time internal event, a team chooses a brief operational debrief or a deeper, nonpunitive after-action review. The deeper review would displace part of the next event’s planning meeting within a fixed paid allowance.
- **Reveal:** Success depended on an improvised workaround that would fail at the next venue. Staff disagree about what caused the good result, but some details are unlikely to recur; the team must select what deserves deeper investigation.
- **Learning objective:** Extract transferable learning by comparing intentions, actions and outcomes rather than assuming success proves the process was sound.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-18 tests a founder’s transferred routine; PPL-17 pools information before a decision. This is collective reflection after execution, with counterfactual and causal uncertainty explicit.
- **Unverified research leads:** Team reflexivity studies; after-action-review meta-analyses; structured debrief research.

### PPL-30 · Nothing in the contract changed. Did the promise?
- **Opening decision:** A firm changes the format of a voluntary development programme while preserving all contractual entitlements. HR can announce equivalent hours or first discuss the expectations created by earlier informal communication.
- **Reveal:** Participants understood the programme as access to client-facing work, while managers understood it as general skill practice. Neither interpretation was documented as a guarantee.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish formal terms from psychological-contract expectations and repair ambiguity without inventing enforceable rights.
- **Distinctness:** PPL-12 gives realistic information before recruitment; GVR-17 sets policy effective dates. Here existing employees and managers reconcile an unwritten expectation after a change.
- **Unverified research leads:** Psychological-contract research; employer–employee expectation studies; CIPD trust reviews.

## STR · Strategy and international business

### STR-21 · Follow the plan. Recognise a better pattern?
- **Opening decision:** A service company reviews a small team repeatedly winning an unplanned customer segment. Should it enforce the agreed segment plan or formally test whether this pattern merits a revised strategy?
- **Reveal:** The apparent success is repeatable across customers but depends on quiet exceptions that obscure its true costs. Neither immediate expansion nor automatic rejection follows from revenue alone.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish intended, realised and emergent strategy; specify evidence and a deliberate review gate before an unplanned pattern becomes a commitment.
- **Distinctness:** STR-08 compares planned responses to future scenarios; STR-30 concerns rebuilding capabilities as a channel changes. This concerns how observed bottom-up action becomes, or fails to become, an authorised strategic direction, not activity-system fit.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original deliberate-versus-emergent-strategy research; strategy-process studies tracing how local initiatives enter resource-allocation decisions.

### STR-22 · A copied product. A copied advantage?
- **Opening decision:** A business facing an imitation can spend its development budget on another visible feature or strengthen a hard-to-observe network of routines and relationships.
- **Reveal:** The rival can reproduce the visible feature quickly but lacks a jointly developed service routine; that routine also becomes costly to maintain if partners leave.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish resource ownership from durable advantage using imitability, causal ambiguity and social complexity.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-06 compares secrecy with disclosure protection; STR-06 shares a capability. This asks why imitation of outputs may not reproduce the system that delivers them.
- **Unverified research leads:** Resource-based view; causal ambiguity and isolating mechanisms; documented imitation cases.

### STR-23 · A profitable division. A useful parent?
- **Opening decision:** A group evaluates whether to retain a small profitable division or sell it to an owner with more relevant operating expertise, holding current standalone performance constant.
- **Reveal:** Corporate services consume management time but contribute little to the division’s key bottleneck; a different parent could add a specific capability, but its offer is conditional.
- **Learning objective:** Evaluate parenting advantage rather than equating standalone profitability with fit inside a corporate portfolio.
- **Distinctness:** STR-05 values combined customer overlap; STR-11 harvests a declining line. This isolates what ownership by this parent adds or destroys.
- **Unverified research leads:** Corporate parenting advantage literature; corporate portfolio strategy studies; public divestment rationales.

### STR-24 · Compete in sales. Cooperate in the foundation?
- **Opening decision:** Rival small firms can build separate noncompetitive training facilities or jointly fund a common training platform while keeping pricing, customers and commercial strategy strictly separate.
- **Reveal:** Training benefits are shared, but one member would contribute a much more reusable curriculum. An agreed compensation and governance mechanism becomes necessary.
- **Learning objective:** Explain simultaneous value creation and value appropriation in a bounded cooperative rivalry.
- **Distinctness:** GVR-18 concerns prohibited trade-meeting topics; STR-06 shares a studio advantage. This focuses on designing contribution and benefit boundaries in a purpose-limited coopetitive project; competition safeguards are fixed, not negotiable.
- **Unverified research leads:** Coopetition research; open collaborative infrastructure cases; authoritative competition-compliance guidance before drafting.

### STR-25 · The best product. The missing complement?
- **Opening decision:** A maker of an unfamiliar but functional product can improve its core features or subsidise training for independent installers who make customers able to use it.
- **Reveal:** Customers understand the product but delay because installation and maintenance are unavailable in their area. Installer economics depend on several brands, not just this maker.
- **Learning objective:** Identify complementary assets and value capture outside the focal firm’s product boundary.
- **Distinctness:** STR-13 concerns early entry before a network forms; ENT-08 licenses an invention. This allocates a mature product’s strategic effort to complements rather than first-mover timing or licensing.
- **Unverified research leads:** Complementary-assets and ecosystem strategy research; primary installer-network accounts.

### STR-26 · One global product. One global learning direction?
- **Opening decision:** A multinational normally adapts headquarters designs locally. A regional unit proposes that a simpler locally developed version become a product for several other markets.
- **Reveal:** The local design solves a shared operating constraint, but needs different certification and support elsewhere. Its low cost alone is not sufficient.
- **Learning objective:** Analyse reverse knowledge transfer and the conditions under which local innovation travels back through a multinational.
- **Distinctness:** STR-04 adapts an exported promise; STR-20 allocates operating authority. This reverses the direction of innovation flow and separates transferability from central versus local control.
- **Unverified research leads:** Reverse innovation and subsidiary initiative research; verified multinational product-development histories.

### STR-27 · One acquisition. One operating system?
- **Opening decision:** After a fictional acquisition whose price and approval are settled, an owner chooses immediate operational integration, protected separation, or staged selective integration for the acquired business.
- **Reveal:** Shared procurement has modest benefits, while customer delivery depends on an interdependent local routine that the acquirer does not yet understand. Shared reporting can proceed without forcing a common workflow.
- **Learning objective:** Match post-acquisition integration intensity and sequence to strategic interdependence and the acquired organisation’s need for autonomy.
- **Distinctness:** STR-05 asks whether customer overlap supports an acquisition. Here the deal is complete: the decision is which operational boundaries to combine, preserve and revisit, not valuation, entry options or corporate financing.
- **Unverified research leads:** Original post-acquisition integration frameworks on autonomy and interdependence; longitudinal integration studies; fictional operating dependencies avoid claims about a particular merger.

### STR-28 · Enter the attractive segment. Cross which barrier?
- **Opening decision:** A midmarket service firm considers moving into a high-margin specialist segment or strengthening its current segment.
- **Reveal:** Specialist buyers require a track record and a referral network that cannot be purchased instantly, even though the firm’s technical quality is adequate.
- **Learning objective:** Explain strategic groups and mobility barriers without treating high observed margins as freely accessible profits.
- **Distinctness:** STR-19 concerns related diversification and missing routines; ENT-16 chooses a first segment. This studies structural barriers between positions within an existing industry.
- **Unverified research leads:** Strategic-groups and mobility-barrier literature; industry procurement qualification evidence.

### STR-29 · Improve the current model. Protect a different one?
- **Opening decision:** A mature firm must place a small exploratory service inside its mainstream unit or in a separately measured experimental unit with clear interfaces and the same funded staffing.
- **Reveal:** Mainstream utilisation targets repeatedly displace experiments; a separate unit, however, would lose access to an important operating capability unless sharing is specified.
- **Learning objective:** Design organisational ambidexterity and interfaces between exploitation and exploration.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-03 balances one custom version against a shared product; STR-20 assigns geographical rights. This concerns different innovation horizons and incompatible performance logics within an established firm.
- **Unverified research leads:** Organisational ambidexterity research; primary corporate innovation-unit accounts.

### STR-30 · A stronger position. For how long?
- **Opening decision:** A firm can optimise around today’s profitable channel or invest in a transition capability for an emerging channel while preserving existing obligations.
- **Reveal:** Customer migration is uneven, and the capability investment has a long learning lead time. Waiting for conclusive share loss could leave too little time to respond.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish current competitive advantage from dynamic capabilities for sensing, committing and reconfiguring.
- **Distinctness:** STR-11 harvests a declining business; STR-29 separates exploratory organisation. This asks when to build a transition capability under ambiguous leading indicators, not whether to close or how to structure a unit.
- **Unverified research leads:** Dynamic-capabilities literature; verified channel-transition histories with retrospective claims clearly bounded.

## ENT · Entrepreneurship, innovation and family business

### ENT-21 · The clever solution. The costly problem?
- **Opening decision:** A founder has an elegant scheduling gadget and must choose between improving its demonstration or investigating the workarounds people already use for the claimed problem.
- **Reveal:** Users dislike one inconvenience, but spend much more time solving a different adjacent problem. This is exploratory evidence, not market-size estimation.
- **Learning objective:** Formulate an opportunity from a consequential problem rather than from attachment to a solution.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-02 tests willingness to buy a defined prototype; ENT-12 decides a segment pivot after product ratings. This precedes the product hypothesis and asks which problem merits solving.
- **Unverified research leads:** Problem-framing and opportunity-recognition research; customer-discovery primary teaching material.

### ENT-22 · Enough pledges. Enough capacity to fulfil?
- **Opening decision:** A founder chooses a capped reward-crowdfunding campaign or an uncapped campaign with a funding threshold. Price, refund disclosures and mandatory safeguards are fixed.
- **Reveal:** Demand above the target would require a production step with a longer lead time and higher support requirements; reaching the target does not guarantee scalable fulfilment.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish fundraising success from the delivery obligation created by reward-based crowdfunding.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-02 validates paid demand and FIN-03 concerns order cash timing. Here contingent pledges, campaign caps and simultaneous promises to many backers form the venture-design problem.
- **Unverified research leads:** Reward-crowdfunding fulfilment studies; official platform creator obligations; verified campaign reports.

### ENT-23 · Use what is available. Build what is repeatable?

### ENT-24 · Paid for the work. Paid for the result?
- **Opening decision:** A startup offering noncritical workflow advice considers a fixed service fee or a clearly defined outcome-linked fee, with identical scope and ethical protections.
- **Reveal:** The customer controls an input required for the outcome, and a demand shock also affects the measure. A simple baseline is not sufficient attribution.
- **Learning objective:** Match a revenue model to controllable value delivery and measurable outcomes before scaling a new offer.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-03 concerns custom-product scope; ACC-06 recognises revenue timing. This tests a new venture’s charging architecture and attribution risk, not accounting treatment.

### ENT-25 · Every customer gets the founder. Can the business grow?
- **Opening decision:** A founder-led service can accept more clients through additional founder hours or redesign a bounded part of delivery into a teachable service package.
- **Reveal:** Customers value the founder’s diagnosis but not every routine production task. Removing all personal contact would change the value proposition; retaining it everywhere caps throughput.
- **Learning objective:** Locate founder dependence within a venture’s delivery model and identify a scalable boundary without assuming automation is the answer.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-09 concerns leadership control and ENT-19 buying an owner-dependent firm. This is endogenous service scaling inside an existing young venture, not succession or acquisition.
- **Unverified research leads:** Entrepreneurial scaling and founder-dependence research; service productisation studies.

### ENT-26 · Endorsed by whom. Credible to whom?
- **Opening decision:** A young venture can seek a prestigious general award or obtain a narrowly relevant demonstration with a respected professional user, using the same development allowance.
- **Reveal:** Buyers and prospective employees evaluate different signals. The award supports one audience’s concern but does not address the buyer’s specific operating risk.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish legitimacy with different audiences from generic publicity or proof of demand.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-11 makes expertise observable to customers; ENT-13 expands a founder’s network. This compares audience-specific legitimacy needs across several stakeholders in an emerging organisation.
- **Unverified research leads:** New-venture legitimacy and liability-of-newness research; signalling studies with explicit audience differences.

### ENT-27 · Learn from one experiment. Keep the learning comparable?
- **Opening decision:** A founding team chooses a bundled offer redesign that can test overall commercial appeal quickly, or slower staged comparisons with predeclared hypotheses and stop/change rules that better identify which change matters.
- **Reveal:** An exhibit lists price, user group and delivery conditions for each past pilot: all changed together. A second exhibit offers comparable next-pilot designs and explicit thresholds, exposing the tradeoff between quick package validation and interpretable component evidence.
- **Learning objective:** Use disciplined hypothesis revision and falsifiable decision rules in venture learning without demanding false experimental precision.
- **Distinctness:** IS-10 allocates experimental units; ENT-12 revises the business after segment evidence. This centres experimental identification and predeclared stop/change rules. ### ENT-28 · A family successor. A fair path into responsibility?

- **Reveal:** One family candidate has strong outside experience but incomplete knowledge of an internal process; a nonfamily candidate has the reverse profile. Both need relevant evidence, and ownership succession need not determine managerial succession.
- **Learning objective:** Separate family ownership continuity, managerial competence and a credible development path.
- **Distinctness:** ENT-09 concerns management handover to an already competent, appointed successor. This precedes appointment and compares candidate development and selection processes; ENT-01 instead concerns selling the family name.

### ENT-29 · An internal idea. An independent venture?

- **Reveal:** The idea needs decisions and customers outside the employer’s normal system, but also depends on a capability the spinout cannot assume it will receive indefinitely.
- **Learning objective:** Compare corporate entrepreneurship with organisational separation, specifying dependencies that survive the boundary change.
- **Distinctness:** STR-29 designs exploration inside an established firm; ENT-08 chooses product sales versus licensing. This defines the new organisation and its transition from employer resources, not an innovation-unit chart or IP monetisation route.

### ENT-30 · One venture story. Several testable assumptions?
- **Opening decision:** With a fixed research budget, a founding team chooses whether to investigate its most uncertain demand assumption or a less uncertain but upstream delivery dependency before further development.
- **Reveal:** A dependency-map exhibit and evidence-cost table show that one cheap-to-test dependency could invalidate several downstream projections; another highly uncertain assumption would not change the immediate action. Learners choose the order, not an experimental design.

- **Distinctness:** ENT-07 chooses funded milestones; ECO-16 evaluates another supplier quotation. ## IS · Information systems, AI and business analytics

### IS-21 · The number improved. Beyond ordinary variation?
- **Opening decision:** A manager chooses a reversible, limited rollout of a service change with monitoring or waits for a larger comparable sample. Both options honestly report uncertainty; delaying also postpones potential benefits.
- **Reveal:** A supplied interval includes a small loss and a useful gain. A decision table gives rollout reversibility, waiting costs and monitoring limits. Sample design is valid; neither option may describe the change as a confirmed improvement.
- **Learning objective:** Interpret statistical precision and practical importance without treating “not significant” as proof of no effect.
- **Distinctness:** IS-11 concerns who responds; OPS-10 concerns a time-ordered process warning. This isolates sampling uncertainty in an estimated difference.
- **Unverified research leads:** ASA statements on statistical significance; authoritative confidence-interval teaching resources.

### IS-22 · One winning result. How many chances to win?
- **Opening decision:** An analyst tests many harmless landing-page variations and proposes rolling out the variation with the lowest p-value, or confirming it on a reserved sample.
- **Reveal:** All initial variations were tried against the same control and the reported winner was selected after seeing the results. The holdout was never used in selection.
- **Learning objective:** Recognise multiple comparisons and selection-driven optimism; separate exploration from confirmation.
- **Distinctness:** IS-10 prevents cross-group spillovers; MKT-16 measures cannibalisation. This concerns the number of statistical opportunities and an honest confirmation set.
- **Unverified research leads:** Multiple-testing literature; original online-experimentation papers; ASA guidance.

### IS-23 · The worst branch improved. Did the intervention work?
- **Opening decision:** A chain gives extra nonpunitive coaching to last month’s lowest-scoring branches and sees improvement. It can expand immediately or compare against a credible contemporaneous group.
- **Reveal:** The branches were selected from an unusually bad month, and similar untreated low-scoring branches also improve. No employee evaluation uses these noisy scores.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish regression to the mean from an intervention effect.
- **Distinctness:** IS-01 questions a causal chain in aggregate data; PPL-26 diagnoses assignment difficulty. This isolates selection on an extreme noisy outcome, not confounding by task mix.
- **Unverified research leads:** Regression-to-the-mean methodology; controlled evaluation teaching examples; primary statistical papers.

### IS-24 · The same customer. Two different records?
- **Opening decision:** A distributor can combine records using exact company-name matches or a reviewed entity-resolution rule using several authorised business fields.
- **Reveal:** A trading-name change produces a false split, while two unrelated firms have similar names. Both merging and failing to merge can distort a relationship measure.
- **Learning objective:** Evaluate false matches and missed matches in entity resolution; keep uncertain links reviewable.
- **Distinctness:** IS-05 aligns quantities and units; IS-20 traces transformations. This asks which records represent the same entity, not what a field means or where a chart came from.
- **Unverified research leads:** Official record-linkage methodology; entity-resolution quality studies; data-quality guidance.

### IS-25 · Recorded at noon. Happened at noon?
- **Opening decision:** An operations analyst groups transactions by the time they reach the reporting system or by a validated event timestamp to diagnose a service peak.
- **Reveal:** One branch transmits records in batches after a temporary connection delay. The apparent noon demand spike partly reflects arrival time rather than customer activity.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish event time from processing time and define an appropriate reporting cutoff for delayed records.
- **Distinctness:** IS-06 concerns information available when forecasting; IS-09 concerns dashboard freshness. This addresses the temporal meaning of a record and late-arrival revisions, not simply reporting more often.
- **Unverified research leads:** Stream-processing event-time documentation; official transaction timestamp/data-quality standards.

### IS-26 · Two confirmations. One order?
- **Opening decision:** A booking tool can treat each retry as a new request or recognise a stable transaction identifier and show the prior result. The design must preserve legitimate repeated purchases.
- **Reveal:** A slow confirmation causes a customer to retry the same intended request; another customer deliberately makes two separate requests with similar details.
- **Learning objective:** Explain idempotency and transaction identity through a business duplicate-prevention problem.
- **Distinctness:** IS-19 reconciles migration records; OPS-08 physically prevents mistakes. This concerns retry-safe digital transactions under uncertain response delivery, not system cutover or a generic second check.
- **Unverified research leads:** Official API idempotency documentation; transaction-processing teaching material; no credentials or production actions involved.

### IS-27 · The sensor is consistent. Is it measuring correctly?
- **Opening decision:** A facilities analyst chooses between averaging more readings from one sensor or spending the same noncritical monitoring budget on calibration against a reference instrument.
- **Reveal:** Repeated readings vary little, but a supplied reference check shows a stable offset. The device is not used for safety or medical decisions.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish measurement precision from accuracy and systematic error from random noise.
- **Distinctness:** IS-12 treats missing values; IS-16 compares predictive error costs. This addresses the measurement process before analysis rather than imputation or forecasts.
- **Unverified research leads:** NIST metrology guidance; authoritative calibration and measurement-uncertainty teaching sources.

### IS-28 · The line looks dramatic. What does the axis say?
- **Opening decision:** An analyst presents a small cost movement using a tightly cropped axis or a paired display showing both absolute level and change, with identical verified underlying data.
- **Reveal:** Viewers infer a much larger budget threat from the first chart; the relevant decision threshold lies outside the visible cropped range.
- **Learning objective:** Choose chart scales and contextual reference points that support the decision without implying that every nonzero baseline is deceptive.
- **Distinctness:** IS-20 verifies lineage and reproducibility; MKT-14 studies consumer reference prices. This isolates truthful visual encoding and the reader’s quantitative inference.
- **Unverified research leads:** Graphical-perception research; official statistical visualisation guidance.

### IS-29 · Assigned to the pilot. Actually using it?
- **Opening decision:** A company evaluates an optional training tool by comparing everyone randomly offered access with controls, or comparing only active users with nonusers.
- **Reveal:** Uptake is voluntary and higher among already motivated staff. A valid random-assignment record remains available; no worker outcomes are used for personnel decisions.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish intent-to-treat evidence from selected participation comparisons, and state which business question each estimate addresses.
- **Distinctness:** IS-10 concerns interference between experimental units; IS-11 concerns survey response. Here random assignment is preserved but adherence is selective.
- **Unverified research leads:** Authoritative intention-to-treat methodology; original encouragement-design experiments; causal-inference teaching references.

### IS-30 · A summary alone. Or bounded retrieval?
- **Opening decision:** A team chooses between two safe internal AI designs: a text-only summariser of user-selected documents, or an approved retrieval workflow restricted to an allowlisted knowledge collection, with read-only access and no external sending.
- **Reveal:** A benign test passage contains instruction-like language unrelated to the task. Both designs must treat it as source content, never authority; retrieval offers broader coverage but adds material requiring provenance and boundary tests.
- **Learning objective:** Compare task coverage with access surface while preserving instruction/data separation in either design. Unsafe obedience to document instructions is not an acceptable option.
- **Distinctness:** IS-02 addresses answer accuracy and handoff; IS-17 assigns human permissions. This concerns AI source-content boundaries and deliberately limited retrieval authority.
- **Unverified research leads:** OWASP prompt-injection guidance; NIST AI risk-management material; official retrieval/tool-security documentation checked at drafting time.


## GVR · Governance, law, ethics and risk

### GVR-21 — A cheap contract. An uncapped exposure?
- **Opening decision:** A company buying event-management services chooses a lower supplier fee with limited contractual recovery for specified losses, or a higher fee with broader recovery. The company is the buyer in both offers; compare total retained exposure, not only the amount it pays.

- **Unverified research leads:** Durable-goods monopoly teaching materials; public markdown policies; empirical strategic-consumer studies.

### ECO-26 — Same target. Same reduction at every plant?
- **Opening decision:** Two fictional, compliant facilities compare a uniform additional reduction standard with a tradable-permit pilot under an identical aggregate environmental target.
- **Reveal:** Their verified marginal abatement costs differ. Calculate a feasible trade and identify who gains, keeping the total cap fixed.
- **Learning objective:** Separate allocation of rights from cost-effective allocation of abatement.
- **Distinctness:** ECO-18 common-pool depletion concerns resource use over time; this case concerns equal total reductions achieved at different total costs.
- **Unverified research leads:** World Bank carbon-pricing materials; environmental economics texts; official emissions-trading explainers.

### ECO-27 — Win the prize. Was the contest worth it?
- **Opening decision:** A business association considers a small winner-take-all voluntary promotional contest versus a fixed participation reward; spending and prize rules are explicit.
- **Reveal:** Rival responses increase total effort expenditure without changing the prize or productive output in the simplified model.
- **Learning objective:** Recognise rent dissipation and distinguish private winning incentives from collective net value.
- **Distinctness:** ECO-02 is an auction transferring money to a seller; this case expends real resources on winning, with dissipation rather than bidding revenue as the mechanism.
- **Unverified research leads:** Contest theory introductions; public procurement bid-cost studies; experimental rent-seeking research.

### ECO-28 — Choose freely now. Restrict a future choice?
- **Opening decision:** A self-employed adult chooses a flexible savings arrangement or a voluntary commitment account with a clearly specified release rule, retaining an adequate emergency reserve.
- **Reveal:** The person anticipates a recurring temptation that they themselves want to resist, but also identifies a plausible expense excluded from the rule.
- **Learning objective:** Explain present bias and the value and cost of self-chosen commitment without making paternalistic welfare assumptions.
- **Distinctness:** FIN-18 cash maturity concerns availability on known dates; this case concerns a person's time-inconsistent preferences and voluntary restriction.
- **Unverified research leads:** Behavioural economics commitment-device research; evaluated savings experiments; consumer-protection guidance.

### ECO-29 — The default stays. Is it the preferred choice?
- **Opening decision:** A fictional cooperative must choose an opt-in or opt-out default for an optional, reversible service, with equally clear disclosure and easy, costless changes.
- **Reveal:** A brief active-choice exercise yields a different pattern from passive enrolment; it is small descriptive evidence, not proof of welfare effects.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish inertia, transaction costs and preference revelation in default outcomes.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-15 framing changes presentation of a benefit; this case changes the choice architecture while holding terms and information fixed. Privacy/consent requirements remain constraints.
- **Unverified research leads:** Behavioural economics default experiments; consumer-choice architecture guidance; public active-choice programme evaluations.

### ECO-30 — A better private deal. A cost outside the contract?
- **Opening decision:** Two fictional neighbouring businesses compare separate noise-control investment with a voluntary negotiated arrangement, within mandatory standards and without bargaining away third-party rights.
- **Reveal:** A third affected business is not party to the original agreement, and negotiating all parties carries a real transaction cost.
- **Learning objective:** Identify externalities, the limits of bilateral bargaining and transaction-cost conditions for negotiated solutions.
- **Distinctness:** SUS-04 is voluntary financing of a non-excludable shared benefit; ECO-30 is a harmful spillover and the reach of a negotiated agreement.
- **Unverified research leads:** Open externality/Coase-theorem materials; community mediation examples; environmental economics case studies.

## HSP · Hospitality, tourism, property and service management

### HSP-21 — The room is larger. Does it feel usable?
- **Opening decision:** A hotel compares two guest-room furniture plans within the same safe, accessible footprint: more amenities or more clear functional space.
- **Reveal:** A representative task walkthrough reveals that luggage, work and dining functions compete for one surface. Guests can complete tasks safely in both layouts, but with different inconvenience.
- **Learning objective:** Evaluate room usability through guest activities rather than floor-area or amenity counts.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-06 is restaurant party-size capacity; HSP-12 route wayfinding. This case is task-level usability inside a room.
- **Unverified research leads:** Hospitality servicescape research; universal design guidance; public guest-room usability studies.

### HSP-22 — A short stay. A long housekeeping workload?
- **Opening decision:** A serviced-apartment operator compares two booking mixes with the same occupied room-nights but different stay lengths; required cleaning quality is fixed.
- **Reveal:** Departures require a materially different workload from stayover servicing. Calculate workload from turnovers, not occupancy alone.
- **Learning objective:** Relate length-of-stay mix to service workload and scheduling.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-03 concerns displacement revenue across nights; HSP-13 interruption preferences. This case concerns housekeeping workload driven by turnovers.
- **Unverified research leads:** Hotel housekeeping workload studies; public serviced-apartment operations guidance; labour-safe scheduling research.

### HSP-23 — A beautiful display. A clear food choice?
- **Opening decision:** A restaurant compares two compliant buffet layouts, both providing complete allergen information and trained assistance: cuisine clusters or meal-function clusters.
- **Reveal:** A small observation study shows guests repeatedly backtracking to assemble a complete meal, although every dish is easy to see.
- **Learning objective:** Design a service sequence around how guests combine choices, not merely display attractiveness.
- **Distinctness:** MKT-10 choice overload concerns assortment size; this case holds assortment fixed and changes the physical assembly journey.
- **Unverified research leads:** Restaurant service-design research; public foodservice layout guidance; authoritative allergen-accessibility requirements.

### HSP-24 — More reviews. A more trustworthy picture?
- **Opening decision:** A tourism operator chooses how to display verified guest feedback: a chronological feed or a clearly disclosed relevance filter, with critical reviews preserved and no misleading suppression.
- **Reveal:** Old reviews concern an operator or facility that has materially changed; neither deleting all history nor averaging indiscriminately fairly describes the present offer.
- **Learning objective:** Interpret review recency, relevance and provenance while preserving transparency.
- **Distinctness:** IS-11 selection bias concerns who responds; this case concerns relevance over time in an existing review record, not respondent representativeness.
- **Unverified research leads:** Tourism review-platform transparency research; consumer authority review guidance; hotel reputation studies.

### HSP-25 — The festival fills the town. Does the town benefit?
- **Opening decision:** A destination organiser compares a concentrated festival weekend with smaller distributed sessions, using local capacity and stakeholder objectives.
- **Reveal:** Some expenditure replaces normal visitor spending and some leaks to nonlocal suppliers. The exhibit separates gross attendance spending from additional local value without claiming a complete multiplier model.
- **Learning objective:** Evaluate event additionality and local value retention.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-09 counts useful networking conversations; SUS-19 tests whether a funded project would occur anyway. This case separates tourism expenditure substitution from leakage to assess additional destination-level value.
- **Unverified research leads:** Public event-impact evaluation manuals; tourism satellite-account explainers; independent festival evaluations.

### HSP-26 — The check-in works. Does the handoff work?
- **Opening decision:** A tour company chooses between a single guest-facing coordinator and separate specialist contact points across transport, lodging and activities; both routes have clear emergency coverage.
- **Reveal:** A change recorded correctly by one provider never reaches another. Students redesign an authorised handoff, not merely add more messages or transfer unnecessary personal data.
- **Learning objective:** Manage service continuity across organisational boundaries.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-02 repairs a failed stay after the event; this case prevents a package-service discontinuity at a provider handoff. IS-05 is data specification, not guest ownership across providers.
- **Unverified research leads:** Tourism service-blueprinting research; public package-travel operations guidance; guest journey coordination studies.

### HSP-27 — A famous attraction. A meaningful route?
- **Opening decision:** A visitor centre compares an itinerary of landmark stops with a thematic route containing fewer landmarks, within the same time and accessibility constraints.
- **Reveal:** The visitors' learning aim requires context connecting sites, but one stop has a short access window. Re-plan sequencing while making explicit what is sacrificed.
- **Learning objective:** Build interpretive coherence rather than maximise the number of sights.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-10 concerns community voice in storytelling; HSP-20 different members' preferences. This case concerns a shared learning purpose and a coherent sequence.
- **Unverified research leads:** Interpretation association guidance; museum/heritage route design studies; tourism experience research.

### HSP-28 — A fully booked spa. A workable appointment book?
- **Opening decision:** A wellness service schedules a fixed mix of nonmedical appointments using either uniform slots or duration-specific slots; staff breaks and hygiene turnaround are mandatory.
- **Reveal:** Two services require the same specialist room for part of the appointment but not throughout. A feasible schedule must coordinate both practitioner and room occupancy.
- **Learning objective:** Understand coupled resource scheduling in a high-contact service.
- **Distinctness:** OPS-07 queue staffing concerns counter capacity; HSP-06 static seating compatibility. This is sequential use of two resources within appointments, not more utilisation at any cost.
- **Unverified research leads:** Service operations scheduling research; reputable spa operational guidance; occupational hygiene requirements.

### HSP-29 — A fair recovery. Must it be identical?
- **Opening decision:** A hotel designs two transparent recovery menus for a stipulated non-safety service failure: equal cash compensation or a guest-selected menu of equivalent operator cost.
- **Reveal:** Guests differ in the usefulness of late checkout, dining credit and cash, but everyone keeps baseline contractual rights. Compare consistency of entitlement with personalisation of remedy.
- **Learning objective:** Distinguish procedural consistency from identical recovery instruments.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-02 solves one guest's disrupted trip; GVR-16 applies a waiver standard. This case designs a general, rights-preserving recovery menu before individual requests.
- **Unverified research leads:** Hospitality service-recovery justice research; consumer remedy guidance; experimental compensation-choice studies.

### HSP-30 — A busy season. A destination all year?
- **Opening decision:** A destination partnership chooses a shoulder-season resident-facing programme or a peak-season visitor programme under the same bounded budget.
- **Reveal:** Off-season transport and opening hours constrain the proposed experience; unused hotel capacity alone is insufficient to make a destination available.
- **Learning objective:** Treat seasonality as an ecosystem coordination problem, not simply vacant rooms to discount.
- **Distinctness:** HSP-03 compares stay-pattern displacement at one hotel; ECO-17 two-supplier launch coordination. This case assesses the connected destination offer across seasons and who it serves.
- **Unverified research leads:** UN Tourism seasonality reports; destination management plans; evaluated off-season tourism programmes.
