PPL-35 · Macau · 15–20 minutes · Participative change · Decision boundaries · Feedback closure

A better plan. Whose input shaped it?

A fixed goal can leave implementation open

Staff participation can improve a change proposal without giving everyone a veto over its purpose. Consultation guidance distinguishes a genuine opportunity to influence implementation from simply announcing a finished decision. The office, timetable and proposals below are fictional; no source guarantees agreement or supplies a Macau legal conclusion.

Your classroom decision

You manage fictional Harbour Desk, a Macau office preparing routine client quotations. Leadership has settled a service-quality goal: every quotation must receive a second-person completeness check before release by 16:00 on its scheduled day. Staffing, pay, contractual hours, breaks, leave and existing employee protections are unchanged.

The team must choose how to place the checking duty within normal paid work. The goal and release deadline are fixed; the checking time and handover arrangement remain genuinely open. There is no redundancy, contract amendment or safety shortcut. Required representative involvement and any applicable legal processes are protected outside this additional design exercise.

A uses the manager’s draft duty rota, placing checking at 11:00, followed by a briefing and factual corrections before release. B uses one paid 40-minute staff design session to examine the draft and alternatives, followed by a manager decision. A is quicker; B can surface practical knowledge but needs preparation and a disciplined scope.

Both routes fit the funded preparation budget. Service cover allows everyone affected to contribute during paid time, directly or through an agreed representative; private written input is also available. The rota must be finalised on Friday for a trial two weeks later. Only one consultation cycle fits, including written input, the session and the response.

The existing staffed checking arrangement remains funded and meets the service goal until a replacement passes review. Neither route authorises an unchecked quotation or an invalid rota. The proposed 11:00 arrangement has not yet been tested against every client-file dependency; neat presentation is not evidence of practical feasibility.

Round 2 · More information

Before either route is committed, staff identify a standard client-file update that arrives at 13:00 on every scheduled release day. It contains information needed for the completeness check. The manager’s draft assumed the file was complete in the morning. Moving ahead unchanged would miss that information; this is not a request to weaken the quality goal.

The operations lead verifies two revised arrangements: a checking slot at 14:00 with a written handover, or a slot at 15:00 with a brief direct handover. Each fits the same staffing, protected breaks and 16:00 release deadline, including enough time for corrections. Neither has an established quality or workload advantage in this exercise.

The manager can correct the draft directly under A, but has not heard how either handover fits colleagues’ routine work. B’s single cycle can still compare the two verified alternatives and identify concerns. Staff cannot reopen the service goal or demand an unlimited series of meetings. The manager must publish the decision and reasons on Friday.

Your decision

Choose the process now. Design the one-cycle consultation or explain a defensible manager-led correction. Say how input can change the plan, how a rejected suggestion will be answered and what happens if a new unresolved constraint prevents safe implementation.

Debrief · Participation can improve the means

The 13:00 dependency invalidates the original 11:00 draft, not the service-quality goal. Staff supplied information the manager lacked. That is useful implementation knowledge, not evidence of resistance or a request for a veto. Both A and B must reject the unchanged draft and preserve the existing compliant service.

I would choose B now because there are two workable arrangements and unresolved experience of the handovers. A remains defensible if the manager obtains sufficient relevant information through its correction route and explains the choice. The reveal supports listening; it does not prove a meeting always beats competent direct management.

Debrief · Make the influence real and bounded

Before the session, share the fixed goal, the two alternatives, their checked constraints, the Friday decision date and the manager’s responsibility. Ask for concrete effects on handovers and normal duties. Do not advertise an open choice if one arrangement has secretly been selected. Explain that suggestions must preserve agreed protections and the quality check.

Use the paid 40 minutes for ten minutes confirming facts, 20 minutes comparing implementation concerns and ten minutes identifying recommendations and unresolved issues. Collect accessible written input beforehand and allow a representative or private route. The time allocation totals 40 minutes; it is a facilitation plan, not a proven optimal formula.

Debrief · Consultation is not a popularity contest

Consider whose work each proposal affects and the evidence behind concerns. Do not count silence as approval or let the loudest group decide. A majority preference cannot override rights, required coverage or the checked deadline. Conversely, a minority concern may reveal a valid constraint even when most people prefer the other arrangement.

The manager retains the supplied decision responsibility, but must genuinely consider relevant input. Consultation does not promise that every proposal will win or every employee will be satisfied. It also does not replace any legal bargaining, consultation or individual-consent requirement that applies outside this simplified exercise.

Debrief · Close the feedback loop

On Friday, explain the chosen arrangement, the input that changed it and the reasons other suggestions were not adopted. For example, distinguish a handover preference from a verified coverage problem. Confirm responsibilities and how to report implementation difficulties. Do not claim staff endorsed the final plan merely because they attended.

If a new material constraint remains unresolved, use the funded existing arrangement and escalate the implementation issue rather than force a defective rota through the deadline. Friday can produce a documented fallback decision. The fixed quality goal is not permission to ignore evidence or use unpaid work to fill a gap.

Debrief · Review the trial without promising acceptance

During the trial, check whether required information reaches the reviewer, checks and corrections finish before release, and duties remain workable within paid hours. Invite feedback from people doing and receiving handovers. Record problems and changes without using disagreement as a performance label. Do not treat fewer complaints as proof that everyone agrees.

The original missing dependency is one concrete finding, not an estimated productivity gain or causal proof of participation’s effect. A useful process links staff knowledge to a decision and explains the result. It can improve a plan while leaving disagreement about the chosen option.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: choose the process and state the decision boundary. Minutes 4–8: use the missing file dependency to separate goal from method. Minutes 8–13: design a single realistic consultation cycle. Minutes 13–18: practise explaining a rejected suggestion and a fallback decision.

PPL-13 allocates scarce preferred leave through fair procedures; PPL-27 compares advance notice arrangements. Here leave and notice are fixed. Unlike PPL-34’s merger identity, the contribution is practical implementation knowledge. Assess honest influence, bounded choice and a visible response, not which employee deserves a particular shift.
