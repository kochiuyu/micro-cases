SUS-33 · Foshan · Greater Bay Area · 15–20 minutes · Industrial symbiosis · Supply interdependence · Backup service

A useful by-product. A dependable input?

An exchange needs a service plan

Industrial symbiosis connects one business’s surplus resources with another’s needs. Heat-recovery guidance also stresses supply reliability, technical fit and agreed responsibilities. The two Foshan businesses and all quantities, prices and emissions factors below are fictional; they are not measured Greater Bay Area performance or local tariffs.

Your classroom decision

You manage Reed Works, a fictional Foshan producer of non-food moulded-fibre packaging. Its neighbouring factory offers residual process heat that is currently rejected unused. Qualified engineers have confirmed suitable temperature, separation, transfer equipment and safe operation. No extra production or fuel burning at the neighbour is required to create the recoverable heat.

Reed needs 1,000 MWh of useful heat delivered to its process over the next year. Product output, quality, staff conditions and delivery dates must remain unchanged. Heat demand cannot be moved between months. The neighbour expects 1,000 MWh recoverable at Reed’s delivery point after transfer losses, but the matched calendar has not yet been reviewed.

Option I renews Reed’s independent, all-year heat-service contract. It costs CNY20,000 fixed plus CNY500 per delivered MWh, covering plant, fuel, operation and maintenance. The service meets the entire demand profile. Its operating emissions factor is stipulated as 0.25 tonnes CO₂e per delivered MWh, including all operating auxiliaries.

Option S leases the new heat link for one year at a fixed CNY320,000, covering connection, equipment, recovered heat, labour, maintenance and end-of-year removal. There is no extra purchase or future lease obligation. Pumping electricity is included in that price but adds 15 tonnes CO₂e for the year in the supplied operating-emissions model.

Choosing S ends access to the equipment supplied under I; that plant is not Reed’s free standby asset. Any backup must be separately contracted. Both complete options can fit the CNY550,000 annual budget. No supply commitment has been signed. First compare the headline opportunity, then inspect timing and the backup quotation.

Round 2 · More information

The supplier’s mandatory planned maintenance closes its heat-producing process throughout Reed’s busiest month. Reed needs 300 MWh in that month and 700 MWh over the other eleven. The neighbour can deliver no heat during shutdown and up to 1,000 MWh during the other months. Its shutdown cannot safely be moved.

Engineering checks confirm the 700 MWh needed outside shutdown can be met at the required times and temperatures. No inter-month heat storage or alternative buyer is available within this proposal. The remaining 300 MWh potential surplus stays unused; it cannot be counted as heat delivered to Reed or as displaced independent supply.

A qualified backup provider offers S an annual availability package for CNY40,000 plus CNY600 per MWh delivered. It has confirmed capacity for the full peak and a tested switch-over that preserves production. The fixed fee includes reserved plant, standby, connection, tests, labour and removal; variable heat includes fuel and operating auxiliaries.

The quoted backup supplies the planned 300 MWh shortfall. Its stipulated operating factor is 0.30 tonnes CO₂e per delivered MWh, including all operating auxiliaries. The availability fee adds no further operating emissions in this simplified model. The heat link’s 15 tonnes includes its complete annual pumping and control electricity, including standby.

For the numerical comparison, all other availability follows the verified plan; no additional random outage is assumed. A real agreement must still assign notice, failure-response and exit responsibilities. The backup is not unlimited free insurance: extra delivered heat beyond 300 MWh would incur the same variable rate and require a revised budget.

Both I and S with the quoted backup meet the same service, safety and environmental requirements. Neither has a grant or carbon credit. Compare complete annual cash costs and the stated operating-emissions boundary. Management has not set a carbon price or ordered you to choose the lowest-cost option regardless of emissions.

Your decision

Would you select independent supply or the heat link with backup? Calculate useful recovered heat, full annual cost and modelled operating emissions, then state the tradeoff and the supply responsibilities your recommendation requires.

Debrief · Match service across time

Only 700 MWh of recovered heat meets Reed’s actual schedule, or 70% of its annual need. The other 300 MWh must come from backup. Annual potential equals annual demand, but heat unavailable in the busiest month cannot be replaced by an unused surplus in another month without a feasible storage or scheduling solution.

Keeping production unchanged is part of the comparison, not an optional extra. The supplier’s maintenance is a known constraint, not a reason to bypass safety. Dependence on another production system creates a service obligation that an annual resource total does not reveal.

Debrief · Price the complete link

I costs CNY20,000 + 1,000 × CNY500 = CNY520,000. S requires CNY320,000 + CNY40,000 + 300 × CNY600 = CNY540,000. The link-only figure suggested CNY200,000 less spending, but the necessary CNY220,000 backup package reverses that comparison: S is CNY20,000 more expensive.

Both fit the CNY550,000 budget: I leaves CNY30,000; S leaves CNY10,000. Do not add the old CNY20,000 fixed fee to S, because that contract is not renewed. Conversely, do not assume its equipment remains available without a contract. No capital expenditure or additional lease year is hidden in these quotes.

Debrief · Count the remaining heat source

I’s modelled operating emissions are 1,000 × 0.25 = 250 tonnes CO₂e. S has 300 × 0.30 = 90 tonnes from backup plus 15 from the link: 105 tonnes. The difference is 145 tonnes, or 58% below I. Ignoring backup would incorrectly report only 15 tonnes and a 235-tonne difference.

The supplier’s original production and its operating emissions are unchanged in both options. Only heat otherwise rejected is recovered, so those unchanged emissions cancel in this specified comparison. This is not a claim that recovered heat is inherently zero-carbon or that every industrial-symbiosis project avoids the same emissions.

The boundary covers the stated heat-service operating emissions, including pumping and backup, not equipment manufacture, construction, upstream fuel supply or full lifecycle effects. The factors are invented assumptions, not regional inventory factors or measured causal reductions. No carbon credit, corporate accounting certification or environmental advertising claim follows from this calculation.

Debrief · Recommend a dependable exchange

S is defensible if the modelled 145-tonne operating reduction justifies CNY20,000 more annual spending and the narrower budget margin. I is defensible if cost and independence take priority. The case supplies no mandatory carbon valuation. Neither lower emissions nor a circular label alone settles that preference.

For S, agree delivered heat quality and timing, planned-maintenance notice, metering, asset ownership, operating responsibilities and funded backup before committing. Name an accountable manager at each firm and test the response to supply interruption. These are diligence priorities, not a substitute for locally appropriate engineering, legal and environmental review.

Monitor delivered recovered heat, backup use, electricity and costs against the joint calendar. If production or shutdown plans change, recheck the complete service rather than keeping the original annual savings claim. Any future storage, third supplier or rescheduling proposal needs its own feasible, priced comparison; none is free in this decision.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: decide whether annual heat and link price suffice. Minutes 4–9: allocate the 700 MWh recovery and 300 MWh backup. Minutes 9–14: reconcile CNY540,000 and 105 tonnes. Minutes 14–18: defend the CNY20,000 tradeoff and identify who acts when the neighbour’s supply schedule changes.

SUS-12 moves electricity consumption within one operation; SUS-20 funds maintenance after opening. Here two independent production systems create a new supply dependency. The annual quantity is technically recoverable but not fully usable at the recipient’s required times. Keep the service-equivalent backup inside both the financial and environmental comparison.
