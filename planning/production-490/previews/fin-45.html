FIN-45 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Option value · Payoff replication · Volatility · No-arbitrage benchmark

The same forecast. A dearer option?

Weekly single-stock options arrive in Hong Kong

On 4 November 2024, HKEX introduced weekly options on ten underlying stocks. The exchange described short-dated stock options as a way to manage exposure around specific events. Its pricing calculator treats volatility as one input among several. The exercise uses a fictional expiry-only call rather than the American exercise terms of HKEX-listed stock options.

Your classroom decision

At a fictional Hong Kong brokerage’s training meeting, analyst Mei is asked whether a HK$4.50 call quote is rich or cheap relative to an exact two-outcome replication. It costs HK$100 to buy one share today. The fictional cash-settled call pays the amount by which the expiry share price exceeds its HK$105 strike; below that price it pays nothing.

Initially Mei models two equally likely expiry prices, HK$90 and HK$110. The stock pays no dividends. The option cannot be exercised before expiry. One share and the option refer to the same underlying and settle at expiry.

Assume fractional stock positions and borrowing or lending at zero interest, with no fees, credit limits, collateral constraints or interim trading. Replicate one call with a stock holding and financing chosen today and kept to expiry. Compare the resulting model value to the quote, not a recommendation to trade.

Round 2 · More information

A colleague supplies an alternative scenario model before the training decision: there are still two equally likely outcomes, but the share might end at HK$80 or HK$120 instead. The spot price, strike, quoted premium, funding terms and time to expiry are unchanged.

The average share price at expiry is still HK$100. The call now pays zero in the down state and HK$15 in the up state.

Your decision

With the wider outcomes, what stock holding and loan reproduce the call, and does the classification of the HK$4.50 quote change?

Debrief · Match the payoff difference

The call payoff changes by HK$5 when the stock price changes by HK$20 between states. A quarter of one share matches that change. The stock alone pays HK$22.50 in the down state, so borrow HK$22.50 today and repay it at expiry.

Debrief · Compare like with like

A quarter of a share pays HK$27.50 in the up state; after the same repayment it pays HK$5. The exact portfolio costs 0.25 × HK$100 − HK$22.50 = HK$2.50. The stated quote is HK$2 above that benchmark.

Debrief · Recompute the portfolio

The call payoff now changes by HK$15 across a HK$40 stock-price range, so hold 0.375 share. Borrow HK$30 to cancel 0.375 × HK$80 in the down state. In the up state the stock holding pays HK$45 before repayment and HK$15 after.

Debrief · The premium verdict reverses

The new exact portfolio costs 0.375 × HK$100 − HK$30 = HK$7.50. A HK$4.50 quote is HK$3 below that value. The mean share price did not change, but the call captures more upside and still pays no less than zero in the down state.

Do not read an objective probability from a quote

The costs come from matched payoffs, funding and the current stock price, not from discounting the supplied 50–50 forecast. In both of these symmetric zero-rate examples the numerical result happens to equal the forecast mean payoff, which does not hold generally.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to frame the decision, 4 for the first calculation and a vote, 4 for the second round, and 5 to defend or revise a recommendation. Ask each group to cite a number and name the assumption that would change its call.

Have students verify both terminal cash flows for each portfolio before using today’s price. Contrast this exercise with pricing a fixed state-contingent claim: the wider states here change the payout generated by the same strike. Two states and a zero financing rate are classroom conveniences, not descriptions of market options or estimates of implied volatility.

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All people, prices, payoffs, quantities and contract terms in the classroom exercise are fictional. The opening news context describes the cited historical developments, not the case’s figures or recommendations.

  1. HKEX 2024 final results, equity futures and options market development. Confirms the November start of weekly stock options on ten underlyings; does not report the model’s premium.
  2. HKEX, May 2024 announcement of weekly stock options. Describes the proposed additions and how weekly expiries fit alongside existing monthly contracts.
  3. HKEX, Weekly Stock Options. Explains the potential use around earnings and other events; the classroom claim has deliberately different exercise terms.
  4. HKEX, Options / Warrants Calculator. Lists volatility and other valuation inputs, distinguishes fitted implied volatility, and notes the American exercise style of listed stock options.
  5. Cox, Ross and Rubinstein, Option Pricing: A Simplified Approach. Original discrete-time replication approach. Its idealised financing and trading assumptions do not yield an observed local quote.
