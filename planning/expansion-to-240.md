## Editorial approach

The expansion should broaden the decisions students can practise, rather than repeat old mechanisms with new businesses. Each proposal specifies a choice, a meaningful second-round reveal, the concept being taught, and its boundary from a nearby existing case. Existing case codes in these comparisons refer to the current collection, not to new sequential global numbers.

- **Start with a business decision.** Specify who must act, what they can choose, the scarce resource or constraint, and why two defensible positions exist. When evidence clearly rules out an option, explain that rather than manufacture false balance.
- **Use one primary learning objective.** Keep technical derivations and terminology in optional debriefs. Self-contained exhibits must carry every fact needed for the vote; students should not have to open a link.
- **Make the reveal do work.** Vary contractual facts, customer purposes, timing, evidence quality, operational constraints, denominators and stakeholder perspectives. Do not make every answer “pilot it,” “collect more data,” or “combine both for free.”
- **Separate fact from fiction.** A researched, dated event motivates the question; the classroom business and numerical assumptions are original, explicitly fictional unless faithfully sourced. A policy announcement does not prove outcomes. Legal duties, safety and individual rights are constraints, not optional trade-offs.
- **Keep illustrations consistent.** Match the established watercolor-and-ink narrative style. Inspect two actual existing images before generating each new scene. Show the opening decision, not the answer or reveal; use fictional adults, no readable figures/text or real logos. Inspect full and card sizes, then export the optimized 760 × 389 JPG. Use accessible HTML tables/charts for exact numbers.
- **Highlight both decisions.** Use the existing pale-gold `student-prompt` box with label, left border and bold question on page and slides; retain the questions and reveal in the student handout. Keep optional references separate.

## ACC · Accounting, audit and management control

### ACC-11 · Same units. Same overhead?

**Opening decision:** A custom badge workshop must quote two repeat orders. Allocate setup-support cost by units, or by the number of setups each order needs? Both orders use the same total material and machine hours.

**Round-two reveal:** One order needs twelve small artwork setups; the other needs two. A supplied activity-cost pool and setup record change product margins, but total company cost does not change.

**Learning objective:** Activity-based costing: a causal cost driver versus a convenient volume denominator.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-02 concerns avoidable branch costs; this case concerns tracing shared batch-level resources to products for quotations.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKICPA management-accounting material and a manufacturer’s dated report describing small-batch customisation; verify the activity mechanism, not invented order economics.

### ACC-12 · The best margin. Per what?

**Opening decision:** A print-finishing shop has one scarce skilled finishing shift. Prioritise the higher contribution per order or the higher contribution per finishing minute, within confirmed customer limits?

**Round-two reveal:** The high-margin order uses three times as much scarce finishing time. A second small time block becomes available, requiring students to recompute the best feasible mix rather than name a permanent winner.

**Learning objective:** Contribution per limiting resource and bounded product-mix decisions.

**Distinctness check:** OPS-03 asks whether speeding one station increases throughput; here capacity is fixed and the decision is the mix of accepted work.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: professional management-accounting guidance on limiting factors plus a dated printing-capacity report; supply every fictional time, demand cap and contribution in the case.

### ACC-13 · Cheaper material. A dearer batch?

**Opening decision:** A ceramics studio’s purchaser bought clay below the standard price. Celebrate the favourable price variance, or assess the purchase together with material usage before renewing it?

**Round-two reveal:** The cheaper batch needed more clay for the same acceptable output. A price/quantity bridge shows the net cost; a documented mix of supplier condition and changed handling prevents assigning all blame to one person.

**Learning objective:** Separate material price and usage variances, then examine their interaction and controllability.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-05 adjusts a budget for output volume. Output is held constant here; input price and physical consumption differ.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKICPA/ACCA variance-analysis guidance and a dated manufacturer input-quality disclosure; do not imply a real supplier supplied faulty material.

### ACC-14 · An invoice issued. Cash likely?

**Opening decision:** A design studio must review an overdue trade receivable. Keep the existing allowance estimate or increase it using the supplied collection evidence? Collection effort continues under either choice.

**Round-two reveal:** A customer’s staged repayment proposal and later missed instalment affect the scenario-weighted expected recovery. The receivable estimate is not a decision to forgive the debt.

**Learning objective:** Receivable impairment estimates versus billing, collection and write-off.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-03 estimates refunds owed to customers; this case estimates recovery of amounts already owed by customers.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKFRS 9 educational material and a listed company’s dated receivables/expected-credit-loss note; simplify a fictional supplied model without presenting it as universal compliance advice.

### ACC-15 · A profit. Where did the cash go?

**Opening decision:** A growing packaging studio has a profitable month but lower cash. Decide whether the first management response should target reported operating expenses or cash tied up in working capital.

**Round-two reveal:** A reconciliation separates non-cash depreciation, an inventory build and customer balances from actual cash payments. A one-off equipment purchase sits outside operating cash flow.

**Learning objective:** Reconcile profit to operating cash flow and distinguish operating from investing uses.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-03 evaluates a new order’s financing gap; this case diagnoses a completed period using an explicit cash-flow bridge.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKAS 7 educational guidance and a dated annual report with an indirect operating-cash-flow reconciliation; use original short figures, not copied statements.

### ACC-16 · Small amount. Important correction?

**Opening decision:** A club treasurer finds a small payment placed in the wrong expense category before issuing the accounts. Correct and explain it now, or aggregate it with ordinary minor differences?

**Round-two reveal:** The payment changes a separately reported related-party total, although overall surplus is unchanged. A supplied reporting policy explains why nature can matter alongside amount.

**Learning objective:** Quantitative and qualitative materiality, with correction distinguished from allegations of fraud.

**Distinctness check:** GVR-06 concerns approval splitting; this concerns how a small classification error affects users’ interpretation of financial information.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: IFRS Practice Statement 2 and a dated HKICPA reporting communication; establish actual disclosure context before using a fictional illustration.

### ACC-17 · Most of the value. Enough of the evidence?

**Opening decision:** An auditor planning a fictional receivables test can examine the largest balances only or combine them with a properly selected sample of smaller balances, within the same budget.

**Round-two reveal:** The untested small-balance population uses a different billing process. Supplied population information shows why large-value coverage does not automatically support conclusions about the remainder.

**Learning objective:** Audit population definition, selection bias and limits of extrapolation; not a formula-only sample-size lesson.

**Distinctness check:** GVR-09 compares review scope across processes. Here the assertion and procedure stay fixed; the selection method determines which population the evidence supports.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKSA/ISA 530 educational guidance and an AFRC inspection report discussing sampling; avoid suggesting a sample guarantees a clean population.

### ACC-18 · Keep the budget. Update the forecast?

**Opening decision:** A service firm’s annual budget remains the agreed performance baseline. With demand changing, use it unchanged for staffing cash planning or add a rolling forecast without rewriting the target?

**Round-two reveal:** A new signed contract changes the next three months but not prior actual results. Students produce separate planning and accountability views instead of substituting one number for both.

**Learning objective:** Different purposes of budgets, forecasts and targets.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-05 flexes a historical comparison for actual activity; this case separates forward planning from an agreed accountability benchmark.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: CIMA/ACCA rolling-forecast guidance and a dated company outlook revision; no claim that forecasting removes uncertainty.

### ACC-19 · A lower return. A better investment?

**Opening decision:** A divisional manager can accept a positive-value equipment upgrade or reject it because it lowers the division’s reported return on investment. The company’s investment threshold is supplied.

**Round-two reveal:** Residual income and absolute incremental profit tell a different story from percentage ROI. An agreed bonus metric explains the manager’s private incentive without changing project cash flows.

**Learning objective:** Investment-centre measurement and goal congruence: ROI versus residual income.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-08 compares project cash-flow valuations; here a worthwhile project is held fixed and the managerial performance measure changes the incentive.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: professional management-accounting guidance and a dated segment-reporting discussion; use a transparent fictional incentive rule, not an allegation about a real manager.

### ACC-20 · An internal price. Two sensible refusals?

**Opening decision:** A component unit has spare capacity while a sister unit can buy externally. Choose an internal transfer-price range that both managers can accept, or leave the units to transact outside.

**Round-two reveal:** An external order would consume some spare capacity. The supplying unit’s opportunity cost changes only for the constrained units, narrowing the feasible bargain.

**Learning objective:** Transfer pricing as internal coordination, with capacity-dependent opportunity cost.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-02 asks whether closing a unit saves cost; STR-03 asks make/buy capabilities. This case isolates internal divisional incentives and a bargaining range, not tax transfer pricing.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: CIMA/ACCA divisional transfer-pricing teaching material and a dated vertically integrated company description; explicitly exclude tax-law advice.

## FIN · Finance, banking and insurance

### FIN-11 · A lower rate. A less certain payment?

**Opening decision:** A business chooses between otherwise comparable fixed-rate and floating-rate borrowing for an approved equipment purchase. Compare total financing expense under supplied rate paths and the value of payment certainty.

**Round-two reveal:** A dated cash plan reveals limited monthly headroom in one period. The lowest expected interest path may breach that operational boundary; a fixed quote is not free insurance.

**Learning objective:** Interest-rate exposure and cash-flow certainty, rather than predicting rates.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-06 compares amortisation structures at stipulated rates; here the repayment structure is fixed and the rate path is uncertain.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKMA/IFEC business borrowing education and dated benchmark-rate reporting; all future rate paths explicitly fictional, no rate forecast or borrowing recommendation.

### FIN-12 · Rent the equipment. Keep the exit?

**Opening decision:** A surveying firm needs a non-safety-critical desktop scanner. Choose a three-year purchase or a fully costed lease, including maintenance and disposal assumptions.

**Round-two reveal:** A shorter project commitment changes expected utilisation, while the lease has a genuine early-exit charge and the purchased scanner has uncertain resale value.

**Learning objective:** Lease-versus-buy comparison including residual value, commitments and flexibility.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-08 evaluates two fit-outs; IS-08 concerns software switching dependence. This case compares ownership and financing/service contracts for the same asset.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: IFEC/SME financing guidance, dated equipment-leasing disclosures and actual published contract features; invent no universal right to cancel.

### FIN-13 · The invoice is sold. Is the risk gone?

**Opening decision:** A wholesaler can wait for a receivable or sell it to a factor for a stated fee. Assess accelerated cash alongside who bears a customer’s failure to pay.

**Round-two reveal:** The contract has recourse for credit losses but separate rules for disputes. Students compare the cash received today with retained contingent obligations rather than treating every sale as risk transfer.

**Learning objective:** Factoring liquidity versus credit-risk transfer and contractual recourse.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-03 finds a funding gap; FIN-05 asks whether an insured event is covered. This case prices the acceleration of receivables while identifying retained obligations.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: HKMC Trade Finance/official SME trade-finance material and a bank’s published factoring explanation; verify recourse definitions and keep legal terms fictional and explicit.

### FIN-14 · Rising cash flows. The same discount rate?

**Opening decision:** An equipment project has two valuation sheets: one in today’s purchasing power and one including price increases. Select the internally consistent sheet or repair both before comparing them.

**Round-two reveal:** The real cash-flow sheet has been discounted at a nominal rate. A supplied matched real/nominal pair yields the same valuation when used consistently.

**Learning objective:** Consistency between nominal/real cash flows and discount rates.

**Distinctness check:** ECO-09 asks what a price increase buys now; FIN-08 compares payback and value. This case isolates an inflation-consistency error in multi-period appraisal.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: official/public investment-appraisal guidance and published inflation measurement explanations; supply assumed inflation rather than predict it.

### FIN-15 · One company. One hurdle rate?

**Opening decision:** A firm considers a familiar contract-service project and a more volatile new-market project. Apply the existing company hurdle rate to both, or examine project-specific risk before ranking them.

**Round-two reveal:** The supplied project scenarios show different exposure to the common economic downturn, even though each has the same expected cash flow. A bounded sensitivity range replaces a falsely precise universal rate.

**Learning objective:** Project risk versus company-average cost of capital; hurdle-rate sensitivity.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-08 holds the discount rate given; STR-08 compares business strategies across futures. This case asks whether a shared financing rate is an adequate appraisal rate for different risks.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: corporate-finance educational material from a recognised university/professional body and dated public appraisal guidance; fictional classroom projects, not investment advice.

### FIN-16 · Two income streams. One bad month?

**Opening decision:** A fictional owner can combine either of two contract portfolios with an existing business line. Both additions have equal expected contribution and standalone variability. Which better stabilises total monthly cash?

**Round-two reveal:** The common scenario table shows one addition weakens when the existing line weakens, while the other offsets it. A later common-shock scenario limits the apparent diversification benefit.

**Learning objective:** Diversification depends on co-movement, not simply the number of activities.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-09 spreads eligible bank deposits for protection/access. This case concerns covariance of operating cash streams, not account count or deposit limits.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: IFEC diversification education and a dated diversified firm’s segment-demand discussion; no promised hedge or use of actual securities.

### FIN-17 · More credit. A tighter promise?

**Opening decision:** A distributor chooses between two affordable working-capital facilities: a lower-rate line with a financial covenant or a higher-rate line with more operational room.

**Round-two reveal:** A fully supplied forecast stresses the covenant calculation and shows the difference between a review trigger, an agreed remedy and immediate repayment. Neither option is described as universally safe.

**Learning objective:** Covenant headroom and financing flexibility beyond headline interest.

**Distinctness check:** GVR-10 tests loss capacity; FIN-11 tests payment volatility. This case concerns a contractual financial boundary and the cost of flexibility.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: published SME facility terms and a listed-company covenant note; verify terminology, distinguish fictional stipulated remedies from actual law.

### FIN-18 · Earn more interest. Available on which day?

**Opening decision:** A studio allocates a known reserve among on-demand cash and term deposits. Choose a maturity schedule that funds dated supplier payments rather than putting the entire balance in the highest-rate tenor.

**Round-two reveal:** One supplier offers a legitimate early-delivery date with a payment consequence. A supplied early-withdrawal charge changes the value of keeping some funds liquid.

**Learning objective:** Cash maturity matching and liquidity ladders.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-09 concerns bank failure/access concentration; this case assumes the bank functions normally and focuses on contractual maturity dates.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: IFEC term-deposit guidance and published bank early-withdrawal terms; fictional rates and cash timetable, no account transaction.

### FIN-19 · The best project. The best set of projects?

**Opening decision:** A small firm has a fixed discretionary capital budget and three indivisible improvements. Pick the feasible combination with greatest total supplied present value, rather than the highest individual return ratio.

**Round-two reveal:** A fourth complementary project creates an explicitly quantified interaction. Students distinguish additive standalone values from the package value without treating all synergies as proven.

**Learning objective:** Capital rationing, indivisibility and combination appraisal.

**Distinctness check:** FIN-08 chooses between mutually exclusive fit-outs; this case selects a portfolio under a hard budget and an explicit interaction.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: professional capital-budgeting guidance and a dated company capital-allocation disclosure; all project values are stipulated classroom estimates.

### FIN-20 · A good year. A distributable surplus?

**Opening decision:** Owners of a fictional business consider a discretionary cash distribution or retention for a documented upcoming operating cycle. Required obligations and legal distribution conditions are supplied and satisfied before the choice.

**Round-two reveal:** A bank line that seemed to replace retained cash expires before the seasonal payment peak. Renewal remains uncertain, so accounting profit alone does not establish affordable payout.

**Learning objective:** Payout policy, retained liquidity and financing continuity.

**Distinctness check:** ACC-15 diagnoses past cash movement; FIN-03 funds a particular new order. This case chooses an owner distribution after protecting the whole operating cycle.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: corporate-finance payout education and a dated company dividend/liquidity explanation; do not invent Hong Kong legal solvency tests or advise an actual distribution.

## MKT · Marketing, retail and consumer behaviour

### MKT-11 · A promise of expertise. What can buyers see?

**Opening decision:** A small repair consultancy can use its promotion budget for a broad quality claim or a clearly bounded demonstration of its assessment process. Which reduces the target buyer’s uncertainty?

**Round-two reveal:** Interviews show buyers worry about being able to understand the diagnosis, not the service’s appearance. The demonstration has a cost and cannot guarantee every repair outcome.

**Learning objective:** Marketing intangible services through credible process evidence.

**Distinctness check:** MKT-08 concerns recognising a familiar product pack; this case concerns evaluating a service before purchase.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: dated Consumer Council service-comparison material and reputable service-marketing research; demonstration evidence must not become an invented certification.

### MKT-12 · Many visits. Where does the journey break?

**Opening decision:** An online craft shop must choose one improvement: attract more qualified visitors or simplify a documented checkout step. A supplied stage-by-stage funnel supports a first allocation.

**Opening decision:** Two firms bid for a lot of mixed surplus stock with uncertain resale value. Choose a bid based on one noisy inspection estimate, under a supplied simple common-value model.

**Round-two reveal:** Winning would mean the bidder’s estimate was higher than the others’, and all estimates concern the same resale value. Students condition on what winning reveals, rather than use a private-value bidding rule unchanged.

**Learning objective:** The winner’s curse and selection information in common-value auctions.

**Distinctness check:** ECO-02 is explicitly a private-value stall auction; this makes value uncertainty shared and winning informative. GVR-01 verifies a single seller claim, not selection among bidders.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary common-value auction research, documented auction design materials and a dated surplus-sale context. Use a small transparent probability table; avoid pretending a universal bid discount is optimal.

### ECO-13 · A promise of quality. Costly for whom?

**Opening decision:** A new repair service wants to distinguish reliable work from low-quality rivals. Choose between a cheap quality slogan and a clearly costed workmanship guarantee that preserves customers’ existing rights.

**Round-two reveal:** The guarantee has very different expected fulfilment costs for reliable and unreliable providers in the fictional model. Students assess whether the signal could separate types, and where verification or opportunistic claims could undermine it.

**Learning objective:** Credible signalling when the signal’s cost depends on underlying quality.

**Distinctness check:** GVR-01 asks a buyer to verify quality; ECO-10 asks how coverage changes prevention. This asks how a seller can credibly communicate pre-existing quality.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary signalling research, consumer warranty guidance and a dated service-guarantee example. Do not imply a guarantee alone proves quality or replaces statutory protection.

### ECO-14 · A low entry fee. A high-use member?

**Opening decision:** A shared studio chooses a pay-per-visit menu or a membership fee plus a lower visit price, with two supplied customer types and no overcrowding. Students choose a menu from explicit candidate prices.

**Round-two reveal:** The lower per-visit price changes how often members attend, rather than only reallocating the same spending. Students trace participation, usage and contribution separately.

**Learning objective:** Two-part tariffs and the separate participation and usage decisions.

**Distinctness check:** ECO-08 bundles two different services; MKT-01 sells a finite multi-trip pass. This combines a fixed access charge with a marginal use price and makes consumption endogenous.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary two-part-tariff literature, current public membership tariff examples and a dated studio or leisure-service context. Do not assert an optimal price beyond the given candidate menu.

### ECO-15 · A cheaper printer. More expensive printing?

**Opening decision:** A small office chooses between two printer-and-consumable packages using stated purchase prices and an anticipated workload. Neither package hides contract terms.

**Round-two reveal:** The cheaper machine uses a more expensive compatible consumable and the workload differs between offices. Students compare the price of a system of complements, not simply the entry item, and identify who prefers each package.

**Learning objective:** Complementary goods, usage-dependent total cost and linked demand.

**Distinctness check:** IS-08 concerns migration costs and provider dependence; FIN-07 compares payment timing. This isolates complementary-product pricing with switching and quality held equal.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: consumer testing bodies’ total-cost methods, public manufacturer consumable information and a dated equipment-pricing example. Use fictional comparable products, not unverified product recommendations.

### ECO-16 · One more quotation. Worth the search?

**Opening decision:** A buyer has a satisfactory quotation and can spend staff time obtaining one more. Choose whether to search again using a small supplied distribution of possible savings and a fixed search cost.

**Round-two reveal:** The additional supplier’s quote must arrive after a stated deadline unless the buyer pays for temporary cover. Students value information net of search and delay costs, rather than assuming more quotations are always better.

**Learning objective:** Optimal stopping in costly search and the value of an additional option.

**Distinctness check:** GVR-03 governs independent supplier evaluation; ENT-02 tests demand before production. This evaluates whether another known information-acquisition step is worth its cost.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary economic search theory, procurement guidance on proportionate competition and a dated small-business purchasing context. Clearly distinguish the model from mandatory procurement requirements.

### ECO-17 · We will launch if you do.

**Opening decision:** Two complementary independent vendors can each invest in a joint event offer. Choose unilateral commitment or a conditional arrangement when each investment pays only if the other also proceeds.

**Round-two reveal:** A simple refundable commitment mechanism can verify that both are ready before either incurs the irreversible cost, but it adds a disclosed administration cost. Students compare coordination gains and cost without coordinating prices or excluding competitors.

**Learning objective:** Coordination failure and credible conditional commitment.

**Distinctness check:** SUS-04 is a non-excludable public-good contribution problem; STR-01 is strategic price rivalry. Here two private complementary investments can both fail through uncertainty about the other’s action.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary coordination-game literature, publicly documented event partnership structures and a dated local collaborative launch. The classroom mechanism is not a legal contract template.

### ECO-18 · The shared resource is open. Will it last?

**Opening decision:** Two operators use a shared, permitted equipment pool with a finite replenishment cycle. Choose unrestricted first-come use or a jointly agreed usage allowance, with ownership and safety duties fixed.

**Round-two reveal:** Each operator benefits from taking one more unit now but reduces the available stock for both in the next period. Students compare individually attractive extraction with the joint path and design a verifiable operating rule.

**Learning objective:** Common-pool resource depletion and intertemporal collective action.

**Distinctness check:** SUS-04 funds a non-rival walkway; SUS-03 allocates one existing court slot. This resource is subtractable and replenishes, so current use changes future availability.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary common-pool-resource research, official resource-management examples and a dated shared-resource story. Keep the fictional stock dynamics explicit rather than generalise to fisheries or water systems.

### ECO-19 · A price cap. Which shortage remains?

**Opening decision:** A fictional temporary market has a posted maximum price and a fixed short-run stock of ordinary nonessential goods. Choose a queue, purchase limit or transparent ballot to allocate stock at that price, with no resale or legal assumptions imported.

**Round-two reveal:** A lower ceiling makes more buyers willing to purchase but does not add stock during the event. Students distinguish price affordability, availability and nonprice rationing, and explain who benefits under the chosen rule.

**Learning objective:** Price controls and nonprice allocation under a fixed short-run supply.

**Distinctness check:** SUS-03 compares allocation rules without changing price-induced demand; this introduces a binding ceiling and excess demand. ECO-03 follows fee incidence after repricing, not a constrained price.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary price-control literature, official published policy evaluations and a dated event-goods context. Avoid extrapolating a one-period model to housing, medicine or essential-service policy.

### ECO-20 · Two sellers merge. Which competition disappears?

**Opening decision:** Two delivery businesses propose a merger with documented operating-cost savings. Recommend advancing the business case or redesigning it before further expenditure, comparing customer alternatives as well as the owners’ gains; required legal review remains separate.

**Round-two reveal:** A supplied customer-substitution table shows that these two are each other’s closest alternatives during the critical delivery window, while several nominal rivals cannot serve it. Students assess the lost competitive constraint alongside efficiencies, rather than use seller counts alone.

**Learning objective:** Merger economics: closeness of substitution, efficiency and potential market power.

**Distinctness check:** STR-05 values an acquisition to its buyer; this assesses effects on customers and competition. GVR-18 governs a trade meeting’s information boundaries, not the economic appraisal of a structural merger.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: official competition-agency merger economics, primary industrial-organisation research and a dated regional consolidation. No legal approval verdict; distinguish predicted efficiencies from verified pass-through to customers.

## HSP · Hospitality, tourism, property and service management

### HSP-11 · A cancellation forecast. Another promise?

**Opening decision:** A small hotel can stop at its physical room capacity or accept one extra future reservation with a fully specified, lawful service-recovery plan and customer protections. Students decide under two clearly supplied cancellation scenarios.

**Round-two reveal:** Cancellations are correlated because several bookings belong to the same event group, invalidating the simple independent-booking calculation. Students revise the booking limit and explain the guest commitment risk rather than maximise expected occupancy alone.

**Learning objective:** Booking-limit design, correlated cancellations and the cost of promising scarce service capacity.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-02 repairs a disruption that already occurred; HSP-03 allocates rooms across stay lengths. This is a prospective acceptance decision under cancellation dependence.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary hotel overbooking research, official consumer booking protections and a dated accommodation disruption. No real hotel is advised to breach bookings; safeguards and alternatives must be explicit.

### HSP-12 · They can read the map. Can they find the entrance?

**Opening decision:** A museum chooses an improved printed visitor map or a sequence of on-site direction cues for a confusing arrival route. Both provide the required accessible information and cost the same.

**Round-two reveal:** A walkthrough shows that wrong turns occur at an unmarked decision point before visitors reach the map display. Students locate the intervention in the arrival journey and preserve visitors’ ability to confirm they are on the right route.

**Learning objective:** Wayfinding as a sequence of spatial decisions, not simply more information.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-08 offers ways to interpret an exhibit’s meaning; this helps guests reach the service in the first place. IS-05 aligns order semantics, not physical navigation.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: official visitor-wayfinding guidance, primary environmental-design research and a dated venue-access improvement. Use a simple fictional route; do not claim universal accessibility from one walkthrough.

### HSP-13 · A tidy room. An unwanted interruption?

**Opening decision:** A hotel chooses a fixed housekeeping window or a guest-selected service window for otherwise identical stays. Required cleanliness, worker safety and emergency-entry rules remain protected.

**Round-two reveal:** Guests’ return times vary and staff need a reliable route plan. Students design an honest request-and-confirmation process with an explicit fallback instead of promising any time the guest chooses.

**Learning objective:** Service customisation under operational constraints and respect for guest privacy.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-07 allocates pace within a meal; this concerns negotiating entry into occupied private space. SUS-12 changes a resource-use schedule, not an individual service-consent process.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: hospitality service-design studies, published hotel housekeeping policies and official privacy or service guidance. Do not treat “do not disturb” as overriding emergency obligations.

### HSP-14 · Follow the script. Recognise the occasion?

**Opening decision:** A café can give hosts a standard greeting-and-offer script or bounded discretion within the same service budget. Choose a design for ordinary visits, with no discriminatory profiling or pressure selling.

**Round-two reveal:** Two guests ask for opposite forms of attention: one wants a quiet working visit, another wants help arranging a small celebration. Students identify which commitments should stay standard and which parts staff may adapt.

**Learning objective:** Standardisation versus personalisation in frontline hospitality encounters.

**Distinctness check:** PPL-08 concerns employees’ task ownership; HSP-09 chooses a whole event’s interaction format. This sets service boundaries within individual encounters, based on expressed preferences.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary hospitality service-standardisation research, documented service training practices and a dated local café/service context. Avoid assumptions about needs based on nationality, age or appearance.

### HSP-15 · Decide before guests travel. Or wait for a better forecast?

**Opening decision:** An outdoor activity operator must set a weather-decision deadline. Choose early confirmation of a funded indoor format or a later decision with better forecast information. Both obey the same non-negotiable safety threshold and disclosed customer protections.

**Round-two reveal:** Some guests begin nonrefundable onward travel before the later update; others are local and value waiting. Students choose a deadline and notification commitment, accounting for reliance created while the operator delays.

**Learning objective:** Service commitment timing: better information can arrive after guests incur irreversible preparation costs.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-02 restores a trip after disruption; this sets the decision and communication deadline before guest reliance accumulates. It is not a trade-off between safety and revenue or another equivalent-room choice.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: official event-weather guidance, primary service-disruption research and a dated cancellation timeline. Supply fictional forecast and travel facts; do not infer real refund obligations or safety thresholds.

### HSP-16 · Lively to one table. Too loud to another?

**Opening decision:** A restaurant chooses one uniform sound environment or two clearly described seating zones within an acoustically feasible layout. Both meet required safety/access standards; choose using guest purposes and a fixed budget.

**Round-two reveal:** A short observation shows conversation difficulty in one zone during busy periods, while another group values a lively atmosphere. Students specify how guests can choose and how the restaurant will monitor comfort without promising silence.

**Learning objective:** Servicescape fit and guest control over the sensory environment.

**Distinctness check:** ECO-07 values noise imposed on neighbours; this concerns the experience of willing guests inside the service setting. HSP-06 changes table-size fit, not atmosphere.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary hospitality acoustics/servicescape studies, official venue guidance and a dated restaurant redesign. Do not turn fictional observations into medical hearing-risk or engineering claims.

### HSP-17 · Keys handed over. Ready to move in?

**Opening decision:** A serviced-office operator can complete a formal key handover on the promised day or offer a staged handover with a fully disclosed temporary workspace. Choose with essential safety and contractual rights already protected.

**Round-two reveal:** The office is physically accessible but two agreed nonessential service features are not ready. Students distinguish possession, operational readiness and acceptance of outstanding items, and create a clear snag-and-completion record.

**Learning objective:** Property-service handover as a managed transition, not a single transaction.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-02 concerns replacing a hotel room after a failure; GVR-11 records a contract variation. This asks how an existing service is handed over and incomplete items remain owned without silently changing the agreement.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: official property-management handover guidance, professional facilities-management standards and a dated regional serviced-space example. Exclude unsafe premises and avoid implying that a customer must waive rights.

### HSP-18 · The highest rent. The right neighbour?

**Opening decision:** A fictional mixed-use courtyard has one vacant unit. Choose between a higher-rent evening venue and a lower-rent daytime service operator, using proposed opening hours, servicing needs and the site’s promised experience for residents and visitors.

**Round-two reveal:** The evening proposal needs deliveries through the residential entrance during quiet hours; the daytime proposal fills a documented service gap but has no proven effect on neighbouring sales. Students negotiate feasible operating conditions before recommending a tenant. Preserve a lawful, feasible evening-use option through costed delivery arrangements so the reveal does not automatically disqualify it.

**Learning objective:** Tenant mix: assess compatibility and whole-site service commitments alongside standalone rental income.

**Distinctness check:** HSP-16 designs restaurant ambience; this governs complementary property uses and shared access. ACC-12 compares contribution: higher rent alone cannot establish a compatible tenant mix.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: RICS commercial property management guidance and a documented mixed-use leasing or tenant-mix decision. Verify operating restrictions; never assume footfall uplift or sales spillovers.

### HSP-19 · Ten minutes to wait. Ten minutes without knowing?

**Opening decision:** A visitor attraction can fund a small capacity increase or more accurate queue information and a genuinely optional return window. Choose using stated costs and guest constraints; the second option does not reduce processing time.

**Round-two reveal:** Guests are most frustrated by uncertain progress and fear of missing their place, but some cannot leave the queue area. Students separate actual waiting, uncertainty and usable choice without disguising a long wait.

**Learning objective:** The experienced wait: information, uncertainty and control are not the same as throughput.

**Distinctness check:** OPS-07 redesigns queues to change completion performance; HSP-19 leaves service capacity unchanged in one option and evaluates guests’ experience honestly. HSP-07 concerns dining-stage pace.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary waiting-experience research, official attraction queue practices and a dated service example. No claim that information can substitute for adequate capacity in every setting.

### HSP-20 · One booking. Several different visits?

**Opening decision:** A guided attraction receives one booking for a party of four adults. Choose one shared route or a clearly coordinated split-and-rejoin format, both feasible and equally funded with consent from the party.

**Round-two reveal:** The four adults express different purposes and preferred depths of explanation; the booking organiser’s preference is not shared by everyone. Students design a real group agreement and reunion point without treating one purchaser as every guest’s voice.

**Learning objective:** Multi-person service demand: purchaser, organiser and individual participants are different roles.

**Distinctness check:** MKT-07 distinguishes gift buyer and user in positioning; HSP-20 concerns jointly delivering an experience to several simultaneous guests. HSP-09 chooses mingling for an entire event, not a booking party’s itinerary.

**Research lead, not yet verified:** Research lead only: primary tourism group-decision research, published guided-visit formats and a dated attraction-service story. Do not infer preferences from family roles or demographic stereotypes.
