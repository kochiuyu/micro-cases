# Teaching plan for Cases 9–16

These eight proposals extend the original collection. The 2024 award acknowledgement covers Cases 1–8. The prices, contracts and rule changes below are classroom assumptions.

| Case | Working title | First decision | Changed fact and second decision | Teaching focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | The last tray of egg tarts | Twelve tarts remain at closing. A café offers HK$6 each. Each cost HK$8 to make. With no other use and no extra cost of selling, do you accept? | Using the tarts in tomorrow’s bread pudding saves HK$7 each after preparation costs. Do you still sell? | Sunk cost and opportunity cost |
| 10 | Who gets the last badminton court? | Two groups want the last evening court. Choose a queue, lottery or auction and explain your objective. | One group can queue at noon while every member of the other group is in class. Would you keep the rule? | Scarcity, allocation and queuing costs |
| 11 | Would you share a ten-trip ferry pass? | Choose a ten-trip price that attracts commuters while occasional riders buy single tickets. | Ten occasional riders can share one pass. Would you change the price or the terms? | Price discrimination and self-selection |
| 12 | The phone with a fresh battery | A used-phone seller asks HK$300 extra for a supposedly new battery. Would you pay? | An independent battery test and an enforceable refund become available. Which test result would change your bid? | Asymmetric information and verification |
| 13 | Two cafés, one lunch rush | A HK$5 discount raises your café’s profit if its rival keeps its price. Do you discount? | The rival can also discount. Use a two-by-two profit table to identify each café’s best response to each rival price. | Strategic pricing and Nash equilibrium |
| 14 | Who funds the covered walkway? | Four shops gain from a shared rain cover. Their combined gains exceed its cost. How much would your shop pledge? | The other three pledges now cover the full cost and construction is guaranteed. Would you contribute? | Public goods and free riding |
| 15 | Pay per parcel? | Would you replace hourly pay with a fee per completed delivery? | The depot counts deliveries immediately, but damage and missed handovers appear days later. How would you revise the contract? | Incentive pay and the trade-off between speed and care |
| 16 | One stall, one bid | Your society knows its private value for a festival stall. Choose a sealed bid when the winner pays its own bid, given an estimate of rival bids. | Keep the rival bids and your valuation fixed. How would you bid if the winner paid the second-highest bid? | Auction rules and bidding incentives |

## Drafting order

Start with Case 9. Put the café’s offer and the bakery’s past cost on the opening slide. Reveal the bread-pudding option only after the first vote. Ask students to identify the forgone alternative in each round.

For each case, use the shared authoring template and verify at least five distinct public online sources. Clearly label invented numbers and distinguish hypothetical rules from actual operator policies. Prepare a cartoon, highlighted student questions, a debrief, instructor notes, presentation slides and a printable handout before adding a case to the classroom collection.
