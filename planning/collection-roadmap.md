## How a lead earns a place

1. Find a specific dated event, looking first in Hong Kong, then the rest of the Greater Bay Area, elsewhere in Asia, and farther afield when the story warrants it.
2. Try the opening vote aloud. Can students take opposing positions in the first minute and give a credible reason for each?
3. Find one fact worth revealing after the vote. It should change the trade-off or make students defend their original choice more carefully.
4. Follow the [authoring template](../authoring/case-template.md), including five distinct public sources, an original illustration, presentation and handout. Identify reported facts and invented classroom assumptions separately.

Case 49 adds reuse-system design: durability is not achieved circulation. Fund the return, cleaning, storage and next-use loop; compare equivalent service and avoid unsupported environmental rankings.

Case 50 adds insurance trigger-versus-limit analysis: raising a payment ceiling does not change an unmet coverage condition. A different loss scenario may still justify a higher limit; alternative triggers carry mismatch risk.

Case 52 adds activity-adjusted cost evaluation: spending above the original plan can be below the allowance for approved actual output. Separate volume, remaining variance, managerial causation and spending authority.

Case 53 adds shared data meaning: technically accepted files can encode the wrong packaging quantity. Verify product-level units and reconcile intended transactions before automating submission.

Case 54 adds hotel distribution economics: compare acquisition and processing costs with commission, using equivalent completed stays. Conditional unit economics do not establish incremental demand or an optimal channel mix.

Case 55 adds upstream risk mapping: two distributors can share one production failure point. Test stock, qualified alternatives and response times rather than equating supplier count with continuity.

Case 56 adds purpose-based data minimisation: an existing-account birthday offer needs a timing field and redemption control, not necessarily a full personal profile. It distinguishes reduced collection from anonymity, security and comprehensive legal compliance.

Case 57 adds assortment and basket effects: compare product substitution and whole-shopping-trip consequences rather than ranking standalone sales alone. Co-purchase flags a hypothesis, not a guaranteed loss of linked baskets.

Case 58 adds founder equity timing: distinguish equal potential allocations from recognition of past work and conditions for future involvement. The discussion precedes signing and prescribes neither a universal vesting schedule nor a legal implementation.

Case 59 adds local two-sided matching: equal aggregate requests and couriers do not establish feasible pairs when place and time constrain access. The missing side determines the marginal usefulness of recruitment; a fixed-window capacity ceiling is not a profit forecast or an empirical estimate of network effects.

## 60-case balance checkpoint · 14 November 2026

All twelve teaching areas now have five cases. Case 60 adds acquisition rationale: customer overlap changes claimed reach without erasing the target’s existing business. Additional value, ownership benefits, price and integration matter beyond a count of clients. The next formal instructor-feedback checkpoint remains 72. Continue with distinct objectives rather than repeating the same calculation in new settings.

Case 61 adds advance payments and earned revenue: a prepaid workshop launch can produce strong cash collections while leaving service obligations. Equal-session allocation separates receipts, delivered performance and unearned revenue without mistaking any for profit.

Case 62 adds repayment timing versus total financing cost: a smaller instalment can protect early cash flow while requiring more payments and a larger total. Compare complete schedules and a cash forecast, without confusing nominal totals with APR or present value.

Case 63 adds B2B lead qualification: prioritise suitable prospects across contact sources rather than list volume alone. Qualification is not an order, and self-selected activity results do not establish campaign causality or return.

Case 64 adds planned maintenance and reliable capacity: compare a scheduled interruption with residual unplanned-disruption risk. Expected costs are conditional, not guaranteed savings, and safety or mandatory maintenance cannot be traded against the classroom threshold.

Case 65 adds hybrid team coordination: identify task-specific shared time before confirming individual schedules, consult affected colleagues and preserve flexibility elsewhere. One dependency does not justify a universal attendance quota or visibility-based performance judgments.

Case 66 adds coopetition: sharing a facility can reduce costs while separate firms continue competing, but the exact resource boundary may change differentiation. A quoted saving that includes access to a distinctive set cannot be retained while assuming that access away. Customer responses and revised terms remain uncertain.

Case 67 adds food-waste intervention fit: a separated composition audit distinguishes potential edible surplus from plate returns and inedible scraps. Donation remains valuable, but candidate weight is not guaranteed accepted donation; investigate prevention while protecting adequate meals and existing safe arrangements.

Case 68 adds innovation-protection fit: confidential drawings do not make a mechanism undiscoverable in a sold product. A confidential internal test prompts pre-launch professional assessment without assuming patentability, guaranteed exclusivity or inevitable failure; hidden know-how and execution remain separate considerations.

Case 69 adds comparative versus absolute advantage: the faster person at both tasks need not do both when each task displaces a different alternative output. Check both deadlines and quantities, not a combined productivity score; task allocation is not a judgment of personal worth.

Case 70 adds restaurant table mix: equal chair counts need not accommodate the same party-size requests. Match whole parties to feasible tables and test an alternative mix before treating one layout as universally better. Counts do not establish revenue, fairness or profit.

Case 71 adds prediction-time information availability: a held-out historical row can still contain information unavailable when a manager must act. Rebuild and retest with genuine decision-time inputs; a corrected model does not inherit the old score or guarantee a staffing benefit.
