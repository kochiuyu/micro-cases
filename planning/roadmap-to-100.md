## Starting point

These questions are leads for research. Industries can cut across teaching areas: an airline story might belong in accounting, a restaurant story in analytics, or a property story in negotiation.

## How a lead earns a place

1. Find a specific dated event, looking first in Hong Kong, then the rest of the Greater Bay Area, elsewhere in Asia, and farther afield when the story warrants it.
2. Try the opening vote aloud. Can students take opposing positions in the first minute and give a credible reason for each?
3. Find one fact worth revealing after the vote. It should change the trade-off or make students defend their original choice more carefully.
4. Follow the [authoring template](../authoring/case-template.md), including five distinct public sources, an original illustration, presentation and handout. Identify reported facts and invented classroom assumptions separately.

## Milestones

- **22 cases:** Complete the six approved directions, reviewing the first three before drafting the next three.
- **40 cases:** Check that every teaching area has a usable entry. Add discipline browsing alongside the existing topic filter as the collection grows.
- **70 cases:** Ask instructors which cases they taught, which reveal changed the discussion, and which decisions they still need. Rebalance the remaining leads.
- **100 cases:** Review for duplicate decisions, broken sources and gaps in teaching coverage. Keep distinct cases when the same concept leads to a genuinely different managerial choice.
