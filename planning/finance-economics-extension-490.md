# Ten more finance and economics cases

The completed 480-case collection is being extended with ten cases: FIN-41–45 and ECO-41–45. The target is 490 cases, with 45 in each of these two areas and 40 in every other area. Existing case numbers and URLs remain unchanged.

The cases address distinct decisions in the Economics for Finance Practice course. They remain self-contained Micro Cases: students need no other reading to attempt the decisions. Planned pages are not counted as published lessons.

| Case | Planned title | Course chapter | Teaching decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FIN-41 | Protection when the portfolio falls | 7 | Why can two equally likely payouts have different market values? |
| ECO-41 | The shares jumped. What changed? | 1 | How much of the share-price move can the contract actually explain? |
| FIN-42 | A buy order. A higher quote? | 15 | How should an incoming buy order change the dealer’s next quote? |
| ECO-42 | Record takings. More sold? | 21 | Which price index supports a claim that retail sales volume increased? |
| FIN-43 | The customer paid. Can we make payroll? | 20 | Which payment receipts will be usable before the merchant’s deadline? |
| ECO-43 | The shelves are empty. Where did sales go? | 23 | Does the evidence point to weak demand or a supply constraint? |
| FIN-44 | The peg holds. Which rate resets? | 28 | What does the currency link fix, and which borrowing costs can still change? |
| ECO-44 | Rates fell. Why did the loan cost more? | 24 | Can a policy-rate cut coincide with a higher loan rate and weaker coverage? |
| FIN-45 | The same forecast. A dearer option? | 7 | Can an option become more valuable without a higher expected stock payoff? |
| ECO-45 | A tariff discount. On how many units? | 26 | Which sourcing offer leaves more contribution after tariffs, quantity changes and qualification costs? |

Each new case must pass source and numerical review, original illustration checks, presentation and interaction checks, automated collection checks, and live publication verification. Sources are included at the end of each case; reported facts and invented classroom terms are distinguished.
