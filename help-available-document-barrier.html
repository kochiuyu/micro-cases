SUS-34 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Administrative burden · Effective take-up · Verification design

Help is available. Can people claim it?

An award budget is not effective access

A programme can offer support on paper while eligible people cannot complete its application. Social-protection guidance distinguishes eligibility, application, verification and receipt. This fictional discretionary community fund examines documentary burden, not a government entitlement or advice about a real claimant.

Your classroom decision

You advise fictional Step Forward, a Hong Kong community fund planning a one-off HK$1,000 transport allowance for eligible adults attending approved training. No awards have been promised. Urgent assistance and existing commitments are separately funded and protected. Students design a process; they do not decide any real person’s eligibility.

For this exercise, 120 adults satisfy the unchanged eligibility rules, established by a separate consented planning assessment. Programme award verification must still occur. Everyone can use the same accessible staffed application desk, language support and review route. Digital access, the income threshold and the grant amount are not changing.

All 120 know about the proposed allowance. In a preparatory exercise, 100 start an application and 60 complete the required evidence pack. The other 40 stop before completion; their reasons are being checked. The remaining 20 do not start. No application has yet received a final award decision.

A spends HK$20,000 on an approved alternative verification route and reserves HK$80,000 for 80 awards. B keeps the present process and reserves all HK$100,000 for 100 awards. Ordinary administration is separately funded under both. A’s additional price includes setup, staff time, training and quality checks, not just a new form.

Both designs satisfy the fictional fund’s applicable verification requirements. If valid applications exceed funded awards, the same announced, independently supervised lottery applies after the deadline, regardless of evidence route. No first-come priority applies. Unused award money cannot be converted into process redesign within this round; later use requires a separate decision.

Round 2 · More information

Follow-up finds that 30 of the 40 incomplete applicants cannot obtain the prescribed employer certificate before the deadline. All 30 remain eligible and want to apply. Their employers can verify the relevant facts through another approved route. The other ten do not intend to complete this round for reasons unrelated to that certificate.

A permits a documented staff check using applicant-authorised payroll records and direct confirmation from an independently verified employer contact. Only necessary eligibility facts are collected. Identity, duplicate-award and training-enrolment checks remain. Applicants choose whether to use this route; no private record is accessed or shared in this classroom exercise.

A preparatory check of 20 separate consented test files reached the same eligibility findings as independent full review. That small check does not prove zero error. The fund’s reviewer has approved the procedure for a monitored trial, with second review of disputed evidence and a correction route. Both options retain required safeguards.

Use this explicit planning scenario: under B, the same 60 finish valid packs. Under A, those 60 plus all 30 document-blocked applicants finish, giving 90. The other 30 eligible adults still do not complete. These are conditional forecasts, not causal estimates or guaranteed future take-up.

Each of the 60 standard files needs ten staff minutes under either option. Each additional alternative-route file needs 20 minutes, fully covered by A’s extra package. Capacity and the deadline accommodate the total. All selected recipients receive the allowance within the round; there is no additional payment-stage barrier in this model.

Your decision

Under the supplied scenario, calculate awards paid, budget used, eligible-population receipt rates and staff review time for each option. Which would you choose, and what must remain monitored before calling the documentary change successful?

Debrief · Capacity is not receipt

B can fund 100 awards but has only 60 valid applicants, so it pays 60 × HK$1,000 = HK$60,000. HK$40,000 remains unused in this round. A receives 90 valid applications but can fund only 80, so the lottery selects 80 and ten valid applicants receive no award.

A pays HK$80,000 in allowances and spends HK$20,000 on the alternative route: HK$100,000 total. It reaches 20 more recipients than B, not 30. Thirty more people complete verification, but the award budget becomes the next constraint. Eligibility alone is not a promise of an award in this discretionary programme.

Debrief · Keep the denominators visible

Among all 120 eligible adults, B’s receipt rate is 60/120 = 50%; A’s is 80/120 = 66.7%, rounded. That is a 16.7-percentage-point increase in this scenario. Completion among the 100 starters rises from 60% to 90%, a different measure with a different denominator.

A still leaves 40 eligible adults without an award: 20 non-starters, ten other non-completers and ten unsuccessful valid applicants. B leaves 60 without one. A cannot be described as universal access, and comparing only approved applications would hide people lost before verification.

Debrief · Count work, not just paper

B reviews 60 × ten minutes = 600 minutes, or ten hours. A adds 30 × 20 minutes = 600 minutes, reaching 1,200 minutes, or 20 hours. Simpler documentary demands for applicants need not mean less staff work. The extra ten hours and associated controls are already priced into A.

A uses HK$40,000 more than B actually spends: HK$20,000 additional allowances and HK$20,000 process cost. Dividing that difference by 20 additional recipients gives HK$2,000 of extra spending per additional recipient in this model. It is not the grant amount, a measured causal cost-effectiveness estimate or proof of long-term value.

Debrief · Preserve the purpose of proof

I would choose A for the stated aim of reaching more eligible adults this round, accepting fewer nominal award slots and more administration. The reveal identifies an obtainable-evidence problem, not low need or unwillingness to cooperate. B’s additional empty slots do not remove that problem under the supplied forecast.

The alternative must establish the same relevant facts rather than waive necessary checks or assume every unsupported claim is true. Document consent, the evidence used, the reviewer and reasons. Explain both routes clearly, minimise retained data, and let applicants challenge errors without losing access to ordinary assistance.

Debrief · Monitor the whole journey

Track awareness, starts, completion, verified eligibility, lottery selection and actual payment separately. Record waits and reasons for stopping, including people who decline contact. Audit errors and reversals as well as completion gains. A trial with no detected mistakes does not establish perfect reliability or authorise unrestricted data sharing.

If valid demand or verification time differs from forecast, report the effect on capacity, spending and exclusions before expanding. Additional awards or a further redesign need funding and approval. Necessary safeguards and existing commitments remain protected; neither lower paperwork nor full budget spending is an end in itself.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: distinguish 100 funded slots from 60 complete packs. Minutes 4–8: reveal the certificate barrier and price the alternative. Minutes 8–13: calculate receipts, costs, rates and staff hours. Minutes 13–18: identify the new budget bottleneck and the checks needed for a responsible trial.

SUS-16 concerns completing a booking through usable channels; SUS-23 changes a benefit withdrawal schedule. Here access channels and eligibility are fixed. The distinctive choice is whether to spend part of an award budget on alternative documentary verification, increasing effective receipt while preserving the purpose of evidence.
