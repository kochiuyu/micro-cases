ENT-14 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Entrepreneurial exit · Earn-outs · Post-sale control

Sell now. Stay to build?

A price paid in stages

On 16 May 2025, Hong Kong-listed Left Field Printing disclosed completion of a bookbinding and print-finishing acquisition. Its consideration included an earn-out linked to earnings over the following three years, and the announcement described a continuing consultancy condition.

That disclosure illustrates a contingent price and continuing involvement, not the terms or outcomes of the invented offers below. No dispute or improper conduct is alleged.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. The sole founder of a Hong Kong exhibition-graphics studio is ready to sell all shares. One buyer offers two packages. A pays HK$900,000 at completion, with no continuing operating role after a common two-week handover. B pays HK$700,000 at completion and a further HK$400,000 if a defined order target is met over the next twelve months. Otherwise that extra payment is zero.

B also provides a twelve-month operating role, twenty paid hours a week, for a separate HK$240,000 annual salary. The founder can fulfil that commitment and would enjoy developing the studio, but also values time for a new project. A provides no salary or working obligation after handover. Neither package has been accepted.

Under either sale the buyer owns the business and protects existing staff and customer commitments. Both are feasible and professionally checked for this fictional exercise. Assume the buyer pays amounts that become due. Compare pre-tax, undiscounted amounts; personal tax and the value of alternative work are not supplied. A clean operating break does not mean every ordinary sale obligation disappears.

Your decision

Which package would you provisionally favour, and why? Separate sale proceeds from pay for work, then identify the target or authority detail you need before valuing B’s possible extra payment.

Round 2 · More information

Before either offer is accepted, the schedule defines the target: at least 120 eligible orders completed during the twelve months after completion. An eligible order is one distinct customer event-graphics job signed and delivered in that period, meeting the agreed quality specification and not cancelled or refunded. Splitting one job into several invoices does not create extra orders. At 119, the earn-out is zero; at 120 or more, it is HK$400,000.

In B the founder controls production scheduling and quality within the funded team. The buyer controls advertising spending, prices and which enquiries its group sends to this studio. No minimum advertising spend or referral volume is promised. The buyer has commercial reasons to allocate enquiries across its operations; this is not evidence of an intention to frustrate the target. No reliable probability of reaching 120 is available.

Both parties receive monthly order records. An independent accountant resolves disputes about the agreed count under a funded procedure. That process can correct counting errors; it cannot supply missing enquiries or override the buyer’s commercial decisions. For this comparison, assume the founder completes the agreed paid role and the salary is paid regardless of the target. A remains available unchanged. Any additional protection would have to be negotiated, not assumed.

Your decision

Would you take A, take B as offered, or seek a change to B? Link your choice to your desired post-sale life and one decision affecting the target; say what you would do if the buyer declines your requested change.

Do not call the maximum a certain price

A pays HK$900,000 for the shares. B pays HK$700,000 or HK$1.1 million for them: HK$200,000 less than A if the target is missed, or HK$200,000 more if achieved. The HK$240,000 salary is separate compensation for a year of work. Adding it to receipts does not make B a better sale price or a better use of the founder’s time.

Influence is not ownership

The founder can improve scheduling and quality but cannot choose advertising, pricing or referral allocation. Effort may help without making the target controllable. Monthly records and an independent count process improve measurement; they do not guarantee the commercial conditions required for the extra payment.

A clear target is valuable even when demanding. The problem is not that 120 is proved unattainable; no probability has been given. It is that the founder must decide how much uncertainty and dependence to accept alongside the continuing role.

Negotiate an exit, not just a headline

B may suit a founder who wants another year in the business and accepts contingent proceeds. A may suit one who values an operating break and certain sale consideration. The founder could request a defined marketing commitment or adjustment for agreed buyer-led changes. The buyer may reasonably resist restrictions that limit integration; the case supplies no accepted improved offer.

If negotiations fail, compare the two actual packages again. Do not assume a free option to take B’s salary, avoid its working commitment and retain its maximum earn-out. There is no supplied probability, discount rate or value of alternative work from which to calculate a universal winner.

Instructor note

First ask students to mark three columns: payment for shares, payment for labour, and decisions after sale. Then reveal the target and control schedule. Require one prioritized proposed change and a credible fallback, rather than rewriting the entire deal for free.

ENT-01 concerns a family trading name; ENT-09 concerns a founder handing over management while retaining ownership. Here ownership is sold and the founder weighs a continuing paid role and contingent consideration. Keep ordinary rights protected and do not turn the exercise into personal tax, investment or legal advice. No real transaction terms are copied into the fictional schedule.
