GVR-26 · Hong Kong · fictional organisations · 15–20 minutes · Dispute-resolution design · Interim arrangements · Proportionality

A dispute pending. What can still continue?

A process needs an outcome and a clock

Hong Kong Judiciary guidance distinguishes mediation, in which parties retain the settlement decision, from a tribunal deciding a claim. Its small-claims guidance also asks prospective claimants to consider cost, time and enforcement. Neither a cheap process nor a favourable decision automatically meets an operational deadline.

Professional mediation rules address agreed scope, costs, participation and settlement authority. The original fictional exercise below supplies its own schedules and costs. They are not court waiting times, institutional fees or a prediction about any actual dispute.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong exhibition supplier and a business customer dispute an HK$18,000 change-order charge. They possess the same signed specification, change request and delivery records; authenticity and versions are not disputed. They disagree about whether the requested work was included in the original price. No party is accused of dishonesty.

Undisputed invoices will be paid on their due dates. Both sides will continue all essential contracted work, safety duties and service commitments. Their advisers confirm that either of the two tailored routes below is available by mutual agreement. There is no urgent legal filing or limitation deadline within the exercise; real deadlines and rights would need separate advice. The student recommends a process, not a ruling on the charge.

Route M begins with authorised managers exchanging a short issue statement today, followed by mediation on day 6. If no agreement is signed that day, a separately appointed independent decision-maker can issue a determination on day 10. Route D goes directly to that decision-maker on day 3. For this exercise both parties have accepted a fair, documents-based procedure, an opportunity to answer each other, and a binding determination of the charge only. This is a fictional contractual arrangement, not a Small Claims Tribunal timetable or an assertion that all expert decisions are legally binding.

All figures are combined incremental costs to both businesses, split equally. The mediator has no power to impose a settlement. The determination costs HK$6,000 whenever used; mediation costs HK$2,000 whether it succeeds or fails. Fees include the specified advisers and administration. No settlement probability or value for preserving the relationship is supplied.

Round 2 · More information

The supplier has also reserved an optional production slot for a separate future display, beyond all existing service commitments. The hold expires on day 4. The customer’s approved purchasing rule requires the disputed charge to be resolved before it releases that optional next order, unless both sides sign a temporary ring-fencing arrangement. If the slot is lost, the next available slot is after the customer’s preferred launch. Existing contracted events are unaffected.

Both authorised managers can agree a temporary standstill on releasing the reserved slot until day 12 for HK$3,000 combined, split equally. This pays for storage and scheduling capacity; it is not payment of the disputed charge and is not refundable. It preserves the slot through either possible outcome of M. No party admits liability, releases its claim or changes undisputed payment dates. Advisers will check the limited written arrangement and preserve applicable rights; merely saying “without prejudice” is not treated as doing that work.

Under D the day-3 determination resolves the charge before the day-4 release decision, so the extra hold is not needed. Under M the hold must be agreed and funded now if the preferred slot is to remain available. Both can afford either route. No reliable value is supplied for the optional launch or a broader negotiated relationship improvement. A settlement or determination may transfer some or all of the HK$18,000 between the parties; do not count that transfer as a combined process resource cost.

Separate the issues

The charge, the next-order slot and the undisputed service are three different issues. Mediation can explore a mutually acceptable arrangement but cannot promise an outcome by day 6. Determination answers the stipulated charge question; it does not itself rewrite the future order, compromise unrelated claims or stop ongoing service. The slot has its own day-4 deadline.

Count the bridge, not just the meeting

Successful M with the hold costs HK$2,000 + HK$3,000 = HK$5,000 combined. Unsuccessful M followed by determination with the hold costs HK$2,000 + HK$6,000 + HK$3,000 = HK$11,000. D costs HK$6,000. Equal shares are HK$2,500, HK$5,500 and HK$3,000 per party respectively. The disputed charge is a separate possible transfer.

If p is the chance that M settles on day 6, its expected combined process-plus-hold cost is 5,000p + 11,000(1−p) = 11,000 − 6,000p. It is below D’s HK$6,000 only when p exceeds 5/6, about 83.3%, ignoring unpriced relationship benefits. No supplied evidence establishes that probability. Do not turn this threshold into a forecast.

Choose and preserve a fallback

D is defensible when predictable timing and bounded cost matter most and both sides accept the narrow decision process. M with the hold is defensible when a realistic chance of settlement or a valued wider agreement justifies the cost and downside. M without the hold is coherent only if the parties knowingly accept losing the preferred slot; it costs HK$2,000 if successful or HK$8,000 otherwise, plus an unvalued operational consequence.

For a hold, name the reserved slot, day-12 expiry, HK$3,000 cost and split, authorised signatories, continued undisputed payments and service, rights not released, and the day-6 decision point and day-10 fallback. The independent decision-maker is not automatically the mediator. Obtain appropriate advice on actual legal effects and deadlines. This exercise is process design, not a ready-to-sign legal instrument.

Instructor note

Take a first vote before revealing the slot expiry. Ask what will still happen while the fee remains disputed. Then compare the complete routes rather than calling mediation always cheaper or determination always hostile. The optional probability threshold is useful only after students distinguish joint resource cost from settlement transfers. Do not ask students to decide the legal merits without the underlying contract.

Unlike GVR-11, all agreement versions are clear; the disagreement concerns their application. All parties, fees, availability, deadlines, purchasing rules and agreed procedural effects are invented. Actual institutional rules are background, not adopted wholesale. A good answer preserves essential performance, distinguishes a temporary hold from settlement, and provides a timed fallback without assuming mediation succeeds.

Original fictional classroom design. All firms, contracts, disputed amounts, fees, schedules, production slots and purchasing rules are invented. Both tailored routes are stipulated as mutually available after advice; the binding effect and day-3/day-10 timing belong to this classroom agreement, not a real court or institution. Mandatory service and undisputed invoices continue. Combined process costs exclude any transfer of the disputed HK$18,000. With a HK$3,000 hold, mediation success costs HK$5,000 and failure followed by determination costs HK$11,000; direct determination costs HK$6,000. The expected-cost threshold p greater than 5/6 is conditional, not an estimated settlement likelihood. No value is supplied for the missed optional launch or relationship benefit.
