STR-14 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Equity entry mode · Local capabilities · Shared control

Own the operation. Share the local knowledge?

Preparing to operate abroad

On 4 November 2026, a Hong Kong Government reply described the GoGlobal Task Force’s support for Mainland enterprises preparing to expand overseas through Hong Kong, including connections with overseas partners. It did not recommend a particular ownership structure.

The business, destination and offers below are invented. The policy programme is context, not funding or endorsement for this fictional Hong Kong firm.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong maker of modular shop-display systems wants a small overseas assembly-and-repair operation. The owners can fund either proposal without harming existing commitments. Both serve the same target customers, can open on the same date and satisfy the destination’s entry rules in this exercise. Neither has been accepted.

A creates a wholly owned company. B creates a new joint-venture company: the Hong Kong maker owns 60% and an established local service firm owns 40%. Each operation needs HK$1.2 million of initial funding, covering setup and the agreed first-year operating plan. B’s partners contribute in those proportions. No further funding is assumed; later expansion needs a new decision.

The maker keeps its existing designs under either route. Under B, the proposed agreement requires both owners’ consent for the annual budget and changes to the product range, despite the maker’s 60% share. A permits the maker to decide these matters alone. The local firm says it will supply practical local knowledge as well as staff. What part of that contribution really needs an ownership partnership?

Your decision

Would you provisionally choose A or B? Explain the control and investment trade-off, and identify which claimed local contribution you would investigate before offering equity.

Round 2 · More information

Due diligence separates two contributions. Registration support, payroll administration and a suitable premises lease can be bought from independent providers under either plan; their full costs are already included. They do not require giving this firm an ownership stake.

The local firm also has a stable team experienced in diagnosing customers’ display problems on site. With customers’ permission, reference checks confirm repeat working relationships and practical knowledge of their installation routines. For B it commits that team to the operation under the funded plan and will train jointly employed staff. Its managers want an ongoing ownership interest and will not sell this team’s service as a stand-alone contract.

A remains viable: its funded recruitment and training plan can build its own service capability. It does not buy these existing relationships, and the speed of building comparable trust is unknown. There are no promised customer orders under either route. B still requires joint budget and product-range decisions; references do not prove future cooperation. You cannot assume the partner’s full contribution is available with A at the same cost.

Your decision

Which ownership route would you now recommend? Separate services you can purchase from capabilities the partner brings, then name one joint decision you must govern and the fallback if acceptable terms cannot be agreed.

Do not exchange equity for a vague label

“Local knowledge” contains different things. Routine administration can be purchased here. A practiced team and established working relationships are different: the proposal ties their deployment and shared learning to an equity commitment. This does not mean every local partner has unique knowledge or that relationships can be owned like equipment.

The smaller cheque buys a smaller claim

B reduces the maker’s initial contribution by HK$480,000: HK$1.2 million minus HK$720,000. The partner supplies that amount; it is not a saving in the operation’s total initial funding. The maker also shares the economic result. Sixty per cent of an unknown outcome cannot be ranked against 100% of another unknown outcome from these figures alone.

The partner’s experience could improve execution, but no earnings forecast establishes that benefit. A retains unilateral strategic decisions and the whole economic result while taking responsibility for building capability. B shares capital commitment and brings a team, while introducing dependence on continuing cooperation.

Make shared control workable

For B, ask how a product-range disagreement would be resolved, who can act while a budget is contested, and what happens if the committed team is withdrawn. Seek a practical learning and continuity arrangement as well as performance expectations. These are proposed terms to negotiate, not rights already secured.

A is defensible if independent control and internally developed knowledge matter more than immediate access to this team. B is defensible if the complementarity warrants shared ownership and workable joint decisions. If essential safeguards cannot be agreed, A remains the funded fallback; a cheaper non-equity version of B has not been offered.

Instructor note

Vote before revealing the reference checks. Ask groups to sort contributions into purchasable services, people-based capabilities and governance obligations. Finish with an ownership recommendation and one consequential decision rule, not a wish list of free safeguards.

STR-02 concerns a distributor buying and reselling goods. STR-06 shares facilities between competitors. STR-12 governs a customer-specific investment. This case chooses ownership of an operating entity abroad. Do not turn it into a nationality stereotype, a claim that majority ownership always gives unilateral control, or a profit calculation without forecasts. Entry legality and funding feasibility are stipulated; actual agreements require jurisdiction-specific professional advice.
