GVR-36 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Systematic remediation · Affected population · Accountable closure

The penalty is paid. Are customers restored?

A penalty and a remedy have different recipients

A penalty and a customer refund do different jobs. In February 2019, Australia’s ACCC reported both a court penalty against Optus and separate customer refunds and outreach commitments. That historical example motivates the distinction, not Hong Kong legal advice. The service company, billing error, obligations and amounts below are entirely fictional.

Your classroom decision

You lead remediation at fictional Hong Kong subscription-service company Harbour Help. A duplicate billing rule has caused a verified error. New duplicate charges have been stopped under a checked temporary control. Forty customers have complained and each is already verified as entitled to HK$200. A review of the full billing population has not yet finished.

The exercise supplies the obligations: every affected customer must receive the same HK$200 for this particular error, whether or not they complained or remain subscribed. This comprises HK$180 wrongly charged plus HK$20 assessed compensation for being deprived of the money. Additional proven harm must be assessed separately; accepting this payment cannot waive other rights.

A fixed HK$50,000 contractual penalty payable to a commercial partner is already fully funded outside the customer-remediation budget. It does not discharge customer entitlements. A separate HK$65,000 is ring-fenced for the current remediation programme. If obligations exceed that provision, the company must obtain more funding, not reduce what customers are owed.

Plan A pays the forty verified customers now and then waits for further complaints. Plan B pays the same forty now while completing a proactive population review and delivery plan for everyone affected. Both preserve the existing complaint route. The finance director says paying the penalty should close the incident and asks whether wider outreach is necessary.

Round 2 · More information

The completed review reconciles all 1,000 accounts exposed to the faulty rule. Exactly 200 paid the duplicate charge: the forty complainants and 160 others. Each has the same verified HK$200 entitlement. Of the 200, 120 are active accounts, including all forty complainants, and 80 are closed accounts. No other affected account remains unidentified in this exercise.

The 120 active-account customers can receive money back through verified original payment routes. Closed-account customers remain entitled, but their old payment routes no longer work. A privacy-reviewed tracing and payment-arrangement process using authorised records is available for HK$6,000. It does not buy external personal datasets or make renewal a condition of receiving money.

The HK$6,000 quote covers the full tracing effort, not a guarantee of reaching every person. Another HK$4,000 covers the completed population review, communications and payment administration for all 200, including the initial forty. Permanent billing correction and independent verification cost HK$10,000. These are complete incremental programme costs, including staff time and fees.

All these programme costs, including the initial forty payments, come from the HK$65,000 provision. The partner penalty remains outside it. The tracing work begins immediately and is scheduled over fourteen days while active-account payments proceed. A named remediation lead owns follow-up; an independent reviewer reconciles payments and unresolved balances rather than treating letters sent as completed redress.

If some people cannot yet be contacted, their entitlement and reserved money remain outstanding under the supplied policy. A missed response is not consent to surrender payment. No arbitrary claim cutoff, voucher, favourable review, renewal or release is required. Additional harm or newly discovered errors trigger separate assessment and funding; they are not silently charged against other customers’ entitlements.

Debrief · The penalty does not restore the customers

The HK$50,000 goes to the partner under a supplied contractual obligation. It does not return a duplicate charge to any customer. Neither paying it nor stopping new charges proves past harm has been repaired. Keep penalty payment, customer restoration and prevention of recurrence as separate responsibilities.

Approve B. All 200 affected customers meet the same supplied entitlement rule; 160 did not complain. Complaint status is not a measure of whether money was wrongly taken. Waiting for those people to discover the error would transfer identification effort and delay back to the people the company must remedy.

Debrief · Reconcile the full cost once

Total customer payments are 200 × HK$200 = HK$40,000. This includes HK$36,000 of duplicate charges and HK$4,000 of assessed deprivation compensation. The forty initial payments account for HK$8,000 of that total, leaving HK$32,000 to the other 160. Do not add the initial HK$8,000 again.

Programme cost is HK$40,000 payments + HK$6,000 tracing + HK$4,000 administration + HK$10,000 permanent correction and verification = HK$60,000. The HK$65,000 provision leaves HK$5,000 unallocated. Including the separately funded penalty gives HK$110,000 across both budgets, not a HK$110,000 charge against the remediation provision.

Debrief · Different delivery work, equal eligibility

Active accounts account for 120 × HK$200 = HK$24,000; closed accounts for 80 × HK$200 = HK$16,000. Closed status changes the payment work, not eligibility. The HK$6,000 tracing cost is additional operating effort and must not be deducted from the HK$16,000 owed to those customers.

Pay the verified active accounts without waiting for every closed-account case. Start tracing in parallel, use proportionate identity checks and explain the amount and reason through accessible channels. Customers should not have to recreate evidence already held by the company. A complaint and review route remains available for an error in the remediation itself.

Debrief · Outstanding money is not a saving

A tracing process can finish its scheduled work without reaching everyone. Report amounts actually paid, amounts still owed, reasons for delay and next actions. Keep unpaid entitlements reserved and pursue the approved follow-up process. Neither a closed account nor silence justifies recognising the unpaid amount as programme savings.

The HK$5,000 unallocated provision is not proof that all future obligations are covered. Assess additional harm or a newly identified population on its evidence and seek further resources if necessary. The fixed HK$200 applies only to the supplied error and assessed loss; it is not a universal compensation tariff or settlement cap.

Debrief · Closure needs delivery and prevention

The lead should reconcile the full affected-account register to successful payments and unresolved balances, with independent checking for duplicates and omissions. Communications should state what went wrong, what is being returned, how to query the calculation and how to verify the sender safely. Do not publish individual customer details.

Test the permanent billing correction against affected and unaffected account types, then monitor later billing cycles for recurrence. A training session or a paid invoice for system work is not evidence that the control works. Report the operational fix and the remaining customer cases separately rather than calling everything closed when the penalty is paid.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: separate the partner payment from customer entitlements. Minutes 4–8: reveal the full population and identify the non-complainants. Minutes 8–13: reconcile HK$60,000 without double-counting. Minutes 13–18: write a closure dashboard that distinguishes money delivered, unresolved entitlements and verified prevention.

This is a paper-based governance exercise, not authority to contact customers, inspect real billing records or send money. All obligations are supplied assumptions. Foreign regulatory and ombudsman material illustrates remediation design; it does not establish the law for this fictional Hong Kong company or authorise trading away customer rights.
