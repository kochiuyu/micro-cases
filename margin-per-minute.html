ACC-12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Limiting resource · Contribution · Order mix

The best margin. Per what?

Industry context

VTC’s summary of its 2025 Print Media and Publishing Manpower Survey describes changing printing activity and demand for technical and digital skills in Hong Kong.

That industry summary does not measure this workshop’s capacity or margins. The shop, requests and all numbers below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong print-finishing shop has 180 productive skilled-finishing minutes available tomorrow after existing commitments, breaks and required checks. Printing, materials and dispatch have enough capacity. Finishing time is the only scarce production resource for these additional requests.

Customers have confirmed they will buy up to six presentation-folder orders, A, and twelve simple-booklet orders, B, at the quoted terms if the shop accepts them today. Each order is a standard batch. The shop may accept any whole number within each limit; there are no minimum quantities, linked orders or prior delivery promises for these new requests. Customers can use another supplier for requests the shop declines.

A contributes HK$90 per completed order; B contributes HK$40. Contribution means sales less all costs that vary with the accepted order, before unchanged rent and scheduled staff pay. Those fixed costs are the same for either mix. Quality and safety requirements are identical whichever requests are selected.

The owner prefers A because each order contributes more. The supervisor wants to check finishing time before allocating the shift. Choose a provisional priority; no new order has been accepted and the checked time sheet arrives before the decision deadline.

Round 2 · More information

The checked time sheet shows that A uses 30 finishing minutes per order and B uses 10. These times include the relevant preparation, handling and quality checks; there are no extra batch changeovers or sequence effects. The stated per-order contributions and customer limits remain unchanged.

First work out the best mix within the original 180 minutes. Before any order is accepted, a qualified colleague then offers one optional 60-minute productive finishing block for an additional all-in HK$100. It includes all extra employer and operating costs; there is no unpaid work, displaced commitment or reduction in breaks or safeguards. The block is available only as a whole, cannot be resold and fits before dispatch.

If purchased, total finishing capacity becomes 240 minutes and the extra HK$100 is deducted separately from the mix’s contribution. No more than twelve B or six A orders can be sold. No future relationship benefit, further resource constraint or additional customer demand is assumed.

Your decision

What mix would you accept with 180 minutes? Would you then buy the extra 60-minute block for HK$100, and what revised mix and net improvement justify your decision?

Put the scarce minute in the denominator

A contributes HK$90 ÷ 30 = HK$3 per finishing minute. B contributes HK$40 ÷ 10 = HK$4 per minute. A has the higher contribution per order, but B makes more contribution from the scarce resource.

In the 30 minutes used by one A order, three B orders would contribute HK$120 rather than HK$90, while those B orders remain saleable. Do not compare contribution percentages or allocate unchanged rent to manufacture a different ranking.

Fill demand, then use the remaining time

Accept twelve B orders first: 120 minutes and HK$480 contribution. That exhausts B demand, leaving 60 minutes for two A orders and HK$180. The 180-minute mix is therefore twelve B plus two A, contributing HK$660.

Six A orders alone would use all 180 minutes but contribute HK$540. Eighteen B orders would yield more on paper, but six would have no customer under the supplied limits. The best feasible mix is not the same as producing only the first-ranked type.

Recompute when capacity changes

With 240 minutes, B remains capped at twelve orders and HK$480. The remaining 120 minutes allow four A orders, contributing HK$360. Total contribution is HK$840 before the additional block charge; after HK$100, the comparable amount is HK$740.

Against HK$660 without the block, the improvement is HK$80. Buy the block on the stated financial criterion and accept twelve B plus four A. All requests are still uncommitted, so this revision breaks no promise. The result remains before the unchanged fixed costs, not total-company profit.

Extra capacity has a bounded value

The additional hour supports two more A orders, adding HK$180 before its charge. It does not add 60 × HK$4 = HK$240 because B demand is already filled. HK$180 is the maximum charge for this exact block to leave the shop indifferent; below it there is a positive improvement.

Do not extrapolate that value indefinitely. At 300 total minutes all six A and twelve B requests could be filled. Further capacity would add no contribution from these requests. Different order sizes, several scarce resources, setup effects or customer commitments would require a fresh feasible-mix check.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes for the provisional priority, 3 for calculating contribution per minute, 3 for the 180-minute mix and 4 for pricing the extra block. Ask students to state quantities, minutes and contribution, rather than only saying B wins.

Opening information is deliberately incomplete; reward a conditional choice rather than treating A supporters as careless. After the reveal the numerical criterion has a clear answer. Keep skilled work, paid time, required checks and existing commitments protected. Ask why the lower-ranked A becomes the use of the next hour after B demand is filled. All inputs are fictional and confirmed only within this exercise; this is not a real staffing or pricing recommendation.
