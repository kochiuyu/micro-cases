SUS-19 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Funding additionality · Delivery and reversal risk · Climate contributions

A carbon project. Would it happen anyway?

Climate finance still needs a counterfactual

On 28 October 2022, Hong Kong’s Green and Sustainable Finance Cross-Agency Steering Group welcomed the launch of Core Climate as a voluntary carbon marketplace. A marketplace’s existence does not establish that every activity adds climate benefit. Our fictional company is considering a direct contribution, not buying a credit or making an investment return.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong company has HK$100,000 for one voluntary climate contribution. Its required environmental actions and own-emissions reduction programme are funded separately and continue. It may support one proposed restoration activity or retain the budget while evidence is clarified. Neither option permits an offset, carbon-neutral or quantified emissions-compensation claim. No credits, financial return or exclusive ownership of climate outcomes are offered.

Project A is an established restoration programme with an experienced team and secured funding; it seeks support for expansion. Project B is a new restoration programme whose proposed activity depends on new finance. Each requests the whole HK$100,000. The initial descriptions do not distinguish exactly which activity is already financed from what this contribution would enable. No transfer or public announcement has been made.

For the exercise, both locations have verified lawful land-use permission, appropriate environmental assessment and informed community agreement. Neither displaces people or shifts an existing legal restoration duty onto a donor. These safeguards do not prove extra carbon storage or lasting delivery. The company can require project-specific milestones, separate budgets and independent reporting before disbursement; it cannot infer tonnes of climate benefit from hectares or money alone.

Round 2 · More information

The funding records show that A’s baseline ten hectares are fully financed and will proceed without this company. Its additional two-hectare expansion is separately budgeted at HK$100,000 and is not covered by the baseline grant. The existing team and planting supply are available, and the expansion budget includes scheduled maintenance and monitoring. No substitute expansion funder is committed by the decision deadline. This supports a funding-gap case for the extra two hectares, not for all twelve.

B proposes six hectares with a total funding gap of HK$100,000; it will not start under its current plan without that finance. Its nursery supply is not yet secured, and its priced maintenance plan depends on a seasonal water-access arrangement still under negotiation. Funds would be held until those delivery conditions are resolved; if they are not, no disbursement occurs. Both proposals face weather and survival risks. Neither has independently quantified additional net carbon storage, a verified long-term reversal response or a demonstrated permanent outcome. More land is not automatically more climate benefit.

Your decision

Which project, if either, would you support on these facts? Separate the additional activity your funding could enable from delivery and durability, and name the evidence required before releasing funds.

Ask what your money changes

A’s existing ten hectares are the funded baseline. The company’s relevant funding question concerns the extra two hectares; supporting A need not simply replace already committed money. B has a financing gap for all six hectares, but a financing gap alone does not make its plan deliverable. Review other funding, budget restrictions and whether the activity would proceed anyway, rather than treating “new” as proof.

Keep additionality and execution separate

A offers a smaller expansion with more implementation arrangements in place. B offers a larger proposed area with unresolved nursery and water arrangements. Six hectares is three times two hectares, but that is an area comparison, not a carbon-benefit ratio. Survival, baseline conditions, effects outside the site, monitoring and reversal risks remain material.

A contribution is not verified compensation

A conditional contribution to either project can be defensible if it meets the company’s stated funding purpose and evidence threshold. Deferral is also legitimate. Record the supported activity, protect the already funded baseline, define milestone evidence and disclose delivery uncertainty. Do not convert an amount paid, a planting event or an area into a carbon-neutral claim. Additional activity, measured net climate benefit and durable storage are separate tests.

Instructor note

SUS-15 evaluates habitat connectivity; GVR-04 evaluates the scope of an environmental claim. This case asks about the funding counterfactual before a voluntary contribution. Carbon-credit integrity principles inform the questions but do not certify these fictional grants or establish formal credit additionality. All areas, budgets and arrangements are original assumptions. No programme standard, credit label or permanence guarantee is conferred.
