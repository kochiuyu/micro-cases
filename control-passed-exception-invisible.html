GVR-35 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Control-testing population · Manual workarounds · Evidence and oversight

A control passed. Was the exception invisible?

A clean dashboard can omit a channel

A control dashboard can be accurate about the records it tests while leaving an important route invisible. Governance and internal-audit guidance links conclusions to scope, risk and evidence. The private distributor, report and transactions below are fictional. This is an internal oversight exercise, not a financial-statement audit opinion or allegation about a real company.

Your classroom decision

You chair the audit committee of fictional private Hong Kong distributor Harbour Supply. Its annual internal report concerns one control objective: every release of an order on customer credit must have documented approval from an authorised reviewer independent of the preparer before goods leave. This is a supplied company rule, not a statement of local law.

The standard workflow blocks release until the required approval is recorded. A validated monitoring procedure checks the reviewer’s authority, separation of duties and approval timestamp against dispatch evidence for every standard-route release. The current report shows 900 tested releases and no exceptions during the stated year.

Management proposes A: accept an unqualified internal headline that the credit-release control operated for all orders. A second committee member proposes B: issue a limited conclusion that states the tested population and explicitly covers any alternative routes and unresolved exceptions before making a broader claim.

The committee must approve the internal report for its Friday meeting, not issue a public assurance opinion. Further necessary review is funded, and the deadline does not require a clean headline. Existing customer commitments and safe dispatch arrangements continue; no student decision executes a credit change, cancels an order or releases goods.

Round 2 · More information

A reconciliation of the complete dispatch register to both release channels identifies 1,000 unique credit releases: 900 standard and 100 manual. Every release belongs to exactly one route. The dashboard query excludes manual records by design. Its 900 is complete for the standard route but incomplete for the committee’s all-release objective.

Branch managers are authorised to use the manual route during system interruptions and for nonstandard credit terms. Permission to use that route does not waive the independent pre-release approval requirement. These releases need more judgement and lack the standard system’s automatic blocking; clean standard-route results cannot establish their control performance.

The reviewer inspects ten manual releases selected for unusual terms, not randomly. Six contain the required independent pre-release approval. For four, a documented timeline confirms that approval occurred only after dispatch. All ten had permission to use the manual route. The four late approvals are actual exceptions to the stated timing rule, not merely missing paperwork.

The other 90 manual releases remain untested. No credit loss, fraud, customer harm or monetary exposure total has been established. The ten selected cases are not a representative estimate for the whole manual route. The local permissions were genuine; their existence does not answer whether the separate credit control operated on time.

Management can implement an immediate, funded independent pre-release check for new manual releases while the reviewer evaluates the historical population and cause. A named operations owner and reviewer are available. This protective step preserves customer service but does not retrospectively correct the four releases or complete the annual effectiveness assessment.

Your decision

Write a defensible two-sentence committee conclusion. Separate standard-route results, known manual exceptions and the untested remainder. What follow-up and current protection are needed, and which percentages would overstate the available assurance?

Debrief · The original percentage has a boundary

The dashboard’s 900/900 = 100% result is valid for the specified approval checks on the standard route. Standard-route releases are 900/1,000 = 90% of all releases by count. Neither figure means 100% control effectiveness across the business or 90% assurance; transaction count is not a measure of risk exposure.

The missing route is not a random ten per cent of an otherwise uniform process. Manual releases involve different terms, judgement and blocking arrangements. Their exclusion matters to the all-release objective even though branch managers authorised use of the route and the standard-route check itself was valid.

Debrief · An approved route can contain a failed control

Permission to use the manual channel and approval of the particular credit release serve different purposes. The first authorises a workflow; the second must independently precede dispatch. Four confirmed late approvals fail that stated requirement. Later approval does not rewrite the historical sequence or prove that the timing control worked.

The four exceptions do not by themselves establish fraud, loss or the formal severity of a financial-reporting weakness. Investigate the circumstances, possible consequences and any other relevant controls. Do not dismiss a known breach because no loss is reported, or invent a loss amount merely because a control failed.

Debrief · Keep the selected cases separate

Ten of 100 manual releases have been tested, with four exceptions and six passes. The observed exception fraction is 4/10 = 40% for those selected cases. Because selection targeted unusual terms, it is not an estimated 40% exception rate for all manual releases, nor a basis for projecting 40 failures.

Four known exceptions among 1,000 total releases give a minimum known count fraction of 0.4%, not a final failure rate. Combining 900 standard passes with six manual passes yields 906 passes among 910 checked releases. That pooled fraction mixes a full route census with targeted selections and is not an all-release assurance score.

Debrief · Report what is known and unknown

A defensible conclusion is: “All 900 standard-route releases met the specified approval checks; the separate manual route comprised 100 releases and was excluded from that dashboard. Four of ten targeted manual checks found approval after dispatch, while 90 manual releases remain untested, so an all-release effectiveness conclusion is not supported.”

Choose B and retain the standard-route evidence rather than hiding it or declaring every control ineffective. Explain the omitted population, selection method, confirmed exceptions and remaining work. This is a limited internal conclusion with explicit findings, not a formal qualified audit opinion or a certification of legal compliance.

Debrief · Repair the monitoring boundary and follow through

Assign the operations owner to ensure the independent check occurs before each new manual release, with funded cover and escalation if approval cannot be obtained. Record route use, approver, timing and dispatch evidence. Keep operational responsibility with management; the reviewer verifies evidence rather than approving transactions on management’s behalf.

Have the reviewer investigate the four cases, assess the manual route’s design and risk, and determine sufficient further testing, including whether examining all 100 is appropriate. Reconcile both channels in future monitoring and report the follow-up timetable to the committee. Verify sustained operation after remediation; a revised query or policy alone does not establish effectiveness.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: challenge the denominator behind the clean headline. Minutes 4–8: reconcile 900 standard plus 100 manual releases. Minutes 8–13: distinguish route permission from timely independent approval and selected-case findings from population estimates. Minutes 13–18: draft a scoped conclusion and assign protective action and verification.

Keep the control objective fixed throughout. Do not reward changing the rule to make late approvals pass, calling unknown cases failures, or treating targeted checks as random sampling. A strong answer preserves valid evidence, makes the bypass channel visible and links unresolved risk to accountable follow-up without inventing financial loss.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All company rules, transactions, counts, checks and findings are fictional. Private company, internal committee reporting only. Approval must be independent, authorised, documented and before dispatch on both routes. The standard-route monitoring procedure is validated for that stated check; 900 passes are not whole-system assurance. The complete reconciled population contains 900 standard and 100 manual unique releases. Manual-route permission is separate from required approval. Ten manual cases are deliberately selected for unusual terms; six meet the control and four have confirmed post-dispatch approval. Ninety remain untested. Four known exceptions are at least 0.4% of total releases, not an estimated final rate; 40% describes only the ten targeted cases. No monetary exposure, loss, fraud or formal weakness classification is supplied. Further work and immediate protective checks are funded; committee oversight and management operations remain separate.
