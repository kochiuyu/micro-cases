GVR-24 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Purpose limitation · Secondary use · Identifiability

Permission for service. Permission for research?

Existing information, a new question

Holding information for one job does not settle every later use. Purpose review, meaningful permission and protection against identification answer related but different questions. Removing names alone does not settle whether an output can be linked back to a person.

This exercise supplies its own reviewed company policy. Apply that policy rather than certify legal compliance. Real research and privacy rules can contain context-specific conditions or exemptions that need qualified review.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong household-equipment hire company legitimately holds 600 recent adult-customer support messages for resolving service queries and improving that service. Its reviewed policy permits a bounded internal service review, but not unrelated individual profiling, external reuse or a claim that every future study is covered. The original operational records remain in their protected support system under the normal justified retention schedule; this project does not extend retention or delete required records.

The manager has 12 funded person-hours this month and wants to understand confusing return instructions. Aggregate review uses eight hours, including authorised analyst work, disclosure checks and privacy review. It produces only broad issue counts from those messages, with identifiers, exact dates, rare categories and quotations excluded or combined as needed. No customer-level file or linkage key leaves the authorised support team. Release requires documented identifiability checks; a table is not automatically anonymous because it is small or has no names.

Voluntary interviews use all 12 hours for an already-approved neutral opt-in invitation, clear purpose explanation, up to 20 adult interviews and protected analysis. Participation is genuinely optional, ordinary service is unchanged by refusal, and no old message is linked to an interview without separately specified permission. The study concerns return-instruction experiences, not health or financial profiles. Recruitment may yield fewer than 20 and a self-selected sample. The staff-time estimates include all stated safeguards; both routes are feasible but cannot be combined this month. Neither route exports raw messages or authorises future purposes.

Round 2 · More information

Before any new analysis or transfer, a proposed output specification arrives. It asks for one row per customer, a coded account identifier and a guessed financial-distress label inferred from message wording, then asks the support team to reconnect the codes to named accounts. It also requests distinctive quotations. The claim is that more detailed rows will make the study more useful; no evidence of accuracy is supplied.

The privacy lead confirms this specification is outside both authorised routes and must not be run. Coding names does not make the planned return linkage anonymous. Neither a service-improvement label nor interview consent for return-instruction experiences permits the new sensitive inference. No customer-level predictions, exports or quotations have yet been created. Do not test the proposal on real messages.

You may keep the eight-hour aggregate design focused on confusing instructions, leaving four hours for another approved task, or use the twelve-hour interview design with the same bounded purpose and unlinked accounts. A different sensitive study would require a separate necessity, legal and ethics assessment and any appropriate specific permission before consideration; no such approval or funded design exists here. Consent is not a shortcut around unnecessary or harmful processing.

Your decision

How would you redesign the proposal after seeing the requested customer-level output? Choose an authorised route within the time budget, explain what insight it sacrifices, and state what must not be produced or shared.

Choose the learning question before the dataset

Aggregate categories can reveal common operational friction across these support messages. They cannot explain every individual experience, and 600 messages are not necessarily 600 different people or a representative sample of all customers. Interviews can ask why instructions confused participants, but the volunteer group does not establish prevalence across all customers.

Choose aggregate review for broad triage of wording problems; choose interviews for exploratory explanations. Either can be justified by the management question. Neither gives permission to create an individual vulnerability profile.

A code is still a route back

The requested code-to-account reconnection deliberately preserves identifiability. Distinctive wording may also identify someone even without a name. Protecting the code key is a security safeguard, not proof that the output is anonymous or that the purpose is permitted.

Keep authorised operational records separate from project outputs. Apply disclosure checks to the combined output and foreseeable linkage, not just each cell in isolation. No universal minimum group size or zero-risk guarantee is supplied here.

Redesign without making privacy the bargaining chip

Remove the financial-distress inference, customer-level return file and distinctive quotations. Document the bounded question, source access, output checks, recipients and retention decision. If the chosen safe route cannot answer an individual-level question, say so; a narrower honest answer is better than presenting prohibited detail as necessary accuracy.

For interviews, explain what is studied, how information is used, who receives results and how withdrawal works, including any practical limits after irreversible aggregation. Record the agreed scope and do not penalise refusal. New questions require review, not an assumption that prior agreement travels with the data forever.

Instructor note

Spend 2 minutes comparing breadth with depth, 3 mapping the proposed data flow and 6 choosing the revised route. Ask who can reconnect codes and what the original purpose covers. The decision is not whether to trade privacy for better predictions: the proposed profiling is outside the supplied authorisation. Legitimate choices remain between two bounded methods with different learning value. No real messages, sensitive inferences or system changes are needed.
