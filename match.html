FIN-01 · Hong Kong · Ticket pricing · 10–15 minutes

Who pays when the star sits out?

Hong Kong Stadium, 4 February 2024. Fans came to an exhibition match hoping to see Lionel Messi play for Inter Miami. The match went ahead. Messi stayed on the bench.

Imagine you are selling tickets for the next exhibition. Your headline player may miss the match. Before sales open, you must decide what to promise buyers.

Debrief · Price the promise

For every 100 protected seats, the organizer collects HK$5,000 in premiums. If the player sits out, all 100 buyers receive HK$400, a HK$40,000 payout. At a 20% chance, the expected payout is HK$8,000. The expected shortfall is HK$3,000, or HK$30 per ticket.

HK$80 is the break-even premium if sales volume stays fixed and administering refunds is free. A lower premium could still make sense if the guarantee attracts enough extra buyers. A risk-averse buyer might pay more than the expected refund for protection.

Decide

Would you raise the premium to HK$80, keep it at HK$50, or drop the guarantee? What assumption drives your choice?

On 9 February 2024, Tatler Asia announced a 50% refund for match tickets purchased through official sales channels. Fans learned of this offer after the game. In the exercise, buyers know the refund terms before deciding whether to purchase.

Instructor note · Running the discussion

First 3 minutes. Ask for a show of hands on each ticket. Ask one voter what probability they implicitly assumed.

Next 4 minutes. Reveal the one-in-five estimate. Ask pairs to price the promise from both sides of the sale.

Final 5 minutes. Ask whether selling more protected tickets diversifies the risk. One player’s absence triggers every claim, so a larger crowd increases the payout due on the same day. Reveal the historical response after students defend a policy.
