STR-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · private label · strategic counterfactual · channel scope

Make the store brand, or face it?

A retailer can own the label without running the factory

Watsons describes qualifying and visiting manufacturers of its own-brand products. Costco says in its annual filing that it purchases or manufactures private-label merchandise and also sells national brands. The retailer, manufacturer and terms below are fictional and do not describe either company’s arrangements.

Your classroom decision

Maple Loom makes premium cleaning cloths under its own brand in Hong Kong. A retailer will launch a store-brand cloth and invites Maple Loom to manufacture it for an initial shop-only run. If Maple Loom declines, the retailer orders from a rival. Neither option removes Maple Loom’s own brand from the market.

Compare annual contribution with a benchmark in which the retailer did not launch any store brand. Each lost Maple Loom brand sale reduces contribution by HK$30. If Maple Loom takes the contract, it earns HK$12 contribution per store-brand unit before a HK$6,000 one-time setup. It has enough capacity for either option.

Forecasts in the exhibit are the losses relative to the same benchmark, not losses on top of the rival case. Assume all other enforceable terms, costs and sales are unchanged.

Should Maple Loom take the shop-only contract? Show the contribution change under each decision and explain why the no-launch benchmark is not the result of saying no.

Round 2 · More information

The retailer revises its offer. If Maple Loom supplies, the retailer would also list the store-brand cloth online, including where shoppers find Maple Loom’s own brand. Forecast lost Maple Loom brand sales rise to 1,100. Paid volume, unit margin and setup do not change. If Maple Loom declines, the rival still supplies the shop-only version with the same 450 lost sales.

Your decision

Would you accept the revised offer? Find the smallest extra annual payment from the retailer that would make acceptance no worse than declining.

First decision: refusal is not the old market

The shop-only contract contributes 2,000 × HK$12 − HK$6,000 − 700 × HK$30 = −HK$3,000 versus no launch. Declining costs 450 × HK$30 = HK$13,500 versus no launch. Choose the contract: it is HK$10,500 better than refusal even though the market as a whole is worse for Maple Loom than no launch.

Second decision: put a price on permission to sell online

The broader offer contributes HK$24,000 − HK$6,000 − HK$33,000 = −HK$15,000. Declining still costs HK$13,500, so refuse as offered. HK$1,500 extra would make the two choices tie. Keeping the shop-only scope would be worth discussing because it changes the source of the loss, not just the payment.

What to take away

A retailer’s fallback changes the supplier’s outside option. A wider channel can make the same paid volume less valuable. Ask what the retailer would do after a refusal and which of your own sales each option replaces.
