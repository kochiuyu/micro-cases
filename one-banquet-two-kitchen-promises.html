One banquet. Which meals can we promise?

Venue wording can distinguish different kinds of meal claims

Hong Kong Tourism Board’s 2024/25 annual report describes working with Hong Kong’s halal certification authority. Hospitality providers also describe which foods are covered and when guests should request them. The policy, suppliers, offers and event below are fictional; nothing here asserts the scope of any real venue’s certification.

Your classroom decision

A Hong Kong conference hotel is arranging a lunch. The organiser requires a verified certified halal meal for every guest who specifically requests one by the final count. The hotel’s standard menu is not represented as meeting that requirement. The hotel has checked the exact offered meals and their evidence with both external caterers and the organiser. Neither option implies the whole venue is certified.

The count could be 12, 16 or 24. Choose one supplier now. Its fixed charge reserves the whole quoted capacity; pay per meal only for the final count. No split order or additional supplier is available after the choice. The special-meal budget is capped at HK$2,400 including the fixed charge, regardless of which count occurs. Standard-menu costs do not vary in this exercise.

Round 2 · More information

Before the hotel commits, the organiser confirms exactly 16 requests and closes the order with the guests’ agreement. No further request is part of this lunch order. Both suppliers’ terms, meal evidence and capacities are unchanged.

Your decision

Which supplier should the hotel choose now? Calculate the cost difference, and say what must be confirmed before using the lower quote.

Debrief · A quote can be cheaper and infeasible

At 12, A costs HK$1,354 and B HK$1,490. At 16, A costs HK$1,722 and B HK$1,770. At 24, A cannot serve the group; its arithmetic price is not an available offer. B can serve 24 for HK$2,330, within the cap. Reserve B while all three counts are possible.

Debrief · Pay for meals, not the maximum

B’s fixed charge reserves capacity, but variable charges use the actual number of meals ordered. It is not necessary to pay for all 28 slots. The higher fixed charge is worthwhile when it preserves the ability to meet the 24-meal commitment.

Debrief · Once the count is firm, compare like with like

With exactly 16 verified orders, A meets the promise and saves HK$48. Confirm the locked count and the exact documented offer and service arrangements before choosing it. If capacity must exceed 16, A is not an eligible saving.

Debrief · Say exactly what has been verified

Tell the organiser which requested meals are covered and how guests receive them. Do not describe the venue or standard menu as certified based on a supplier’s meal evidence. If the count or evidence changes, pause confirmation and renegotiate without promising an unverified substitute.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to check both schedules, 4 to decide after the update, and 5 to discuss precise customer wording. Ask students to distinguish promised coverage from expected demand.
