SUS-39 · Hong Kong · fictional district pilot · 15–20 minutes · Public-service co-production · Feedback and accountability · Reports versus resolutions

Citizens report it. Who closes the loop?

Participation needs a service response

Public reporting can reveal local service needs, but receiving a report is not the same as resolving its subject. A useful participation process includes an understandable response and a responsible service team. The district, budget and records below are invented, not an assessment of an actual Hong Kong agency.

Your classroom decision

You coordinate a fictional Hong Kong district drain-observation pilot. Adult residents may voluntarily report visible surface concerns from ordinary safe public footpaths. They must not approach floodwater, enter roads or drains, lift covers, sample water or attempt repairs. Established emergency reporting and professional inspections continue separately under both options.

Next month’s additional HK$10,000 can fund A: accessible outreach and safe-reporting guidance, with a planning estimate of sixty extra reports; or B: a case coordinator, progress feedback and ten extra professional service visits. These are fixed, all-in fictional packages. Neither changes the routine reporting channels or any existing service obligation.

Existing funding provides twenty professional service visits next month under either option. One visit addresses one location’s case, including inspection, feasible minor work and a documented outcome. Some cases need further work: a visit is not a guaranteed resolution. B’s ten extra visits are additional capacity, not replacements for existing staff.

This month the pilot received 120 reports and recorded twenty location cases as resolved, with professional verification. There was no opening backlog. The dashboard has not linked reports to distinct locations. The outreach team calls 120 reports “120 places covered” and wants to expand collection. Decide whether that claim justifies A.

Residents can decline or stop reporting without penalty. Funded safeguards, accessibility and necessary participant support remain under either package. Paid professionals retain technical assessment, priority and repair responsibility. Reports are not instructions to fix something blindly, and recruitment targets must never encourage unsafe observation or unnecessary personal information.

Round 2 · More information

A checked register links all 120 reports to sixty distinct drain locations, with exactly one case per location this month. Twenty cases are professionally verified as resolved and forty remain unresolved. Forty reports relate to the resolved cases; eighty relate to the unresolved cases. No report concerns a new episode after a resolved case reopened.

Thus sixty reports are additional reports beyond the first at each location. Residents describe repeating reports because they cannot see whether anyone has accepted responsibility or acted. Their accounts identify a feedback concern, not proof that every repeat was caused by missing updates. A repeat may still supply new evidence of deterioration.

B includes twenty paid coordinator-hours. At the supplied half-hour allowance per existing unresolved case, this covers checking its route, recording the responsible team and sending an accessible status update to its reporters. The allowance includes all forty cases’ linked reporters. Routine new-case handling remains funded separately; no software purchase or public disclosure of identities is needed.

The ten extra visits can serve ten distinct existing unresolved cases, selected by the professional team using urgency and need, not report popularity. Unresolved ownership questions go to the authorised district lead; the coordinator cannot invent another agency’s powers. Major work, emergencies and statutory duties use their existing routes and are not delayed to meet this pilot schedule.

A’s sixty extra reports remain an estimate; their unique locations, urgency and overlap are unknown. B does not guarantee ten completed repairs, prevent all repeat reports or remove the backlog. Both packages remain affordable, and neither closes ordinary channels for new locations or concerned residents.

Your decision

Reconcile reports, distinct cases and unresolved cases. Which package would you choose now, and how would you connect a resident’s report to ownership, professional action and feedback? Calculate B’s coordinator time and maximum distinct existing cases its extra visits can address, without calling those visits completed resolutions.

Debrief · Count three different things

There are 120 reports, sixty distinct location cases and twenty verified resolutions. Forty cases remain unresolved. The resolved share of this case cohort is 20/60 = one-third, not 20/120. The latter mixes completed cases with report submissions. Neither ratio measures drainage safety across the district.

Sixty reports are beyond the first per location: 120 − 60. Among unresolved cases, eighty reports minus forty first reports leaves forty repeats; resolved cases account for forty minus twenty = twenty repeats. Reconcile forty plus twenty = sixty. Repeated reports are not sixty additional geographic locations.

Debrief · Prefer the service loop here

Choose B on the revealed evidence. It connects already identified needs to a responsible service route, professional capacity and a response to participants. A might be preferable where important areas are not represented, but this record does not show that another sixty submissions would add sixty locations or resolve an existing case.

B’s coordinator allowance is 40 × 0.5 = twenty paid hours. It covers routing and status communication, not engineering assessment or repair. A message acknowledging receipt must not be labelled a verified resolution. Give each case a responsible team, its current state and the next expected update or escalation.

Debrief · Capacity is not completion

B increases next month’s funded visits from twenty to thirty. Its ten extra visits can address at most ten distinct existing unresolved cases as specified. If all ten independently achieve verified resolution, they could reduce the opening forty-case backlog by ten; that is a conditional maximum for the extra visits, not a forecast.

Existing visits, new reports, complex work and future reopenings affect the month-end queue. The case supplies no final backlog prediction. Do not subtract thirty automatically from forty, count a second visit as another location, or close a record merely because it was referred to a professional team.

Debrief · Make the resident’s role meaningful

Acknowledge the observation with a case reference, explain the responsible route and provide status through the resident’s chosen accessible channel. When work is verified, explain the outcome; when more work or another authority is needed, explain what remains and who follows up. Invite relevant corrections without asking residents to undertake technical inspection.

Link repeat reports to the case while preserving new information, dates and the ability to flag worsening conditions. Do not discard repeats automatically or penalise a concerned resident. Share only appropriate service information; reporters’ identities and contact details need not appear in a public status view.

Debrief · Measure the whole process

Track distinct locations reached, accepted responsibility, elapsed waiting time, professional findings, verified resolutions and updates delivered. Examine which neighbourhoods or reporting formats are underrepresented. A larger report count may reflect engagement, duplicate submission or worsening conditions; a smaller count may reflect resolution, disengagement or barriers. Neither direction alone proves success.

Instructor note

Use four minutes for the package choice, five for the report-to-case reconciliation and six to design an understandable status route. SUS-17 matches voluntary hours to service coverage; IS-14 evaluates alert accuracy and checking cost. This case instead connects citizen contribution to accountable follow-through without treating more observations as more service delivered.
