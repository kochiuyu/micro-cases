ACC-42 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · HKAS 1 · Current and non-current liabilities · Loan covenants · Non-adjusting events

The lender granted more time. When did the right change?

The loan maturity date is not always the balance-sheet answer

Hong Kong’s amendments to HKAS 1 on liability classification and covenants applied to annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2024. They distinguish conditions the borrower must meet by the reporting date from those due only later. The financial statements can show a loan as non-current while still warning readers it could become payable sooner.

Your classroom decision

As at 31 December 2025, a fictional Hong Kong packaging supplier has two equipment loans. The draft balance sheet lists both as non-current because their scheduled principal repayments are due in 2029. Mei, the controller, reviews the agreements before the accounts are authorised on 20 February 2026. The group has not adopted HKFRS 18 early.

Both lenders require interest cover of at least 3.0, using contractual operating earnings divided by interest. Each lender can demand its principal immediately if its own covenant is breached. The loans do not trigger each other. There are no other call rights, covenants or scheduled principal payments in the first twelve months after year-end.

A’s November test is only checked by its lender in January. The evidence confirms that A’s ratio was 2.9 on the required date and its lender already had the right to demand repayment from then. B’s first required ratio uses June 2026 figures. If calculated on the year-end figures instead, B’s ratio would also be 2.9.

A’s lender grants a binding grace period on 20 January 2026, effective when signed, through 31 March 2027. During it the borrower can rectify the breach and the lender cannot demand repayment. The company has HK$28m of current assets and HK$10m of current liabilities before classifying these two loan principal balances.

Debrief · A’s right was lost before year-end

A is current: the missed November condition allowed its lender to demand payment at 31 December. January’s check confirms an earlier breach; it does not move the compliance date. January’s signed grace period changes the contract only from January, so it cannot restore the right at year-end.

Debrief · B’s covenant belongs to the future

B is non-current. It is not required to comply until June, and that test is based on June figures. A December ratio below 3.0 warns of possible difficulty but is neither a breach nor a reason to classify B as current.

The original draft would show a current ratio of 28 ÷ 10 = 2.8. Move A’s HK$18m into current liabilities: the correct ratio is 28 ÷ (10 + 18) = 1.0. HK$12m of the two loans remains non-current. Classification changes presentation, not the cash or principal owed.

Debrief · Tell readers about both risks

For A, explain the November breach and the HK$18m current principal. Disclose the January grace period as a material non-adjusting event: describe its duration and effect on the lender’s ability to demand repayment of the HK$18m. It does not change the reporting-date numbers.

For B, disclose the HK$12m carrying amount, the 3.0 covenant, the June compliance date and the 2.9 ratio calculated using December figures. That ratio is evidence of risk to report, not the actual June result.

Debrief · A December letter changes the year-end right

In the alternative, A’s substantive right to defer settlement existed at 31 December, with a grace period ending more than twelve months later. The lender could not demand repayment during that period. A is non-current, along with B, and the ratio is 2.8. The December letter is not a subsequent event.

B’s note does not go away. A higher current ratio does not remove the June covenant risk or provide cash. A grace period agreed before year-end that ended inside the next twelve months would not, on these facts, establish a right to defer for at least twelve months.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 3 to map the dates, 4 for the classification and ratio, 4 to write the notes, and 4 for the alternative letter. Ask each group to point to the date the right existed, not to when the team got the evidence.

The note prompts focus on the two timing issues, not every applicable borrowing or liquidity disclosure. The agreed terms and enforceability are supplied facts. No waiver request, refinancing, profit adjustment or payment occurs in this exercise.
