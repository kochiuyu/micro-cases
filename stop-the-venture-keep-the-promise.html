Stop the venture. Keep the promise?

Stopping trade is not the final task

On 2 September 2026, Australia’s business.gov.au published a reminder that record, tax and registration obligations can continue after trading stops. It is Australian administrative guidance, not Hong Kong law or a report about the studio below. Our Hong Kong founders, customer commitments and handover terms are original fiction.

Your classroom decision

Two founders of a small Hong Kong paper-design studio have decided to stop taking new work and close the venture. The business is solvent. They have sufficient ring-fenced funds and paid capacity to meet every existing obligation, including customer work, employee and contractor entitlements, suppliers, tax, records and appropriate insurance. Professional advice and administrative closure work are separately funded. This is an operational wind-down exercise, not insolvency or legal advice.

They must choose an approach before announcing dates to customers. F retains their delivery team for a funded eight-week final fulfilment period. T seeks a staged handover to a qualified studio, with customers’ informed agreement, while retaining whatever work cannot be transferred. Both routes protect original quality, delivery dates, support and refund rights. No customer must accept a replacement or waive a right to help the founders leave sooner.

The founders prefer to reduce their own delivery workload, but value a dependable end to the venture. They remain available for the full eight weeks under either route. A handover introduces coordination and monitoring work; retaining everything preserves direct control but keeps the team occupied. An adequate contingency funds original delivery if the handover does not proceed. No additional sales, unpaid work or future funding is needed. The founders have not yet sorted the live project files. What must be mapped before they can describe a credible closure timetable?

Round 2 · More information

The verified obligation map separates three groups. Twelve prepaid design orders are due by the end of week six: eight standard-format orders and four bespoke orders. Three other clients hold paid support promises lasting through week eight. Six further people only requested information; they paid nothing, received no accepted offer and hold no service or follow-up promise. The six must not be described as completed orders or transferred customers.

The qualified successor has documented paid capacity for the eight standard orders and two of the three support clients, on the original terms. It cannot deliver the bespoke work or support the third client’s specialist format. Seven standard-order customers freely agree to the documented handover; one prefers the original studio. Both eligible support clients agree. Necessary information is shared only within their authorised handover; there is no blanket transfer of the enquiry list. The original studio remains responsible for oversight and the funded fallback until agreed completion is confirmed.

Under T, the original team therefore retains five orders through week six and one support promise through week eight. Under F it retains all twelve orders and all three support promises. Customers keep all rights under either route; a refund is an option only where lawfully agreed, not an automatic substitute for promised work. No compensation or waiver is assumed. All work fits the protected paid capacity and budget. The successor can reduce founder delivery work, but cannot make the final support obligation disappear or justify immediate administrative dissolution.

Your decision

After mapping the commitments, would you choose F or T? Allocate the twelve orders and three support promises, distinguish the six enquiries, and explain what must remain in place until the final commitment is resolved.

Close new sales before closing responsibility

A stop-sales date prevents fresh commitments while existing ones are fulfilled or lawfully resolved. The last order, final support period and administrative completion need not occur together. Record the owner, due date, delivery evidence and fallback for each promise rather than announcing one unsupported final date.

Transfer only what can actually travel

Under T, seven standard orders transfer and five remain: four bespoke plus the standard customer who declines. Two support promises transfer and one remains. Twelve equals seven plus five; three equals two plus one. Technical suitability and customer agreement are separate gates. A qualified successor’s general willingness does not transfer an obligation by itself.

Keep a funded tail

The remaining support lasts through week eight. Retain competent paid staff, a reachable contact, records and the resources needed to honour it. Administrative deadlines must be met throughout the wind-down. Complete closure only when the applicable conditions are met, not simply after a classroom vote or the last service date. Monitor accepted handovers and keep the promised fallback until completion; do not distribute the protected funding merely because delivery moves elsewhere.

Instructor note

F can be reasonable when direct control and fewer handoff dependencies matter most. T can reduce original-team delivery work while preserving duties, but adds coordination and verification. No quantified reputation benefit, probability of failure or cost ranking is supplied. The six enquiries are neither prepaid orders nor a permission to market or transfer personal data; communicate the end of new bookings without inventing obligations or revenue.
