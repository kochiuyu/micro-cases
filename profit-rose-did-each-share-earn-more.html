ACC-45 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Earnings per share · Time-weighting · Convertible debt · Anti-dilution

Profit rose. Did each share earn more?

Listed companies distinguish basic and diluted earnings per share

Global Corn Group’s 2025 results show a public example in which assumed bond conversion was dilutive in one year but excluded as anti-dilutive in the next. The case below uses a different, fictional company and simplified earnings.

Your classroom decision

You review the annual results of a fictional Hong Kong-listed equipment distributor. Its profit attributable to ordinary holders rose from HK$58m to HK$60m. All profit is from continuing operations. The communications draft says that earnings per ordinary share also rose.

Last year 100m ordinary shares were outstanding throughout. This year began with 100m and the company issued 40m new shares and became entitled to the cash consideration on 1 July. Treat the halves as equally long for this exercise. There were no buybacks, bonus issues, preference shares or other share changes.

A convertible note was outstanding from the start of this year and not actually converted. It would add 10m ordinary shares if converted at the start. The only change to the numerator from assumed conversion is the attributable after-tax interest saving shown below. There were no potential ordinary shares last year.

Fictional inputThis year
Profit attributable to ordinary holdersHK$60m
Ordinary shares, first half100m
Ordinary shares, second half140m
Shares from assumed conversion10m
After-tax interest avoided on conversionHK$3m
Your decision

What basic and diluted EPS should the distributor report? Is its claim that basic earnings per share rose supported?

Round 2 · More information

Before the results are final, an independent worksheet review finds HK$6m of the same already-recorded total after-tax interest relates to the convertible, not HK$3m. Some interest previously attributed to another loan was actually on the note. Total recognised profit, actual shares and the note’s conversion terms do not change.

Your decision

Should assumed conversion still be included in diluted EPS? How much after-tax interest saving would make assumed conversion exactly neutral?

Debrief · Weight the shares actually outstanding

Basic EPS uses 100m × ½ + 140m × ½ = 120m shares. Basic EPS is HK$60m ÷ 120m = HK$0.50, down from HK$0.58. Profit rose while basic EPS fell. The year-end 140m alone is not the annual denominator.

Debrief · Rebuild both sides

With assumed conversion, earnings would be HK$63m and the denominator 130m shares. HK$63m ÷ 130m is about HK$0.4846, below HK$0.50. Include the potential shares and interest adjustment. This is diluted EPS, not the number actually earned by or distributed to noteholders.

Debrief · Test before including the note

With the corrected HK$6m saving, the hypothetical calculation is HK$66m ÷ 130m ≈ HK$0.5077. Including it raises EPS, so the note is anti-dilutive and excluded. With no other instrument, diluted EPS equals basic EPS of HK$0.50.

Debrief · Find the dividing line

Let x be the after-tax interest the company would avoid, in HK$m. At (60 + x) ÷ 130 = 0.50, x is 5. The conversion amount only reduces EPS when x is below that threshold. Changing the note’s attributed cost did not itself change actual profit.

Instructor note

Give four minutes for the denominator, four for the conversion test, four to replay and three for a revised investor sentence. Ask where both figures go and why a day-count or time weighting rule must be consistent. The exercise uses one convertible, one continued business and no valuation or accounting advice.
