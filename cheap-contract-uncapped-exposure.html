GVR-21 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Contractual risk allocation · Liability caps · Excluded losses

A cheap contract. An uncapped exposure?

A cap is only one part of the bargain

Public UK procurement contract terms illustrate that monetary caps, excluded loss categories and exceptions can sit in the same responsibility section. New Zealand’s model-contract guidance likewise treats a limit as a choice to assess, not a universal requirement. These are public-sector examples from other jurisdictions, not rules for every Hong Kong company.

This case uses invented commercial terms and a supplied interpretation. No real dispute, contract or supplier is described. It teaches a risk-allocation calculation, not how a court would decide enforceability.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You are the commercial manager of a Hong Kong company buying event-management services for an indoor product showcase. One competent supplier offers two packages for the same service, staff, checks and delivery dates. A has a lower fee; B offers broader financial recovery. Nothing has been agreed or paid. You are preparing a recommendation, not signing a contract.

The table shows the cover-sheet figures. An aggregate cap means one ceiling across the specified claims, not a separate allowance for every cost item. Both caps are expressly subject to an attached loss schedule; you must review it before final approval. No failure probability, expected-loss estimate or insurance payment is supplied. The company can fund either fee and has a separately funded contingency sufficient for the planning loss considered later, without using money committed to wages or other obligations.

All normal event, venue, accessibility and safety requirements remain protected. The exercise concerns ordinary commercial performance losses only, not injury, fraud or liabilities that cannot legally be restricted. Appropriate advisers handle real legal questions. Compare the given terms as stipulated; do not assume a larger insurance certificate changes what the supplier owes.

First decide which loss enters the calculation

A’s sponsor-refund exclusion removes HK$50,000 before the cap is applied. Only HK$30,000 enters A’s calculation, so compensation is the smaller of HK$30,000 and HK$40,000: HK$30,000. The buyer retains the HK$50,000 refund loss. A cap is a ceiling on covered compensation, not a promise to pay the cap or a limit on the buyer’s own loss.

B includes both categories. HK$30,000 plus HK$50,000 is HK$80,000, below its HK$100,000 aggregate cap. Under the supplied assumptions the buyer recovers HK$80,000 and retains none of these two loss amounts. That is not protection against every possible event loss.

In this scenario B costs HK$6,000 more in fee but provides HK$50,000 more compensation, leaving its fee-plus-retained-loss burden HK$44,000 lower. With no relevant loss, A costs HK$6,000 less. Without probabilities, these figures do not prove which package has the lower expected cost. The calculation supports an informed choice, not an automatic rule to choose the highest cap.

Ask for the change that actually matters

Raising A’s cap alone from HK$40,000 to HK$100,000 would still recover only HK$30,000 in this scenario: the sponsor category remains excluded. Removing that exclusion alone while leaving A’s HK$40,000 cap would permit HK$40,000 compensation and leave HK$40,000 of the HK$80,000 loss with the buyer. Coverage and amount must be read together.

A targeted request could include required sponsor refunds caused by the specified breach and agree an aggregate limit reflecting the relevant exposure. The supplier may seek a higher fee or decline. Record the revised wording, price and approval together; do not assume negotiation produces B’s protection at A’s price.

Recommend a bounded decision

B is defensible if the manager values recovery for sponsor obligations enough to pay the premium. A can also be defensible if the authorised decision-maker consciously retains the specified exposure within funded capacity and judges the premium unattractive. Merely saying “A has a HK$40,000 cap” is not such a rationale. A recommendation to negotiate must state its fallback if no acceptable revision arrives.

Before a real award, review the complete documents with appropriate advisers, the relevant losses and scenarios, claim process, payment capacity and any actual insurance terms. Contractual recovery does not itself prevent an operational failure or immediately produce cash. The case assumes collectibility and timing to make the allocation visible; reality requires checking those assumptions.

Instructor note

Use 3 minutes for a provisional choice and missing-detail question, 4 for the schedule calculation, and 5 for a revised recommendation or specific negotiation request. Ask whose liability is capped, what is excluded, and whether the cap is aggregate. Challenge “A pays HK$40,000 automatically” and “B eliminates risk.” Then ask whether raising A’s cap alone solves the excluded-category problem. The title concerns the buyer’s exposure not being bounded by the supplier cap, not an allegation of unlimited supplier liability. No court interpretation, enforceability advice or expected-loss optimisation is required. Preserve all customer and safety obligations.
