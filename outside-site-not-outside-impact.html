Cleaner here. Dirtier somewhere else?

A reporting boundary is not the edge of the atmosphere

Greenhouse-gas accounting distinguishes emissions within an organisation’s reporting boundary from emissions associated with its wider value chain. Outsourcing can change where an activity appears in an inventory without necessarily reducing its physical emissions. A decision comparison needs equivalent output and consistent coverage.

The manufacturer, quotes and inventories below are invented. This is a limited greenhouse-gas comparison, not a complete life-cycle assessment, assurance statement or legal reporting determination.

Your classroom decision

A small manufacturer must choose next year’s processing arrangement for one fixed annual batch of components. It can upgrade its local operation or buy the same processing service from a qualified outside firm. Both meet all applicable standards and the same quality, delivery and worker protections. Existing obligations and transition support are funded. No emergency, unsafe option or job-loss decision is hidden in the choice.

Both options are affordable. The quoted annual cash costs include installation or setup, processing, relevant transport and maintenance for this one-year decision. They are not full lifetime costs. The site report covers only emissions attributed to activity inside the manufacturer’s own site, including its purchased electricity. It is not labelled a complete company inventory. The outside provider is neither owned nor controlled by the manufacturer.

The two proposals hold output constant; there is no demand growth or rebound. The manager values both lower cost and lower greenhouse-gas emissions, but has not assigned a carbon price or made one objective overriding. Figures are prospective estimates, not measured savings. A comparable process-and-transport inventory is due before the contract decision.

Round 2 · More information

The review supplies a consistent annual inventory for the same batch. For the local upgrade, the 60 tonnes CO2e inside the site report plus 5 tonnes from external processing-related transport give the relevant components. For the outside route, 10 tonnes remain inside the site, 70 tonnes arise from the provider’s processing attributable to this batch, and 10 tonnes arise from the associated transport.

These components are disjoint, use the same greenhouse-gas conversion basis, and are not sums of overlapping company-wide inventories. Other upstream materials, equipment-related emissions, product use and end-of-life emissions are stipulated equal across the two options and excluded from both sides of this limited comparison. No offsets or avoided-emission credits are included. Cost quotes are unchanged. Local air quality, water, waste, biodiversity and complete lifetime effects have not been evaluated.

Your decision

After including the outside processing and transport, which option would you recommend? Calculate both comparable totals and state exactly what environmental claim the evidence supports.

Rebuild the same boundary

Local upgrade: 60 + 5 = 65 tonnes CO2e for the annual batch. Outside route: 10 + 70 + 10 = 90 tonnes CO2e. The outside route reduces the narrow site figure by 50 tonnes but has 25 tonnes more in this comparable process-and-transport inventory. An excluded supplier is not an emission-free supplier.

Keep the trade-off visible

The local option costs HK$10,000 more for 25 tonnes less in the stated annual comparison. Dividing gives HK$400 of extra cash cost per tonne of difference for these two options. This is not a market carbon price, a social damage estimate or evidence of a cost-effective global optimum. Without an agreed priority, the arithmetic does not settle the manager’s choice.

Make a defensible recommendation

Choose local if the organisation accepts the extra HK$10,000 for the stated 25-tonne difference. Choose outside if the documented cash-cost priority prevails, while acknowledging that it has higher emissions within this comparison. Either recommendation must reject the claim that the lower site number alone establishes lower overall emissions. Compliance is a prerequisite, not proof of environmental superiority.

Report a limited finding honestly

A precise statement is: “For the same annual batch, the local upgrade is forecast at 65 tonnes CO2e across the specified processing and transport components, versus 90 for outsourcing.” Name the boundary and forecast status. Do not claim the local option is greener in every respect, zero-emission or proven better over its entire lifetime. Real supplier data would require comparable allocation, boundaries and verification.

Instructor note

0–4 minutes: vote using the cost and narrow site figures. Ask what activity would move outside the site. 4–8 minutes: reveal the components, draw two boundaries and sum each inventory. 8–12 minutes: discuss the HK$10,000 versus 25-tonne trade-off. 12–15 minutes: write a recommendation and a carefully bounded public claim.

This is not GVR-04’s changing output denominator: batch size is fixed. Nor is it a subsidy, offset, rebound or lifecycle procurement-cost case. Do not sum the supplier’s whole inventory into the manufacturer’s inventory or count the same activity twice. Scope categories require a consistent consolidation policy; a narrow site report is not automatically a complete Scope 1/2/3 disclosure.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All figures and circumstances are fictional. Constant output; safe and compliant options; comparable one-year cash costs; no invented carbon price. Local disjoint components 60+5=65; outside 10+70+10=90; site difference 50; comparison difference 25; cost difference 10,000; ratio 400 HKD/t only for this pair. Common omitted components explicitly equal, no offsets, no double counting, no full LCA or quantified non-GHG benefits. Five substantive source bodies read on 16 November 2026; short GHG Protocol articles directly resolve outsourcing and purchased-services boundary questions, not independent impact studies. Source rules are conceptual, not legal advice.

  1. GHG Protocol: Accounting for outsourced operations. Control/equity approach determines classification; outsourced activity is not automatically outside all reporting.
  2. US EPA: Determine organisational boundaries. Consistent consolidation approaches and relationship to value-chain reporting. Not a Hong Kong legal requirement.
  3. US EPA: Life Cycle Assessment, Principles and Practice (2006). Goal and scope, inventory and impact interpretation; warns against transferring burdens outside a narrow comparison. Historical methods reference.
  4. US GSA: Life cycle assessment. Common performance unit, explicit boundaries, data availability and different impact categories. No building result transferred.
  5. GHG Protocol: Minimum and optional Scope 3 category boundaries. Purchased services and transport categories clarify boundary coverage. No claim that this short exercise meets the complete standard.
