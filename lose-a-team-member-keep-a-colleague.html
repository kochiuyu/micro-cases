PPL-19 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Internal mobility · Department and firm interests · Transition planning

Lose a team member. Keep a colleague?

A career can move across a business

In its 2024 sustainability report, DBS described career reviews and internal mobility as part of employee development. This is an employer’s account of its practices, not proof that a particular transfer prevents resignation. The Hong Kong firm, employee and transition choices below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Mei works in customer operations at a fictional Hong Kong business-services firm. She voluntarily applied for an openly advertised internal process-design vacancy and passed its fair, job-relevant selection. The receiving department has approved the appointment. Pay, grade and existing benefits are unchanged; she wants the new role. The unresolved issue is her release date, not whether her manager can veto the appointment.

HR, Mei and both department heads have confirmed that two dates are acceptable under the agreed internal process: next Monday, or after two working weeks of paid handover. Either date will be recorded by mutual agreement, with no further extension without renewed agreement. Employment rights, hours, leave and customer commitments remain protected. There is no unpaid work, retaliation, forfeited entitlement or assumption that Mei must remain employed forever.

Immediate release lets Mei join the next live project induction. The current team has funded, experienced temporary cover able to maintain all commitments using existing records, but loses the opportunity for a longer direct handover. The later date lets Mei document recurring exceptions with her named successor during normal paid hours. The receiving team can instead provide funded individual induction and a later live-project start without breaching commitments. Both complete transition packages fit the approved budget; no monetary value or resignation probability is supplied.

Your decision

Would you release Mei next Monday or agree the two-week handover before her internal move? Explain what the organisation gains and what each department must cover.

Round 2 · More information

In a voluntary career conversation, Mei explains that she wants to develop process-design skills rather than move up another level in customer operations. A pay rise in the old role would not provide that work. She has an external interview for related work but no offer; nobody knows whether one will follow. This is her account, not a measured probability of leaving.

Mei still accepts either of the two stated release dates, provided the agreed date is honoured and the new role is real. The receiving head confirms that the duties are process design under either route. Cover, budgets and both induction arrangements remain available. No date has yet been committed. The manager must recommend one of the two feasible transitions, not invent a retention bonus, block the transfer or promise guaranteed retention.

Your decision

Does Mei’s career explanation change your release recommendation? Choose a date, protect the agreed transition, and distinguish losing a department member from losing an employee.

Use the organisation’s boundary

Immediate release transfers capability sooner to the receiving team; it does not remove Mei from the firm. The two-week route invests in the current team’s continuity, but postpones her start on a live project. The department head should state which benefit matters more here rather than count every internal departure as organisational attrition.

Listen without manufacturing a forecast

Mei identifies work content and career direction, not pay, as the relevant attraction. Her explanation weakens a proposal to retain her indefinitely in the old role. It does not establish that she will resign during a two-week agreed handover, that the external interview will become an offer, or that immediate release guarantees she stays.

Make the transition credible

Either date remains defensible. Recommend Monday if early access to the live project outweighs the extra direct handover; recommend the later agreed date if documenting recurring exceptions is more valuable. Name the cover and induction owners, record the release date and duties with Mei, and respect that agreement. The case supplies no probabilities or priced benefits from which to calculate a uniquely optimal date.

Instructor note

PPL-01 asks an individual to evaluate external opportunities; PPL-16 allocates training for staff coverage. Here selection and cover are already feasible: an employer coordinates an internal career move across departmental boundaries. A bounded, consensual handover is not automatically talent hoarding. Research on managerial incentives and practitioner examples motivate the question but do not diagnose this fictional manager or predict this employee’s behaviour.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All employee, role and transition facts are original fiction. Selection is completed fairly; only two mutually acceptable release dates remain. Both plans are funded, protect commitments and existing rights, and contain meaningful opportunity costs without invented monetary estimates. Two working weeks is a fixed agreed handover, not an indefinite veto. External interview is not an offer or a resignation probability. Both routes preserve the substantive new role. DBS sources share one employer and are not independent evaluations. Acas is UK guidance, not a Hong Kong legal opinion.

  2. Haegele: Talent Hoarding, American Economic Review, 2026. Actual research examines manager incentives and internal applications using one large manufacturing firm, surveys and manager rotations. Context and design do not diagnose our fictional manager.
  3. CIPD podcast 226: Closing skill gaps from within, 21 January 2026. Actual transcript discusses lateral careers, managerial barriers and succession arrangements. Practitioner observations, not controlled evidence of retention probabilities.
  4. DBS: Learning and career growth. Actual page describes cross-functional exposure and internal career moves. Related employer account, not an independent outcome study.
  5. Acas: Considering changes to employees’ contracts. Actual UK guidance discusses agreement, alternatives and fair procedures. Supports a general consultation boundary, not a statement of Hong Kong law or a claim that every change has identical legal rules.
