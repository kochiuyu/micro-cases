GVR-12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Records retention · Evidence preservation · Accountability

Delete on schedule. Preserve an exception?

Industry context

In September 2025, Hong Kong’s Privacy Commissioner explained in Hong Kong Lawyer how anonymisation requires attention to both direct and indirect identifiers and re-identification risk.

That discussion illustrates why removing names is not automatically enough. This fictional case instead asks when identifiable correspondence still has a defined purpose. Its retention periods and preservation procedure are supplied classroom policy, not legal deadlines or advice about a real dispute.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong display workshop normally deletes resolved customer correspondence 90 days after closure. This approved schedule covers service emails only; invoices, contracts and other records with separate requirements are outside this exercise. The records coordinator has checked that tonight’s scheduled batch is otherwise eligible for deletion.

This afternoon a customer challenges whether one delivery included the agreed fittings. The old job is in tonight’s batch. No court case or official preservation order is stated. The workshop’s supplied internal policy nevertheless requires a temporary preservation exception for potentially relevant correspondence once a specific delivery is disputed. The manager authorises the exception; the coordinator documents and implements it. Preserve material fairly, including evidence that may favour the customer, without changing original content.

The manager wants the delivery evidence available tomorrow. You can identify the customer’s principal thread now, but have not checked attachments or other staff mailboxes. One colleague proposes letting tonight’s routine deletion run after copying that thread. Another proposes pausing the affected deletion while the coordinator verifies what is relevant. A pause protects against an incomplete search but delays disposal of other customers’ information. You must give a practical instruction before the batch runs, not settle the dispute.

Your decision

Would you let deletion proceed after saving the principal thread, or pause the affected deletion pending a scope check? Specify what must be verified before either instruction is safe.

Round 2 · More information

The coordinator confirms that the affected mailbox contains 600 messages eligible for tonight’s routine deletion. An initial search identifies 18 messages potentially relevant to the disputed delivery, including attachments and delivery-date discussions. Some have no order number in the subject. The remaining 582 are provisionally unrelated, not yet independently checked. Nothing has been deleted, whichever opening recommendation you made.

Two feasible controls are available. A pauses deletion of all 600 for 48 hours under restricted access while the coordinator and a second checker validate the search and separate the relevant material. B completes the same validation this evening, preserves the confirmed relevant originals with attachments and metadata in a restricted case archive, then releases only confirmed unrelated eligible messages for tonight’s deletion. B requires two staff-hours now and postpones a non-urgent internal stock summary until tomorrow; A uses those two staff-hours tomorrow. No delivery, pay, break, safety or legal obligation is displaced.

Either control can preserve the full relevant set; 18 is a provisional count, not a limit. Both include checking other staff mailboxes and attached files before declaring the preservation scope complete. Under the supplied policy the manager records the reason and scope, assigns the coordinator as owner and reviews continuing need in 30 days. That is a review date, not automatic permission to delete. Release requires documented manager approval after the dispute-related purpose and any other applicable requirement have been checked. If the search is uncertain, do not delete uncertain material merely to meet the schedule.

Your decision

Choose A, a restricted 48-hour pause, or B, verified separation tonight. Define the retained set, authorised access, review owner and condition for later disposal. What happens to the 582 provisionally unrelated messages?

A schedule needs a controlled exception

The dispute creates a specified preservation purpose under the supplied policy. Copying only the convenient thread is not enough until the scope is checked. The task is neither to destroy inconvenient evidence nor to turn one complaint into permanent retention of everything. Preserve relevant records even if they undermine the workshop’s account.

The count is a starting point, not the scope

600 − 18 = 582 messages are provisionally unrelated. Search beyond subject-line order numbers: check people, dates, attachments and connected discussions, and obtain the second check. The 18 may grow or shrink after validation; do not equate a search result with a complete preservation record.

Keep required originals and their context intact. A name-redacted summary may omit who agreed what and when; it is not automatically an adequate substitute for evidence. Anonymisation is a separate technique requiring its own assessment, not a label for an unchanged mailbox.

Two defensible implementations

B is attractive when tonight’s two staff-hours and second check can genuinely complete the work: it reduces unnecessary retention sooner at the cost of postponing the internal stock summary. A is defensible if a careful overnight check is less reliable: it permits a bounded 48-hour pause, restricts access and commits the same work tomorrow.

For A, log the temporary broad scope and deadline, then narrow it after validation; do not silently renew the whole-mailbox pause. For B, deletion is limited to confirmed unrelated, otherwise eligible material. Under either option, uncertain material remains protected pending clarification. The figure 582 is not an unconditional deletion instruction.

Preservation has an owner and an end process

A workable instruction names the disputed delivery, relevant threads, attachments, dates and custodians; limits access to the assigned coordinator, second checker and responsible dispute handler as needed; and records the reason, locations and actions. The manager authorises the exception and any later release. The coordinator checks implementation, including other identified mailboxes, rather than assuming a copied folder changes every deletion rule.

At the 30-day review, confirm whether the purpose still exists, whether scope needs revision and when to review again. Do not delete automatically on day 30, and do not retain indefinitely because the review was forgotten. Once release is authorised and no other applicable requirement remains, apply the approved disposal procedure to released material and relevant controlled copies, documenting completion. A real dispute may require specialist advice and different duties.

Instructor note

Allow 3 minutes to discuss the incomplete thread copy, 4 to choose the timing of validation and 4 to write a five-line preservation instruction. Both A and B can be defensible; assess completeness, restricted access, ownership and release conditions rather than reward maximum retention.

This is a supplied-policy governance exercise, not legal advice or a request to delete real records. Do not import foreign government procedures as Hong Kong law. The 90-day, 48-hour and 30-day periods are invented and serve different purposes: routine retention, temporary verification pause and review checkpoint. Distinguish this lifecycle decision from collecting fewer fields or restoring backups.
