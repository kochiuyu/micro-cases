FIN-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Finance · Payment timing · Incremental benefit

A discount for paying sooner. Sooner than what?

In the news · Financing decisions and cash timing

On 7 May 2026, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched the CargoX Pilot Programme to explore the use of cargo and trade data in SME trade finance.

This is context for examining real payment information, not evidence that a particular discount improves financing. Our supplier, invoices and costs below are fictional and unrelated to participation in the pilot.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. An exhibition-display supplier has ten existing, undisputed invoices of HK$100,000 each. All were issued on day 0 and are formally due on day 60. It is considering a one-off 2% discount for payment on day 10. If offered, all ten customers would pay HK$98,000 on day 10; this is an explicit classroom assumption.

The sales manager assumes that, without the offer, all ten would pay on day 60. Under that assumed baseline, each accelerated invoice avoids HK$3,000 of all-in bridging-finance cost between days 10 and 60. The HK$98,000 receipt is sufficient to avoid that bridge. An invoice already paid on day 10 creates no such financing cost to save.

The HK$3,000 is a supplied, fully avoidable marginal cost, not an interest rate or bank quote. No financing charge has been incurred. Committed finance and a separately funded reserve make either plan feasible. Existing sales and all other costs and outcomes are unchanged. The offer has not been made.

Separate discount takers from accelerated payments

Five customers already pay on day 10. Their discount reduces receipts by 5 × HK$2,000 = HK$10,000 without changing timing or avoiding any of the specified financing cost.

The other five really move from day 60 to day 10. Their HK$10,000 of discounts avoids 5 × HK$3,000 = HK$15,000 in financing cost, a HK$5,000 benefit for that group. Ten takers therefore do not mean ten accelerated payments.

Recalculate against the actual scenario baseline

The blanket offer avoids HK$15,000 and costs HK$20,000: a HK$5,000 net cost, rather than the opening HK$10,000 benefit. On these stipulated cost-only facts, do not make that blanket offer.

Equivalently, without it the supplier collects HK$1,000,000 and incurs HK$15,000 of relevant financing cost, leaving HK$985,000 in this comparison. With it, receipts are HK$980,000 with no relevant bridge cost. These are receipt-and-financing comparisons after cancelling loan principals, not end-of-day bank balances or whole-company profit.

A due date is not a payment forecast

The contractual day-60 deadline did not establish that every customer would wait until day 60. The relevant benefit depends on how the offer changes payment relative to what would otherwise happen.

Compare real baseline timing and actual take-up with genuinely avoidable costs. Here the offer’s terms, take-up and marginal financing cost are supplied, not estimated from a bank product or a general customer survey. Do not turn the 2% discount or HK$3,000 charge into an annual interest rate.

Redesign without assuming the remaining problems away

A smaller discount, different timing or a practically justified eligible group might be worth assessing. Retaining existing arrangements is also an option. The case rejects this blanket offer on its facts, not all early-payment incentives.

Before a narrower alternative, check customer relationships, contractual consistency, fairness, administration and future incentives. Do not casually reward deliberate lateness or assume targeting only the later group is already permissible and workable. The stated calculation supplies no solution to those implementation questions.

Instructor note

Require a provisional yes under the stated opening assumption, or a clear reason for another position, followed by specific evidence that would reverse it. After the reveal, have students draw two payment timelines rather than ten identical acceleration arrows.

Optional extension: if all ten take the same discount, at least seven invoices must genuinely accelerate for financing savings of HK$3,000 each to cover HK$20,000. That is a threshold within this toy model, not a prediction of customer behaviour.

No new sales, defaults, disputes, collection costs, tax effects or other benefits should be added. The exercise compares incremental payment timing, not bank products or investment advice.
