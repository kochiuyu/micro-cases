PPL-09 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · People · Workplace conflict · Mediation

Separate the colleagues. Or repair the handoff?

In the news · Workplace mediation

Hong Kong’s 6 May 2024 announcement of Mediation Week scheduled a workplace mediation seminar for 7 May.

This historical programme supplies context, not evidence that mediation will resolve the fictional dispute below.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Mei handles customer changes at a Hong Kong exhibition-graphics studio; Arun coordinates production handoffs. Both are experienced. Mei says requests go unanswered. Arun says interruptions leave no reliable record of the latest revision.

One informal conversation with the owner produced no agreed routine. The owner needs a workable arrangement for the next four weeks, without changing their specialist duties. This is an ordinary communication dispute, with no allegation requiring a separate safeguarding or misconduct investigation.

Under either route, urgent issues can reach the owner. Only the approved current layout proceeds through existing release checks. Pay, agreed hours, breaks and customer deadlines remain protected.

Route for this cycleResources and tradeoff
A · Owner relayRoute all change discussions between the colleagues through the owner: 30 minutes on each of 20 working days. Start immediately; defer nonurgent supplier-development work.
B · Offer voluntary mediationHK$3,000 for an impartial qualified provider’s preparation and half-day session, plus four paid hours for each colleague. Attempt a working agreement they choose themselves.

The fee is affordable. Paid time and suitable cover are available without overtime or missed deadlines; normal wages continue. B still requires owner involvement, including urgent handoffs while awaiting the session. No resolution is guaranteed within the quoted session.

A uses 10 owner hours. B uses eight paid participant-hours plus the fee; these are not directly comparable cash costs. If either colleague declines or no workable agreement emerges, the owner must maintain or choose a clear management arrangement.

Your decision

Would you use the owner relay for this cycle or offer mediation first? Make a provisional choice and name one fact about willingness, suitability or operating needs that could change it.

Round 2 · One fact changes

In separate preliminary conversations, both colleagues voluntarily say they would try a neutral session. They consent to sharing these specific concerns with the owner.

Mei explains: “When a customer is waiting, I need to know when someone will respond. I do not need every change made immediately.” Arun explains: “I can acknowledge urgent requests, but I need one agreed written version before releasing work. I am not refusing to speak to Mei.”

Both want direct collaboration to work. The provider has no personal stake or reporting relationship with either colleague and finds this ordinary dispute suitable to attempt. There is no agreement yet and no demonstrated improvement.

Your decision

Does this information change your route? If you offer mediation, propose two practical points for the colleagues to consider and explain what the owner should do if agreement fails.

Separate management authority from mediation

The owner can allocate work and maintain deadlines. A gives an immediate, named route for requests, but takes attention away from supplier development and can leave the colleagues dependent on the relay.

A mediator helps the colleagues explore and agree their own way forward; the mediator does not decide who wins or impose the routine. Willingness makes B more credible, but the owner still has operating responsibilities.

Listen for the need behind the complaint

“Requests go unanswered” and “I keep being interrupted” sound incompatible if one means instant completion and the other means no contact. The new information points to more specific needs: predictable acknowledgement and a reliable revision record.

Those needs might fit together. That is a reason to explore an agreement, not proof that every disagreement has disappeared. No one needs to become friends or admit a personality fault.

Turn the conversation into workable terms

Two proposals for discussion are: record every change in one shared revision log, with urgent requests flagged; and agree an acknowledgement routine during staffed coverage that states when a decision can be made.

Acknowledgement is not automatic approval or a promise of immediate completion. The colleagues must decide whether the terms work, and the owner must confirm feasible cover and responsibilities. Existing approval and release checks remain.

Agree with the provider what may be shared and the limits of confidentiality. Give the owner the agreed operating terms, not an assumed right to every private comment.

Review the handoff, not whether people become friends

If an agreement is reached, review it after two weeks: are requests acknowledged, are conflicting versions appearing, and how do both colleagues experience the routine? Adjust practical terms rather than declaring the relationship repaired after one session.

B is defensible when the owner values a chance to restore direct working and can support the attempt. A remains defensible if consent is withdrawn, an agreement is unworkable or operating support cannot be sustained. It is reversible, not automatically a punishment.

The fee, eight paid staff-hours and 10 owner hours do not establish a financial winner. Neither long-term benefits nor the value of displaced work is given.

Instructor note

Take an initial vote before revealing willingness. Ask students to describe the opportunity cost of A and the uncertain return on B without treating either time allowance as free or as a priced cash loss.

After the reveal, require two specific proposed terms and a fallback. A student-written routine is not a mediated agreement unless the colleagues willingly accept it. The managerial fallback is a separate use of management authority, not a mediator’s verdict.

This is conflict resolution between competent colleagues, not onboarding, job redesign or a channel for reporting misconduct. If harassment, discrimination, retaliation, violence, fraud or safety concerns arise, appropriate protection, investigation and formal routes must not be bypassed. No option waives rights. Overseas guidance here supplies general process ideas, not Hong Kong legal advice.
