PPL-40 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Recruitment sourcing · Network reach · Consistent assessment

Helpful connection. A narrow recruitment pool.

A fair assessment cannot assess someone who never hears

Recruitment has two separate gates: hearing about a role and demonstrating the skills it requires. Transparent information and consistent assessment matter at both. The retailer and all records below are invented. This exercise allocates outreach effort, not jobs, and supplies no demographic ranking or legal finding.

Your classroom decision

Fictional Harbour Retail is planning its next intake of stock coordinators in Hong Kong. The published entry requirements are accurate stock-record handling, clear handoff communication and basic spreadsheet skills. Particular schools, employee connections and prior use of its internal software are not requirements. Software training follows hiring.

A routine public vacancy page and ordinary employee referrals will remain available under either plan. The additional outreach budget is HK$6,000 for one complete package. A funds paid staff time to explain the role and share the public advert through their existing networks. B funds wider placement and accessible information sessions through an open community employment channel.

Both packages are available at the same all-in cost and duration, without charging applicants. The common advert gives the same pay, duties, hours, criteria and application deadline. Accessible application formats and appropriate assessment adjustments are separately funded under both. No referral bonus or preference affects assessment.

The objective is to reach more people who meet the job requirements but may not encounter the routine advert. Referrals can explain unfamiliar work and connect interested people; open outreach can reach beyond existing networks but may require more screening. Assessment capacity is funded for all applicants, and neither supplier guarantees hires.

Which additional outreach package would you choose? Identify what evidence would distinguish broader access to eligible applicants from simply generating more contacts, while preserving the common assessment standard.

Round 2 · More information

A completed earlier pilot used both channels for the same role, advert and period. After applying the same published minimum criteria, eighteen eligible applicants reported the referral channel and twenty reported the open channel. Ten eligible applicants encountered both. These are channel-contact counts, not mutually exclusive groups or hiring outcomes.

All eighteen referral-channel applicants came through contacts associated with three training institutions. Among the ten eligible open-channel applicants not reached through referrals, eight had attended other institutions and two the same three. The remaining ten open-channel applicants are the shared group. Institutional affiliation was examined only as an aggregate indicator of sourcing reach.

Attendance at any particular institution is neither a merit score nor a requirement. No protected characteristic, individual assessment score or subsequent job-performance result is supplied. All pilot applicants were considered under the same rules; none was discarded for using either channel. These historical people are not waiting for the new budget decision.

The current choice concerns the next campaign. No reliable forecast establishes that its response will repeat the pilot. The manager argues that referrals already find capable applicants, so wider outreach is unnecessary. The recruitment lead asks whether that conclusion confuses assessment quality with access to the pool.

Your decision

Calculate the pilot’s distinct eligible applicants and the additional reach of its open channel beyond referrals. Which package now better serves the stated objective, and what must not change in candidate assessment?

Debrief · Count people once

The pilot reached 18 + 20 − 10 = 28 distinct eligible applicants. Adding the two channel totals without removing overlap would wrongly report thirty-eight. The open channel reached ten eligible applicants not reached by referrals. Conversely, eight referral-channel applicants were not reached by the open channel.

The eight applicants from other institutions are a subset of those ten open-only applicants, not eight more people. The record shows a network-reach difference in this pilot. It does not establish that school background predicts ability, that referrals are inferior, or that every person outside the three institutions would apply.

Debrief · Broaden sourcing, not standards

B is the stronger next investment for the stated aim of reaching beyond existing networks. The pilot shows that the open channel encountered eligible people referrals missed. Routine referrals continue, so the recommendation does not reject their value. It adds deliberate reach where employee connections have been concentrated.

A remains a legitimate sourcing method, especially when employees explain unfamiliar work well. Its supporters should identify evidence that more effort within these networks will reach additional eligible people, rather than assuming existing success demonstrates complete coverage. Neither channel’s historical count guarantees the next campaign’s result.

Debrief · Do not turn access into preference

Keep the advertised job requirements, structured assessment and common scoring rubric. Assess each applicant’s demonstrated skills, with appropriate access arrangements. Do not award points for being referred, attending a less represented institution or entering through the chosen channel. Broader sourcing does not entitle anyone to a job or lower the required standard.

Explain the application route publicly and give all applicants the same substantive role information. Employee contacts should share the advert, not privately nominate a preferred shortlist or upload someone’s personal details without permission. Keep voluntary channel monitoring separate from assessment and avoid collecting unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

Debrief · Evaluate the next campaign

Track distinct completed applications, eligible applicants, channel overlap, assessment progression and actual outreach cost under consistent definitions. Ask whether the campaign reached people not otherwise reached, without assuming every extra application was caused by the outreach. Recruitment outcomes also depend on timing, labour-market conditions and the role offered.

HK$6,000 is the cost of either proposed extra package, not a cost per hire. The case supplies neither next-round hires nor reliable attribution of hiring outcomes to a channel. Review reach and process barriers before repeating the investment; do not retrospectively change selection standards to make the funded channel look successful.

Instructor note

PPL-07 designs the assessment itself; PPL-15 allocates an internal development assignment. Here assessment standards are fixed and the decision is upstream sourcing. Ask why a good test cannot reveal the ability of people who never hear about the job. Do not infer demographic discrimination, quotas or individual merit from the institutional pattern.

  1. ILO · 2016 general principles for fair recruitment · published summary February 2019. Original non-binding principles support transparent terms, worker protection, accurate information and no recruitment fees charged to jobseekers. They do not prescribe this fictional outreach budget.
  2. CIPD · Inclusive recruitment: Guide for employers. Professional guidance discusses varied outreach, referral-network limitations and access arrangements. Its UK legal examples and reported studies are not treated as Hong Kong rules or predicted effects.
  3. Acas · Advertising a job · updated June 2026. Official guidance explains why different advertising channels can reach people a single channel misses, and why clear requirements and accessible applications matter. UK legal duties are not imported.
  4. US EEOC · Best Practices of Private Sector Employers · historical task-force report. The report’s hiring appendix cautions against relying solely on employee referrals and discusses accurate job information and consistent procedures. Historical US guidance, not current Hong Kong law or a causal referral study.
  5. Hong Kong EOC · Recruitment Planning: Shortlisting and selection. Original guidance supports relevant, objective and consistent criteria and assessment standards set in advance. It does not establish a violation or permit selecting candidates by institution. The PDF may be intermittently unavailable.
