STR-26 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Reverse knowledge transfer · Subsidiary innovation · Transferability

One global product. One global learning direction?

Useful knowledge can travel towards headquarters

Research on multinational firms examines knowledge moving from foreign subsidiaries to headquarters, rather than only in the opposite direction. Receiving a design does not automatically mean the recipient can use it: interpretation, adaptation and working relationships matter.

This equipment company is fictional. It illustrates reverse knowledge transfer without claiming that any country inherently produces simpler or better innovations. All product tests, costs, requirements and budgets are invented; no real product is being certified or recommended.

Your classroom decision

You lead the product council of a multinational with headquarters in Hong Kong. Normally headquarters develops a label printer and regional units adapt language and packaging. A regional engineering unit instead developed a compact battery-powered printer for mobile stock-check teams that often lack a dependable network connection. It stores approved label templates locally and can complete its defined printing tasks offline. The normal headquarters model needs a network connection for those tasks and offers more central reporting features.

The compact design has completed the regional unit’s own acceptance programme and is in limited local use. The group owns the relevant design rights and may share them internally. Its estimated factory cost is HK$900 per unit, compared with HK$1,500 for the headquarters model. Those are factory costs, not landed costs, selling prices or lifecycle margins. Local use and a cheaper bill of materials do not yet establish suitability elsewhere.

The council has ten funded engineering days next month. Route A spends them on an already planned reporting feature for the headquarters model. Route B uses the same ten days for a joint regional–headquarters transfer assessment and small internal pilot, postponing that feature by one month. The feature has no contracted deadline; postponement still has an opportunity cost. Neither route authorises external launch, certification claims or customer commitments. The next decision is whether to investigate the regional invention as a group product candidate, not replace every global product immediately.

Your decision

Would you use the ten engineering days for the planned headquarters feature or a transfer assessment of the regional design? Explain what headquarters would need to learn, rather than assuming that low cost or local success makes the product globally ready.

Round 2 · More information

Two other regional units report the same specific constraint: mobile teams must print during four-hour periods without a reliable network connection. In twelve supervised internal sessions using the candidate design, all specified labels printed correctly under that test condition. The users value portability and offline operation, not simply fewer features. These sessions cover a narrow task and selected staff; they are not sales, a market forecast or evidence of every operating condition.

A preliminary requirements check also identifies unresolved requirements. Destination A’s proposed procurement programme requires independently documented conformity evidence for its specified product configuration; the existing local evidence does not cover that configuration. Destination B requires service instructions in its working language, trained repair staff and locally stocked replacement parts. Neither package is complete. These are stipulated programme requirements, not statements of national law. The local engineering team also uses a setup and fault-check routine that is only partly documented; sending design drawings alone will not transfer it.

No external rollout may occur until destination requirements are verified and met. A follow-on engineering programme, compliance work and support costs have not been priced. The existing twelve sessions and preliminary check were completed within prior routine work and did not consume the proposed ten-day allocation. The same ten-day allocation remains available for the internal assessment; the council must decide whether the evidence justifies using it and define what the assessment must return. The headquarters feature remains a feasible alternative.

Your decision

Would you pursue the regional design as a candidate for selected markets? Specify the knowledge-transfer team, the tests and destination-specific gates, and the evidence needed before approving a rollout rather than just another prototype.

Reverse the learning flow, not the evidence standard

The regional unit originated a solution and headquarters would learn from it. That is the reverse of the firm’s usual headquarters-to-region development pattern. Transferring it later to other units also involves lateral diffusion. The strategic opportunity is a reusable answer to an operating constraint, not a rule that all simpler products should become global products.

Identify what actually travels

The transferable core is portable offline printing of the defined labels. The receiving units need design files, template rules, setup steps, fault diagnosis and an explanation of why the original team made its trade-offs. Joint work between the original engineers, headquarters engineers and destination service teams can reveal know-how missing from drawings. Headquarters should credit the originating team and test understanding by having recipients configure and diagnose the device themselves.

Keep the pilot distinct from launch

The twelve sessions support further investigation of one task under one condition. They do not complete Destination A’s conformity evidence or Destination B’s support package. Assign an owner to identify the applicable destination requirements and independent assessment route; do not treat local acceptance as automatically portable. Build the language, repair-training and parts plan into the candidate offering rather than leaving support for after sales.

Cost is a starting point

The factory-cost difference is HK$600, or 40% of the headquarters model’s HK$1,500. The two products provide different features, and additional adaptation, conformity assessment, logistics, warranty and support costs are unpriced. The saving is not a 40% margin improvement or a business case. Confirm target customers’ need, willingness to pay and the fully supported economics before a commercial decision.

A bounded recommendation

Use B here if the council values learning about the shared constraint enough to defer the reporting feature. Allocate the ten days to a jointly agreed task test, a recipient-led setup demonstration, a destination-requirements gap list and a costed proposal for any next stage. The output is a go, revise or stop recommendation, not launch approval. Stop or redesign if recipients cannot reproduce the task, required assurance is infeasible, support is unavailable or supported economics do not meet the company’s subsequently agreed investment test. Do not invent those missing costs or demand commitments.

Instructor note

Allow 3 minutes for the first allocation, 5 for the reveal and 7 for a transfer plan. Route A remains defensible if the manager explains the opportunity cost and preserves a route for evaluating the regional idea later. A strong B answer names the original engineers as knowledge contributors, not merely a low-cost factory. Ask what would be lost if headquarters copied only the hardware.

Distinguish product origin, knowledge flow and decision authority. This case neither assumes regional autonomy guarantees innovation nor transfers all approval rights to the originating unit. Unlike ordinary export adaptation, the source of the innovation is the subsidiary. Avoid national stereotypes and the claim that twelve successful sessions or a cheaper component list establishes global demand. Certification and procurement details are fictional and require qualified verification before any real-world application.

Original fictional multinational, products, factory costs, ten-day allocation, twelve sessions and destination programmes. Group ownership permits internal design sharing. Regional acceptance covers local use only. Four-hour offline task tests are narrow and supervised, not representative demand or general reliability estimates.
