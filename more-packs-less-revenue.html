ACC-39 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Sales variance · Price and mix · Revenue reconciliation

More packs sold. Why less revenue?

One gap can conceal offsetting effects

A revenue gap can contain offsetting changes in selling prices, total units and the proportions of products sold. The bakery and all figures below are fictional. This is a reconciliation of completed sales, not a demand experiment, a profit forecast or a new pricing decision.

Your classroom decision

Fictional Harbour Bakery sells two comparable six-bun packs in Hong Kong: Plain and Filled. Each pack counts as one sales unit. Product specifications and pack sizes remain unchanged. The controller must explain this month’s revenue shortfall before the owner evaluates the sales team.

The owner proposes saying: “Revenue missed budget, so the team sold too little.” The controller proposes a bridge separating price from the quantity-and-mix effect. Decide which explanation the opening records support. All amounts are net sales revenue in HK$, with no returns, sales taxes, exchange effects or other revenue categories.

Use positive numbers for increases in revenue and negative numbers for decreases. First change budgeted product quantities to actual product quantities while holding each budget price fixed. Then change to actual prices on actual quantities. Call these the sales-volume and sales-price effects respectively; their sum must equal actual minus budgeted revenue.

Budget sales are 600 Plain packs at HK$10 and 400 Filled packs at HK$20. Actual sales total 1,200 packs and HK$13,200 revenue. The controller’s checked subtotal, pricing the actual product quantities at their respective budget prices, is HK$15,600. The product-level actual record follows in round two.

Your decision

Calculate the total revenue gap, the sales-volume effect and the sales-price effect under the stated convention. Does the shortfall establish that too few packs were sold, that unit prices alone explain everything, or that someone performed poorly?

Round 2 · More information

The completed record shows 840 Plain packs at HK$8 and 360 Filled packs at HK$18. These are the same 1,200 packs and HK$13,200 revenue, not additional sales. Actual Plain share is 70% of packs, whereas budget Plain share was 60%. No record establishes why customers bought this mix.

Now split the sales-volume effect. First scale budget quantities to the actual total while preserving the budget unit mix: 60% Plain and 40% Filled. Value that hypothetical basket at budget prices. Its difference from budget revenue is the total-quantity effect. Actual quantities at budget prices minus that hypothetical subtotal is the mix effect.

All completed sales were properly approved and fulfilled. Costs, margins, competitor moves, promotions, availability and customer switching evidence are not supplied. The owner can request a joint commercial review before judging performance. Neither the original budget nor a favourable revenue component proves that a product, employee or pricing policy was successful.

Your decision

Calculate the hypothetical basket and its revenue, then reconcile quantity, mix and price effects to the unchanged HK$800 shortfall. Explain why adding the sales-volume effect again would double count. What concise message and next evidence request should go to the owner?

Debrief · Two effects first

Budget revenue is 600 × 10 + 400 × 20 = HK$14,000. Actual minus budget is HK$13,200 − HK$14,000 = −HK$800. The opening sales-volume effect is HK$15,600 − HK$14,000 = +HK$1,600; the price effect is HK$13,200 − HK$15,600 = −HK$2,400.

The bridge is HK$14,000 + HK$1,600 − HK$2,400 = HK$13,200. Actual total packs exceed budget by 200. However, the +HK$1,600 component combines changes in total quantity and product mix; it is not yet a pure measure of selling 200 extra packs.

Debrief · Hold the mix constant

At 1,200 packs and the budget 60:40 unit mix, the hypothetical quantities are 720 Plain and 480 Filled. At budget prices their revenue would be 720 × HK$10 + 480 × HK$20 = HK$16,800. These are comparison quantities, not additional, missing or promised sales.

The total-quantity effect is HK$16,800 − HK$14,000 = +HK$2,800. Equivalently, the budget average price is 60% × HK$10 + 40% × HK$20 = HK$14, and 200 additional packs at that fixed mix give 200 × HK$14 = HK$2,800.

Debrief · Change the proportions

Actual quantities at budget prices give 840 × HK$10 + 360 × HK$20 = HK$15,600. Relative to the hypothetical basket, 120 more Plain packs add HK$1,200 and 120 fewer Filled packs subtract HK$2,400. The net mix effect is −HK$1,200.

Quantity plus mix equals the original sales-volume effect: +HK$2,800 − HK$1,200 = +HK$1,600. The mix component captures a larger share of the lower-budget-price pack, not proof that customers were forced to substitute or that Filled stock ran out.

Debrief · Price the actual packs

Plain’s price effect is 840 × (HK$8 − HK$10) = −HK$1,680. Filled’s is 360 × (HK$18 − HK$20) = −HK$720. Together they equal −HK$2,400. Use actual product quantities here; using budget quantities would miss the price effect on the quantity differences.

Final reconciliation: HK$14,000 + HK$2,800 − HK$1,200 − HK$2,400 = HK$13,200. The three effects total −HK$800. Do not also add +HK$1,600: that is the subtotal already represented by quantity plus mix.

Debrief · Averages need interpretation

The overall realised price per pack fell from the budget average HK$14 to HK$13,200/1,200 = HK$11. This HK$3 difference is not entirely a selling-price cut. At the actual mix, budget prices average HK$13; mix accounts for HK$1 and the within-product price changes for HK$2.

The decomposition order is explicit. Another consistent bridge can allocate interaction terms differently while reaching the same total. Do not mix components from different conventions. These are revenue effects valued at selling prices, not contribution or profit effects valued at margins.

Debrief · Report without assigning blame

Tell the owner: “We sold more packs, but the mix shifted toward the lower-priced product and realised product prices fell. Together these offset the positive total-quantity effect. The revenue shortfall is HK$800.” Favourable and adverse describe this revenue comparison, not an employee’s merit.

Request price approvals, promotion terms, availability, customer demand and the budget’s basis, then examine relevant product costs and margins separately. A lower selling price could support another objective; a higher-priced pack need not be more profitable. No causal or profit conclusion follows from this arithmetic alone.

Instructor note

Spend four minutes on the two-part opening bridge, five on the hypothetical budget-mix basket and five on reconciliation and managerial wording. Ask students to distinguish total-quantity effect from sales-volume subtotal. Keep all three comparison baskets visible rather than treating each favourable or adverse amount as a separate business event.
