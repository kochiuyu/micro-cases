FIN-44 · Kowloon East, Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Linked Exchange Rate System · HIBOR · Reset dates · Interest-rate exposure

The peg holds. Which rate resets?

A weak-side intervention in Hong Kong

On 26 June 2025 the HKMA reported that it bought HK$9.42 billion from banks against US dollars when the weak-side undertaking was triggered. It expected the banking system’s Aggregate Balance to fall to HK$164.1 billion on 27 June.

In the linked system the HKMA offers conversion to banks at HK$7.75 and HK$7.85 per US dollar, on the strong and weak sides respectively. A smaller Aggregate Balance can put upward pressure on local funding rates. It does not mechanically reset every loan.

Your classroom decision

You are the treasurer of fictional Kowloon East furniture maker Keyline Workshop. All revenues and payments in this exercise are in Hong Kong dollars. A manager says that staying inside the exchange-rate band means your HK$68,000 earmarked interest reserve cannot come up short.

Facility A is HK$6 million. It resets today at a 2% HIBOR fixing and again on day 30. The planning forecast for the next fixing is 4%. Its contractual annual rate at each reset equals that fixing plus 1 percentage point and remains locked for that 30-day period.

Facility B is HK$4 million. Its existing all-in rate is 3.5% and does not reset until day 60, after the modeled accrual ends. HIBOR is the Hong Kong dollar interbank benchmark. Both principals stay unchanged. Interest uses 30 days in each period and a 360-day year.

Both loans’ accrued interest is paid on day 60. There are no principal repayments, fees or other cash flows. The owners will allow a one-time top-up of up to HK$8,000 from a separate discretionary balance, but only if requested by day 40. Requests and the reserve are in whole dollars.

Your decision

Will the HK$68,000 reserve cover the forecast, and how high can the day-30 HIBOR fixing be before you need a top-up?

Round 2 · More information

On day 30, under a fictional tighter local funding scenario, the observed next fixing is 6%, not 4%. Today’s first-period accrual is already locked, and B’s contract is unchanged. The actual 26 June intervention does not determine the fictional size or timing of this move.

The top-up window remains open through day 40. Interest is still payable on day 60.

Your decision

Which part of your two-loan interest forecast changes, and what minimum whole-dollar top-up must you request by day 40?

Debrief · Apply the reset only to its period

A’s interest is HK$6m × 3% ÷ 12 + HK$6m × 5% ÷ 12 = HK$40,000. B adds HK$4m × 3.5% × 2 ÷ 12, or HK$23,333.33 to the nearest cent. Forecast total HK$63,333.33 leaves about HK$4,667 in the reserve.

Debrief · Find the reserve threshold

B and A’s first month use about HK$38,333.33. This leaves about HK$29,666.67 for the second month on A. Divide by HK$6m and annualise by multiplying by 12. Subtract the 1-point spread: the reserve tolerates day-30 HIBOR up to about 4.9333%.

Debrief · Isolate the exposed cash flow

At 6% HIBOR, only A’s second month reprices, to 7%. It costs HK$35,000 instead of HK$25,000. Add the HK$10,000 extra to the original forecast: total interest is about HK$73,333.33.

Debrief · Request the top-up in time

The reserve will fall short by about HK$5,333.33. Ask for at least HK$5,334, rounded up to cover the entire payment, by day 40. That fits under the HK$8,000 cap. Waiting until the payment date would miss the funding window.

Debrief · Explain the channel, not a forecast

At the weak side the HKMA can buy HK dollars from banks, drawing down their clearing balances. Tighter liquidity can raise local interbank funding rates. Keyline faces that channel only where and when its contract allows a new fixing.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 for the context, 4 for the reserve forecast and threshold, 4 for the revised fixing and notice, and 5 to explain the channel. Ask groups to locate the one cell whose rate re-fixes.

The convertibility undertakings apply to banks; they promise neither a retail loan rate nor a specific HIBOR reaction. No exchange conversion occurs for this borrower. The loans, fixing path, dates and reserve are fictional, not a reconstruction or forecast.
