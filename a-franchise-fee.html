ENT-43 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Franchise economics · Replication test · Royalty design · Operating contribution

A franchise fee. A replicable outlet?

A franchise show puts both sides in the room

HKTDC described its December 2019 Hong Kong International Franchising Show as an opportunity for franchisors and prospective franchisees to meet, with consulting and support services also represented. That is context for the setting, not proof that a promising format can be replicated profitably. This kiosk and its terms are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Pui operates a Hong Kong snack kiosk and has been asked to offer a first franchise for a second site. She can instead run a company-funded replication test at the second site with a hired manager, keeping the original kiosk open. The test would measure what happens without the founder managing the counter.

The table gives monthly operating inputs. Fixed costs include a full market-rate manager role in both columns. Under a franchise the outlet pays head office a royalty of 6% of all sales revenue. The head office spends HK$8,000 per month to train and support the outlet. The outlet and head office must each meet its own normal-month operating contribution floor: HK$12,000 and HK$2,000, respectively.

Compare recurring contributions only. There is no assumed upfront fee, and all setup investment, financing, taxes and runway are outside this screen. Passing both floors would justify testing actual results and startup costs, not signing a franchise immediately.

Round 2 · More information

The company-run test finds a workable smaller site. Normal monthly sales are now forecast at 5,800 items and fixed outlet costs are HK$68,000. Price and variable cost stay the same. Head-office support is re-estimated at HK$9,500 per month. The required contribution floors are unchanged.

Suppose a different royalty can be negotiated; no costs or volumes move as a direct result. The head office receives exactly what the franchisee pays. Use the post-test forecasts rather than assuming every future site is identical.

Your decision

Does the original 6% royalty meet both floors now? Find the range of royalty payments that would, and test whether a 7% royalty fits.

Debrief · Measure the original unit on its own

The existing kiosk earns 6,000 × (HK$30 − HK$13) − HK$62,000 = HK$40,000 a month. At the initial proposed site, HK$180,000 in sales produces a HK$10,800 royalty. The franchisee keeps HK$3,200 and head office keeps HK$2,800 after support. Only head office meets its floor. Run the test, not the franchise.

Debrief · One stronger outlet still needs viable support

After the test, the franchisee has 5,800 × (30 − 14) − 68,000 = HK$24,800 before royalty. Monthly sales are HK$174,000. At 6% the franchisee pays HK$10,440 and keeps HK$14,360. Head office keeps only HK$940 after HK$9,500 support. This time the head office misses its floor.

Debrief · Solve for payments both sides can live with

Head office needs at least HK$9,500 + HK$2,000 = HK$11,500 in royalties. The franchisee can pay at most HK$24,800 − HK$12,000 = HK$12,800. That range is approximately 6.61% to 7.36% of sales. At 7%, HK$12,180 transfers, leaving HK$12,620 at the outlet and HK$2,680 at head office.

Debrief · A viable range is not a promise

The range depends on normal-month estimates from one test. Before any sale, check weak months, opening investment, support to multiple sites, whether the store can run with another operator, and all applicable local obligations. A one-off fee is not recurring operating contribution.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to build two ledgers, 5 to solve the bounds, and 4 to discuss what a replication test still would not prove. Have students cross out the royalty when adding the two contributions, but keep it when testing each partner’s floor.
