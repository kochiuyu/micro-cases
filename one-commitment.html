Three small orders. One big commitment?

In the news · Supporting integrity in business

An ICAC report published on 14 September 2026 described business representatives joining the new Private Sector Integrity Centre’s launch after a meeting on 10 September.

This provides local context for practical business controls, not a finding against any company or a source of the approval limit below. The studio, policy and requests in this case are invented.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small Hong Kong design studio is refreshing its client meeting room. Its coordinator can approve a known purchase requirement up to and including HK$20,000. Above that, the owner must approve the whole requirement before commitment.

This is an invented internal policy, not a Hong Kong statutory threshold. It also prohibits dividing a known requirement solely to avoid higher approval. Normal supplier checks still apply at any amount.

The same project lead submits three separate requests: HK$18,000 for furniture, HK$18,000 for lighting and HK$18,000 for installation, each including all charges. None has been approved, accepted with the supplier, delivered or paid.

The owner is away until tomorrow. The project lead wants to book the work today, but the written offer remains open through tomorrow. There is time for review; no emergency exception is supplied.

Round 2 · One fact changes

The project lead supplies the original quotation and scope. All three items were planned together as one known meeting-room package, totalling HK$54,000, before any request was prepared. No supplier commitment has yet been made.

Separate delivery dates and invoices were requested for administrative convenience. The combined scope, package price and approval policy have not changed. None of the elements is an unforeseen later requirement. The project lead says splitting the paperwork avoids waiting for the owner’s signature.

There is no evidence of a kickback, inflated price or unfit supplier. The owner can review tomorrow while the offer is still valid.

Your decision

Can you now approve the three forms locally? What should be submitted for approval, and can separate delivery dates and invoices still be used?

Apply the limit to the known requirement

Three amounts of HK$18,000 total HK$54,000. The original scope establishes one known requirement, exceeding the coordinator’s HK$20,000 authority by HK$34,000. Three forms do not create three independent grants of authority.

The issue is not that staged paperwork is always wrong. It is that this studio’s supplied policy applies to the whole known requirement, and the facts now show what that requirement is. Local approval is insufficient under that policy.

Escalate the complete decision before commitment

Pause commitment and send the owner the HK$54,000 scope, original quotation and all related requests together. Make the total amount, inclusions and offer-validity period visible. The owner can review tomorrow, so there is no stated need to invent an emergency route.

Approval is not automatic. Normal budget, value, specification and supplier checks still apply. Do not book the work first, backdate permission or treat a later signature as the supplied pre-commitment approval.

Keep useful administration without bypassing authority

Separate deliveries, line items or invoices can still be useful after the appropriate total-scope approval. They do not have to be merged into one document merely to make the control work.

Cross-reference each order to the full requirement and approval record so reviewers can see the known exposure. Teach staff when to aggregate and escalate, rather than banning every staged delivery or instalment invoice.

A pattern is a reason to check, not proof of guilt

Multiple purchases from one supplier do not automatically establish improper splitting. A genuinely new, unforeseeable later need or independently justified scope may warrant different treatment under the organisation’s actual policy.

Examine what was known when committing, how the requests relate and the governing authority rule. Here the original quotation resolves that uncertainty. It does not establish bribery, a criminal offence or a basis for an employment sanction. The decision required is a proportionate approval correction before anything is committed.

Instructor note

Before the reveal, ask what evidence would show whether the requests are related. Do not let the number of forms substitute for the definition of a requirement. After the reveal, ask exactly what the owner must see and whether separate invoices can remain.

A strong answer pauses commitment, presents the whole known scope and preserves legitimate administrative flexibility. It should not assume the owner will approve, accuse the supplier, or import a threshold from another institution.

The HK$20,000 limit is entirely fictional. The case teaches delegated-authority scope, not a universal legal procurement rule.
