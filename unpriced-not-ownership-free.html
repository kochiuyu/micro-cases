FIN-23 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Convertible financing · Valuation cap · Contingent dilution

No price today. No dilution tomorrow?

Financing context

Startup funding can buy shares now or create a contractual right to shares later. Securities-regulator education distinguishes a future-equity agreement from current stock, while financing guidance explains conversion discounts and valuation caps. The exact contract and its definition of the share base matter.

This is a deliberately simplified fictional financing exercise, not a standard SAFE, loan document or recommendation to invest. Real instruments can have materially different conversion, repayment and shareholder rights.

Your classroom decision

Two founders of a fictional Hong Kong software business jointly own all 1,000,000 existing ordinary shares. They want HK$500,000 for an optional customer pilot. Existing operations, wages and reserves remain funded without it; declining or delaying the pilot is feasible.

P, priced equity: an investor offers HK$500,000 for new ordinary shares at HK$2.50 each. Closing takes ten working days. C, a simplified future-equity agreement: another investor offers the same HK$500,000 in two working days. No shares issue immediately; the amount converts automatically at the next qualifying priced round under the terms below. Neither offer has been signed. Costs and investor support are otherwise equal in this exercise.

The pilot booking expires in five working days. C can fund it in time; P cannot. There is no bridge finance, extension or renegotiated offer available. The pilot may be useful, but no profit forecast or value for meeting this deadline is supplied. Faster funding is a benefit to assess, not evidence that the pilot will succeed.

C converts at the lower of two prices: 80% of the next round’s new-investor price (a 20% discount), or HK$2,000,000 divided by a fixed reference base of 1,000,000 founder shares. The cap base excludes C’s conversion shares and all next-round shares. The cap and discount are alternatives, not cumulative reductions. The next-round price is not yet known.

For this limited comparison C has no interest, maturity or repayment schedule and is not treated as debt. Only the stated qualifying-round conversion path is analysed; no conversion or future funding is guaranteed. There are no options, other convertibles, fees, anti-dilution adjustments or founder subscriptions. Issued shares have identical ordinary economic and voting rights. Exit, failure, tax and actual legal drafting are outside this classroom model and require professional review, not assumptions from this exercise.

Round 2 · More information

Before choosing, the founders add a conditional next-round scenario: in either route, a later investor would put in HK$1,000,000 at HK$5 per new share. This is a scenario, not a committed investor or guaranteed valuation. The price corresponds to HK$5,000,000 when multiplied by the original 1,000,000-share reference base; it is not the same pre-money valuation of the entire company under both routes.

Under C, conversion happens immediately before that new-money share issue. Under P, the first investor’s shares have already been issued. The next investor receives the number of shares purchased at HK$5 in either route. All terms, the pilot deadline and the original HK$500,000 funding amount remain unchanged. Calculate the two routes separately; neither route receives both initial offers.

Your decision

Under the scenario, which conversion price applies to C, and what do the founders own after the new round under each route? Would the faster funding justify the difference for you, given what remains unknown?

First find the conversion price

Discount price: 0.80 × HK$5 = HK$4. Cap price: HK$2,000,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = HK$2. The lower price, HK$2, applies. C therefore receives HK$500,000 ÷ HK$2 = 250,000 shares. Do not apply the 20% discount again to the cap price.

The cap limits the price used to determine conversion shares in this contract. It is not a promise of business value, a guaranteed exit price, or a cap on the founders’ total dilution. Its explicitly defined share base is essential.

Then build each share table

P issues HK$500,000 ÷ HK$2.50 = 200,000 shares now. Before the new round, founders own 1,000,000 ÷ 1,200,000 = 83.33%. C instead issues 250,000 shares on conversion, leaving founders at 1,000,000 ÷ 1,250,000 = 80% immediately before new money.

The later investor receives HK$1,000,000 ÷ HK$5 = 200,000 shares under either route. P’s final total is 1,400,000: founders 71.43%, first investor 14.29%, later investor 14.29%. C’s total is 1,450,000: founders 68.97%, converting investor 17.24%, later investor 13.79%. Percentages are rounded; use the share counts to reconcile the exact totals.

The founder difference is about 2.46 percentage points after the new round. The new investor’s percentage differs because equal cash at an equal per-share price buys equal shares, not necessarily an equal percentage of differently sized share totals.

A real trade-off, not free financing

P fixes the initial share price and leaves founders with more ownership in this scenario, but misses the pilot deadline. C can fund the pilot on time, at the cost of more shares on the stated conversion path. Postponing avoids either commitment now but loses this booking. Existing operations remain viable in all three choices.

No value for the pilot or probability of the later round is supplied. Therefore the case cannot establish whether the extra dilution is worth faster cash. A defensible C answer names the missing pilot evidence; a P or postponement answer recognises the opportunity it forgoes. A larger percentage alone is not a forecast of greater personal wealth.

Change the future price, not the contract

The cap binds when 0.80 × the next price is above HK$2: that is, when the next price exceeds HK$2.50. At HK$2.50 the two formulas tie. Below it, the discounted price applies and C issues still more shares for the same HK$500,000.

For example, at a hypothetical next price of HK$2, the discount price is HK$1.60 and C would issue 312,500 shares before that round’s new money. This is a sensitivity check, not a second forecast. The lower future price does not preserve the earlier 250,000-share calculation.

Instructor note

Allow 3 minutes for the timing choice, 5 for conversion and share tables, and 5–7 for the commercial discussion. Require students to identify the price before dividing the investment by it. Put the conversion before the new-money issue. Do not multiply two dilution percentages before deriving the underlying share counts.

This model intentionally specifies an exclusion-based reference share denominator and ordinary shares; it is not a calculation template for current post-money SAFEs. Actual agreements can define capitalisation differently and include rights omitted here. No jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion, recommended security purchase or actual financing action is being made. Keep the scenario conditional; “no shares yet” does not mean ownership-free funding or guaranteed conversion.
