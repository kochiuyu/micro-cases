ECO-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Economics · External costs · Noise mitigation

A cheaper delivery. Cheaper for whom?

In the news · Business noise beyond the transaction

On 9 August 2023, Hong Kong’s Environmental Protection Department sought views on proposed noise-control amendments, including controls on shop and hawker loudspeakers.

This historical consultation illustrates concern about business noise affecting others. It is not current-law advice or a delivery rule. Our shop and unloading choices below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small Hong Kong neighbourhood shop receives goods through a shared access area beside homes. For its next delivery, standard handling A costs the shop HK$100; quieter handling B costs HK$180. The retailer wants to control expense without disregarding neighbours.

Both move the same goods at the same time, with the same truck trip and service quality. Safe working arrangements are unchanged. All handling costs that differ are included; common costs cancel. For the exercise, both methods are feasible subject to all applicable requirements. An economic calculation cannot override restrictions, rights or safety.

To compare disturbance in a common unit, use the fictional monetary-equivalent estimates below. They are not household bills, compensation payments, fines, measured Hong Kong damages or a conversion from decibels. No money is transferred to residents. The estimates cannot capture every person’s experience.

Per deliveryA · StandardB · Quieter service
Shop’s handling costHK$100HK$180
Estimated disturbance borne by neighboursHK$150HK$20

The neighbours’ estimate is separate from the shop’s invoice. Sales, emissions, congestion and worker safety do not change in this simplified comparison.

Your decision

Which method is cheapest for the shop alone, and which would you recommend when neighbours’ disturbance also counts? Make a provisional recommendation and name the evidence most likely to change it.

Round 2 · One fact changes

The carrier offers method C at the same delivery time: a maintained trolley with quieter wheels, protective matting at the noisy contact points and a trained handling procedure. It adds HK$40 to A’s cost, including equipment allocation, setup and staff time. The shop would therefore pay HK$140 per delivery.

In this fictional scenario, a site-specific assessment and resident feedback support an estimated disturbance cost of HK$30 for C. The assessment concerns the actual noisy handling points; it does not claim to silence the truck or eliminate all noise. Other assumptions remain unchanged.

This is useful positive evidence, not a real trial or a guarantee. Compared with B, C costs the shop less but leaves neighbours with a slightly higher disturbance estimate. No compensation arrangement has been offered.

Your decision

Would you now choose C or keep B? Compare the combined modelled cost and who bears it. Name one check or operating condition you would require before using C.

An invoice can leave costs outside the transaction

A costs the shop HK$100 but also imposes an estimated HK$150 of disturbance on neighbours: HK$250 combined. B costs the shop HK$180 and neighbours an estimated HK$20: HK$200 combined.

B asks the shop to spend HK$80 more while reducing estimated disturbance by HK$130. Its combined modelled cost is HK$50 lower. A remains cheapest on the invoice alone.

A negative externality is an adverse effect borne by someone outside the transaction that is not reflected in its price. Here the neighbours’ disturbance is the external cost. It is not another charge already included in the handling invoice.

A practical improvement changes the options

C costs HK$100 + HK$40 = HK$140 for handling, plus HK$30 estimated disturbance: HK$170 combined. That is HK$80 below A and HK$30 below B in this model.

Mitigation creates a useful new option rather than merely asking the shop to choose between the original two methods. The extra equipment and procedure are not free; their relevant costs are explicitly inside the HK$40. Do not add them twice.

C has the lowest combined modelled cost under these estimates. This is not a measured societal optimum or a claim that every impact has been priced correctly.

A lower total is not a gain for everyone

Against B, C saves the shop HK$40 while increasing neighbours’ estimated disturbance by HK$10. The combined reduction is HK$30, but neighbours do not automatically share the shop’s gain. Nobody receives compensation in the stated choices.

C can be defended for its lower combined cost, subject to credible operation and attention to affected people. B can still be defended for giving neighbours stronger protection. Explain the distribution as well as the total.

A monetary estimate is not permission to impose harm or a substitute for residents’ rights. If a method is not allowed or cannot operate safely, the economic comparison does not make it available.

Make the chosen method work in practice

For C, confirm the equipment is maintained, matting addresses the assessed contact points, staff follow the handling procedure and access remains safe. Name who receives resident feedback and who corrects recurring disturbance. A credible assessment supports a decision; it does not eliminate the need to check actual operation.

For B, confirm that the quoted quieter service really supplies the lower-disturbance method being compared. Keep quantity and timing comparable when reviewing either option. A lack of complaints alone does not establish zero disturbance.

Treat the estimates as assumptions worth revisiting. They show why costs outside an invoice matter, not that every decision must be reduced to one sum or delayed indefinitely.

Instructor note

Require an initial recommendation using both the shop’s and neighbours’ perspectives. After the reveal, ask who gains and who loses when moving from B to C. Do not reward “C is best for everyone”: the neighbours’ estimate rises from HK$20 to HK$30.

Optional sensitivity: with C’s shop cost fixed at HK$140, a disturbance estimate of HK$60 makes its combined cost tie B’s HK$200. Above HK$60, B has the lower combined figure. This is a conditional threshold, not a permitted noise limit.

All values are invented; disturbance values are monetary equivalents, not surveyed willingness to accept or measured harm. Do not infer compensation, legal clearance, emissions savings or a universal rule for deliveries. The distinct lesson is private versus external cost, with technology changing the tradeoff.
