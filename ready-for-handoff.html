PPL-04 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · People · Onboarding · Knowledge transfer

The handbook is complete. Are they ready?

In the news · Learning through work, not just information

On 5 August 2026, the Hong Kong Productivity Council announced its three-month Career Launchpad programme for graduates. Its design combined real projects, structured learning, mentoring, feedback and HR buddy support.

That announcement describes a development approach, not proven results from an evaluated programme. It prompts a practical management question: what must a newcomer be able to do, beyond completing learning material? The print shop below is fictional and is not an account of HKPC’s programme.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You own a small Hong Kong print-and-binding shop. Two new order coordinators have read a current, accurate handbook and passed a rules quiz. An experienced colleague has been named as their buddy.

The coordinators must collect specifications, obtain the customer’s approval of a sample layout and hand a complete job folder to production. They do not operate machinery. Until readiness is checked, an experienced employee approves every live handoff.

You can redirect at most six experienced-staff hours this week. They could improve reusable illustrated examples and a searchable FAQ, supervise practice with the recruits, or remain on current production work. A mixed allocation is possible. Extra hours, overtime and new hires are not available in this decision.

The handbook is sound. Better examples could help future recruits as well as the current pair. But experienced time is scarce, so additional guidance and coaching are not free.

How would you use the six experienced-staff hours: improve reusable guidance, supervise practice, or keep the time on current orders? What would count as evidence that the recruits are ready?

Round 2 · One fact changes

In a short rehearsal using a sample order, not a live customer job, both recruits correctly explain the handbook rules. But neither can assemble and hand over the complete job folder without repeated prompting. When sales and production give conflicting instructions, they do not know who can resolve the conflict.

The named buddy is already fully assigned to production this week. There is no protected coaching time. The six hours can be freed by moving six hours of that colleague’s routine production work to next week; the owner confirms this can be done without breaking a customer deadline. It will leave less spare capacity next week.

No real job has been released incorrectly. The rehearsal reveals a readiness and support gap, not proof that the recruits are incapable.

Your decision

Would you change the allocation after this rehearsal? Specify who will coach, what work makes room for it, and what the recruits must demonstrate before independent handoffs.

Find the missing learning step

The quiz and rehearsal measure different things. Knowing a rule does not establish that someone can organise an end-to-end job, notice a missing approval or resolve conflicting instructions appropriately.

Use the current handbook during practice rather than discarding it. An experienced colleague can model a sample handoff, let each recruit try another, give feedback and then observe a fresh attempt. The owner must also clarify who resolves sales–production conflicts; recruits should not have to invent authority.

Make the support real

Do not simply tell the buddy to “help when free”. Name a coach and book time. Identify the routine work being moved and check next week’s capacity. Six hours is a resource limit, not a guarantee of competence.

One defensible trial is four hours of supervised practice and two hours documenting the recurring questions it reveals. Another allocation can work if it addresses the demonstrated gap. The case supplies no measured learning rates, so it cannot prove an optimal split.

A documentation-first plan now needs to explain how it will test application and provide accessible feedback, not just add more pages. Keeping all six hours on production leaves readiness unresolved, so independent handoffs should wait.

Choose a readiness check

Observe each recruit preparing a fresh sample order: gather the specification, verify the approved layout, assemble the folder and identify the correct person to consult when instructions conflict. Check the quality of the completed handoff and the reasoning behind escalation.

Do not certify readiness from attendance, confidence or a single memorised example alone. If the recruits still need prompting, continue supervised work and revise the support plan. The case does not set a universal number of practice attempts or hours.

Key idea: an onboarding checklist records what was provided; a work sample shows what someone can apply.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the resource vote, 3 for the rehearsal reveal and 5–7 to design the coaching schedule and readiness check. Ask students to state exactly what gets delayed when coaching is protected. Keep documentation as a useful complement, not a straw alternative. Discuss the manager’s responsibility for clear escalation and workload, rather than blaming the buddy or recruits. No real customer error is assumed.
