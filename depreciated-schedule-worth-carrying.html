ACC-25 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Depreciation and impairment · Cash-generating units · Recoverability

Depreciated on schedule. Still worth carrying?

An allocation schedule is not a recoverability test

Cathay’s 2025 annual-results notes discuss recoverable amounts at cash-generating-unit level and explain the use of discounted cash-flow estimates. This is an example of a reporting assessment, not evidence that a working machine should always be written down to its resale price.

The bookbinding workshop and all figures below are fictional. The supplied simplified accounting policy is for learning, not a professional compliance opinion for a particular Hong Kong entity.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong bookbinding workshop is preparing its year-end internal report. Its enclosed cutter works safely and meets specifications. A sustained fall in demand for its printed-book service has reduced expected future work; the controller has identified an impairment indicator. Correct scheduled depreciation of HK$24,000 has already been recorded this year, leaving the cutter at HK$120,000. A reviewed current estimate puts its fair value less all disposal costs at HK$80,000. There is no decision to sell or abandon it.

The production manager proposes closing without any further adjustment because the schedule was followed. A colleague instead proposes immediately reducing the cutter to HK$80,000. You have time before finalising the report to obtain the cash-inflow map and reviewed use-value estimates; no statutory filing deadline is at stake. At present you do not know whether the cutter generates largely independent customer cash inflows.

Apply this classroom cost-model policy: impairment tests recoverability, separately from systematic depreciation. Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less disposal costs and value in use, the present value of expected net cash flows from continued use and final disposal. If individual recoverable amount cannot be determined because independent inflows are absent, use the smallest supported asset group generating largely independent inflows. Recognise any excess of the relevant carrying amount over recoverable amount as a current expense. Do not average the two valuation measures or use a desired profit target.

Round 2 · More information

The reviewed process map shows that the cutter and a binding unit jointly deliver one finished bookbinding service. Neither machine produces independently saleable intermediate output; there is no active market for that intermediate work. Customer receipts arise only from the completed service. The line is the smallest group with largely independent inflows. The cutter’s separate value in use cannot be determined and cannot be approximated by its sale estimate. Both machines will continue in use; neither is physically damaged or individually scheduled for disposal.

The cutter’s carrying amount is HK$120,000 and the binding unit’s is HK$60,000, both after all current depreciation. Reviewed line estimates on a consistent asset and cash-flow basis are HK$165,000 value in use and HK$150,000 fair value less disposal costs. The use value is already discounted and includes necessary operating costs and final disposal; do not discount it again. These are supported estimates, not guaranteed sale proceeds. An unrelated design-consulting activity has separate inflows and cannot be added to this line merely because it is profitable.

For this bounded exercise the two machines are the whole assessment unit. No goodwill, corporate assets, working-capital balance, liabilities, lease, revaluation, tax or other asset allocation is involved; the reviewed estimates and carrying amounts use that same boundary. Allocate any line impairment in proportion to their carrying amounts, but not below each asset’s applicable floor. With no separate use values determinable, the supplied floors are HK$80,000 for the cutter and HK$40,000 for the binding unit, their respective fair values less disposal costs. No floor is breached by the calculated allocation. Remaining useful lives and zero residual values have also been reviewed: both still have five years of straight-line use from next year.

Your decision

With the production-line evidence, what impairment adjustment would you recommend? Calculate the group comparison and the supplied allocation, and explain why neither the cutter’s sale estimate nor another profitable activity settles this test.

Review before recording an unsupported amount

The demand evidence triggers a recoverability review under the supplied policy. Correct depreciation does not cancel that indicator. But the HK$80,000 disposal estimate alone does not establish the cutter’s recoverable amount when continuing-use value matters. Obtain the inflow boundary and supported estimates rather than choosing between a zero loss and an unsupported HK$40,000 loss.

Compare like with like

The line’s carrying amount is HK$120,000 + HK$60,000 = HK$180,000. Its recoverable amount is the higher of HK$165,000 and HK$150,000: HK$165,000. The required additional expense is HK$180,000 − HK$165,000 = HK$15,000. The post-adjustment line balance is HK$165,000.

Do not compare the cutter alone at HK$120,000 with the entire line’s HK$165,000. Do not compare the line at HK$180,000 with the cutter’s HK$80,000 disposal estimate. The asset perimeter must match on both sides.

Apply the bounded allocation

The cutter holds two-thirds of the HK$180,000 balance and receives HK$10,000 of the HK$15,000 impairment. The binding unit receives HK$5,000. Revised balances are HK$110,000 and HK$55,000, respectively, both above their supplied floors of HK$80,000 and HK$40,000.

The floor check limits the allocation; it does not tell you to mark each asset down to its floor. The total revised balance, HK$110,000 + HK$55,000, reconciles to HK$165,000. No second HK$40,000 cutter loss is added.

Keep time and cash separate

The current-year depreciation already recorded remains in place. The HK$15,000 is an additional current impairment expense, not a replacement depreciation charge or an immediate cash payment. No whole-company profit or cash balance is supplied.

With the stated unchanged five-year remaining lives and zero residuals, next year’s depreciation is HK$22,000 for the cutter and HK$11,000 for the binding unit. This allocates their revised amounts prospectively; it does not undo this year’s charge. Actual future estimates still require review.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes to distinguish the opening numbers, 3 to identify the inflow boundary and 6 to calculate and reconcile. Ask why the profitable design activity cannot hide this line’s shortfall. Challenge four errors: treating accurate depreciation as proof of recoverability, using sale value alone, comparing different asset perimeters, and recording the impairment twice. The case supplies reviewed valuations; it does not teach students to manufacture discount rates or certify real reports.
