ECO-48 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Reserve price · Value of waiting · Expected present value · Retendering

Is a better bid worth waiting for?

When a land tender ends without a sale

In July 2024 the Hong Kong Government rejected the only bid for a Chai Wan site intended for private subsidised flats. The bid fell short of the reserve price, the minimum acceptable price. The public sale did not go ahead. That historical outcome is the backdrop only. Below, a fictional private owner can choose freely between the stated options; this is not a decision about waiving a government reserve.

Your classroom decision

Kai represents a fictional private landowner. The best current offer for its development site is HK$360 million, payable now and expiring today. The owner can accept it today or let it lapse and retender in six months. Assume the site, permitted use and sale terms are otherwise unchanged, and both options are feasible.

Retendering costs HK$8 million today for holding and preparation. Six months from now, a strong market leads to a firm HK$408 million sale; in a weak market the site stays unsold and the owner’s continuation value then is HK$306 million. That is a net estimate of what retaining the asset is worth at that date, not sale proceeds or an additional payment. Use a 2% discount rate for the six months and compare expected present values. Ignore other costs and benefits.

Market in six monthsProbabilityValue to owner at that date
Strong75%HK$408m sale
Weak25%HK$306m value of keeping the site
Your decision

Which option is worth more today? What minimum probability of a strong market would make the owner willing to wait? Explain why an unsold site contributes HK$306 million, not zero, in the weak outcome.

Round 2 · More information

A planning team still believes there is a 75% chance of a strong market. But it now estimates that a six-month delay costs HK$17 million today rather than HK$8 million. Everything else stays fixed.

Your decision

Does the new delay cost change the recommendation? What is the largest total delay cost the owner could bear and still choose to wait at a 75% chance of a strong market?

Debrief · Compare values at the same date

Discount the six-month values by dividing by 1.02. A strong market is worth HK$400 million today, and a weak market is worth HK$300 million today. In the weak outcome the site still belongs to the owner; zero cash at that date does not mean zero economic value.

Debrief · Find the probability threshold

At 75%, the present value of waiting is 0.75 × 400 + 0.25 × 300 − 8 = HK$367 million. That beats the HK$360 million bid by HK$7 million. If p is the probability of a strong market, waiting is worth (292 + 100p) million Hong Kong dollars today; it matches or beats the bid when p is at least 68%.

Debrief · Put a price on delay

At 75%, the value before delay costs is HK$375 million. At the revised cost, waiting is worth HK$358 million and accepting HK$360 million is better. HK$15 million in present-day delay costs is the break-even point, not the HK$17 million revised estimate.

Instructor note

A stronger future bid matters only alongside the chance of getting it, the value when no sale occurs and the cost of waiting. Ask which weak-market assumption students would challenge first. A government seller may also weigh housing delivery or be bound by a published mechanism. A private owner’s expected-value comparison does not choose a government reserve.
