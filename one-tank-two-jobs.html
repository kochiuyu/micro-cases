SUS-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Water reuse · Stormwater detention · Shared storage

One tank, two jobs. How much space should stay empty?

Water harvesting and stormwater storage serve different purposes

Hong Kong’s Drainage Services Department describes stormwater detention and a water harvesting system within the Happy Valley scheme. Harvested water there serves non-potable uses such as irrigation and toilet flushing. The exercise below models a different, fictional facility with one shared tank; it does not describe how Happy Valley operates.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong sports ground has 100 m³ of dual-use storage. In this exercise a qualified team can allocate it between separate compartments; treated water and new inflow never mix. Before a forecast storm, they must choose how much treated water to keep for an independently approved, routine non-potable use tomorrow. Unallocated capacity is reserved for new inflow. The question is storage allocation, not how to operate equipment.

Keep retains 60 m³; Make Room retains 30 m³. Both amounts are safely available initially. Any planned removal of water has already been approved and used elsewhere, with no value counted for this site. Compared only at this site, retaining usable water cuts next day’s purchased water one-for-one, up to demand. Do not rely on new storm inflow for tomorrow’s use.

The site requires enough empty space to capture the full stipulated inflow before any later release. Among policies satisfying that requirement, minimise next-day purchased water. There are no other flows before the storm ends; any water not retained in the pre-storm allocation is irrelevant to tomorrow’s water bill.

Debrief · Make room before counting reuse

Keep leaves 100 − 60 = 40 m³ empty, and Make Room leaves 100 − 30 = 70 m³. Both capture 35 m³. Keep covers all 50 m³ of next-day demand, while Make Room needs 20 m³ purchased, so choose Keep initially.

Debrief · The heavier forecast binds

At 55 m³, Keep lacks 15 m³ of empty space. Make Room still captures all inflow and requires 20 m³ purchased, so it is the only qualifying written policy. No claim that the site can safely take excess water follows from this calculation.

Debrief · Work backwards from the space requirement

At most 100 − 55 = 45 m³ can be retained before the storm. An approved 45 m³ retention would reduce purchased water to 50 − 45 = 5 m³ and still reserve 55 m³. The arithmetic is an upper bound under fixed estimates, not a usable safety buffer for real rain.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 to build the volume balances, 4 to rethink the forecast and 5 to discuss margins and water quality. What does the model purposely exclude when it ignores reuse of new storm inflow?
