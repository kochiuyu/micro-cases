ECO-46 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Price regulation · Indexation · Affordability · Fare credits

The fare cap bites. Where does the increase go?

A Hong Kong rail fare freeze did not erase the increase

A May 2025 paper from MTR Corporation to Hong Kong’s Legislative Council reported that controlled fares would stay unchanged for 2025/26. The formula gave +1.45%, inside a ±1.5% threshold, so that amount rolled forward. Earlier deferred adjustments were tracked separately under the affordability arrangements. The teaching case uses a fictional railway with different rules and does not predict MTR fares.

Your classroom decision

Kai advises the regulator of a fictional Hong Kong commuter railway. The weekly pass costs HK$100. For two years, the new formula rate is half inflation plus half transport wage growth, minus a 0.5 percentage-point productivity deduction. The table gives the forecast inputs and the regulator’s caps.

The regulator must choose how to treat any excess over the cap in Year 1. Under Carry, add that excess in percentage points to the new Year 2 formula rate before applying the Year 2 cap. Under Reset, cancel the excess. Under Carry, track any remaining excess after Year 2 but do not charge it in this exercise.

Each year’s increase applies to the previous year’s fare. There is no starting balance to carry. Each of 20,000 households buys one pass for 50 weeks each year regardless of fare. The regulator wants to keep yearly net spending at or below HK$5,200 for households with tight budgets, and needs HK$1.50m more in Year 2 net fare receipts than Reset would raise to fund scheduled renewal work. Assume no other new funding.

Round 2 · More information

The regulator can offer an annual fare credit only to households with tight budgets in Year 2. The credit reduces net fare receipts dollar for dollar. Ignore administrative costs; assume all eligible households receive the credit and that it changes neither pass sales nor the posted price.

The first estimate was that 10,000 tight-budget households would be eligible. An updated count puts the number at 15,000. Assume all other facts remain unchanged.

Your decision

What minimum credit would make Carry affordable? Can Carry meet both goals at 10,000 eligible households? What about 15,000?

Debrief · Apply the formula, then the cap

Year 1’s formula gives half of 3% plus half of 6%, minus 0.5 percentage points: 4%. The 1% cap holds the pass to HK$101 and annual spending to HK$5,050 under both policies. Carry stores the unused 3 percentage points; Reset cancels them. Neither policy collects the excess from riders in Year 1.

Debrief · Compare the two fare paths

Year 2’s new formula gives 2%. With Carry, the proposed increase is 5% and the 4% cap binds. The weekly fare is HK$105.04 and annual spending is HK$5,252; HK$10,302 over both years. One percentage point is still deferred. With Reset, the fare is HK$103.02, or HK$5,151 for Year 2 and HK$10,201 over both years. Nothing is deferred.

Debrief · An immediate cap can move the pressure

Reset keeps annual spending below HK$5,200 but provides none of the HK$1.50m extra renewal funding. Carry raises HK$101 more per household in Year 2, or HK$2.02m gross on 20,000 households. That covers the renewal goal before credits, but unassisted households with tight budgets are HK$52 over their yearly ceiling.

Debrief · Price the credit before adopting it

A HK$52 credit brings an eligible household’s Year 2 net spending to HK$5,200. For 10,000 households, the credit costs HK$520,000, leaving exactly HK$1.50m of extra renewal receipts. At 15,000, it costs HK$780,000, leaving HK$1.24m. Carry with the full credit then falls HK$260,000 short. Neither unassisted policy meets both goals.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to trace the fares, 4 to test the credit, and 5 to recommend a plan. Ask students to state whether they would seek new funding, revise the renewal schedule if feasible, or accept that the two stated goals cannot both be met with these options.

Distinguish a percentage-point balance from dollars and a posted fare from net spending. This is a nominal budget test for a fixed number of journeys, not a full welfare or demand forecast. The Year 2 rollover affects later decisions but is not money collected or a receivable today.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The opening describes a dated decision of the real MTR Corporation. All scenario figures, rules, people and funding decisions are fictional; do not use them to infer real MTR policy or future fares.

  1. HKSAR Government, Outcome of 2023 review of MTR Fare Adjustment Mechanism. The review describes the formula, the revised productivity term, earlier amounts blocked by the affordability cap, and maintenance and renewal objectives.
  2. MTR Corporation, MTR Fares for 2025 (May 2025 LegCo paper). The paper distinguishes the ±1.5% threshold that froze controlled fares from previously deferred increases under the separate affordability arrangements.
  3. MTR Corporation, MTR Fare Adjustment for 2025 (April 2025 LegCo briefing). The briefing shows the formula inputs, fare adjustment history and separate fare promotions. It does not report the fictional credit in this exercise.
  4. MTR Corporation, 2025 annual results. The results connect recurring railway fare revenue to maintenance and upgrades and confirm the 2025/26 freeze and carry-forward.
  5. MTR Corporation, Financial Sustainability. The company describes its fare-setting framework alongside other revenue and financing sources. The exercise deliberately omits those sources.
