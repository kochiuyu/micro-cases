GVR-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Governance · Supplier improvement · Responsible purchasing

A supplier failed. Must the relationship end?

In the news · Supporting responsible supply chains

On 3 February 2026, HKPC announced renewed cooperation with amfori, including practical training and support for responsible supply chains.

This was an announcement of intended collaboration, not evidence of improvement at a particular factory. Neither organisation is associated with the fictional supplier below.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small Hong Kong fabric-accessory brand is choosing a supplier for its next order. Its agreed supplier code requires a week’s notice of ordinary roster changes and consultation with affected workers. This is an invented contractual standard, not a statutory notice rule. No emergency exception applies.

A review found repeated ordinary weekend-roster changes with only one day’s notice at its established supplier last quarter. Workers described difficulty arranging family commitments. Management accepts the finding and has stopped that practice, named an accountable manager and proposed a six-week improvement review.

No new order begins on a promise alone: appropriate verification and worker protection are required. Completed work will be paid on agreed terms regardless of the sourcing choice. No automatic-termination policy is assumed. The review does not establish all legal compliance; any legal duties need separate competent review and cannot be waived.

A · Conditional renewalB · Planned supplier change
Established supplier: HK$40,000Alternative supplier: HK$43,200
Only after verified improvement, with monitoring and consequences for recurrenceAlready reviewed against the same code, with no adverse finding; close existing obligations responsibly

Both total quotes meet the specification and required delivery date. The additional HK$3,200 is affordable. No review guarantees future conduct; the difference is not a price placed on worker harm.

Your decision

Which route would you provisionally choose for the next order: conditional renewal or a planned supplier change? Name the evidence or behaviour that would reverse your choice.

Round 2 · One fact changes

Six weeks later, an independent reviewer checks roster-change records covering the whole period and privately interviews a sample of affected workers. The records and interviews corroborate that the agreed notice and consultation operated during that period. No recurrence is found within the reviewed evidence.

This is not a guarantee, an interview with every worker or legal certification. The buyer’s own team has also agreed to freeze specifications before production and jointly assess later requests rather than simply insist on the old deadline.

The buyer’s change gives the improvement plan practical support. It does not prove the buyer caused the earlier failures or remove supplier management’s responsibility. The decision remains whether the evidence and safeguards now support renewal or a planned change.

Your decision

Does this evidence justify renewal now, or would you still change supplier? Set one continuing check, one escalation trigger and who owns the decision.

Let verified improvement count without calling it a guarantee

The six-week evidence is stronger than a management promise alone: it covers records across the period and includes private accounts from affected workers. It can reasonably change a provisional decision about continued engagement.

Its limits still matter. A sample is not every worker, absence of a finding is not certainty and a reviewed period is not a prediction of all future conduct. Renewal, if chosen, should rest on this bounded evidence and an accountable continuing plan.

Define ownership and consequences

Name buyer and supplier managers responsible for follow-up. Set a review date, examine actual roster-change exceptions and maintain protected worker feedback without retaliation. Make the conditions for continuing the relationship clear.

Repeated failures or refusal of appropriate access should trigger suspension and reassessment, with responsible exit if credible mitigation is unavailable. Serious harm requires an immediate appropriate response, not waiting for a routine review. These examples do not replace law, contracts or competent assessment.

Check the buyer’s own operating practices

Freezing specifications and jointly assessing later requests can make the agreed standard more practicable. A buyer should not demand compliance while ignoring how its own changes affect the work.

That does not excuse the supplier or establish causation for the past failure. Monitor whether the new planning arrangement operates in practice. Stable specifications are not a guarantee against every scheduling problem.

A planned exit can still be responsible

Changing supplier remains defensible if confidence is too low, cooperation fails or the severity of a situation requires it. The alternative has been reviewed, but future monitoring remains necessary there too.

Pay completed work on the agreed terms, close obligations properly and consider effects on affected people. Do not merely transfer the same purchasing pressures to a new supplier. The HK$3,200 difference is a comparable quote difference, not a calculated value of harm or proof of which route is ethically correct.

Instructor note

Require a provisional route before showing the positive evidence. Conditional renewal is a direction for managing the relationship, not permission to restart before verification. After the reveal, ask for a choice, an owner, a check and an escalation trigger.

Do not make the lesson either automatic abandonment or endless chances. A supplier that exposes and corrects a problem should be assessed on that evidence, not automatically judged worse than an unexamined supplier. Here the alternative is also reviewed.

The case alleges no physical danger, forced labour or unpaid wages. It makes no criminal finding or legal-compliance certification. All company details and quotations are fictional.
