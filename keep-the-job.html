ENT-10 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Entrepreneurship · Hybrid entry · Founder time

Keep the job. Start the business?

In the news

A Hong Kong government feature published on 11 October 2026 discussed one-person businesses and both full-time and part-time entrepreneurship.

It does not establish which route would work for Mei, the fictional plant-care founder below.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Mei is an experienced indoor-plant carer preparing a small workplace plant-care service. She currently has an unrelated administrative job. Her venture materials and skills are her own; appropriate outside-work permission, registration, insurance, safe methods and site-access permissions have been professionally checked for this exercise.

For the next three months, she can take an employer-approved three-day job on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, earning HK$18,000 take-home pay per month. Tuesday and Thursday provide 16 planned venture hours a week. Alternatively, she can leave after proper notice and devote 40 planned weekday hours to the venture. Returning to this employer is not guaranteed.

Mei has allocated HK$90,000 to this three-month decision. Emergency funds and other protected obligations are separate and unavailable. Personal living costs are HK$20,000 a month. Either route has the same fully costed HK$18,000 venture and close-down envelope, covering the agreed operating scope and reserves. The downside plan assumes no sales receipts.

Five independent workplaces have expressed interest in quotations, not placed orders. Their routine plant-care visits could fit Tuesday or Thursday, but initial assessment and onboarding access has not yet been arranged. Mei has promised no work she cannot fulfil.

Both routes are affordable within the allocated HK$90,000. The additional HK$54,000 draw under B equals three months of forgone take-home pay. These are planning cash exposures, not business losses or profit forecasts, and not necessarily amounts paid on day one.

A preserves the employment relationship and more funds. B provides broader availability and more founder time. Neither schedule guarantees sales. No overtime, unpaid helper or unlimited evening work is assumed.

Your decision

Would you keep the reduced-hours job or start full-time for these three months? State the benefit worth paying for, and one fact that would change your choice.

Round 2 · More information

The five prospective clients confirm their initial on-site assessment and onboarding windows. Two can meet on Tuesday or Thursday. Three can meet only on Monday, Wednesday or Friday during the launch period, when their authorised facilities contacts are present.

They have considered alternatives: evening, weekend and remote contact cannot replace these visits. After onboarding, routine care could still fit Tuesday or Thursday. This is a launch-access constraint, not evidence that ongoing service is impossible under A.

A lets Mei pursue the two accessible prospects now and keep contact with the others for later, without assurance they will wait. B lets her pursue all five now. None has ordered: three extra opportunities are not three extra sales.

No newly available leave, helper or subcontractor fits either costed plan. Job duties, rest and personal commitments remain protected; venture work cannot take A’s paid employment hours.

Your decision

Does broader customer access justify the additional HK$54,000 draw and leaving the job? Choose a route and specify what evidence would justify continuing or changing it at the three-month review.

A salary changes the downside, not the whole answer

Under A, the monthly personal shortfall is HK$20,000 − HK$18,000 = HK$2,000. Three months cost HK$6,000; adding the HK$18,000 venture envelope gives HK$24,000.

Under B, living costs are HK$60,000 over three months. Adding the same venture envelope gives HK$78,000. The HK$54,000 difference is forgone take-home pay, not a prediction of lost profit.

A calendar can change the value of available time

Routine visits fitting two weekdays did not establish that every stage of customer acquisition would fit. The reveal makes B’s broader availability useful for three additional initial assessments.

It still supplies no conversion probabilities, customer revenue or profit. Forty hours do not imply two and a half times the output of 16 hours. The value of the extra access remains a judgement, not a computed return.

Both routes can be deliberate entrepreneurship

A is a genuine launch with two accessible prospects, not “doing nothing”. It suits a founder who values preserving funds and employment while learning within that scope. Later access to the other prospects is uncertain.

B is defensible if timely access and focused effort are worth its funded exposure and loss of the continuing job. Being able to afford it does not establish that it is worthwhile. Neither choice proves greater commitment or guarantees a sustainable business.

Set a review that can change the commitment

At three months, review signed work, receipts against all costs, delivery workload and whether the venture fits Mei’s goals and available time. Compare evidence with the chosen route’s purpose, rather than counting quotations as customers.

Any proposed work beyond the costed scope requires a fresh budget before a promise. Future employment income is not assured, and protected funds do not become venture funds by default. Mei may decline either route if neither fits her goals.

Instructor note

Require an A/B priority and a reason tied to customer access, founder time and the downside Mei is willing to carry. Do not manufacture a break-even sales target or expected return from missing margins and probabilities.

The lesson is employment alongside entrepreneurial entry, not an employer’s hybrid-attendance policy or a paid production pilot. Both routes have lawful, safe arrangements stipulated; this is not guidance for a real person to resign, use savings or ignore an employment restriction.

The research includes observational and conceptual work, not a causal demonstration that either route succeeds for this founder.
