GVR-42 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Internal audit follow-up · Operating effectiveness · Management reporting · Independent verification

The fix is installed. Is the finding closed?

Audit follow-up tests whether reported action was implemented

Hong Kong’s Audit Commission describes following up on matters raised in its reports. The Institute of Internal Auditors likewise distinguishes management’s progress report from the follow-up assessment that confirms implementation. The organisation and closure policy in this case are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Jo chairs the audit committee of a fictional Hong Kong cultural venue. An internal review found supplier insurance renewals had been missed when notices sat in an unattended mailbox. Management has installed a shared queue and a reminder rule so renewal notices should be assigned for review within two working days. Staff continue to check insurance separately before permitting any supplier onsite.

Management reports that it replayed 20 archived notices using the new set-up before launch, and all 20 were assigned within two days. It asks the committee to close the finding now. Alternatively, the committee can record that the change was installed but still needs independent verification.

For this finding, the committee’s fictional closure rule requires independent testing of 20 actual notices per month over two consecutive months after the latest correction, with no late assignment in either sample. The reviewer selects from the incoming-notice log and checks assignment timestamps. Tracking remains open until those conditions are met. The validation budget is HK$20,000; the separate onsite checks remain funded.

Your decision

Would you close the finding now or record it as installed but awaiting verification? What does the replay establish, what is still missing, and can the initial independent test fit the budget?

Round 2 · More information

After two months the reviewer finds 20 of 20 timely in Month 1 and 19 of 20 in Month 2. The one late notice was assigned on the fourth working day while the primary reviewer was on leave. The separate checks prevented uninsured onsite work; no service or insurance loss is established.

Management adds a backup assignee before Month 3. An initial replay shows that the backup can receive and assign a notice. Management points to the combined 39 of 40 live results and asks for closure. The original HK$20,000 was a total independent-testing budget; the first two months have already used HK$12,000.

Your decision

What is the combined live pass rate, and why does it not decide closure? What fresh evidence is required after the backup change, and how much more testing money must the committee obtain?

Debrief · Record implementation without claiming closure

A perfect replay supports what the new set-up did with selected archived notices. It does not test actual workloads, handoffs or staffing gaps. Record that management installed the change and keep the finding open. The first two months of independent review cost 2 × HK$6,000 = HK$12,000, within the budget.

Debrief · The average is not the closure rule

The live sample passed 39 of 40 checks, or 97.5%. Month 1 passed all checks; Month 2 failed one. The two clean consecutive months are absent before the backup change. Finding one assignment late documents a failed control instance; it does not establish a venue-wide failure rate or a lapse in insurance.

Debrief · Restart the evidence window

The backup is a new correction. Its successful replay is evidence that the route can work in a test, not that it operates reliably. The rule calls for two consecutive clean live months after the change, so the earliest qualifying evidence is Months 3 and 4, assuming both pass. The earlier clean month cannot be carried forward.

Debrief · Make the funding gap visible

Two further months cost HK$12,000, but HK$20,000 minus HK$12,000 leaves HK$8,000. The committee needs HK$4,000 more. Keep the finding open, request funding, name the management owner and independent reviewer, and continue separate onsite checks. Ask the reviewer to include backup coverage where it occurs and distinguish a staged backup test from live evidence.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes to read, 4 to discuss design and live evidence, 4 for the update, and 5 to defend the status and funding request. The two-month, zero-exception rule is an invented condition, not a professional standard or a statistical proof of perfect future operation.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The venue, finding, software set-up, sample plan, timing, budget and results are invented. The reported test is an internal management decision, not external assurance, an opinion on insurance cover, or a conclusion about any actual organisation.

  1. Hong Kong Audit Commission, Value for money audit. Describes continuing follow-up and an annual clearance exercise for matters selected by the Public Accounts Committee. Does not govern the fictional venue.
  2. The Institute of Internal Auditors, Global Internal Audit Standards two-way mapping. Reproduces Standard 15.2 on confirmation of implementation by risk-based follow-up and status tracking, not the fictional sample rule.
  3. The Institute of Internal Auditors, Communicating Final Engagement Results. The practice guide describes confirming corrective actions, named ownership, tracking and statuses.
  4. PCAOB, Auditing Standards, AS 2201. Distinguishes design from operating effectiveness and discusses the evidence provided by test timing and duration in financial-reporting audits. This case is not one.
  5. U.S. Government Accountability Office, Standards for Internal Control in the Federal Government. Frames design, implementation and operation as distinct aspects of internal control in its federal-government context. Not directly applicable to this venue.
