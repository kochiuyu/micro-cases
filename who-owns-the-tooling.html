ENT-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Supplier switching · Tooling ownership · Compatibility · Expected-cost thresholds

Who owns the tooling? Can it move?

Tooling ownership and location can differ

A 2023 manufacturing accommodation agreement filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission distinguishes customer-owned tooling held by a supplier from other categories of equipment. The fictional Hong Kong venture below also negotiates where a tool is held and who can take it away. The cited contracts do not govern this case.

Your classroom decision

Kit is buying a dedicated mould for a reusable household accessory from a first manufacturer in the Greater Bay Area. Option Vendor pays to use a tool that the supplier owns and will not release. Option Own pays more for a tool that the venture owns and has a documented right to remove if it switches supplier.

Kit estimates a 70% chance of moving production to a second qualified supplier within the horizon. Initial production costs, quality and revenues are identical. The table lists all differing costs, in cash and without discounting. There is no resale value. If there is no switch, no further tooling cost is incurred.

Initial outlayHK$18,000HK$54,000

Your decision

Which contract should Kit choose at the stated switching probability? Calculate the expected costs and the probability at which the two contracts tie.

Round 2 · More information

Before committing, the potential second supplier confirms that the owned tool is not compatible on its own. It can use the tool if Kit pays a further HK$22,000 for a verified adapter, in addition to the original move-and-validation cost, only if it switches. The Vendor replacement remains a complete quote. The 70% estimate is unchanged.

Your decision

Does the new evidence change the choice? Calculate the revised costs and the largest extra adapter charge that would make Own no more expensive than Vendor at 70%.

Debrief · Price what happens if production moves

At 70%, Vendor costs HK$18,000 + 0.70 × HK$72,000 = HK$68,400 on average. Own costs HK$54,000 + 0.70 × HK$12,000 = HK$62,400. Choose Own in the initial model.

Debrief · Find the threshold

Own pays HK$36,000 more now to save HK$60,000 if Kit switches. It ties Vendor at 36,000 ÷ 60,000 = 60%. It costs less at higher switching probabilities if the move-and-validation terms remain as first quoted.

Debrief · Confirm compatibility before valuing ownership

Own’s switch cost becomes HK$34,000 and its expected cost becomes HK$77,800, versus HK$68,400 for Vendor. Choose Vendor on these costs. Own had a HK$6,000 advantage before the adapter. A switch-only adapter can cost up to HK$6,000 ÷ 0.70, approximately HK$8,571, to preserve that advantage.

Debrief · Negotiate an operational handover

Ask for a dated inventory, right to collect, and responsibility for condition, transfer and validation costs. An ownership claim alone does not make a tool available or compatible. The numerical comparison assumes the quoted plans both meet production needs.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 for expected costs, 4 for the update, and 5 to discuss evidence. Ask which assumptions could be checked with the second supplier before signing rather than treated as probabilities.
