ENT-33 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · University licensing · Freedom to operate · Overlapping patent rights

A patent to use. Freedom to sell?

Permission under one patent

A patent can let its owner exclude others within its claims, territory and term. It does not automatically permit every component of a complete product to be made or sold. Licensing the university’s rights and checking third-party rights therefore answer different questions. The university, patents, offers and product below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

You co-found a proposed Hong Kong university spinout developing a desk-mounted cable organiser. Its university owns patent U covering a particular adjustable guide mechanism. Ownership is settled for this exercise. The intended product also uses a separate fastening component; the whole-product rights assessment has not yet been completed.

The university offers commercial permission under U to make and sell the guide mechanism in Hong Kong for desktop cable organisers. It retains ownership and research use, and promises no other commercial licensee in that defined field during the three-year term. Other fields and territories are excluded; there are no sublicensing rights or automatic rights to future inventions.

That exclusivity concerns only U. The university gives no assurance about third-party patents or permission to use their inventions. No customer data, university name endorsement or unrelated laboratory assets are included. The venture must complete qualified whole-product review before any manufacture or sale; students are not interpreting real patent claims.

Route A pays HK$100,000 now for the full university licence. Route B pays HK$20,000 now for a four-week reservation and document-access stage, then optionally HK$80,000 for the identical commercial licence. The initial payment is credited toward HK$100,000, not added to it. Both first-stage payments are non-refundable.

B’s reservation prevents the university offering this field to another company for four weeks, but grants no manufacturing or sales permission. Under either route, a separate HK$30,000 qualified freedom-to-operate review finishes at the end of week four. Review uses documents only; no third-party technology is made or used during it.

The available budget is HK$240,000. The original design’s subsequent development and required testing cost HK$50,000 and take four more weeks after clearance. These are complete fixed quotes for this exercise. Both routes receive identical technical support. No sales royalties, maintenance payments, taxes or other charges fall within this bounded development programme.

The objective is a cleared, tested product ready for a possible Hong Kong launch by week twelve, not guaranteed sales. The reservation can be exercised after the review on its final day. No customer promise has been made; pausing is possible. Ongoing trading costs and demand must be assessed separately before any actual launch.

Round 2 · More information

Consider this hypothetical week-four outcome under either route: the review identifies patent T covering the fastening component required by the original design. For this fictional scenario, take as supplied that T is in force in Hong Kong and that commercial use of that original component requires its owner’s permission. This is not a finding about any real patent or product.

The university does not own T and cannot license it. No T licence has been offered, priced or promised, and no exception or expiry may be assumed. U remains necessary for the guide mechanism. Paying more for exclusivity under U does not remove the separate T issue.

A documented alternative fastening design avoids T while retaining the U mechanism. For this exercise only, qualified review has cleared that complete alternative for the stated Hong Kong activity and period. Its development and required testing have a complete fixed HK$110,000 quote and take eight weeks after the week-four review, replacing the original HK$50,000 programme.

The alternative meets the same stipulated product requirements if its tests pass; passing is not assumed in advance. The revised quote includes all extra design work, testing and final clearance checks, with no separate T payment. Compare both routes as if each had been chosen at week zero; this classroom simulation makes no actual payments or commitments.

Your decision

How should the venture proceed now? Recalculate the alternative’s full budget and timeline under both routes, distinguish a reservation from permission to trade, and state the conditions that must hold before a launch commitment.

Debrief · Name the permission, not a blanket right

The university can permit acts covered by U and promise exclusivity within its stated field, territory and term. It has not transferred ownership or sold worldwide control of every cable organiser. Its permission does not waive T’s owner’s rights. A licence under one patent is not whole-product freedom to operate.

Debrief · What staging changes

With the original development estimate, either completed route totals HK$100,000 + HK$30,000 + HK$50,000 = HK$180,000. Route B is not a cheaper completed licence. Before review, A commits HK$130,000 including review; B commits HK$50,000. B leaves HK$80,000 uncommitted while the rights question is investigated.

I favour B before review because identical eventual rights and timing are available, while less money is irreversibly committed. If the project pauses after review, B’s HK$20,000 reservation and HK$30,000 review costs are still spent. Staging buys a decision point, not a refund, guaranteed clearance or free product development.

Debrief · The reveal does not invalidate U

The separate fastening right blocks the assumption that the original design can proceed solely under U. It does not show U is worthless, invalid or unnecessary. The alternative still uses U. Nor does the review mean the university owns or can grant a licence under T.

A possible negotiation with T’s owner is not an available priced route. Do not invent its consent or assume a licence will cost less than redesign. The supplied alternative is a specifically cleared scenario, not an instruction to make superficial changes to a real product and declare infringement impossible.

Debrief · Reconcile the alternative once

Under B, HK$20,000 reservation + HK$30,000 review + HK$80,000 exercise + HK$110,000 development = HK$240,000. Under A, HK$100,000 licence + HK$30,000 review + HK$110,000 development also equals HK$240,000. The alternative replaces the original HK$50,000 estimate; do not add both programmes.

The original route would require four review weeks plus four development weeks: week eight. The alternative requires four plus eight: week twelve, meeting the stated deadline exactly. It uses the entire budget and leaves no spare time. Neither route becomes uniquely affordable just because the review exposes a separate patent.

Debrief · Proceed conditionally, not automatically

Prefer the staged route and investigate the cleared alternative within its quoted scope. After review, proceeding requires exercising U’s option within its deadline and confirming the complete HK$110,000 programme. Release for sale still depends on tests passing, clearance remaining applicable and a separately viable trading plan. No transaction is being approved here.

Pausing remains defensible if the exact budget or deadline leaves unacceptable execution risk. No customer must be disappointed by delay because none has a commitment. A real rights review depends on the design, activity, territory and date; changed components, manufacturing locations or markets require renewed assessment, not reliance on the university’s reputation.

Minutes 14–18: distinguish conditional development readiness from a permitted, safe and commercially viable launch.
