HSP-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Guided tours · Language access · Service design · Live interaction

Two languages. One guide?

Museums can offer two ways to hear a story

Hong Kong Palace Museum publishes both audio-guide and guided-tour information, with Cantonese, English and Putonghua available for specified offerings. Those formats are not automatically interchangeable. This fictional museum sets its own promises, choice of languages and staffing assumptions.

Your classroom decision

A Hong Kong museum has 30 Cantonese-speaking and 15 English-speaking visitors interested in a demonstration tour. For this exercise each person wants just one of these languages. A bilingual guide is available for a 70-minute block. No booking is confirmed. Choose a format that serves as many people as possible while allowing the promised chance to ask questions in their chosen language.

For each language, the live story takes 22 minutes and discussion needs 8 minutes. A live bilingual session costs only the sum of those blocks and can host all 45 people. The gallery room can hold all visitors. The guide can run two live single-language sessions too, but must take a 10-minute reset between them.

Round 2 · More information

Before reservations open, the allocated guide block falls to 58 minutes. There is a tested English recording that visitors can hear beforehand at no extra cost or capacity limit. Eleven of the 15 English-language visitors say they will accept that format if it still includes live English questions. The other four want a fully live tour and can choose a later fully live booking. All 30 Cantonese-language visitors still want the fully live visit.

A hybrid puts those 11 English visitors and the 30 Cantonese visitors in one visit: use the English recording first outside the guide block, then present the 22-minute Cantonese story and eight minutes of questions in each language. The test confirms a complete equivalent story, suitable devices and no additional guide time for setup. The other available choice is one single-language live tour in the 58-minute block.

Your decision

What would you offer now, and to whom? Compute the time and participation for the hybrid. Explain how to describe the option without counting the four visitors as served today.

Debrief · Both allocations initially fit

One live bilingual session takes 2 × (22 + 8) = 60 minutes and leaves 10 minutes of room in the guide block. Two single-language sessions take 60 + 10 = 70 minutes. Both serve 45 people. Bilingual is defensible for time buffer; separate sessions can be preferable if visitors value an uninterrupted single-language experience.

Debrief · Check the changed guide block

With just 58 minutes, neither original format fits. One single-language live visit uses 30 minutes and serves at most 30 Cantonese visitors or 15 English visitors. An abbreviated bilingual session cannot substitute if it drops promised discussion time.

Debrief · Offer the hybrid only to willing visitors

Hybrid uses 22 + 8 + 8 = 38 minutes of guide time and serves 30 + 11 = 41 people. It is the largest acceptable format in this block. Offer a later fully live visit to the four remaining English-language visitors; do not list them as accommodated today.

Debrief · Sell the correct experience

Tell visitors that the English story is a recording and live questions are still included. Check recording access, how people signal for questions and whether the format works for those who choose it. Count acceptance and delivered service separately.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to measure guide time, 4 for the hybrid, and 5 to discuss which formats are truly substitutes. Ask students to explain why an available language on a device is not itself the same as a live tour.
