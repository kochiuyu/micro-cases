STR-17 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Competitive response · Segment economics · Service differentiation

Defend every customer. Defend the right position?

Different service models in one market

Singtel’s financial review for the year ended 31 March 2019 describes the introduction of GOMO as an all-digital mobile product aimed at digital natives and millennials. That is a regional example of a differentiated service model, not evidence of its profitability or a competitor’s response. Our Hong Kong business-support provider and low-cost entrant below are original fiction, unrelated to Singtel’s accounts.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong provider supports small firms using a standard booking application. A new entrant offers a lawful self-service subscription at HK$800 per account per month. Our provider charges HK$1,000 and includes trained human support for setup and coordination. It can either match HK$800 for every renewing account while maintaining the promised support, or keep HK$1,000 and concentrate its limited renewal effort on explaining and demonstrating its support capability. These are two prepared responses; there is no funded second brand or newly built low-cost platform in this cycle.

There are 100 existing accounts: 60 usually complete routine tasks themselves and 40 regularly need more complex coordination. These are observed service-use categories, not demographic labels or automatic profitability rankings. All accounts retain their existing contract rights, safety, accessibility, privacy and essential support. New renewal terms are transparent and voluntary; no customer is coerced or cut off mid-contract. The provider has competent paid capacity for both responses. Both communication budgets are equal and funded.

Management fears losing account numbers but has not yet combined support costs with the proposed prices. The entrant’s future response and customers’ renewal decisions are unknown. Choose a provisional response and identify the evidence needed to defend it, rather than assuming that a larger customer count means a stronger competitive position.

Round 2 · More information

A completed internal cost review supplies the next month’s incremental service cost per renewed account: HK$200 for routine-use accounts and HK$850 for coordination-intensive accounts. These include application costs and all paid support resources that vary with each renewal; they are stipulated avoidable costs, not allocated fixed overhead. Common fixed costs remain unchanged and funded separately. The current classification and these costs are assumed stable for this one-month comparison, not forever. More intensive support may be valued, but it also costs more to supply.

Use two explicitly hypothetical renewal scenarios, not forecasts or findings from a customer survey. In both, matching retains all 60 routine-use and 40 coordination-intensive accounts. Under the support-position response, scenario X retains 50 routine-use and all 40 coordination-intensive accounts; scenario Y retains only 30 routine-use and all 40 coordination-intensive accounts. Departing customers choose the entrant voluntarily at renewal. The 40 coordination-intensive renewals are assumptions for these scenarios, not proof that this segment is loyal. The team must choose its response and specify what it will verify before committing a wider rollout. Account totals alone and the label “high-support customer” cannot settle the choice. The support capability must remain real: trained staff, usable documentation and dependable coordination, not merely a premium-price slogan. No service cuts, hidden fees or price discrimination are included.

Your decision

Which response would you choose now, and under which renewal assumptions? Calculate monthly contribution in X and Y, identify the break-even number of routine-use renewals for the support-position response, and state what evidence could change your recommendation.

Revenue is not contribution

At HK$800, a routine-use renewal contributes HK$600, but a coordination-intensive renewal contributes −HK$50. Matching all 100 accounts therefore produces 60 × HK$600 + 40 × (−HK$50) = HK$34,000 before common fixed costs. Retaining every account is not the same as earning the best contribution. These are avoidable costs by assumption; arbitrary overhead allocations would not justify the same inference.

Higher support needs do not automatically mean higher profit

At HK$1,000, routine-use contribution is HK$800 and coordination-intensive contribution is HK$150. Scenario X gives 50 × HK$800 + 40 × HK$150 = HK$46,000 from 90 accounts. Scenario Y gives 30 × HK$800 + 40 × HK$150 = HK$30,000 from 70 accounts. Thus X favours the support-position response by HK$12,000, while Y favours matching by HK$4,000. Neither scenario has an assigned probability.

Locate the retention boundary

If all 40 coordination-intensive accounts renew as stipulated, they contribute HK$6,000 at the existing price. Matching’s HK$34,000 is equalled when 800 × n + 6,000 = 34,000, so n = 35 routine-use renewals. More than 35 favours the support position on this one-month contribution measure; fewer favours matching. This boundary changes if the 40 renewals, costs or matching-retention assumption change.

Defend a capability, not a label

The strategic question is which needs the provider can meet distinctively at a sustainable cost, and what it will not imitate. Maintaining trained coordination while copying a self-service price does not copy the entrant’s operating model. Equally, calling customers “support-seeking” does not make them profitable or guarantee renewal. Validate renewal behaviour, cost stability and the support advantage before using the scenario as a forecast. Later rival reactions and longer-term capability effects remain unmodelled.

Instructor note

Take a provisional vote before revealing costs. Require learners to distinguish total accounts, revenue, incremental contribution and company profit. Accept either response with explicit scenario dependence and an evidence plan. Challenge “keep all customers” and “premium customers are best” equally. The exercise does not solve a Nash equilibrium, estimate market demand or recommend abandoning contractual support. All obligations and voluntary customer choice remain protected.
