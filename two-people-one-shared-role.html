PPL-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Job sharing · Paid handoffs · Role continuity

One shared job. How much time is really covered?

One role can have two postholders

Hong Kong Labour Department guidance lists job sharing as a flexible-work arrangement. An NHS employer’s job-share policy also notes that time for handover needs to be planned and can have a cost. The museum desk below is fictional and does not adopt either organisation’s terms.

Your classroom decision

A Hong Kong museum is testing one visitor-booking coordinator role shared by two qualified adult colleagues who have freely agreed to the pilot. Together they have 40 paid in-role hours in the week, as measured after breaks. The pilot requires at least 38 hours of unique public desk coverage. A single person can provide desk coverage at any given hour.

A uses a shared record and two half-hour updates at separate times, one per colleague away from the desk. B schedules one two-hour joint handover while one colleague remains available to serve the desk during the overlap. The 40 paid hours include the work of both colleagues. Apart from the stated time, there is no overlap or absence. Both plans can place their hours so the counted coverage meets the needed windows.

Your decision

How much unique desk coverage does each plan provide? Which would you try, and what should both colleagues record so visitors do not have to start again?

Round 2 · More information

The museum adds a new group-booking workflow whose unsettled exceptions require both colleagues to review and agree together before the shift changes. They cannot have the discussion at the public counter. A’s separate updates can still handle routine cases but cannot satisfy the new joint review. No real booking or person is involved in the exercise.

A separate trained floater can provide up to 2.5 hours of paid coverage during the required two-hour private handover. The two sharers’ hours are fixed and no one needs to be available outside them. The funded fallback defers new discretionary bookings if coverage cannot be maintained.

Your decision

How would you redesign the handover and meet the desk minimum now? Show the coverage calculation and name who owns an unresolved item while the other colleague is off duty.

Debrief · Count each covered hour once

A provides 40 − 1 = 39 desk hours. B uses four paid person-hours in the simultaneous handover but provides two unique public desk hours during it, leaving 40 − 4 + 2 = 38 hours. A leaves more slack; B creates a shared discussion. Neither permits doing the handover unpaid.

Debrief · Fund privacy instead of assuming overlap is cover

A private joint handover takes both people off the desk. B now gives 40 − 4 = 36 desk hours from the two sharers. Use two paid floater hours during the handover to restore 38, within the 2.5-hour allocation. That means 42 paid person-hours across three workers, not 40 hours magically producing simultaneous coverage.

Debrief · Keep one service promise

Use one shared record with the issue, last action, next step and named on-duty owner. Both sharers have the same defined permissions; the on-duty person owns the next action unless escalated to a named manager. The floater receives only routine items they can handle. Review misses and handoff rework, not willingness to answer unpaid messages.

Instructor note

Have students draw the two-person timeline, then erase the overlap’s public coverage when the review moves off the desk. Ask how a job share differs from two independent part-time jobs and why the approved coverage fallback matters.
