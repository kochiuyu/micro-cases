ACC-35 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Estimate changes · Prior-period errors · Evidence timing

New information. Or an old mistake?

When did the information become available?

A revised estimate is not automatically evidence that an earlier report was wrong. Equally, calling an overlooked fact new information does not erase an error. Accounting guidance distinguishes genuine updates from failures to use reliable information available at the time. This fictional case supplies its own simplified policy.

Your classroom decision

You are controller of fictional Harbour Repair Tools, a Hong Kong equipment-service business preparing Year 3 financial statements with Year 2 comparatives. Year 2 statements were already authorised and issued. The question concerns one diagnostic machine; the straight-line method and zero residual value remain appropriate throughout.

The machine cost HK$90,000 and was paid for and available for use at the start of Year 1. Its supported original life was six years. The business recorded HK$15,000 depreciation in each of Years 1 and 2, leaving HK$60,000 at the start of Year 3.

The manager says genuinely new operating information arose at the start of Year 3, shortening expected service to the end of Year 4. On that account, the remaining life became two years then. The manager proposes spreading the recorded HK$60,000 over Years 3 and 4 without changing Year 2.

Apply genuine estimate changes from their effective date to the current and, where relevant, future periods. A material prior-period error instead arises from failure to use reliable information available when the earlier statements were authorised and reasonably obtainable for their preparation. Correct that error retrospectively when its effects can be determined.

All depreciation is an operating expense, not inventory cost. Ignore tax, impairment, revaluation, financing, separate components and disposal proceeds. No policy or method change is proposed. The manager’s account still needs documentary verification; classification follows the evidence, not which treatment gives the preferred current profit.

Round 2 · More information

The file check finds a reliable maintenance assessment delivered to finance at the start of Year 2. It documented newly changed operating conditions then and supported service only through the end of Year 4: three years remaining from the start of Year 2. The later assessment merely repeated that conclusion.

The Year 2 assessment was in the accessible equipment file and should reasonably have been considered when preparing Year 2 statements. It was omitted from the depreciation worksheet through an administrative oversight. Its conclusion was supported at that time, not reconstructed using later results. There is no genuinely new Year 3 estimate.

Year 1’s HK$15,000 charge was correct: the changed conditions arose only at the start of Year 2. The Year 2 omission is material for this exercise, and its exact effects are practicable to calculate. The Year 3 statements are the first set authorised after discovery; disclosure and comparative correction are required.

At the start of Year 3, service is still expected to end at the end of Year 4, with zero residual value. There is no impairment or other adjustment. Determine the corrected opening carrying amount before calculating current depreciation. The original purchase cash payment does not change.

Your decision

After the file check, calculate corrected Year 2 depreciation, the comparative correction, opening Year 3 carrying amount and Year 3 depreciation. Explain why the error correction is not an extra Year 3 operating expense and why Year 1 remains unchanged.

Debrief · The provisional estimate calculation

On the manager’s initial account, HK$60,000 divided by two remaining years gives HK$30,000 depreciation in each of Years 3 and 4. A genuinely new Year 3 estimate would not by itself rewrite a reasonable Year 2 estimate. This answer is conditional on the account being true, not a choice of convenient treatment.

Check when the changed circumstances arose, when reliable evidence existed, who could obtain it and what the prior reporting team should reasonably have considered. The date someone finally opens a file is not necessarily the date information first became available. A worse outcome alone also does not establish an earlier mistake.

Debrief · Reconstruct the missed Year 2 update

At the start of Year 2, the correct carrying amount was HK$90,000 − HK$15,000 = HK$75,000. The supported revised remaining life was three years. Correct Year 2 depreciation is therefore HK$75,000 ÷ 3 = HK$25,000, rather than the HK$15,000 recorded.

Year 2 depreciation was understated by HK$10,000. Its closing carrying amount should have been HK$50,000, not HK$60,000. With tax and other effects excluded, Year 2 profit and closing retained earnings were overstated by HK$10,000. Year 1 is not affected by this Year 2 omission.

Debrief · Put the correction in the right period

Restate the Year 2 comparative expense and closing asset amount in the Year 3 statements. The corrected opening Year 3 carrying amount is HK$50,000, and opening retained earnings decrease HK$10,000 relative to the uncorrected ledger. Opening Year 2 balances remain unchanged because the error arose during Year 2.

Do not charge the HK$10,000 correction as an extra Year 3 operating expense. It corrects the prior-period comparative and carried-forward balances. Preserve the originally issued version and explain the restatement; do not silently replace its history. The exercise excludes impracticability, so it is not a reason to avoid correction.

Debrief · Calculate current expense on the corrected base

Year 3 begins with HK$50,000 and two years remaining, so depreciation is HK$25,000 in Year 3 and, if estimates stay unchanged, HK$25,000 in Year 4. Year 3 closes at HK$25,000. The full schedule is HK$15,000 + HK$25,000 + HK$25,000 + HK$25,000 = HK$90,000.

Compared with the provisional HK$30,000 proposal, correct Year 3 depreciation is HK$5,000 lower, but this is not a reason to prefer error treatment. The evidence requires it. Neither treatment changes the original cash payment, proves a resale value or supplies a complete profit figure.

Debrief · Explain the omission, not a convenient label

Disclose the nature of the material omission and the correction to affected comparative line items under the supplied policy. Retain the dated assessment, evidence of delivery, calculation and approval trail. Connect engineering reviews to finance’s estimate-review process so an accessible report is not lost between teams.

The original revision at the start of Year 2 should have been a prospective estimate update then. Failing to apply it created the error now being corrected retrospectively. The discovery date does not turn the missed update into a new Year 3 estimate. Do not use hindsight to invent different earlier assumptions.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: calculate the conditional HK$30,000 charge and request evidence. Minutes 4–9: place the earlier assessment on a timeline. Minutes 9–14: reconstruct HK$25,000 Year 2 expense and the HK$10,000 correction. Minutes 14–18: reconcile opening balances, current expense and the unchanged purchase payment.

ACC-08 chooses a depreciation method for a first report using evidence about consumption. Here straight-line allocation is fixed and earlier statements have been issued. The central distinction is genuinely new information versus an omitted available report, with prospective estimation separated from retrospective correction.
