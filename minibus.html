FIN-02 · Hong Kong · Asset valuation · 10–15 minutes

What would you bid for the red minibus?

A red minibus owner offers you the vehicle and the right to operate it for one year. The owner wants HK$110,000, payable at year end. You keep the fares, cover the running costs and return the bus at the end. You can walk away today.

Your forecast for 250 operating days is HK$2,000 a day in fares and HK$1,500 a day in costs, including what your own working time is worth. Treat the forecast as certain for this round.

Your decision

Would you take the lease for HK$110,000? What is the most you could pay and still do as well as walking away?

Round 2 · A competing service

Before you sign, a competing bus operator announces a parallel service starting halfway through your lease. For the final 125 days, you expect daily fares to fall to HK$1,800. Your costs and first-half forecast stay the same.

Your decision

Do you sign at the original price, make a lower offer, or walk away? Give the owner your revised ceiling.

Debrief · What changed the bid?

At first, HK$500 per day for 250 days leaves HK$125,000 for the lease. Paying HK$110,000 leaves you HK$15,000 ahead of your alternative. With competition, the second half earns only HK$300 per day. The new ceiling is 125 × HK$500 + 125 × HK$300 = HK$100,000. At the asking price you would lose HK$10,000 relative to walking away.

Why can the bid fall while the bus is still full today? A buyer values the earnings ahead. The HK$200 daily revenue loss over 125 future days cuts the bid by HK$25,000 today. For a licence that can earn income for many years, buyers would also consider how long the loss lasts, alternative routes, and the present value of future earnings.

Instructor note · Running the discussion

Minutes 0–3: take a vote and ask for a maximum bid. Minutes 3–7: reveal when the competing service starts and ask pairs to renegotiate. Minutes 7–12: ask whether the same announcement would change the bid if the competitor started the day after this lease expired. For this lease it would have no direct effect. A longer-lived licence could still lose value.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for the classroom decisions is provided above. These sources document the background and are optional reading.

  1. Transport Department, Minibuses. Explains the operating flexibility of red minibuses.
  2. LegCo Research Office, Public light buses in Hong Kong. Describes the feeder role and market served by minibuses.
  3. LegCo Panel on Transport, Policy on Public Light Buses. Gives the historical licensing background.
  4. Transport Department, Transport Planning and Design Manual, Volume 9. Explains why demand and competing transport modes matter in service planning.
  5. Transport Department, Red-to-green conversion scheme. Documents two route conversions, an example of how future operating arrangements can change.

The lease, earnings, competing route and dates are classroom assumptions. All amounts are in Hong Kong dollars. Ignore within-year interest and assume no resale rights, route changes or other income during the lease.
