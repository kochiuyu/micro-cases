MKT-35 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Source of volume · Cannibalisation threshold · Portfolio contribution uncertainty

A new product. Added sales or moved sales?

Gross launch sales are not incremental demand

Sales of a new variant may come from the brand’s existing product, competing brands or occasions that previously generated no category purchase. Those sources have different implications for portfolio contribution. Research and commercial forecasting guidance distinguish gross launch volume from incremental demand. All brand, study and financial figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

You manage fictional Hong Kong snack brand Harbour Crunch. For the next uncommitted month, keeping only its original pack is expected to sell 1,000 packs. Each sells for HK$24 with complete variable cost of HK$14, leaving HK$10 contribution. Existing accepted orders are separately protected and excluded from these figures.

A premium flavour variant is forecast to sell 400 packs at HK$34 with complete variable cost of HK$20, leaving HK$14 contribution each. Its avoidable launch campaign and setup cost HK$3,200, not yet spent or committed. Premium describes positioning and price, not a health or nutritional advantage.

Both products have the same pack weight. Capacity, shelf space and normal funded staff time can accommodate every supplied scenario without displacing production. Prices, other products, quality and existing fixed costs remain unchanged. All incremental production, distribution and launch costs are included; there is no further inventory or setup charge.

The opening worksheet treats all 400 premium purchases as additional and forecasts HK$2,400 extra contribution. You can approve the complete launch or retain the original-only range while seeking better evidence. Neither offer is announced. No future brand benefit, competitive defence or customer lifetime value has been priced into the comparison.

Round 2 · More information

An exploratory concept exercise asks adults what they would buy on the same occasion if the premium pack were unavailable. Answers distinguish an original pack, a competitor’s pack and no snack-category purchase. These stated intentions are not observed switching or a representative forecast. The planning team uses them to construct sensitivity scenarios, not confidence intervals.

In the central scenario, 200 of the 400 premium purchases replace one original pack each; 140 replace a competitor’s pack and 60 arise on previously unserved occasions. Original demand becomes 800. Competitor switching and new occasions add 200 purchases to Harbour Crunch, but only the 60 represent additional category purchases under this scenario.

The lower-switching scenario has 160 own-brand switches, 160 competitor switches and 80 new occasions. The higher-switching scenario has 280, 80 and 40 respectively. Each still totals 400 premium purchases. All own-brand substitutions are one-for-one; there are no other lost original purchases, basket effects or timing shifts.

No probabilities are assigned to these three scenarios, and other outcomes remain possible. For this sensitivity calculation only, hold total premium volume, prices, margins and launch cost fixed while varying its source. Management wants the break-even number of own-brand switches before deciding how much uncertainty it is willing to accept.

The proposed launch remains all 400 premium packs or none this month. A smaller test, different channel or revised price would need a separately costed proposal and new demand assumptions. There is time to defer without breaking commitments, but no free pilot or proven benefit from waiting is supplied.

Your decision

Calculate incremental portfolio contribution in all three scenarios and the break-even own-brand switching count. Express that threshold using both premium volume and original-only volume as denominators. Recommend a decision without inventing scenario probabilities or treating stated intentions as observed behaviour.

Debrief · Start with the whole portfolio

Original-only contribution is 1,000 × HK$10 = HK$10,000. The launch worksheet adds 400 × HK$14 minus HK$3,200 = HK$2,400, but omits original contribution lost through switching. Let S be the number of premium purchases that replace original purchases. Incremental portfolio contribution is HK$2,400 minus HK$10 × S.

An own-brand switch earns HK$14 on the premium pack but forgoes HK$10 on the original, adding HK$4 before launch cost. A purchase drawn from a competitor or a new occasion adds HK$14 to this brand. Do not count the same displaced original contribution twice or assume every switch is automatically harmful.

Debrief · Reconcile the three scenarios

At S = 200, incremental contribution is HK$2,400 − HK$2,000 = HK$400. Total portfolio contribution is 800 × HK$10 + 400 × HK$14 − HK$3,200 = HK$10,400. The premium pack can grow portfolio contribution even though half its volume comes from the original product.

At S = 160, the increment is HK$800 and total contribution HK$10,800. At S = 280, the increment is −HK$400 and total contribution HK$9,600. Premium sales remain 400 in all three cases. The variation comes from which purchases they replace, not a capacity shortage or changed premium margin.

Debrief · Find the threshold and name its denominator

Break-even requires HK$2,400 − HK$10 × S = 0, so S = 240. Fewer than 240 own-brand switches give a positive increment; more give a negative increment. Exactly 240 leaves portfolio contribution unchanged at HK$10,000. This threshold is conditional on every other supplied assumption.

The 240 switches are 240/400 = 60% of premium volume, but 240/1,000 = 24% of original-only volume. These are different ratios, not contradictory estimates. Label the denominator whenever reporting cannibalisation. In the central scenario the corresponding ratios are 50% and 20%, respectively.

Debrief · Uncertainty is part of the recommendation

The central scenario supports a small positive increment, while the supplied higher-switching scenario loses HK$400. No expected value or probability of loss can be calculated without scenario probabilities. The three cases are sensitivity checks, not a statistical uncertainty band or a complete list of possible outcomes.

I would defer the full launch until the team can better assess whether switching is plausibly below 240 and whether the downside fits management’s risk tolerance. Launching could be defended if management knowingly accepts the uncertainty. Neither decision follows automatically from the central estimate, and waiting itself has no quantified value here.

Debrief · Learn about the counterfactual

Ask what purchasers would otherwise buy on the same occasion, then compare those intentions with appropriately designed in-market evidence when a funded test is available. Observe original and premium contribution together, with a credible no-launch comparison. A decline in original sales alone can reflect seasonality, availability or promotion rather than causal substitution.

Future evaluation should also vary total demand, costs and prices rather than treating this one-dimensional threshold as permanent. Distinguish new-to-brand purchases from new category demand and repeat purchases from initial trial. Any proposed strategic benefit or smaller launch needs its own evidence and cost, not an invented offset to a weak portfolio result.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: challenge the standalone worksheet. Minutes 4–8: reconcile the three purchase sources. Minutes 8–13: derive the 240-switch threshold and its two denominators. Minutes 13–18: defend a decision under unweighted scenarios and identify evidence that would change it.

Ask students to separate the arithmetic threshold from the decision. Correct calculations do not establish how likely a scenario is or management’s willingness to accept loss. Require them to label each denominator and state which evidence or assumption would make them change their recommendation.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All businesses, study details and figures are fictional. Original-only volume is 1,000 packs at HK$10 contribution; premium volume is 400 at HK$14. The HK$3,200 launch cost is avoidable. Capacity and shelf space are ample; existing orders are protected. Own-brand substitution is one-for-one, with no additional basket or timing loss. Lower, central and higher own-brand switching counts are 160, 200 and 280. Each scenario totals 400 premium purchases. These are unweighted sensitivity scenarios, not confidence intervals or causal estimates. Baseline contribution is HK$10,000; incremental contribution is HK$2,400 minus HK$10 per own-brand switch. Scenario increments are HK$800, HK$400 and minus HK$400. Break-even at 240 switches equals 60% of premium volume or 24% of original-only volume. A smaller test needs a separately costed proposal; no strategic benefit or value of waiting is assumed.

  1. OpenStax · Principles of Marketing · 9.2 Product Items, Product Lines, and Product Mixes. Primary educational account distinguishes product lines and mixes and notes internal competition and added variety costs. No universal claim that more variety increases profit is adopted.
  2. ACCA · Relevant costs. Primary technical guidance identifies changed future cash flows and foregone benefits as relevant, while unchanged allocated costs and sunk spending are not. The fictional launch cost remains avoidable.
  3. Yang and Hu · New product development considering product cannibalization, development cost and production cost · 2016. Original Hong Kong Polytechnic University modelling research combines demand overlap and development/production costs. Its monopolistic demand geometry and optimisation results are not copied or claimed to predict this snack launch.
  4. van Heerde, Srinivasan and Dekimpe · Estimating Cannibalization Rates for Pioneering Innovations · 2010. Original research decomposes demand into internal substitution, competitive draw and primary demand, with time variation. Its Lexus application is not evidence for the fictional forecast or a simple interview-based causal estimate.
  5. Ipsos · Evaluating Source of Volume and Cannibalization for New Packaged Goods Line Extension Ideas · 2004. Original commercial methodology discusses counterfactual purchase questions, purchase allocation and calibration to market evidence. It distinguishes denominators; no sample-size guarantee or vendor performance claim is imported.
