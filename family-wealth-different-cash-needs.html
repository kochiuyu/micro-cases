ENT-39 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Owner liquidity · Family governance · Cross-purchase financing

Family wealth. One business, several owners’ needs.

Ownership can be shared without identical cash needs

Family owners can share a commitment to a business while needing different amounts of personal liquidity. A dividend pays all holders according to their rights; a voluntary share transfer changes ownership. Separating those mechanisms can widen the choices without pretending that liquidity, financing or control is free.

Your classroom decision

Fictional Harbour Components is a Hong Kong family manufacturer. Adult siblings Alex and Bo each own 40% of its ordinary shares; Casey owns 20%. Each share has the same dividend and voting rights. Alex and Bo work in the firm at separately approved pay. Casey works elsewhere. Management appointments are not being decided.

All three have sufficient outside household income or ordinary earned pay for routine living costs. Casey wants at least HK$100,000 personal cash by the end of next month for an independent project and would consider reducing, but not eliminating, ownership. Alex and Bo prefer reinvestment. Nobody must disclose private household details to justify a preference.

After all operating obligations, safeguards and reserves, HK$1,000,000 is available for this decision. Plan G invests HK$800,000 in a second production cell and proposes a HK$200,000 dividend. Plan D invests HK$400,000 upgrading the existing line and proposes a HK$600,000 dividend. Both dividends would be paid at the end of next month.

Qualified advisers have confirmed both packages fit the supplied solvency, distributable-reserve, covenant and approval conditions. Employee pay, safety and reserves remain protected. Neither package has been approved or promised. No additional company borrowing is available. These fictional checks are not a legal test for another company.

Both projects are technically feasible, but their demand, risk and return forecasts are not supplied. More investment is not automatically better. The owners must select a capital package and a fair process for owner liquidity. Current ownership remains unchanged unless an acceptable separate transfer is completed before the dividend entitlement date.

How much dividend would each sibling receive under G and D at the current ownership percentages? Which package meets Casey’s stated cash target? Explain why the cash target does not by itself establish the best investment project or entitle one owner to a special dividend.

Round 2 · More information

A transparent proposal lets Casey voluntarily sell ten percentage points of the company, half of Casey’s holding, to Alex and Bo. Each would buy five percentage points for HK$150,000. The aggregate HK$300,000 price is a negotiation assumption acceptable for further discussion, not an independent valuation finding or an agreed sale.

Each buyer would pay HK$50,000 personally on completion, then HK$50,000 principal after one year and HK$50,000 after two years. Interest is 5% annually on each buyer’s opening unpaid principal, paid with those instalments. The company supplies no payment, loan, guarantee or security and is not obliged to increase dividends.

Each buyer has demonstrated separately earmarked personal funds for the initial payment and assessed non-company resources for later principal, interest and transaction costs. Future repayments do not depend on company dividends or project success. These are fictional affordability assumptions, not a guarantee against personal default. Casey must assess the deferred-credit risk.

All three would receive the same valuation information and independent advice, with conflicts documented. Completion remains conditional on their informed agreement and legal, tax, transfer-restriction and creditor-protection checks, including security and default terms. Transaction costs are separately funded by the buyers. If completed, all ten percentage points transfer before the dividend entitlement date.

Your decision

If the voluntary transfer completes and G is chosen, calculate the new ownership, each dividend and Casey’s cash by next month. Show the later aggregate principal and interest due to Casey. Does this create a feasible additional option, and what does it fail to prove about G or the transfer?

Debrief · Start with equal rights per share

Before any transfer, G’s HK$200,000 dividend pays Alex HK$80,000, Bo HK$80,000 and Casey HK$40,000. D’s HK$600,000 pays HK$240,000, HK$240,000 and HK$120,000 respectively. Only D meets Casey’s HK$100,000 target through the proposed dividend alone.

Paying a larger pro-rata dividend directs cash to all three owners, not only the person seeking liquidity. D allocates HK$400,000 less to investment and HK$400,000 more to dividends than G. That describes the allocation trade-off; without project returns it does not identify a superior business strategy.

Debrief · Separate company cash from buyer cash

After the proposed transfer, Alex owns 45%, Bo 45% and Casey 10%. G’s dividend would then be HK$90,000, HK$90,000 and HK$20,000. Casey also receives two initial personal payments of HK$50,000, for HK$100,000 from the share sale.

Casey’s cash by next month is therefore HK$120,000: HK$100,000 sale proceeds plus HK$20,000 dividend. The company still spends only its HK$800,000 investment and HK$200,000 dividend. It does not fund the HK$300,000 purchase price. Buyers’ personal obligations must not be silently shifted onto company cash.

Debrief · A claim is not cash today

The aggregate unpaid purchase principal after completion is HK$200,000. At year one, the buyers together owe HK$100,000 principal plus 5% × HK$200,000 = HK$10,000 interest. At year two they owe the final HK$100,000 principal plus 5% × HK$100,000 = HK$5,000 interest.

Total scheduled sale receipts are HK$100,000 now, HK$110,000 after one year and HK$105,000 after two years: HK$300,000 price plus HK$15,000 financing interest. These are dated promised amounts, not a present value or guaranteed collection. The deferred HK$200,000 is not available to spend next month.

Debrief · Liquidity changes ownership and risk

Casey would retain a 10% equity interest, half the previous voting and dividend share, while becoming a creditor of the two buyers for the unpaid price. That is not equivalent to receiving D’s dividend while retaining 20%. Future equity upside, distributions, influence and credit exposure differ.

Alex and Bo each gain five percentage points and personally undertake HK$150,000 of purchase principal plus HK$7,500 scheduled interest. They are not receiving extra shares for free. A repayment plan dependent on unpromised company dividends would reopen the business funding problem; the supplied independent funding assumption is essential.

Debrief · Choose the package and the process separately

The proposal makes G compatible with Casey’s immediate cash target if all transfer conditions are satisfied. It does not prove G’s project is better than D’s, that HK$300,000 is a fair price, or that Casey should accept seller credit. Review commercial forecasts and owner preferences rather than forcing agreement from the arithmetic.

D remains a possible choice without a transfer. G without an agreed liquidity arrangement does not meet Casey’s stated target through dividends alone. If Casey declines, do not treat a majority preference as consent to sell; return to a negotiated allocation or other independently assessed option.

Debrief · Document a repeatable policy

Agree how owners may request liquidity, obtain valuation information, find willing buyers and evaluate payment security, instalment affordability and default remedies. Distinguish family discussion from the company’s formal approval bodies. Apply transparent conflict procedures and confirm dividend entitlement dates before anyone relies on a figure.

A recurring policy can make future discussions more predictable without promising unlimited buyouts or permanent dividends. Preserve company obligations and the right not to trade. A family constitution does not itself supply funding, settle every legal issue or make a seller’s deferred claim risk-free.
