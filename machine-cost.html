ACC-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accounting · Asset recognition · Equipment cost

One machine invoice. One asset cost?

In practice · Equipment cost and annual expense

In its 2024/25 report, the Office of the Communications Authority Trading Fund describes recording equipment at cost, less accumulated depreciation and impairment, and allocating cost over estimated useful lives.

This is a local reporting example, not a claim about a particular purchase or accounting error. The printing studio and package below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A printing-service studio has paid HK$150,000 for a package described on the cover sheet as “new machine and getting started”. The machine will provide services for several years. No external financial report has been issued.

The owner proposes recording the entire package as equipment and allocating its cost over use. A colleague thinks the entire cash payment should reduce this year’s profit immediately. You have not yet seen the itemised breakdown.

Classroom rule: Record eligible costs of acquiring the machine and getting it physically ready for its intended use as an asset. Recognise received services that do not create an eligible asset as expenses. “Capitalise” means record as an asset, not raise funds.

The exercise follows the IAS 16 and IAS 38 principles described in the optional sources. It is financial accounting, not tax accounting or a claim that every Hong Kong business uses identical reporting rules.

Round 2 · One fact changes

The itemised record shows the following completed purchase and services. All figures include all relevant charges.

ItemAmount
Machine purchaseHK$120,000
Delivery to its operating locationHK$8,000
Necessary installation and testing of the machineHK$12,000
Completed operator-training courseHK$10,000
TotalHK$150,000

The machine is delivered, installed and has passed the necessary testing. Benefits are probable and cost is reliably measured. The separately priced course teaches staff to use the already-ready machine; it does not install, alter or test it, or create a separate software or licence asset.

Both acquisition and training are completed and paid. There is no prepaid future course, and no tax, rebate, dismantling obligation, financing cost or other fee to add.

Your decision

What amount belongs in the equipment record, and what amount is an expense now? Does expensing the completed training mean it was a bad investment?

Sort what was acquired, not which invoice contains it

The machine purchase, delivery and necessary installation and testing belong in its initial cost under the supplied facts: HK$120,000 + HK$8,000 + HK$12,000 = HK$140,000.

The completed operator course is a HK$10,000 expense. It prepares people to use a machine already physically ready, rather than bringing the machine to its required location and condition. A single package can therefore contain different accounting categories.

Useful does not automatically mean an accounting asset

Training can be commercially valuable even when its cost is recognised as an expense. Expected future benefits alone do not establish an eligible asset. The classification does not say employees lack value or that the studio should cut training.

The owner’s equipment proposal is closer for the multi-year machine, but the whole HK$150,000 should not remain in that record after the breakdown arrives. Equally, treating the entire payment as an immediate expense ignores the eligible machine cost.

Separate payment from expense recognition

Cash paid is HK$150,000 whichever label is used. The correct initial classification is HK$140,000 equipment cost and HK$10,000 completed training expense. Changing the label does not save cash.

The equipment cost will also be allocated over use through depreciation under the exercise’s accounting basis. Recording an asset does not mean its cost will never affect expense. The timing and amount require the relevant useful-life and other assumptions.

Keep the evidence behind the record

Retain the itemised invoice, delivery and installation evidence, testing completion and the course description. Document why each component qualifies for its classification. Do not choose the accounting label to reach a desired profit.

Nothing in this case establishes supplier misconduct. A combined commercial package can be reasonable even though the buyer must separate its accounting components.

Instructor note · optional depreciation extension

For an optional extension only, assume the machine is ready at the start of a full reporting year, used on a straight-line basis over five years, with zero residual value and no impairment or estimate changes. All current-period charges go to expense; no inventory or work in progress absorbs them.

Annual depreciation is HK$140,000 ÷ 5 = HK$28,000. Adding completed training gives HK$38,000 first-year expense. Capitalising all HK$150,000 instead would show HK$30,000 depreciation and omit the immediate training expense, understating that year’s expense by HK$8,000.

Correct year-end carrying amount would be HK$112,000, compared with HK$120,000 under that incorrect treatment. Across five years, the simplified total acquisition and training cost recognised remains HK$150,000. This is timing, not a cash saving or tax allowance calculation. Keep the main vote focused on HK$140,000 asset cost and HK$10,000 training expense.
