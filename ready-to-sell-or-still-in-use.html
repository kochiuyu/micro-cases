ACC-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Held-for-sale classification · Depreciation cutoff · Fair value less costs to sell

Ready to sell, or still in use?

The accounting follows when the asset is sale-ready

HKFRS 5 distinguishes assets whose carrying amounts are recovered mainly through sale from those still recovered through use. Sino ICT Holdings’ 2025 annual report gives an example of revisiting the classification as the disposal timetable changes. The machine and all figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

On 1 October, a Hong Kong carton-finishing business commits to sell a single owned machine, starts active marketing at a reasonable price, and expects to close in February with little prospect of reversing its plan. It refuses to let a buyer take the machine until a replacement is installed. This restriction is not usual or customary in such sales.

The manager wants to classify it held for sale immediately and stop depreciation. The controller says it remains plant and equipment until it is available for immediate sale on normal terms as well as highly probable to sell. There were no prior impairment losses or revaluations; assume recoverable amount under the ordinary in-use rules is at least carrying amount before any held-for-sale classification.

Available in the 1 October decisionAmount or estimate
30 September carrying amountHK$480,000
Monthly depreciation while in useHK$12,000
1 October fair valueHK$450,000
Direct incremental costs to sellHK$15,000
Your decision

Which accounting position should the controller take on 1 October? If the machine remains unavailable through November, what carrying amount and depreciation should the controller report at 30 November?

Round 2 · More information

The replacement arrives. On 1 December, the owner authorises immediate transfer in the machine’s present condition on customary terms. All sale-probability criteria remain met. Assessed fair value is then HK$420,000 and costs to sell remain HK$15,000. At 31 December, fair value is HK$436,000, costs are unchanged, and the machine has not sold but still meets the classification criteria.

Your decision

Show classification, depreciation, loss or gain and carrying amount at 1 and 31 December. Why is the sale of this machine not a discontinued operation?

October and November

The buyer cannot take the machine, so the October plan alone does not qualify. Continue depreciation: 2 × HK$12,000 = HK$24,000, leaving HK$456,000 at 30 November. Do not apply the October held-for-sale fair value test to equipment that has not met the classification criteria.

Classify when both conditions hold

On 1 December measure at the lower of HK$456,000 and HK$420,000 − HK$15,000 = HK$405,000. Recognise HK$51,000 loss and cease depreciation from 1 December. The company has not yet recognised a sale or received any proceeds.

Revisit the measurement

December’s net fair value is HK$436,000 − HK$15,000 = HK$421,000. Recognise a HK$16,000 gain, within the HK$51,000 cumulative loss available to reverse, and report HK$421,000 on a separate held-for-sale line. No December depreciation. The single machine is not a separate major line of business or geographical area.

Instructor note

Ask students to identify the classification date at another company that withdraws an internal restriction partway through the year. Then ask why a rise in net fair value above HK$456,000 would not let this business record all the increase in this example.
