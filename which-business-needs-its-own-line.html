ACC-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Operating segments · Quantitative thresholds · External revenue coverage

Which business needs its own line?

The business through management’s eyes

IFRS 8 sets out how to report operating segments. Cathay Pacific’s 2025 annual report illustrates its management reporting basis: its segment results use the earnings measure evaluated by the executive directors. The following HKFRS 8 disclosure exercise uses a fictional group, not Cathay figures.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong marine-services group’s decision maker regularly reviews discrete results for all five separately managed businesses to allocate resources and assess performance. All five are operating segments. Marketing calls all of them “Integrated Marine”; their economics and relevant characteristics differ, so no aggregation into a reportable segment is justified.

A segment qualifies if it reaches 10% of combined segment revenue (including internal sales), combined segment assets, or the larger of total profit across profitable segments only and the absolute total loss across loss-making segments only. Then test whether separate reportable segments cover at least 75% of consolidated external revenue. This is the group’s first year in scope, with no prior-year reportable segments; management adds the largest remaining external contributor first if needed, and sees no additional benefit from voluntary separate disclosure.

Round 2 · More information

The decision maker’s corrected inspection report shows a HK$5m loss instead of a HK$1m profit. No other figures or business characteristics change. These are two drafts for the same year; no prior disclosure is being withdrawn.

Your decision

Does the correction change which operating segments get separate lines? Compute the revised profit-or-loss threshold and coverage; show where the remaining external sales sit.

Initial thresholds

Combined segment revenue is 85 + 40 = HK$125m, so the revenue cutoff is HK$12.5m. Assets total HK$100m, so the asset cutoff is HK$10m. Total profit among profitable units is HK$28m versus HK$4m in absolute losses: the result cutoff is HK$2.8m. A and B alone pass the 10% tests.

Initial external coverage

A and B account for 61/85 = 71.8% of external revenue. At 70/85 = 82.4%, adding C (largest remaining external revenue) clears 75%. D and E go to “all other segments”, with HK$15m external sales.

After the correction

Profitable units now earn HK$27m combined; loss units have HK$9m in absolute losses. The cutoff is HK$2.7m, so D qualifies on its HK$5m loss. A, B and D now reach 69/85 = 81.2% without C; C and E sit in “all other segments”, with HK$16m external sales.

Reconcile to the group

In both drafts, eliminate HK$40m of internal sales from HK$125m combined segment revenue to reach HK$85m consolidated external revenue. The “all other” category is described separately from that elimination. Required prior-year presentation or a decision to disclose a small segment voluntarily could change an actual report.

Instructor note

Ask one group to deliberately use net segment profit or total revenue in the 75% fraction. Have another group identify which denominator belongs to each gate and why a loss can itself trigger disclosure.
