ACC-26 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Consolidation · Unrealised inventory profit · Entity and group perspectives

A group sale. A group profit?

The reporting question

An invoice between companies can be valid in each company’s records without creating a sale to an outside customer for the group. Consolidated reporting presents the controlled businesses together. Published consolidation guidance therefore distinguishes internal trading from profit earned through external sales.

The following is an invented reporting exercise, not a company announcement. It isolates one batch and its gross profit before tax, rather than a complete set of financial statements.

Your classroom decision

You prepare a batch-level consolidation worksheet for a fictional Hong Kong group. A parent wholly owns Supply Co and Shop Co throughout the exercise; both are fully consolidated. Supply buys 1,000 identical kits from an outside supplier for HK$80 each, then completes a valid sale of the entire batch to Shop for HK$100 each. Delivery and acceptance are complete. No kits have yet left the group at the first reporting snapshot.

The controller asks whether the group can show the sum of the companies’ figures or needs a consolidation adjustment. Your job is not to change the agreed transfer price, reverse the legal sale or move stock. Show group inventory and batch gross profit, plus the adjustment from the entity totals.

There is no opening inventory, other trading, freight, processing, impairment or inventory loss. The kits all have the same cost and their net realisable value exceeds both stated cost amounts. Both companies use the same reporting date, currency and policies. Ignore income tax in this gross-profit exercise; real consolidated accounts can require deferred-tax adjustments. All amounts are HK dollars.

Round 2 · More information

At a second reporting snapshot later in the same period, Shop has sold and delivered 600 kits to unrelated customers for HK$150 each. All revenue-recognition conditions for those sales are met; there are no returns, discounts or further costs. The other 400 kits remain in Shop. Supply’s completed internal sale and its HK$20,000 entity gross profit are unchanged.

Shop now reports external revenue of HK$90,000, cost of sales of HK$60,000, gross profit of HK$30,000 and closing inventory of HK$40,000. These are updated cumulative figures for the same batch, not additional figures to add to the first snapshot. Prepare the second worksheet afresh. One colleague wants to eliminate the original HK$20,000 profit again in full; another says the external sale means no profit adjustment is needed.

Your decision

At the second snapshot, how much internal profit still needs elimination, and what group revenue, cost of sales, gross profit and inventory would you report?

First snapshot: no outside sale yet

The group still holds all 1,000 kits at its outside acquisition cost: 1,000 × HK$80 = HK$80,000. It has no external revenue or cost of sales and no batch gross profit. The HK$100,000 internal sale is removed from group revenue and cost of sales; the HK$20,000 profit embedded in stock is removed from inventory and added to cost of sales. Together these give revenue 0, cost of sales 0 and gross profit 0.

Second snapshot: only the unsold portion

The internal uplift is HK$100 − HK$80 = HK$20 per kit. Only 400 kits remain, so unrealised profit is 400 × HK$20 = HK$8,000. Group closing inventory is HK$40,000 − HK$8,000 = HK$32,000. The 600 kits sold outside carry group cost of 600 × HK$80 = HK$48,000. External revenue is HK$90,000, so group gross profit is HK$42,000.

Make the bridge visible

The entities’ combined revenue is HK$190,000 and cost of sales HK$140,000. Remove the full internal sale of HK$100,000 from both, then add HK$8,000 unrealised profit to cost of sales: group revenue HK$90,000; cost of sales HK$48,000. Combined entity gross profit HK$50,000 less HK$8,000 equals HK$42,000. Full internal revenue elimination and partial remaining-profit elimination are different steps.

Neither proposed shortcut works

Eliminating all HK$20,000 at the second snapshot would suppress HK$12,000 already earned through external sale of 600 kits. Eliminating nothing would leave HK$8,000 profit on stock still inside the group. The correct answer depends on how much of this batch remains, not on whether any external sale occurred.

Consolidation entries are worksheet adjustments, not a denial that the companies traded. They do not automatically cancel a valid invoice, change tax filings or undo the entity ledgers. Profit and cash are also different: collecting an internal invoice does not create an external group sale.

Instructor note

Give three minutes to the first worksheet and four to the second. Require quantities and per-unit amounts before journals. Compare the direct outside-customer calculation with the entity-to-group bridge. Ask why internal revenue is removed in full even after some stock is sold outside.

This differs from transfer-price negotiation: ownership and price are fixed and the sale is complete. The reveal changes stock remaining, not the reporting principle. No choice to ignore required consolidation is acceptable. Keep the discussion at batch gross profit; taxes, acquisition accounting, non-controlling interests and other balances would be needed for full financial statements.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All entities, transactions and amounts are fictional. The exercise covers one batch and gross profit before tax. Full consolidated accounts require additional balances and potentially deferred tax. Standard-setter and professional teaching sources are related guidance, not independent empirical studies.
