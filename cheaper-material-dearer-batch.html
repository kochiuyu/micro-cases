ACC-13 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Material variances · Cost control · Responsibility

Cheaper material. A dearer batch?

In the news

In annual results announced on 24 March 2026, Hong Kong-listed Nexteer Automotive attributed its gross-profit increase partly to manufacturing and material cost improvements, alongside increased volume.

That is a company-level explanation, not a finding about cheap inputs or defective supplies. The independent ceramics studio and all figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong ceramics studio has completed 500 acceptable mugs of one unchanged design. Its current, attainable clay standard allows 1 kg per acceptable mug, including normal waste, at HK$10 per kg. Required quality and safety checks are unchanged.

The purchaser tried an approved alternative clay at HK$9 per kg, with the same delivery and payment terms. The first report highlights the 10% lower price. It has not yet shown how much clay the batch consumed.

The owner must decide what to say about the purchase before discussing a repeat order. Recognise the lower unit price, or describe it as an overall materials-cost saving? No repeat order has been placed; the production record will be reviewed before commitment.

Round 2 · More information

The checked record shows 600 kg bought and consumed at HK$9/kg to complete those same 500 acceptable mugs. There was no opening clay, closing inventory, recoverable scrap or unfinished production in this batch. The 600 kg includes all clay lost in making it. There are no other clay charges or credits.

Two changes were documented. The alternative clay was firmer, though within the agreed acceptance specification, and required different preparation. Separately, the studio changed how it stored trimmings: some dried before they could be reused. The records do not establish how much of the extra consumption each change caused.

For a repeat batch, the original clay remains available at HK$10/kg and the alternative at HK$9/kg on otherwise unchanged terms. The owner can return to the original or arrange a controlled, approved trial of the alternative with the previous handling method restored. Existing review capacity can support the trial; neither route guarantees the old usage rate. Required output quality and safety cannot be reduced. Labour, energy and other costs still need separate evaluation.

Your decision

Reconcile the batch's clay cost into a price difference and a usage difference. Would you return to the original clay or condition another trial on better evidence? Explain what the figures establish and what they do not establish about responsibility.

Build one cost bridge

The allowance for the actual 500 acceptable mugs is 500 kg × HK$10 = HK$5,000. The actual 600 kg at the standard price would cost HK$6,000. Paying HK$9 for those 600 kg instead costs HK$5,400.

The extra 100 kg adds HK$1,000 at the standard price: an adverse usage variance. The HK$1 reduction on all 600 kg saves HK$600 against that price: a favourable price variance. Thus HK$5,000 + HK$1,000 − HK$600 = HK$5,400, or HK$400 adverse overall.

This bridge holds acceptable output constant. Do not calculate the price effect on only 500 kg and combine it with a usage effect priced at HK$10: that would omit the discount on the extra 100 kg.

An arithmetic split is not a blame split

The purchaser obtained a lower unit price, but this batch did not beat its clay-cost allowance. Both statements belong in the report. Neither the HK$600 nor HK$1,000 assigns causal responsibility to an individual.

Firmer clay and changed recovery practice are leads for investigation, not a measured allocation of the extra 100 kg. Review preparation, handling, waste and the standard with purchasing and production together. Do not call the supplier dishonest or the operators careless from these totals.

Use the report to design the next comparison

Returning to the original reduces uncertainty about the input, but this batch does not prove its next consumption will be 500 kg. A controlled trial keeps the lower-price opportunity open but needs a recorded method, comparable acceptable output and measured consumption, waste and other relevant costs.

Specify what evidence would justify renewal and who will review it. A cheaper kilogram is not automatically a cheaper mug. Clay cost per acceptable mug here is HK$10.80 against a HK$10 allowance; it is not total production cost, selling price or profit.

Instructor note

Ask for a precise first-round message rather than a premature supplier verdict. Reveal consumption and construct the three amounts in order: HK$5,000, HK$6,000, HK$5,400. Students should identify the favourable price effect and adverse usage effect without confusing either with causation.

Finish with a supplier recommendation conditional on evidence. Returning to the original and a controlled alternative trial can both be defended; no forecast of either result is supplied. Unlike ACC-05, output does not change: this case decomposes the remaining material cost, rather than flexing for more units. Unlike ACC-11, there is no support-cost allocation between orders. Keep mix/yield decomposition, disciplinary decisions and profit forecasts outside the exercise.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All studio, clay, prices, quantities, standards, handling records and choices are original fictional assumptions. Standard applies to the actual 500 acceptable mugs, includes normal waste and remains valid for comparison; no retrospective revision hides the result. Bought and consumed quantities coincide, so purchase-based and consumption-based price differences coincide here. In real cases with inventory movements, specify the price-variance convention before reconciling. The bridge is arithmetic, not a causal experiment or attribution of responsibility. Both clay types meet the stated acceptance specification; no safety or output-quality compromise is permitted. Firmer condition and changed storage are documented, but their separate consumption effects are unknown. Repeat quotes do not guarantee yield; other production and trial costs are not supplied as a profit comparison. The technical review uses existing available capacity, not an assertion that staff time is economically free.
