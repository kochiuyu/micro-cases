STR-01 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Strategic pricing · Nash equilibrium

Two cafés, one lunch rush

Hong Kong in the news · 3 June 2025

After opening 12 Hong Kong outlets in half a year, Luckin Coffee was offering a HK$15.90 first-order deal through its app. A rival café must decide whether matching a discount will attract customers or simply reduce its margin.

Your classroom decision

You run East Café. West Café has announced its regular lunch price. Your analyst expects East to earn HK$100 this lunch if you hold price and HK$130 if you offer a HK$5 discount. Choose before the lunch bell.

Your decision

What would you do if West’s price really were fixed? How much would your choice gain?

Debrief · Compare the choices

Against a regular price, discounting earns 130 instead of 100. Against a discount, it earns 80 instead of 60. The same comparisons hold for West. Both discount at the unique Nash equilibrium, earning 80 each.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–3: vote and compare reasons in pairs. Minutes 3–7: reveal the changed fact and vote again. Minutes 7–12: reconcile the answers. Both prefer 100 each, but either can gain by cutting when the other holds. Ask a pair that chose regular prices to identify the profitable deviation. Ask discounting pairs which assumed payoff would have to change to remove that incentive.
