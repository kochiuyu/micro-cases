STR-24 · Hong Kong · fictional firms · 15–20 minutes · Coopetition · Value creation and appropriation · Contribution governance

Compete in sales. Cooperate in the foundation?

Cooperation can have a deliberately narrow boundary

Research on cooperative rivalry examines how firms collaborate in some activities while continuing to compete in others. A shared resource can avoid duplication, but the participants still need to agree who contributes what and who can use the result. A saving for the group does not settle the distribution question.

The firms and quotes below are invented. The exercise designs a limited training proposal, not an executable agreement or legal clearance. Appropriate review would be needed before any real collaboration.

Your classroom decision

Three small Hong Kong document-design firms, A, B and C, compete for customers. Each wants the same optional twelve-month refresher in generic document formatting using public examples, not confidential customer work or proprietary production methods. Required training is already complete. Each can buy a separate package for HK$12,000. These fixed package quotes remain available, including after any new material is found.

A shared proposal costs HK$18,000: HK$9,000 to commission a reusable curriculum and HK$9,000 for the venue, independent facilitator, hosting, administration and appropriate project review. Each firm pays HK$6,000 for equal training access for the same number of participants. Equivalent learning scope, scheduling access and quality are stipulated for the shared and separate routes. Paid attendance time is separately funded and identical. Both routes are affordable; none depends on winning new sales.

The project would cover only this beginner training. An independent coordinator controls the agenda, approved materials, access and records. No selling prices, discounts, bids, customers, output plans, employee pay, hiring coordination or other commercially sensitive company information may be shared or coordinated. No exclusivity, customer allocation, no-poaching arrangement or restriction on independent learning is proposed. Staff attendance lists remain with the coordinator, not rival firms. Participants continue to choose prices, customers and commercial strategy independently.

One representative per firm can review training quality and access, but changes in scope require separate review and consent. Do not bargain away competition, privacy or access safeguards to lower cost. You are recommending a design for review; no agreement, payment or content transfer has occurred.

Round 2 · More information

A’s content audit finds an existing generic curriculum that it owns and has authority to license. It has been checked for the same learning scope and contains no customer information or commercially sensitive operating methods. The independent facilitator confirms that it can replace the HK$9,000 external curriculum without extra adaptation work. The HK$9,000 venue/facilitator/hosting package remains unchanged. A has no new delivery or maintenance work under this proposal; the coordinator’s package handles it.

A is willing to offer a non-exclusive twelve-month internal-training licence for HK$6,000 to the shared project. Ownership remains with A; the permission does not include resale, sublicensing or use beyond the agreed project. All three firms would pay an equal HK$5,000 into the project account. That account would pay HK$9,000 to outside providers and HK$6,000 to A for the licence. The fee is A’s proposed term, not an independently verified fair value.

B suggests instead paying only the HK$9,000 outside-provider bill, HK$3,000 each, with A providing the curriculum free. A has not consented to free use. The group can accept a mutually agreed licence subject to review, continue with the original external-curriculum proposal, or use separate packages. None is entitled to copy A’s material without permission. A would have no extra votes or access to rivals’ commercial information merely because it supplied the curriculum.

Your decision

Would you recommend the HK$6,000 curriculum licence, the external curriculum, or separate packages? Trace who pays and receives cash, then propose one contribution rule and one exit safeguard without changing the competition boundaries.

Debrief · Create common value without merging the businesses

The opening shared package costs HK$18,000 instead of HK$36,000 for three separate packages. It avoids duplicating a basic training resource under the supplied equivalent-quality assumptions. Each firm initially saves HK$6,000 of cash expenditure. This is not an estimate of extra sales, higher productivity or overall profit.

The shared activity is narrow; the firms remain rivals elsewhere. The stated safeguards are conditions, not negotiable options. A common platform is not permission to exchange commercial plans, and labelling a project “training” does not itself establish compliance.

Debrief · Cash contributions are not the whole contribution

Under A’s licence proposal, each firm pays HK$5,000. A also receives HK$6,000: its net project cash flow is an inflow of HK$1,000. B and C each have an outflow of HK$5,000. Across the three firms the net outflow is HK$9,000, matching payment to outside providers. The HK$6,000 licence payment is a transfer within that group, although it is a real cost to the project account.

Compared with the original shared proposal, the project account needs HK$3,000 less funding: HK$15,000 instead of HK$18,000. Outside-provider spending falls HK$9,000 because existing content replaces new commissioning. Those are different measures. Neither measures the full value of A’s rights, historical creation effort or competitive consequences.

Debrief · Negotiate permission, not presumed generosity

Equal cash payments need not imply equal contributions: A also permits a specified use of its existing material. Nor does contributing that material automatically justify any fee A asks. B and C can evaluate the proposed access against the external-curriculum fallback; A can evaluate the permission against keeping its rights unshared. A’s historical creation cost is not supplied and cannot be invented as a fairness calculation.

The HK$3,000-each suggestion is not a currently agreed option because it assumes free permission. A voluntary contribution, a fee or a contribution credit could work only with explicit consent and accounting. Do not count the HK$6,000 licence payment as another saving on top of the reduction in outside spending. If agreement fails, use an available fallback rather than copying material or expanding collaboration into commercial coordination.

Debrief · Make the narrow project governable

A practical proposal records the named material, permitted learners and uses, duration, contribution amounts, payment approval and delivery responsibilities. A discloses its supplier interest and does not unilaterally approve its own fee; the other firms retain genuine consent and access to the external-curriculum alternative. Equal training access and independent business decisions remain intact.

Agree how new jointly funded revisions may be used, who maintains them, how disputes are escalated and how withdrawal affects remaining access and prepaid services. No indefinite free right is implied by an initial licence. At expiry, any continuing use needs an agreed right; implementation should not promise to erase knowledge already learned. Obtain appropriate legal and competition review rather than treating this list as a valid contract template.

Instructor note

Start with a vote on shared versus separate training and ask students to mark the boundary around the common resource. Reveal the existing curriculum after they have explained the initial HK$18,000 saving. Draw two ledgers: the project account and the three participating firms together.

Ask who provides content, who pays cash and who receives the fee. Separate total value creation from the allocation of benefits. A strong answer can favour the licence, external content or independent packages with a stated governance reason; it cannot assume free permission or trade away competition safeguards. Do not request confidential business data, personal employment terms or actual contracts. No external reading is required.
