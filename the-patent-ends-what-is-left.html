STR-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · post-expiry competition · manufacturing know-how · licensing

The patent ends. What is left?

A patent and a production capability are different

Hong Kong’s Intellectual Property Department describes a maximum term of 20 years for a standard patent (R), subject to renewal. Hong Kong’s IP Trading Centre distinguishes the invention in a published application from associated know-how. The fictional case assumes one patent is ending; it does not ask whether anyone can use a real patent or process.

Your classroom decision

Bright Latch makes a small reusable display fitting in Hong Kong. Assume its patent expires tonight and does not restrict the rival’s planned production afterwards. A rival can already make the fitting from public information, but rejects many pieces on inspection. Its buyers take only passing units.

Bright Latch can keep its later-developed production routines to itself or sell the rival one year of confidential coaching. The coaching improves the rival’s yield immediately. The fees and losses below are incremental one-year contribution effects on Bright Latch versus a benchmark with no rival. Ignore tax and discounting. The coaching fee is net of delivery costs.

The rival gets HK$10 per accepted unit, spends HK$6 per attempted unit, and receives nothing for rejects. Use a batch of 100 attempts to compare its economics. Fixed costs and constraints beyond those stated are ignored.

Your decision

At what yield can the rival cover its variable cost, and should Bright Latch take the coaching offer now?

Round 2 · More information

The rival independently trials a new fixture. Without coaching, the rival now expects a 65% passing fraction and Bright Latch expects HK$75,000 of displaced contribution over the year instead of HK$25,000. The coaching outcome is unchanged.

Your decision

Would you take the same offer after the rival’s trial, and what is the lowest net fee you would accept under the revised forecast?

First decision: a working product is not yet a viable run

A batch costs HK$600. At 55% the rival makes HK$550, for a loss of HK$50; at 75% it makes HK$750, for a gain of HK$150. It covers variable cost at HK$600 ÷ HK$10 = 60 passing units, or 60%. Keeping the routines costs Bright Latch HK$25,000 relative to the benchmark; coaching nets HK$95,000 − HK$130,000 = −HK$35,000. Turn the offer down. The initial loss still allows some rival sales while the rival learns.

Second decision: license against the new alternative

At 65%, the rival earns HK$650 − HK$600 = HK$50 per 100 attempts without coaching. Bright Latch now compares −HK$75,000 without coaching with −HK$35,000 under the offer. Take the offer for a HK$40,000 improvement. The fee must at least cover the extra displacement of HK$130,000 − HK$75,000 = HK$55,000.

What to take away

The patent expiry opens an option for the rival. Yield determines when that option can support a business at the stated price. Value coaching against the rival’s likely independent progress, not against a world where the rival never appears.
