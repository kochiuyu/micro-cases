GVR-14 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Gifts and hospitality · Appearance of influence · Business-purpose approvals

A training visit. A supplier’s holiday?

Industry context

In the June 2026 issue of Hong Kong Engineer, an ICAC-authored article discussed accepting advantages and entertainment. It distinguished the legal categories and warned that lavish or frequent hospitality can create a sense of obligation in supplier dealings.

The supplier, invitation and policy below are original fiction. No actual business or person is accused. The case uses an explicit employer policy to make a practical decision; it does not ask students to decide whether a criminal offence occurred.

Your classroom decision

A buyer at a fictional Hong Kong packaging distributor needs to learn how to configure equipment already purchased. A supplier offers a two-day technical workshop. The agenda contains supervised setup practice on a demonstration machine and troubleshooting with an engineer. It is useful training, not a sales negotiation, and the purchase price and warranty are already settled.

A is an employer-funded working visit: the firm pays the complete HK$4,800 quote for travel, one standard hotel night, ordinary meals and training. B provides the identical agenda and working itinerary at the supplier’s expense, with no entertainment, personal extension or companion initially included. Neither has been booked or accepted. A separate remote option costs the employer HK$900 and covers the same explanations and live questions, but not supervised hands-on practice. All three fit the learning deadline. Remote attendance meets the basic training need; hands-on practice may be valuable, but no productivity or failure-rate improvement has been measured.

The fictional employer’s policy permits supplier-funded necessary working travel only after written approval from an independent owner, a recorded agenda and itemised costs, and confirmation of a legitimate purpose without an obligation to favour the supplier. The buyer cannot approve their own benefit. Companion costs and personal leisure are prohibited. Supplier funding is also barred while that supplier is a current or prospective bidder in any company tender, even a different product category. Employer-funded technical contact is not automatically barred, but needs a documented boundary from tender decisions and information.

At this point no tender is active and the supplier is not a known prospective bidder. The owner can fund A without reducing required operations, or consider B under the stated process. Another tender is scheduled six months away; no bidder list is established. Applicable law and any stricter obligation remain controlling. These are fictional policy conditions, not statutory gift-value limits or a universal legal safe harbour.

Round 2 · More information

Before approval, the supplier sends a revised package: it adds HK$3,200 for a companion’s travel and HK$2,400 for two leisure nights after the workshop. These HK$5,600 extras are personal benefits, not required for the agenda. Nothing has been accepted, paid or booked, and declining the extras creates no cancellation charge.

Separately, the company brings its other tender forward to next month. It concerns a different product category, and this supplier has now expressed an intention to bid. The buyer is due to contribute technical specifications, although not make the final award. The independence condition therefore matters before any bid scoring. Under the supplied policy, removing the holiday extras alone does not restore eligibility for supplier-funded travel while the firm is a prospective bidder.

The supplier confirms that the original working-only visit remains available for the employer to buy at HK$4,800, and remote training remains HK$900. Both still meet the deadline; there is no safety emergency or threatened withdrawal of warranty. The independent owner can authorise either learning route with a recorded agenda, employer payment and separation from the tender: no discussion of confidential specifications, competitor bids or favourable treatment during training. The owner will review the buyer’s tender role separately. There is no evidence of an explicit request for a favour or completed misconduct.

Your decision

Which learning arrangement would you authorise now: the employer-funded working visit or remote training? Specify what you decline, who approves and pays, and what you record about the approaching tender without making an unsupported accusation.

Separate the agenda from the benefit

The working visit and the personal extension are not one indivisible learning activity. Companion travel HK$3,200 plus leisure nights HK$2,400 equals HK$5,600 of extras. Reject those extras under the explicit policy. Their removal does not erase the supplier’s new prospective-bidder status, which independently bars its funding. Reclassifying the whole package as “training” would conceal this distinction.

Preserve useful learning without supplier funding

A costs the employer HK$4,800 and retains supervised practice. Remote training costs HK$900, saving HK$3,900 while foregoing that practice. Both satisfy the basic need and deadline. Choose A if the additional practice is worth that employer-funded difference; choose remote if it is not justified on the available evidence. Do not invent a guaranteed reduction in breakdowns to make A win. Neither choice accepts the personal extras or supplier-funded travel.

Permission is a process, not a personal assurance

The buyer should retain both versions of the invitation, declare the offered extras and bidder connection, and submit the agenda, itemisation and proposed employer payment for independent approval before accepting. Record the decision and the decline; do not erase the original offer or use a vague expense description. A later reimbursement is not the plan here: nothing has been accepted. The owner reviews the tender role and contact boundary separately rather than assuming that paying for travel solves every influence risk.

Policy and law are not identical

Hong Kong anti-bribery guidance distinguishes advantages from the narrower legal category of entertainment and addresses employer permission in private-sector business. Calling something hospitality does not classify every travel or accommodation benefit as entertainment. Employer policy can impose additional restrictions. The class need not pronounce on criminal liability: the stated policy already resolves which funding is unavailable. Any real concern should be taken through the appropriate compliance or legal route, not settled by a student vote or a belief that a small value is always safe.

Instructor note

Ask pairs to choose the initial funding route and list what must be documented. Reveal the revised package and prospective-bidder status separately: would removing only the companion and leisure costs be enough? Require a usable learning arrangement rather than “cancel everything”. Accept either employer-funded format with a reasoned HK$3,900 practice trade-off. Reject self-approval, concealment or the claim that no final-award authority means no influence. Do not ask students to diagnose a crime or infer the supplier’s intention from an invitation alone.
