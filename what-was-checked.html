GVR-09 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Governance · Review scope · Oversight

Externally checked. What was checked?

In the news · Evidence for oversight

On 19 December 2024, HKEX announced corporate-governance enhancements, including stronger provisions on reviewing and disclosing risk management and internal controls.

This historical announcement concerns listed-company governance. The privately owned printing company below is fictional; the exercise does not apply listing requirements to it.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A privately owned Hong Kong commercial printer has three branches and a small board. It recently introduced a shared administrative order system. The board wants evidence about order-related controls before choosing its next improvement priorities.

It can fund one additional review this quarter for HK$18,000. Both proposals finish before the same board meeting. Ordinary management monitoring and required checks continue; technical printing quality and safety arrangements are unchanged.

No fraud, security breach, injury or urgent unresolved incident is known. The decision is what evidence to commission, not whether to stop a known unsafe activity.

HK$18,000 reviewWork includedImportant limit
A · External specialistIn-depth testing of user-access approvals, changes and removals at all three branches last quarter, using records, selected transactions and exception follow-up.No review of order amendments or collection handovers.
B · Internal quality reviewerTrace selected orders through approval, amendment and customer collection across all three branches.Less depth in access testing; not an independent external review.

A’s specialist is competent, independent and unconflicted. B’s trained reviewer has relevant experience and did not design or operate these administrative controls. B’s price includes review time and workload cover, not extra unpaid work.

Neither proposal tests every transaction or guarantees future effectiveness. Neither is a full-company or financial-statement audit. Calling A an “external controls check” would not broaden its scope.

Your decision

Which review would you commission first, and why? State the board question it would answer and one important question it would leave open.

Round 2 · One fact changes

The board clarifies its immediate uncertainty: are order changes communicated consistently from acceptance through customer collection, especially at the newest branch? Routine access-monitoring reports exist, but their reliability has not been independently established.

The internal reviewer confirms a feasible plan within the budget: read-only access to histories and amendment logs, conversations with relevant staff at every branch, and records selected by the reviewer rather than branch managers.

The report will go directly to the board. Managers may correct factual errors but cannot suppress findings. It will state the selection method, period, exclusions and unresolved evidence gaps.

No findings exist yet. These arrangements support credible work; they do not turn the internal reviewer into a fully independent internal-audit function or prove the controls work.

Your decision

Does this change your first commission? Write two sentences stating what must be examined and what the report must not claim. Who owns follow-up, and what coverage remains for a later decision?

Choose the question before choosing the label

A can provide detailed evidence about how access was approved, changed and removed. Its external status does not make it evidence about amendment communication or customer collection.

B follows more of the order journey, with shallower access work. That is useful for the board’s clarified question, but wider coverage does not make it exhaustive or equivalent to an independent audit.

Turn the selected route into a review commission

For B, a possible two-sentence instruction is: “Trace selected orders and amendments through approval, branch communication and collection at all three branches, recording the period, selection approach, evidence and exceptions. Report directly to the board with exclusions and unresolved questions; do not conclude that all company controls are effective.”

This is an original classroom instruction, not professional engagement wording. B is now a defensible first choice because its work fits the clarified uncertainty and has practical evidence access.

A remains defensible if the board judges access uncertainty more consequential. In that case, commission the deeper access work explicitly and retain order-change handovers as an unanswered review priority.

Show what the selected review leaves open

User access: A tests deeply; B examines access only as part of selected order journeys. Order amendments: B follows them; A does not. Collection handover: B follows it; A does not.

These are differences in work, not scores of assurance. Three covered stages do not mean three times the confidence. Results must stay within the selected records, period and evidence.

Existing monitoring continues whichever review is commissioned. Its presence does not establish reliability, and neither this commission nor a later combined review should be treated as a guarantee against failure.

Keep action ownership after the report

Management must assign corrective actions to named people with dates. The board challenges the evidence, resources appropriate responses and checks follow-through. The reviewer reports and may verify agreed completion, rather than taking over daily operations.

The board cannot transfer oversight responsibility by buying a report. New urgent concerns require an appropriate immediate response, not a wait for the next quarterly review.

Both quotes cost the same. No fraud-loss probability, expected saving or financial winner is supplied. A wider combined engagement would require its own feasible scope and resources.

Instructor note

Ask students to write a commission, not select the most reassuring badge. Before the reveal, require the question answered and the gap left by each proposal. After it, require scope, report limits, a follow-up owner and a later coverage decision.

Do not treat B as free, fully independent internal audit or proof of control effectiveness. Its workload cover, evidence access and direct reporting are supplied safeguards, not findings. A is a competent legitimate alternative, not a compromised provider.

This is a review-depth and coverage decision, not a financial audit, legal opinion, procurement conflict or public allegation. HKEX and UK guidance provide context and general role distinctions, not requirements imposed on this private fictional company.
