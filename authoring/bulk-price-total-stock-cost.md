# A bulk discount. A lower total stocking cost?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 280 -->

OPS-24 · Hong Kong · fictional reseller · 15–20 minutes · Quantity discounts · Order quantity · Inventory carrying cost

![Two fictional stationery-reseller colleagues consider plain cartons and an unmarked order folder beside orderly shelves.](../bulk-price-total-stock-cost-scene.jpg)

## The unit price is one part of the stocking decision

Inventory teaching distinguishes the price paid for goods, the cost of placing orders and the cost of carrying stock. A quantity discount may apply to the whole order or only to units above a threshold. Those are different invoices even when the headline discounted price looks identical.

The reseller, quotes and quantities below are original fiction. Sources explain the method, not market prices or actual supplier behaviour. No external reading is needed.

## Opening decision

You manage replenishment of one standard, non-perishable stationery pack at a small Hong Kong reseller. Demand is exactly 1,200 packs per year, uniform throughout the year. Compare repeating one fixed lot size: 100, 200, 300 or 600 packs. Those are the only delivery-lot options offered for this exercise; do not mix sizes or split a purchased lot into staged deliveries. The business continues beyond the planning year.

The usable storage area holds at most 300 packs, including the new receipt. No overflow space is available, and access and safety clearances cannot be reduced. Each replenishment arrives in full exactly when the previous lot reaches zero. There are no shortages, damaged packs, safety stock, existing stock or supplier delays in this simplified model. Use average stock Q ÷ 2 for a repeating lot of Q packs, a smooth-demand teaching approximation.

Each order incurs HK$50 of relevant delivery and processing cost. Holding one pack for an entire year costs HK$10 in relevant carrying resources, so annual holding cost is average packs held × HK$10. This is an absolute per-pack rate, not 10% of purchase price; it stays fixed between quotes and includes the model’s relevant carrying costs. Do not add fixed rent or salaries again. All routes are funded without borrowing or taking money from protected commitments.

The initial written quote is all-units: for a lot below 300, every pack costs HK$10; for a lot of 300 or more, every pack costs HK$9. These prices apply to each order for the full year. Charges other than the stated ordering and holding costs are included; ignore tax and discounting. No purchase is authorised yet. The stated objective is minimum annual purchase plus ordering plus holding cost among feasible offered lot sizes.

## Evidence

| Opening assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual demand | 1,200 packs, uniform and certain |
| Available fixed lot sizes | 100, 200, 300 or 600 packs |
| Usable storage capacity | 300 packs maximum |
| Relevant ordering cost | HK$50 per delivery/order |
| Annual relevant holding cost | HK$10 per average pack held |
| Opening price schedule | Below 300: HK$10 each; 300 or more: HK$9 for every pack |

**Student question:** Which fixed lot size would you provisionally choose under the all-units quote? Compare annual purchase, ordering and holding costs, and explain why storage capacity must be checked before price.

## Round two

Before any order or contract is accepted, the supplier withdraws the mistaken all-units quote and sends the only valid replacement: on each order, the first 200 packs cost HK$10 each and only packs beyond 200 cost HK$9 each. The count resets on every order; annual purchases do not accumulate towards the threshold. The reseller can decline this supplier; for this exercise compare the same offered lot sizes under the replacement quote.

Demand, quality, ordering cost, holding cost, funding and the 300-pack physical capacity remain unchanged. The 600-pack lot is still unavailable operationally, despite being listed by the supplier. No extra storage, split delivery, rebate or new supplier quote is supplied. Do not apply the lower marginal price to the first 200 packs.

**Student question:** Which feasible lot size would you choose under the corrected incremental quote? Show the cost of one order before annualising it, and explain why the lower marginal price is not the price of every unit.

## Debrief · Keep three annual costs separate

For a feasible lot Q, orders per year = 1,200 ÷ Q. Annual purchase cost = orders per year × the invoice for one order. Annual ordering cost = orders per year × HK$50. Annual holding cost = Q ÷ 2 × HK$10. Compare their sum. The delivery lot is the peak stock, not the average; a 600-pack lot cannot fit merely because its average stock would be 300.

## Debrief · The opening all-units comparison

At 100 packs, twelve orders cost HK$12,000 to purchase, HK$600 to order and HK$500 to hold: HK$13,100. At 200, six orders cost HK$12,000 to purchase, HK$300 to order and HK$1,000 to hold: HK$13,300. At 300, four orders cost HK$10,800 to purchase, HK$200 to order and HK$1,500 to hold: HK$12,500.

The 300-pack lot is cheapest among the feasible offered sizes under the initial quote. Relative to 100, it saves HK$1,200 in purchases and HK$400 in orders, while adding HK$1,000 of holding cost: a net HK$600 saving. This is not a claim that 300 is optimal among every imaginable quantity.

## Debrief · Price the actual incremental invoice

A 300-pack order now costs 200 × HK$10 + 100 × HK$9 = HK$2,900, not HK$2,700. Four orders purchase 1,200 packs for HK$11,600. Add HK$200 ordering and HK$1,500 holding: HK$13,300. The effective purchase price is HK$2,900 ÷ 300, about HK$9.67, not HK$9.

The 100-pack route remains HK$13,100 and the 200-pack route remains HK$13,300. Choose 100 among the supplied feasible options for the stated objective. Compared with 100, the 300-pack route now saves only HK$400 in purchases and HK$400 in orders, less than its HK$1,000 extra holding cost. It is HK$200 more expensive. A lower price for the last pack does not prove a lower annual cost.

## Debrief · What would reopen the decision?

The conclusion depends on the stipulated cost and demand model, not a rule that small or large lots are always better. With real variable demand, delivery delays, expiry, changing prices or quantity-dependent carrying costs, revisit the model before adopting the result. Relevant costs must reflect resources that change with the choice; an allocated fixed cost is not automatically a saving.

The case compares supplier-offered quantities, not a continuous economic-order-quantity optimisation. A proposal for another lot size, different ordering process or safe storage expansion needs a real quote and its own costs. Do not invent free space or reject a physical constraint because a spreadsheet suggests a cheap lot. Confirm which units receive the discount before committing.

## Instructor note

Ask pairs to cross out infeasible quantities first. Then give three minutes to build three columns: purchase, order and hold. Take a provisional vote before the corrected quote. Require students to write the invoice for a 300-pack order before multiplying by four.

Debrief the HK$800 annual difference between the two 300-pack purchase totals and the switch from 300 to 100. Common errors include charging HK$9 to all packs under the corrected quote, applying a threshold to annual demand, using peak stock for holding cost, using average stock for capacity, and forgetting purchase cost. No calculus, external reading or assumptions about uncertain demand are required.
