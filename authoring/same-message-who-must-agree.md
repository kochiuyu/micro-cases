## A satisfied contact is not the whole buying centre

Organisational purchases can require agreement among people with different responsibilities. Technical enthusiasm, financial approval and authority to issue an order need not sit with the same person. Buying-centre research and guidance support checking those roles rather than assuming the most responsive contact controls the decision. All organisations, figures and approval rules below are fictional.

## Opening decision

You lead account sales for fictional Foshan packaging supplier LoopCrate. One electronics customer is considering returnable transport crates. Its operations manager has welcomed your demonstration and says the sample meets the handling specification. Purchasing and finance have not yet reviewed the proposal. You have no purchase order or permission to start delivery.

Your team has one funded half-day for the next account step. Choose A: repeat the technical demonstration with operations, or B: ask purchasing to coordinate a discussion of approval roles, required evidence and next steps. Either fits the same allowance; preparing the short shared evidence pack for B is included.

The unchanged offer requires CNY60,000 upfront, including crates, delivery, setup and training. No discount or different technical specification is proposed. The seller has estimated monthly cash savings, but the customer has not yet checked those estimates. Operations enthusiasm therefore establishes neither an approved business case nor a signed commitment.

Purchasing has said that any further account meeting must be arranged through its normal channel. Operations offers another demonstration slot tomorrow. The salesperson worries that asking about approvals will interrupt momentum. You must decide what the next step should establish, not merely which contact seems most interested.

## Round two

Purchasing supplies the customer’s actual process for this exercise. Operations must sign technical acceptance. Finance must validate the cash assumptions and approve the budget. Purchasing checks supplier eligibility and commercial terms, then issues the purchase order only after both approvals. No one of these three steps substitutes for the others.

Operations confirms the tested sample meets the specification but has not signed the acceptance record. Purchasing has not completed its supplier and terms checks. Finance has not approved the budget. There is no hidden fourth approval in this exercise, but a technical pass alone cannot authorise this order.

The customer’s finance analyst now supplies planning estimates: CNY8,000 per month of avoided disposable-packaging purchases, less CNY3,000 per month for crate collection, cleaning and replacements. Both are incremental cash flows at the planned volume. There are no staff-time savings, inventory releases or other cash benefits included.

For this fictional screening rule, finance requires simple payback of at most 12 months and documented support for the cash estimates. The CNY60,000 is the entire initial cash outlay. The outlay is paid at completed installation; net savings begin immediately and accrue evenly; no additional upfront costs, taxes, financing effects or working-capital changes enter this simplified screen.

The analyst also asks for a downside calculation using net savings of CNY4,000 per month. Neither scenario is a guaranteed outcome. Planned volume and collection costs still need documented validation. Passing the arithmetic screen is necessary, but finance retains budget approval and purchasing retains its checks and order authority.

**Student question:** Prepare a shared approval map and calculate base-case and downside payback. What should each function receive next, in what sequence, and what must remain unclaimed? Explain why three contradictory sales messages would not solve this account’s problem.

## Debrief · Map roles before chasing enthusiasm

B better addresses the known information gap. Operations can provide technical evidence and acceptance, but cannot alone authorise spending or an order. Ask purchasing who signs each approval, what evidence each needs and how completion is recorded. Do not infer authority from job title, responsiveness or enthusiasm in a demonstration.

A could be useful if a technical question remained unresolved. Here the sample already passes the stated specification; repeating it does not resolve finance’s cash evidence or purchasing’s eligibility and terms checks. Preserve the operations relationship while using the customer’s authorised coordination channel, not a bypass to pressure finance.

## Debrief · One offer, different evidence

Use one consistent offer and assumption sheet. Operations receives the specification, test record and acceptance form. Finance receives the initial outlay, supported cash-flow assumptions, payback and downside calculation. Purchasing receives the supplier documents, proposed terms and approval status. Each function sees the dependencies rather than an isolated promise designed only to please it.

Purchasing can coordinate parallel evidence collection: operations documents its technical acceptance while finance checks cash assumptions and purchasing reviews supplier eligibility and terms. The final order waits for both approvals and completed purchasing checks. A meeting, a favourable comment or a circulated proposal is not itself an approval.

## Debrief · Calculate the financial gate

Base-case net cash savings are CNY8,000 minus CNY3,000 = CNY5,000 per month. Simple payback is CNY60,000 / CNY5,000 = 12 months. Equality satisfies the supplied at-most-12-month screen. That result is conditional on the estimates and timing; it does not establish that finance has validated them or approved the budget.

At CNY4,000 net savings per month, payback is CNY60,000 / CNY4,000 = 15 months, failing this screen. A seller should disclose this sensitivity rather than hide collection costs or promise a guaranteed 12-month recovery. Ask which volume and cost evidence would let the customer judge the plausible range.

## Debrief · Do not confuse a screen with a full valuation

Simple payback measures time to recover the initial outlay from the specified cash savings. This simplified rule ignores the time value of money and cash flows after recovery. It neither ranks all possible investments nor establishes profitability, environmental superiority or a legally binding commitment. The customer’s stated gate is the task here.

If validated savings do not support the screen, acknowledge the shortfall and ask whether the customer wishes to reconsider the project or its authorised criteria. Do not silently change the threshold, price, specification or forecast. No production or delivery starts without the required order and agreed implementation arrangements.

## Debrief · Advance a coordinated decision

Close the next meeting with named functional owners, evidence requests and an agreed review date, all through purchasing. Record technical acceptance, finance approval and procurement completion separately. A useful next-stage measure is a completed evidence review, not how often the operations contact replies or how many demonstrations the seller runs.

The appropriate message remains the same proposal, with transparent evidence relevant to each role. Connecting the roles helps the customer make a coherent decision; it does not guarantee a sale or justify manipulating internal disagreements. The customer may still decline even after receiving a complete and accurate pack.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: choose the next contact step and list unknown authorities. Minutes 4–8: draw the three-function approval map. Minutes 8–12: calculate 12-month and 15-month payback. Minutes 12–18: assemble the shared pack and specify what cannot yet be promised. Keep the role sequence central rather than turning the case into a valuation exercise.

MKT-07 distinguishes a product’s user from an individual gift buyer; MKT-26 clarifies a customer value calculation. Here the same organisational account needs coordinated technical, financial and purchasing approvals. All financial benefits are explicitly cash savings; the central mistake is treating one satisfied participant as the whole buying centre.
