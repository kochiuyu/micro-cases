# A strong threat. A costly commitment?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 402 -->

STR-34 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Credible commitment · Sequential decisions · Irreversible capacity

![Two adult managers in a fictional Hong Kong protective-cover workshop compare the existing work area with a planned additional production bay and a simple branching decision diagram.](../strong-threat-costly-commitment-scene.jpg)

## A response must make sense when its turn arrives

In a sequential decision, a statement about future action is not enough. Ask whether the decision-maker would still choose that action when the moment arrives. A capacity investment can change those incentives, but its own cost can outweigh the strategic benefit. The following small game is wholly fictional, not a prediction of rival behaviour.

## Opening decision

You manage fictional HarbourFit, a Hong Kong supplier of reusable protective covers for exhibition equipment. For one bounded new niche programme, choose F: retain flexible existing capacity, or E: install an additional specialised production bay before a potential rival decides whether to enter. Existing contracts, staffing protections and product quality remain unaffected.

The sequence is fixed: HarbourFit chooses capacity; the rival observes the installed capacity without cost and chooses enter or stay out; after entry, HarbourFit chooses moderate or large output. Both firms know the supplied payoffs and maximise their own programme cash surplus. There is no repeated-game reputation or private information.

Both output levels are feasible under either capacity choice. Expansion lowers operating costs sufficiently to change HarbourFit’s preferred output after entry. The table already includes operating receipts, ongoing costs and the rival’s entry costs. Only HarbourFit’s one-time expansion cost K is omitted. Flexible capacity requires no new fixed outlay.

All table amounts are HK$100,000 units on a common present-value basis. Unchanged business is excluded; no other cash flows, salvage value, financing or tax effects enter this classroom model. Before knowing K, you can diagnose credibility but cannot approve expansion. No capacity investment has yet occurred.

## Evidence

| Outcome | F | E, before K |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rival stays out | 8 / 0 | 10 / 0 |
| Entry; moderate output | 4 / 2 | 5 / 2 |
| Entry; large output | 1 / −1 | 7 / −1 |

**Student question:** Work backwards under F and E. Would a promise to choose large output after entry be credible in each case? Predict the rival’s response under the supplied assumptions, and identify the missing information needed before approving expansion.

## Round two

Before either option is chosen, a complete installation quotation establishes K = 7 units, or HK$700,000. It is paid if E is chosen and is wholly unrecoverable, whether the rival enters or stays out. No refund, resale or redeployment value exists. Deduct K from every HarbourFit payoff in the E column.

The operating table is otherwise unchanged. Both options remain fundable; no deadline forces an investment. These are independent production choices, not communication or agreement with a rival. No below-cost pricing, customer exclusion, sabotage or regulatory evasion is proposed. The abstract payoff table does not establish legal clearance for any real competitive conduct.

For this exercise, the only demand and operating outcomes are those supplied. If actual demand or cost assumptions proved wrong, the installed bay’s cost would still be unrecoverable. No probabilities for additional scenarios are provided; do not invent an expected-value forecast beyond this bounded model.

**Student question:** Choose F or E before payment, including K = 7. Explain why a credible commitment can still be unattractive. Find the expansion-cost threshold that changes the choice, holding all operating payoffs and the observed sequence fixed.

## Debrief · Start with the last decision

Under F, after entry HarbourFit prefers moderate output: 4 exceeds 1. A verbal claim that it will choose large output is therefore not credible in this one-shot model. The rival anticipates moderate output, compares entry payoff 2 with staying out at 0, and enters. HarbourFit receives 4 units.

Under E, HarbourFit prefers large output after entry: 7 exceeds 5 before K. Subtracting the same already-paid K from both outcomes leaves that ranking unchanged. The rival anticipates a payoff of −1 from entry and chooses 0 by staying out. The installed capacity changes incentives; words alone do not.

## Debrief · Count the commitment cost before choosing

With K = 7, expansion followed by no entry gives HarbourFit 10 − 7 = 3 units, or HK$300,000. Flexible capacity followed by entry gives 4 units, or HK$400,000. Choose F in this model. Preventing entry is not the objective; programme value after all relevant costs is.

For completeness, expansion’s entry branches give 5 − 7 = −2 with moderate output and 7 − 7 = 0 with large output. Large output remains preferred if entry occurs. Credibility survives the cost correction, but the initial investment loses to flexibility. Do not deduct K from the rival’s payoffs.

## Debrief · Separate two decision dates

Before installation, K is avoidable and must be included when comparing F with E. After installation, K cannot be recovered and does not change the ranking between moderate and large output. These statements are consistent: the same payment matters for choosing a commitment but not for undoing an already-paid cost.

An expensive sunk payment by itself does not make a response credible. Here the specified production technology changes the operating comparison from 4 versus 1 to 5 versus 7. Without that change, paying a large irreversible bill would not magically make the previously inferior response rational.

## Debrief · Find the threshold

Expansion yields 10 − K after the rival stays out; flexibility yields 4 after entry. They tie when K = 6, or HK$600,000. Below 6, expansion wins; above 6, flexibility wins. At exactly 6 either is optimal within this model. This comparison assumes K does not change the operating payoffs.

The threshold is conditional, not a recommended real investment price. Check whether capacity is actually installed, observed and difficult to reverse, whether it changes later incentives, and whether the payoff estimates are credible. If those assumptions fail, the rival’s predicted response may change even if the quotation remains the same.

## Debrief · Keep the model within its limits

The rival is not assumed frightened or irrational: it responds to the supplied future incentives. There is no negotiation, coordination or commitment by the rival. Observability is explicitly supplied, not inferred from a press announcement. In a real setting, uncertain information, additional choices and competitive rules require separate analysis.

A mathematical entry-deterrence result is not legal or ethical approval of a commercial tactic. Competition guidance distinguishes vigorous competition from potentially abusive conduct in relevant circumstances. This case teaches sequential reasoning and cost discipline, not a strategy for forcing actual businesses out or avoiding competition obligations.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–5: solve each post-entry output choice, then the rival’s entry choice. Minutes 5–10: reveal K and compare net programme values. Minutes 10–15: calculate the threshold and explain the two decision dates. Ask why an unsupported verbal threat cannot replace the production technology in the table.

STR-01 uses simultaneous price choices; STR-08 compares strategic offers across customer scenarios without probabilities. This case instead fixes an observed sequence and solves later incentives before comparing an irreversible commitment’s initial cost. Do not average entry and no-entry branches: entry is a strategic choice here, not an assigned random event.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All firms, technology, timing and payoffs fictional. Units HK$100,000; common present values. F has no new fixed outlay. E costs K=7, un recoverable with no salvage/redeployment, deducted from every incumbent E outcome, never rival. Operating figures include all other programme cash flows, rival entry cost and ongoing costs. No tax/financing/unchanged-business effects. Complete information, free actual observation, one-shot sequential choices and own-payoff maximisation are stipulated. No real market prediction or legal clearance. F: moderate 4 exceeds large 1, entry 2 exceeds out 0, incumbent 4. E: large 7 exceeds moderate 5 regardless common K, rival out 0 exceeds entry−1, incumbent 10−7=3. E entry net−2 or 0. Threshold K 6. Real-world demand uncertainty is not assigned probabilities. All choices remain fund able and existing obligations protected.
