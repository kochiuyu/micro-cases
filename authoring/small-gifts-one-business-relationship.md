# Small gifts. One business relationship?

GVR-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Gift register · Aggregation control · Independent review

![Two colleagues check an open register beside several blank parcels in a service office.](../small-gifts-one-business-relationship-scene.jpg)

## A recorded benefit may still need review

Hong Kong’s Independent Commission Against Corruption discusses why repeated or excessive benefits can create potential conflicts. Some published corporate policies pair declarations with independent approval and records. The rolling threshold and all gift entries below are fictional, not a legal safe harbour or a finding of misconduct.

## Opening decision

Lau reviews a fictional Hong Kong distributor’s sample gift register on Day 90. One employee entered every gift received and the amounts shown were recorded correctly. No single entry was flagged by the register’s HK$500 transaction alert. There is no tender in this exercise.

The board also requires independent review when gifts to the same recipient from the same business group total at least HK$700 in the last 30 calendar days, Days 61 through 90 inclusive. Use verified business-group links only. Keep older entries for the audit trail but exclude them from this window. The rule orders a review, not an automatic misconduct finding.

Each row is a gift item, not a duplicate or a share of a meal. All five gifts went to the same employee, and the host codes are stable. Initially the documented groups of hosts A, B and C are separate. Hold the quantities and stated values fixed.

## Evidence

| Fictional register | Host | Gift value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 60 | A | HK$260 |
| Day 63 | A | HK$230 |
| Day 71 | B | HK$250 |
| Day 82 | A | HK$240 |
| Day 90 | C | HK$300 |

**Student question:** Which business group triggers independent review on current evidence? Explain why all entries can be recorded correctly and the one-row alert still cannot answer the group-policy question.

## Round two

The register owner receives verified records that A and B belonged to the same business group throughout Days 61–90. C remains separate. One manager proposes simply changing B’s label going forward, without recomputing the current review.

**Student question:** Recompute the Day 90 totals. Who should review the affected entries, and how should Lau correct the register without erasing its original records?

## Debrief · Apply the window and group key

Day 60 is excluded. Initially A has HK$230 + HK$240 = HK$470, B has HK$250 and C has HK$300. None reaches HK$700, and the HK$500 row alert does not address a sequence of sub-threshold gifts.

## Debrief · Correct the key and rerun the check

With the verified link, A and B together total HK$470 + HK$250 = HK$720. That is HK$20 above the review threshold. C still totals HK$300. Keep the original host codes, add the dated group mapping and rerun the affected window rather than rewriting the raw ledger or changing only future rows.

## Debrief · Review is not a verdict

Refer the gifts to an independent reviewer rather than the employee or hosts. Check the business context, original approval and whether a conflict needs action, while preserving evidence and avoiding unsupported allegations. The class threshold is not legal advice.

## Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes reading, 4 to apply the window, 4 to repair the grouping, and 5 to design a review. Ask why aggregating all three hosts or including Day 60 would fail the policy, and what to do with unverified links.
