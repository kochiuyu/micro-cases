## In the news

On 17 September 2026, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority announced a Base Rate of 4.25%. Its release explains the formula and the rate’s role in the Discount Window. That monetary-policy rate is not a supplied investment-appraisal rate for every business.

The company, projects, probabilities and rates below are fictional classroom assumptions, unrelated to any announced investment.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong business-services company is considering two small one-year projects. A expands its familiar document-support service; B launches a new discretionary event-design service. Each requires HK$100,000 now and forecasts HK$112,000 of net cash at the end of one year. Both are operationally feasible, lawful and affordable; existing commitments remain funded. Neither has been approved.

The company normally screens projects at an 8% annual cost of capital, estimated for its established activities and current financing mix. Its analyst proposes using that rate for both projects. A second analyst wants a project-risk review before treating the two appraisals as equivalent.

For this teaching comparison, all amounts are nominal HK dollars, after the project’s operating costs, taxes and working-capital movements. There are no other receipts, residual values or later obligations. Financing cash flows are excluded because the discount rate accounts for capital providers’ required return. The firm’s financing mix will not change. Choose how to brief the approval meeting, not an actual financial transaction.

## Evidence

| Initial forecast | A: document support | B: event design |
|---|---:|---:|
| Outlay now | HK$100,000 | HK$100,000 |
| Expected net cash in one year | HK$112,000 | HK$112,000 |
| Company screening rate | 8% | 8% |

**Student question:** Would you present both projects as equally attractive at the company’s 8% rate, or make that comparison provisional pending a project-risk review? Calculate the initial net present value and state what the common rate assumes.

## Round two

The analyst supplies a deliberately simplified two-state forecast. The same economy-wide state affects both projects. A downturn has probability 50% and a stronger economy 50%. A produces HK$108,000 in the downturn and HK$116,000 in the stronger economy. B produces HK$84,000 and HK$140,000 respectively. These are the complete one-year net cash amounts under the same definitions as the initial forecast; they are invented assumptions, not historical estimates.

Both still have expected cash of HK$112,000. B’s cash moves much more with the shared economic state. The team has not estimated a market beta, comparable-project required return or definitive project-specific cost of capital. The two states alone cannot supply one.

For discussion, test A at 6% and 8%, and B at 10% and 14%. These are illustrative sensitivity rates, not estimated confidence intervals, promised financing terms or established fair rates. Apply them to the expected cash, without a separate risk haircut. A funded review of comparable activities and their financing can be completed before approval; no investment deadline is lost by reviewing. The company may proceed only after its appraisal is justified, or decline either project.

**Student question:** What would you now put in the approval briefing? Show the sensitivity results, distinguish a test rate from an estimated required return, and explain what evidence is needed before accepting or rejecting B.

## An identical mean is not identical risk

A’s expected cash is 0.5 × HK$108,000 + 0.5 × HK$116,000 = HK$112,000. B’s is 0.5 × HK$84,000 + 0.5 × HK$140,000 = HK$112,000. Their equal averages conceal different exposure to the same economic state. B is not riskier merely because it is new; the supplied cash pattern identifies the concern.

## Make the rate assumption visible

Net present value (NPV) is discounted expected cash less the outlay. At 8%, each gives HK$112,000 / 1.08 − HK$100,000 = about HK$3,704. This is a conditional valuation, not proof that each project deserves the same rate. A company-average rate is a starting benchmark whose risk and financing assumptions need to fit the project. Market-related risk matters here; not every source of operational uncertainty mechanically earns a larger premium.

## Test the boundary, then gather evidence

For A, NPV is about HK$5,660 at 6% and HK$3,704 at 8%. For B, it is about HK$1,818 at 10% and −HK$1,754 at 14%. Either project’s one-year break-even discount rate is 12%: HK$112,000 / HK$100,000 − 1. The B test range crosses that boundary. It does not prove that the correct rate exceeds 12%.

Keep the 8% calculation as an explicitly provisional benchmark, and add the risk-sensitive review rather than erase it. Compare economically similar activities, their exposure to market conditions and financing assumptions. B could remain worthwhile at a justified rate below 12%; it could fail at a justified rate above it. Do not choose a rate merely to obtain the preferred answer. No market data supplied here settles that estimate.

## Instructor note

Begin with the equal NPVs, then ask what changes when the economic-state table is revealed. The lesson is selecting and defending an appraisal rate, not guessing a risk premium from two outcomes. Both positive expected cash and adequate funding are insufficient to establish value at the appropriate opportunity cost.

FIN-08 compares project timing at a supplied rate; FIN-14 keeps nominal cash and rates consistent. Here currency, timing and financing mix are held fixed while project business risk differs. Students may retain the company rate as a screening reference, but should not present it as validated for B. This is a bounded corporate-finance model, not personal investment advice or a recommendation about an actual company.
