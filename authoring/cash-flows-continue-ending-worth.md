# Cash flows continue. How much is the ending worth?

FIN-26 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Terminal value · Sustaining investment · Project net present value

![A workshop manager and financial analyst compare two plain forecast sheets beside refurbishment equipment.](../cash-flows-continue-ending-worth-scene.jpg)

## The end of the forecast is not the end of the business

On 29 April 2026, HKICPA published teaching guidance explaining how a terminal value represents cash flows beyond an explicit forecast period and must be discounted from its own date. The arithmetic is compact, but the assumed cash flow must be sustainable.

The workshop and offers below are fictional. This is a project-appraisal exercise, not an investment recommendation. The discount rate, tax basis and growth assumption are supplied, not estimated from markets.

## Opening decision

You review a fictional equipment-refurbishment workshop project for a Hong Kong business. The project costs HK$900,000 today and produces net free cash flow of HK$100,000 at each of the next three year-ends. Those three forecasts already deduct all operating costs, taxes, working-capital needs and investment required during years 1–3.

Two appraisals differ only in what happens after year 3. Continue values the post-year-3 operation as a flat annual cash-flow stream beginning at the end of year 4. Exit uses a firm, counterparty-secured option to sell the entire operation at the end of year 3 for net HK$800,000. This hypothetical option remains available on the same terms in both rounds. The net proceeds already cover taxes, transaction costs and all required handover expenditure. Selling transfers all later receipts and obligations to the buyer; do not add a perpetuity to exit proceeds.

Use the supplied consistent 10% annual discount rate for both alternatives. All cash flows are nominal HK dollars, after tax and before financing. Growth is 0%, below 10%. Continuing is feasible indefinitely only with recurring asset replacement and all required upkeep. The Continue analyst labels HK$120,000 as annual free cash flow from year 4 onward, but its cost schedule has not yet been reconciled. Neither report has been approved.

## Evidence

| Common or terminal input | Amount / timing |
| --- | --- |
| Initial outlay | 900,000 today |
| Explicit cash flow | 100,000 at each year-end 1, 2 and 3 |
| Continue: stated annual cash flow | 120,000 from year-end 4 forever; growth 0% |
| Exit: net sale proceeds | 800,000 at year-end 3, in addition to year-3 cash flow |
| Year-3 present-value factor | 1 ÷ 1.10³ = 0.751315 |
| PV of the three 100,000 cash flows | 248,685, rounded |

**Student question:** Which appraisal presently supports the higher project value, and which post-year-3 assumption would you require the team to substantiate before approving it?

## Round two

The reconciled engineering schedule finds that the Continue estimate omitted HK$50,000 of sustaining expenditure every year from year 4 onward. This is actual recurring cash spending, after any tax effects, required to keep the stated service capacity and receipts. It is not depreciation, optional expansion or a one-off catch-up bill. The first payment is at the end of year 4 alongside that year’s receipts. It stays flat in nominal terms in this teaching model.

There are no offsetting receipts or cost reductions. All other cash-flow inputs remain unchanged. The years 1–3 forecasts already include their required expenditure, so do not deduct this amount there. The Exit option’s net HK$800,000 is unchanged and includes its handover obligations; the buyer bears later sustaining expenditure. The business accepts only projects with positive net present value. It can decline the project without penalty today.

**Student question:** After correcting the sustaining expenditure, which terminal route has the higher value, and should the business approve this HK$900,000 project under its stated rule?

## Put the ending at the right date

For the flat stream, terminal value at the end of year 3 is the first year-4 cash flow divided by 10%: HK$120,000 ÷ 0.10 = HK$1,200,000. Its value today is HK$1,200,000 × 0.751315 ≈ HK$901,578. Add the common HK$248,685 explicit-period value: about HK$1,150,263. Less the HK$900,000 outlay gives initial Continue NPV of HK$250,263.

## An exit is an alternative, not an extra

Exit contributes HK$800,000 ÷ 1.10³ ≈ HK$601,052 today. Total project value is about HK$849,737, with NPV of −HK$50,263. On the initial inputs Continue is higher, but only if its stated post-forecast cash is genuinely available after sustaining the operation. The supplied secured exit is a separate alternative, not a market multiple or an extra asset added to continuation value.

## Correct the sustainable cash flow

Annual continuation cash falls to HK$120,000 − HK$50,000 = HK$70,000. Corrected terminal value is HK$700,000 at year 3 and about HK$525,920 today. Total Continue value is HK$774,606 and NPV −HK$125,394. The missing expense reduced present value by HK$50,000 ÷ 0.10 ÷ 1.10³ ≈ HK$375,657.

## A better route can still be a bad project

Exit is now worth about HK$75,131 more than Continue today, but both NPVs are negative. Under the supplied positive-NPV rule, decline the HK$900,000 project. Do not approve it merely because Exit is the less costly terminal route. The break-even initial outlay using the better route is about HK$849,737; positive NPV requires a lower outlay, all else unchanged.

## Instructor note

Give three minutes to compare the initial appraisals and identify the unverified cost schedule. Supply the reveal and allow four minutes to correct the terminal value. Finish by distinguishing the best terminal route from project acceptance. Use full precision until rounding to the nearest dollar.

The lesson holds discount-rate selection and inflation consistency fixed, unlike FIN-14 and FIN-15. The 0% growth stream is a deliberately narrow assumption, not evidence that real assets last forever without renewal. Students must not omit required maintenance or count both exit and continuation. The secured exit is an invented contractual assumption, not a generic claim that sale forecasts are risk-free.
