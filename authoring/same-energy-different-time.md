# The same electricity. A different time?

SUS-12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Load shifting · Energy use · Emissions intensity

## In the news

On 5 March 2026, CLP Power announced completion of its smart-meter rollout and described how hourly usage information can help customers manage electricity. Its release also discussed demand-response programmes and a residential electric-vehicle time-of-use tariff.

The facility, rates and emissions profiles below are entirely fictional. They are not CLP tariffs, measured Hong Kong hourly emissions, or an assertion that this business qualifies for a utility programme.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A small events facility charges a bank of non-emergency equipment batteries for the following day. Its existing evening routine uses 100 kWh at the meter and costs HK$150 per operating day. The team can choose one of two assessed replacement schedules for a short trial.

A moves the same charging task overnight. B charges in the afternoon and removes an unnecessary top-up. A qualified assessment confirms that both meet identical next-day equipment needs and the required reserve, using approved controls and safety procedures. Essential services, battery life and staff working conditions are unchanged. B does not create a later catch-up charge.

Both trials are affordable. No new equipment is needed. Each includes HK$20 per day of additional paid coordination; no extra travel, fuel use or other operating effect is assumed. Use the complete incremental daily cash and metered-energy figures below, not an actual utility bill.

## Evidence

| Daily task | Existing evening | A · Overnight shift | B · Afternoon, less top-up |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Metered electricity | 100 kWh | 100 kWh | 80 kWh |
| Fictional energy rate | HK$1.50/kWh | HK$0.80/kWh | HK$1.40/kWh |
| Extra coordination | HK$0 | HK$20 | HK$20 |
| Total task cost | HK$150 | HK$100 | HK$132 |

Charging losses are included. Fixed charges and other facility loads are unchanged; no additional demand charge, rebate or fee. No emissions factor is yet supplied.

**Student question:** Which trial would you choose, A or B? Separate daily cash cost from electricity use, and say what further evidence you would need before describing either as lower-carbon.

## Round two

The analyst supplies the following invented time profile, checked for internal consistency within this exercise. It is not a forecast or measurement of a real electricity grid. For this comparison, treat each factor as constant throughout its charging window and apply it to the electricity at the facility meter.

| Window | Modelled electricity CO₂ factor |
| --- | --- |
| Existing evening | 0.50 kg CO₂/kWh |
| A · Overnight | 0.60 kg CO₂/kWh |
| B · Afternoon | 0.20 kg CO₂/kWh |

Multiply task kWh by the matching factor to compare attributed electricity emissions. These are average-factor allocations, not marginal estimates of power-station changes caused by this small trial. All energy, cost and service assumptions remain unchanged. The team must choose the priority; no carbon price or mandatory ranking has been set.

**Student question:** Keep or change your trial choice? Calculate attributed electricity emissions for the existing routine, A and B, then explain the trade-off your recommendation accepts without claiming measured grid-wide savings.

## Cost, energy and timing are different

A costs 100 × HK$0.80 + HK$20 = HK$100 per day, saving HK$50 against the existing task. It still uses 100 kWh. Moving consumption to a cheaper window is not itself a reduction in electricity use.

B costs 80 × HK$1.40 + HK$20 = HK$132, saving HK$18 while using 20 kWh less. The supplied assessment preserves the required service; this is removal of an unnecessary top-up, not a recommendation to undercharge equipment.

## Apply the factor for the chosen window

The existing task is allocated 100 × 0.50 = 50 kg CO₂. A is allocated 100 × 0.60 = 60 kg: ten more despite its lower cost. B is allocated 80 × 0.20 = 16 kg: 34 less than the existing routine.

B costs HK$32 more than A but has 44 kg less attributed electricity CO₂ in this model. Money, kWh and kg CO₂ answer different questions; no single column automatically determines the decision.

## Separate using less from shifting time

For a simple decomposition, shifting the original 100 kWh to the afternoon factor would allocate 20 kg CO₂. Reducing that afternoon task to 80 kWh brings it to 16 kg. Thus B combines a different time profile with lower use; do not attribute the entire 34 kg difference to the 20 kWh reduction.

This decomposition is arithmetic within the supplied profile, not a causal experiment. Nor does the case establish that afternoons are cleaner than nights in Hong Kong or elsewhere. Real profiles change with location, generation and conditions.

## Choose a trial and bound the claim

A is defensible if the facility prioritises the lowest daily cash cost and explicitly accepts higher attributed electricity emissions under the supplied model. B is defensible if lower use and lower attributed emissions justify HK$32 more per day than A. Both preserve service; neither is unaffordable.

For a real trial, verify meter readings, tariff eligibility and complete charges, safe operations, any rebound use and the relevant emissions-factor method. Keep a cost result separate from an energy result and an emissions estimate. A lower average-factor allocation is not proof of avoided grid emissions, lifecycle benefit or compliance with a corporate reporting standard.

## Instructor note

Use three minutes for the initial cost-versus-kWh vote, four for the time-factor reveal, and four for a recommendation naming its objective. Ask groups to label every result with HK$, kWh or kg CO₂. Keep the two-part decomposition as optional depth.

This is temporal energy management, not GVR-04’s intensity-versus-total advertising claim or SUS-08’s landlord–tenant sharing of upgrade savings. No installation, tariff application or unsafe operating change is authorised by the case. The profiles are invented to expose a possible conflict between objectives, not sourced hourly Hong Kong data. Do not replace the stated average factors with an assumed universal off-peak advantage.
