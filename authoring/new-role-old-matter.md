# A new adviser. An old matter?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 477 -->

GVR-40 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Post-public employment · Role boundaries · Continuing confidentiality

![Three adult association colleagues and a prospective adviser discuss separate plain folders at a Hong Kong meeting table, with no government insignia or readable documents.](../new-role-old-matter-scene.jpg)

## Expertise can travel; authority does not

Hong Kong’s Civil Service Bureau distinguishes post-service outside-work controls by officer category. Its directorate regime considers conflict and public perception, with permission and conditions where applicable. The fictional association below must respect those controls while recognising that public-service experience alone does not establish wrongdoing.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong industry association is considering a paid adviser who retired from a directorate civil-service post. This is not a former politically appointed official. The relevant authority has confirmed that the person remains within an applicable post-service restriction period. No appointment is accepted and no work has begun.

The association will not start any role until the required prior permission is granted for its exact scope and all applicable conditions are met. Its own approval cannot replace the authority’s decision. A paid association role is not treated as exempt merely because the association describes itself as non-profit.

Plan A assigns six hours of general member-training preparation to the adviser, using published materials outside the former official’s portfolio. An unconnected professional team handles four hours of case-specific work. Plan B gives the adviser all ten hours, combining the same training with the case-specific assignment. Each complete package costs HK$10,000 and is affordable.

A separates responsibilities but needs a documented handover of public materials. B offers one point of contact and potentially less coordination, not a proven quality advantage. Both can meet the deadline if their scope is permissible and approved. The association has not yet checked whether the case-specific assignment overlaps the adviser’s former duties.

## Round two

The case-specific work concerns an unresolved regulatory application by an association member. The prospective adviser personally participated in reviewing that same matter during the final year of public service and had access to non-public deliberations. The proposed work includes privately commenting on the member’s response, even if somebody else submits it.

The documented restriction applicable to this fictional candidate bars direct or indirect work on this former matter. Continuing confidentiality must also be respected. These are binding conditions for the classroom decision, not restrictions the association can waive. Refraining from signing the submission would not remove the proposed behind-the-scenes involvement.

The independent team has no connection to the former review, has the member’s authorised current documents and can complete the four-hour task without the adviser’s input. The six-hour public-material training task is confirmed outside the former portfolio and excludes this case, former matters, government bidding and contact with former colleagues on the association’s behalf.

The adviser has disclosed the connection before receiving case files or beginning work. There is no evidence of improper favour, a job promise during public service, leaked information or attempted influence. Required permission for the narrowed role is still pending. The association can defer appointment without missing the case response deadline.

**Student question:** Can B proceed if the adviser stays off the final submission? Set out an implementable scope for A, the information barrier and permission gate. Who should do the pending case work, and what should happen if the role later expands?

## Separate a useful career from a prohibited assignment

B cannot proceed on these facts. Private review of a response is still involvement in the same former matter, even without a signature, meeting or direct approach to government. A non-disclosure promise cannot make that assignment permissible, and calling it general strategic advice would not change its substance.

The disclosed connection does not prove misconduct or make every future job unsuitable. Propose A’s genuinely separate training role for the required permission, and use the independent team for the case work. Do not start even the narrowed paid role while its approval and conditions remain unresolved.

## Make separation work in practice

Exclude the adviser from case documents, email threads, meetings, draft reviews and informal briefings about the former matter. Name an unconnected association manager to supervise the independent team and receive its advice. The adviser must neither coach that team privately nor pass information through another member.

Use only authorised public materials for the training assignment and record its topics and boundaries. Do not seek confidential recollections, internal contacts or an implied government endorsement. Public availability of one document does not remove the separate bar on working on the same former matter.

## Keep the approval tied to the actual work

Retain the past-duty declaration, proposed role, permission decision and conditions in appropriately restricted records. Give the adviser and supervising manager clear written responsibilities. Check compliance during the engagement, rather than treating one approval letter as unlimited permission for any later request.

If a new task enters the former portfolio or involves a former matter, stop that part and seek the required review before proceeding. Do not assume the passage of a guessed cooling-off period eliminates every confidentiality or applicable restriction. A change in title is not a change in the underlying assignment.

## Explain the practical decision fairly

A uses six adviser-hours and four independent-team hours: ten hours for the same HK$10,000 as B. It preserves useful training without giving the adviser the restricted case task. The independent team’s ability to meet the deadline removes any claimed need for a shortcut; permission for the separate role remains a genuine gate.

Explain the scope and controls accurately to the relevant decision makers without publishing sensitive case details or implying guilt. Public trust depends on a defensible process and actual conduct, not merely the adviser’s reputation or a confidential declaration nobody implements.

## Instructor note

Ask students first to design a scope for clearance, not approve an appointment. Reveal former participation before taking a second vote. GVR-03 manages a current procurement reviewer’s family connection; GVR-14 separates supplier hospitality from legitimate training. ## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

The association, retired official, approval status, assignments, costs and case facts are fictional. The adviser is a former Hong Kong directorate civil servant, not a politically appointed official or non-directorate employee. The authoritative classroom record confirms an ongoing applicable post-service restriction period; no universal cooling-off duration is stated. The paid role is not covered by an assumed unpaid non-commercial exemption. No role has been accepted, work started or permission issued. The supplied rule bars direct or indirect work on the former matter and requires prior relevant-authority permission for any permitted role; the association cannot waive it. The public-material training task is stipulated outside the former portfolio and still requires approval, not automatic clearance. Each complete plan covers ten paid task-hours for HK$10,000: A assigns six adviser-hours to training and four independent-team hours to the case; B assigns both tasks to the adviser. There is no evidence of misconduct, a job promise during service, improper influence or confidential disclosure. This is not an exhaustive legal account; a real appointment would require qualified review. Foreign rules are not imported. No real appointment, filing, contact or personal-data transmission is proposed.
