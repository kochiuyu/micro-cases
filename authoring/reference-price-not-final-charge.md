# HK$550 on screen. HK$550 on the card?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 519 -->

MKT-43 · Shenzhen and Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Price presentation · Currency choice · Customer comprehension

![A shopper and shopkeeper consider two payment choices on an unlabelled display beside a boxed cookware bundle.](../reference-price-not-final-charge-scene.jpg)

## The familiar currency is not necessarily the final price

Hong Kong’s Consumer Council reported in 2015 on its survey of overseas credit-card fees, finding that costs can differ between a shop’s currency and a cardholder-currency conversion. The fictional Shenzhen checkout below concerns how a retailer explains that distinction, not which currency every shopper should choose.

## Opening decision

A Shenzhen cookware shop sells the same bundle for RMB 500, including all shop charges. A visitor using the example Hong Kong card may choose to pay in RMB or explicitly accept a merchant conversion quote. The product and retailer revenue are the same in both routes; the shop receives no currency commission.

Choose between A, a large HK$550 reference estimate above RMB 500, and B, RMB 500 first with the HK$550 estimate beside it. Both label the estimate, offer both payment currencies equally without a preselection, and show transaction terms before consent. The difference is prominence. Do not replace or conceal any disclosure.

For this exercise only, use the example rates and fees in the exhibit. The HK$550 listing estimate is just RMB 500 converted at the example reference rate, before issuer fees. The merchant’s HK$572 quote is fixed for this example card and includes all merchant-conversion charges; this card has no additional issuer charge on that route.

## Evidence

| Route | Example exchange input | Added fees in the exercise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay RMB 500 | HK$1.10 per RMB estimate | Issuer adds 1.5% to converted amount |
| Accept merchant HKD quote | HK$572 fixed quote | None beyond the quoted total |

**Student question:** Which layout would you test first? Compare the estimated amounts paid by the two routes and say what shoppers need to understand before selecting either one.

## Round two

In a fictional randomized mock checkout, 200 adult volunteers saw A and 200 saw B. Same product, prices, exchange terms and audience. A had 130 mock completions and B had 120. On a separate question asked of every assigned volunteer, 128 in A and 168 in B correctly identified the HK$550 as an estimate rather than a guaranteed HKD charge. Nobody made a purchase.

For a separate sensitivity check, suppose the issuer’s conversion rate in the RMB route actually turns out to be HK$1.13 per RMB rather than HK$1.10. The 1.5% fee and the merchant’s HK$572 quote do not change. Round the final amount to the nearest cent.

**Student question:** Which layout would you take into the next controlled trial? Compare completion and comprehension, recalculate the example RMB-route charge, and give one check before interpreting either layout as commercially better.

## Debrief · A comparison needs the same cost basis

At HK$1.10 per RMB, the issuer route is HK$550 before its fee and HK$558.25 after it. The all-in fixed quote is HK$572. The estimated advantage of the RMB route is HK$13.75, but the RMB amount and the HKD amount on the card statement are different concepts. Keep the explicit choice and reason for the difference visible.

## Debrief · One result affects a different objective

A completes at 65%; B at 60%. Comprehension is 64% versus 84%. For this small mock test, B sacrifices five percentage points of completion while gaining twenty of comprehension. Neither measure is realized margin or the joint probability of informed completion; the counts do not give their overlap. Test B next if avoiding mistaken expectations matters and measure both outcomes together.

## Debrief · A ranking can reverse

At HK$1.13 per RMB, the converted RMB total is HK$565 before fees and HK$573.48 after rounding. That is HK$1.48 above the same merchant quote. Do not call either option universally cheaper or recommend a card. Observe how often shoppers correctly predict the order currency, and verify actual issuer terms and settled costs in any approved future evaluation.

## Instructor note

Give students five minutes to compute the first estimate before showing the mock outcome and rate change. Ask them to separate prominence, consent, actual terms and possible charges. Stop any test that distorts or obscures a charge; this exercise does not prescribe a universally compliant interface.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All shop terms, mock trial counts, exchange rates, tags, quotes and customer scenarios are hypothetical. No actual card data, purchases or financial advice. The actual credit-card terms and network requirements can differ.

- [Hong Kong Consumer Council · Overseas credit card transaction fees (2015)](https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/470-credit-card-transaction-fees). Opened the original survey-release body. Documents its survey and distinction between local-currency billing and merchant conversion. Historic terms are not current rates.
- [HKMA · Credit Cards](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/smart-consumers/credit-cards/). Opened consumer protection guidance. Foreign transaction terms vary by issuer; compare exchange rate and fees. Does not endorse either fictional route.
- [Visa · Decoding Dynamic Currency Conversion](https://www.visa.com/en-us/personal/travel/dynamic-currency-conversion). Opened first-party breakdown of amounts and currencies, rates, markup and affirmative choice. The case is about merchandising reference-estimate placement, not varying prominence between payment choices.
- [Visa · Optimize Your Cross-Border Ecommerce Payment Acceptance](https://usa.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/download/merchants/VBS-01-AUG-14-Optimize-Cross-Border-Ecommerce.pdf). Opened first-party merchant PDF and read its checkout section. Distinguishes currency switching and reminders about charges; not a determination of compliance for the mock display.
- [Visa · Exchange Rate Calculator](https://www.visa.com/en-us/personal/travel/exchange-rate-calculator). Opened first-party calculator explanatory text, including rate timing and issuer fees. Estimates may differ from billed amounts and calculator rate does not apply to merchant conversion. Three Visa documents are distinct sources, not independent institutions.
