# Our biggest customer. Our most valuable relationship?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 147 -->

MKT-13 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Customer value · Cost to serve · Retention

![Two stationery-supplier colleagues compare varied and standard subscription packs beside account folders and a telephone headset.](../valuable-relationship-scene.jpg)

## In the news

In results announced on 27 March 2026, Hong Kong-listed Vobile reported growth in subscription-service revenue alongside its other business activities. Subscription income makes continuing customer relationships visible, but revenue alone does not value each relationship.

Our stationery supplier, customer groups and forecasts are invented and unrelated to Vobile. No customer-level margins or retention results are attributed to that company.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong stationery subscription supplier has HK$4,000 for one optional account-planning-call pilot. It must choose between two groups of 20 existing business accounts: custom-pack subscribers and standard-pack subscribers. All have consented to being considered for this research; no sensitive personal characteristics are used.

Custom-pack accounts buy HK$6,000 each per active year; standard-pack accounts buy HK$3,600. All have renewed for the first coming year. Existing fulfilment, complaint handling, accessibility and agreed service remain unchanged for both groups.

The equal-cost pilot includes design, paid staff time and evaluation. Within the chosen group, ten accounts will be randomly offered an extra voluntary planning call and ten will retain normal service for comparison. The other group continues normal service. Nobody has promised that the call increases renewal. Choose where to learn first, not whom to abandon.

## Evidence

| Initial planning information | Custom packs | Standard packs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Existing accounts | 20 | 20 |
| Sales per active account-year | HK$6,000 | HK$3,600 |
| First coming year | Already renewed | Already renewed |
| Cost to pilot in this group | HK$4,000 | HK$4,000 |

**Student question:** Which group would you provisionally choose for the planning-call pilot? Explain why annual sales might guide your first impression but cannot establish either relationship value or the return from extra service.

## Round two

The finance and service teams supply the following fictional baseline forecasts without the new calls. Annual amounts apply only while an account is active. Product/fulfilment costs include goods and delivery; separate account-service costs cover order changes and support, with no double counting.

| Per active account-year | Custom packs | Standard packs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | HK$6,000 | HK$3,600 |
| Product and fulfilment cost | HK$3,600 | HK$1,800 |
| Account-service cost | HK$1,500 | HK$800 |
| Forecast annual renewal probability | 50% | 80% |

Use only the three coming years. Year one is active for everyone; each subsequent renewal has the stated conditional probability, unchanged across the two renewal opportunities. An account that leaves does not return within this model. Active-year sales and attributable costs stay constant. Common overhead and past acquisition costs are excluded, and there is no discounting: calculate three-year expected contribution, not full lifetime value or company profit.

The forecasts are uncertain, not guarantees or measured effects of the call. For sensitivity, also consider standard-pack renewal at 30% instead of 80%, with every other assumption unchanged. This is an alternative scenario, not a confidence bound. No probability is assigned to which forecast is correct.

**Student question:** Which group would you test first now? Compare three-year expected contribution after serving costs, check the lower-renewal scenario, and identify the additional evidence needed before claiming that the HK$4,000 pilot pays for itself.

## Start after the cost of serving

Per active year, custom packs contribute HK$6,000 − HK$3,600 − HK$1,500 = HK$900. Standard packs contribute HK$3,600 − HK$1,800 − HK$800 = HK$1,000. More sales do not compensate for the extra serving cost in this forecast.

These amounts are contribution after the specified attributable costs, not full accounting profit. The exercise stipulates these costs as the resources consumed by active accounts; it does not claim that dismissing an account instantly removes fixed overhead.

## Follow the same accounts through time

For custom packs, probabilities of being active in years one, two and three are 1, 0.50 and 0.25. Expected contribution per starting account is HK$900 × (1 + 0.50 + 0.25) = HK$1,575; for 20 accounts, HK$31,500.

For standard packs, the corresponding probabilities are 1, 0.80 and 0.64. HK$1,000 × (1 + 0.80 + 0.64) = HK$2,440 per starting account, or HK$48,800 for 20.

At the alternative 30% renewal assumption, standard packs give HK$1,000 × (1 + 0.30 + 0.09) = HK$1,390 each, or HK$27,800. The ranking reverses. Do not add new replacement customers to this fixed starting cohort.

## A valuable relationship is not a proven campaign return

The baseline model makes standard packs a reasonable first pilot under the main forecast. Choosing custom packs can instead be defended as testing whether planning calls can reduce costly changes or help retain a currently less durable group. Neither response is supplied as a fact.

The HK$48,800 and HK$31,500 would be expected under normal service; they are not gains caused by the pilot. Subtracting HK$4,000 from either does not establish campaign return. Compare incremental contribution and service costs against the normal-service comparison group, then subtract the pilot cost. Ten accounts per arm will provide limited precision, not a guaranteed causal conclusion.

Use the result to refine forecasts and service design, not to deny existing commitments or label people by financial worth. Full lifetime valuation would also require a longer justified horizon, time-value treatment and appropriate additional cash flows.

## Instructor note

Vote from sales first, then reveal serving costs and renewal assumptions. Work with one starting account before multiplying by 20. Ask why the third-year probability is the product of two conditional renewals, not another independent 80% share of the original group.

Keep the decision at pilot prioritisation, not a claim that the highest baseline value always deserves the most marketing money. Test sensitivity and distinguish forecast value from responsiveness. MKT-05 concerns one product’s effect on a shopping basket; MKT-06 qualifies new prospects. Here existing subscription relationships are followed over time. Do not treat the deliberately simplified, undiscounted three-year model as a universal CLV formula.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All supplier figures, consent, groups, forecasts, eligibility, costs and pilot design are fictional. B2B account grouping follows the subscribed service, not protected characteristics or personal vulnerabilities. All first-year contracts have renewed; later renewal probabilities are conditional on remaining active and held constant only as an explicit classroom assumption. No reactivation, new-account acquisition, cross-selling, referral effects or unmodelled revenue are included. The forecast is for the original 20 accounts in each group, not changing cohort composition. Product/fulfilment and account-service costs do not overlap. Common overhead, past acquisition costs, discounting and value beyond three years are excluded transparently; thus the computed number is three-year expected contribution, not full lifetime value, valuation or accounting profit. Baseline forecasts do not assume a call effect. The HK$4,000 pilot is paid incremental research/service expenditure, outside baseline serving costs, and cannot be justified merely by subtracting it from baseline value. A randomized offer with ten accounts per arm is possible here but small and not a promise of conclusive results; analyse the offer assignment, including nonparticipation, before attributing effects. Existing service rights and voluntary participation remain. Source bodies/sections listed were read, not all appendices or cited studies.
