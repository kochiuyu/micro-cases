## A promise before a payment

A loan guarantee can require future payment even when no cash leaves the guarantor today. Financial education stresses the obligation’s scope and affordability; liquidity guidance examines demands that arrive together. The business, guarantee terms and scenario probabilities below are invented, not a real loan recommendation.

## Opening decision

You prepare a board briefing for Harbour Fold, a fictional Hong Kong packaging business. A supplier asks it to guarantee a loan. Nothing has been signed. Choose A: keep the proposal under consideration for further review; or B: decline it. Your immediate task is the board’s liquidity screen, not contract approval.

The lender would advance money to the supplier, not Harbour Fold. Harbour Fold pays nothing at inception and receives no guarantee fee. Management hopes to preserve a supplier relationship, but no incremental cash benefit is established. Passing the screen would not prove the guarantee worthwhile.

For this exercise, a valid call occurs at the end of month three only if the supplier cannot pay its specified loan obligation. Harbour Fold must then pay exactly HK$100,000 that day. Otherwise it pays zero. This is the total all-in cap, including any interest, fees and enforcement costs.

The proposed obligation ends after that date and any required payment, with no renewal, earlier call, other guaranteed debt or additional claim. These are supplied teaching terms, not standard guarantee law. Any eventual contract would require independent professional review and separate board approval; students are not asked to sign.

Immediately before any call, Harbour Fold has HK$180,000 available in a strong-sales outcome or HK$60,000 in a weak-sales outcome. These are total accessible cash balances after all earlier obligations, not revenue or an extra inflow. No separate reserve, credit line, asset sale or owner injection is available.

The board requires at least HK$40,000 to remain after any guarantee payment in every modelled outcome. That amount is reserved for the next essential obligations and cannot fund the guarantee. No creditor delay, unpaid wages or tax deferral is permitted. Earlier cash requirements are already covered.

The first worksheet below is a provisional joint forecast. Each row is one mutually exclusive month-three outcome. Its probabilities sum to 100%; all other combinations have zero probability in this initial model. Sales weakness and a supplier call are initially assumed never to coincide. Check that assumption before recommending further consideration.

The cash balances and supplier repayment scenarios are held fixed for this liquidity screen. Declining means no guarantee payment; possible later effects on supply relationships are unquantified and require separate review. Any recovery from the supplier arrives after the payment deadline and supplies no cash for this screen.

## Round two

Before any commitment, the team corrects the joint forecast. Harbour Fold and its supplier depend on the same event-order cycle. In the revised model, there is a 75% chance of strong sales with no call, and a 25% chance of weak sales with the full HK$100,000 call. No other outcome occurs.

The chance of Harbour Fold’s weak sales remains 25%. The chance of a guarantee call also remains 25%. What changes is their coincidence: the call now arrives precisely when available cash is HK$60,000. Amounts, deadline, cap and the HK$40,000 protected balance are unchanged.

This perfect alignment is a supplied stress scenario, not an empirical claim about all suppliers. No financing has been arranged to bridge a gap. The lender has not offered a smaller guarantee, and the supplier has not promised timely reimbursement. The existing proposal can still be declined without a cancellation charge.

**Student question:** Does A still pass? Compare expected payment and expected residual cash with the initial model, then calculate the weak-outcome shortfall. What maximum all-in guarantee could preserve the HK$40,000 balance under the revised model, if a lender separately agreed?

## Debrief · No initial payment is not no exposure

The promise creates a possible HK$100,000 cash requirement at a specified date. The supplier receives the loan proceeds; Harbour Fold cannot count them as its own funding. Nothing is payable at inception, but the future payment is not optional once a valid call occurs under the hypothetical terms.

The 25% call probability gives an expected payment of 0.25 × HK$100,000 = HK$25,000. The actual payment is zero or HK$100,000, never HK$25,000 in these scenarios. An expected amount is neither a reserve already held nor the maximum obligation.

## Debrief · First screen, not final approval

Initially A leaves HK$180,000, HK$80,000 and HK$60,000 in the three table rows. Each is at least HK$40,000, so the initial screen passes. The minimum cushion above the protected balance is HK$20,000. This conclusion depends on the assumption that weak sales and a call never coincide.

I would allow further investigation, not recommend signing. The business purpose, value received, actual terms and broader risks still need scrutiny. Declining remains reasonable because a feasible promise is not automatically a beneficial one; no quantified supplier benefit offsets the expected payment in this exercise.

## Debrief · Same averages, different ability to pay

After the reveal, A leaves HK$180,000 in the strong outcome. In the weak outcome, the arithmetic is HK$60,000 − HK$100,000 = −HK$40,000. That negative figure is an unfunded deficit, not permission to overdraw. A fails the board’s screen.

The gap to paying the call alone is HK$40,000. The gap to paying it while retaining the protected HK$40,000 is HK$80,000: required cash HK$140,000 minus available HK$60,000. Using the protected money would still not meet the full call and would breach the board’s condition.

Expected payment remains HK$25,000. Expected pre-call cash remains 0.75 × HK$180,000 + 0.25 × HK$60,000 = HK$150,000. Expected residual cash is therefore HK$125,000 in either model. That average includes an unfunded negative outcome after the reveal; it does not make the promise payable in every outcome.

## Debrief · A conditional ceiling

Let G be the all-in guarantee cap, with the revised call pattern unchanged. In the weak outcome, HK$60,000 − G must be at least HK$40,000. Thus G must be no more than HK$20,000. At exactly HK$20,000 the protected balance survives, but there is no extra cushion.

A smaller cap is a negotiation possibility, not an available contract. The lender might refuse or alter the borrowing terms and repayment probabilities. Those changes would require a new analysis. No source establishes that HK$20,000 is commercially acceptable, legally sufficient or a recommended real-world guarantee.

## Debrief · Decline the current proposal

I would decline the unchanged HK$100,000 proposal under the revised screen. B makes no guarantee payment and leaves HK$180,000 or HK$60,000; both exceed the protected balance. Management can investigate different support arrangements separately, without pretending that uncommitted funding solves this deadline.

The decisive question is when a cash call arrives relative to the guarantor’s own resources. This is not a second income stream or a change in the loan’s interest rate. Neither unchanged expected payment nor unchanged expected cash reveals the joint downside by itself.
