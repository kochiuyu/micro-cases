## Distance is more than a journey

International-strategy research treats distance as more than physical separation. Export guidance likewise asks firms to examine customer practices, language, permissions and payment alongside travel. These are prompts for investigation, not a licence to infer business behaviour from nationality. The comparison below supplies fictional customer-specific evidence rather than claims about real countries.

## Opening decision

Fictional FrameDesk, based in Hong Kong, helps business clients document ordinary administrative workflows. It is selecting one first export pilot: City N, a nearby Asian city, or City F, a more distant one. Both cities are invented labels, not disguised claims about named jurisdictions. The service gives no regulated legal, medical or engineering opinion.

Both candidate client groups have passed the same demand and payment-reliability screening. The comparison uses six completed, paid projects in either city, producing HK$180,000 contribution after ordinary delivery costs but before the separately listed market-entry costs. This matched scenario is not a sales forecast or a signed six-project commitment.

The firm has one funded pilot team and can support either market, not both at once. Existing client obligations, lawful working hours and reserves are protected. Its objective is the larger contribution after all differing pilot costs, subject to delivering the same service standard. There is no additional strategic-value estimate favouring either market.

Complete travel quotes for the pilot are HK$12,000 for N and HK$36,000 for F. They include all required trips and related paid travel time. N is physically nearer and cheaper to visit. The team has not yet received the consolidated dossier on other changes needed to serve the two client groups.

Lawful supply, entry, data-handling and tax arrangements have been separately checked for both fictional routes; every associated differing cost is included in the forthcoming dossier. No permission or professional recognition is assumed from proximity. No contract has been signed, and no client is waiting on a promised launch date.

## Round two

The dossier separates customer requirements from government rules. N’s client group requires a paid assessment of the team’s training portfolio under its own supplier standard: HK$30,000. F’s group accepts the existing credential with HK$6,000 verification. Both routes are feasible before launch. These are commercial acceptance processes, not statutory licences or automatic international qualification recognition.

N’s clients require procurement and workshop materials in a language the team does not currently support. Qualified translation and facilitated sessions cost HK$24,000. F’s selected clients use the team’s existing working language, adding no translation cost. These verified preferences describe these clients, not everyone living in either city.

N’s clients use a reporting workflow requiring HK$18,000 adaptation and testing; F’s need HK$8,000. Neither change weakens quality or data protection. N’s agreed payment timetable adds HK$6,000 financing cost; F’s milestone payments add none. These are complete quoted charges, not unpaid invoice balances or assumptions about national payment honesty.

Including travel, the differing pilot costs are therefore N: 12,000 + 30,000 + 24,000 + 18,000 + 6,000; F: 36,000 + 6,000 + 0 + 8,000 + 0. Each charge is counted once and is additional to costs already deducted from the HK$180,000 scenario contribution.

Both complete packages are affordable and fit the team’s funded capacity, including assessment, translation and testing. Neither customer has yet contracted the six projects. The same demand uncertainty remains, and the pilot can be declined. All requirements and quotes apply only to the stated client group and pilot period.

## Debrief · Price the complete operating fit

N’s total is HK$90,000; F’s is HK$50,000. N saves HK$24,000 on travel but requires HK$64,000 more in the other listed changes: HK$78,000 versus HK$14,000. Its net cost disadvantage is therefore HK$40,000.

After these costs, the matched scenario leaves HK$90,000 for N and HK$130,000 for F. Prioritise F under the stated objective, subject to customer commitments and readiness. These are incremental pilot comparisons, not whole-company profit, guaranteed sales or a lifetime valuation of either market.

## Debrief · Translate differences into activities

Physical distance affects visits and paid travel here. The customer’s supplier-assessment process affects acceptance work; language affects materials and facilitation; reporting practices affect implementation; payment timing affects financing. The useful question is what FrameDesk must do differently, with what resources, for these customers.

Cultural, administrative, geographic and economic distance are useful lenses, not four universal scores. Client language does not describe a whole culture; a private credential check is not a national licensing barrier. Some differences cross categories. Cost each actual activity once rather than adding a second “distance penalty” for the same work.

## Debrief · Preserve gates before ranking

A cheaper market is not selectable if the firm cannot lawfully supply or meet required standards. Here both routes have feasible, funded compliance and readiness paths. That assumption permits a cost comparison; it does not mean that buying translation or passing a client assessment automatically supplies every legal authorisation.

Before commitment, verify the relevant current rules, assessment completion, tested materials, data arrangements, accepted deliverables and payment terms with responsible parties. Keep the documented distinction between legal permission, professional competence and a particular buyer’s acceptance. None can simply stand in for the others.

## Debrief · Keep demand separate

The six-project scenario makes operating costs comparable; screening does not create six orders. Confirm actual scope, price, delivery dates and authorised purchase commitments before incurring entry costs. If demand or contribution differs between the client groups, recalculate rather than preserve F’s ranking by assumption.

At the supplied costs, N would need HK$40,000 more pre-entry contribution than F to tie the final contribution. More than HK$40,000 would reverse the ranking, all else unchanged. This is a sensitivity boundary, not a forecast that extra demand or a price premium exists.

## Debrief · Reuse capability selectively

F fits more of this team’s current language, credentials and reporting method despite longer travel. Another exporter with different capabilities could face the opposite adaptation pattern. Distance is relative to the service, the firm and the selected customers, not simply a property of a place on a map.

After a pilot, review actual delivery effort, customer acceptance, payment timing and repeat demand. Entry work may create reusable capability, but its future value is not priced here. N remains a possible later market; rejecting it for this pilot is not a judgment about its people or long-term potential.

## Instructor note

Ask students to name a concrete activity for every claimed difference. Then reconcile the HK$24,000 travel advantage with HK$64,000 extra adaptation and financing cost. Do not accept a national-cultural explanation when the dossier supplies only client-specific requirements.
