# A large market. A reachable first business?

## Need, access and purchasing are different

The World Health Organization describes substantial unmet need for assistive technology and barriers including procurement, funding and workforce capacity. Need is not the same as a startup’s sales. Market-sizing methods therefore distinguish broad opportunity from reachable customers. All business counts, prices, rules and forecasts below are fictional.

## Opening decision

You help founders of ClearPage, a fictional Shenzhen startup making an adjustable tabletop reading stand. Its first customer segment is fixed: adult community-activity centres in one defined sales territory. Centres buy shared stands; individual users do not buy from this venture in the first year. No medical benefit is claimed.

For this exercise, product suitability, safe use, required compliance and delivery capability have been verified for the specified nonclinical setting. These are assumptions, not a real product approval. No user health records are collected. The task is estimating first-year reachable sales before a staffing recommendation, not choosing a different segment.

Pitch A multiplies an illustrative population of one million possible users by CNY2,000 per stand, producing CNY2 billion. It labels that figure a market opportunity. It provides no purchase frequency, payer budgets, device-sharing rate, replacement cycle or evidence that every counted person wants this product.

Pitch B starts with a deduplicated list of 80 purchasing centres in the fixed territory. Provisionally, it assumes the team can complete one qualified sales opportunity with every centre during year one. Each successful centre buys exactly ten stands once. There are no repeat orders, consumer sales or sales outside the list.

For this teaching calculation, management assigns a 50% chance of a completed first-year order to each qualified opportunity. The rate is an unvalidated planning assumption, not an observed conversion result. All successful orders are delivered and paid within year one. Qualified conversations and eligibility alone are not purchases.

Net selling revenue is CNY2,000 per stand. All attributable variable costs, including manufacturing, delivery, setup, support and applicable transaction charges, total CNY1,200 per stand. Contribution is the difference. Base overheads and working capital are separately funded; neither the market headline nor forecast contribution is cash already in the bank.

The founders are considering a first-year implementation coordinator costing CNY180,000, all employment costs included. The role would relieve founder workload, not increase sales-contact capacity or change these prices and costs. Existing funded operations can safely deliver up to 800 stands without the hire; deferral would not break a customer promise.

Management will recommend this additional annual commitment only if the chosen first-year forecast contribution at least covers CNY180,000. This is a supplied minimum screen, not proof that hiring creates value. No candidate has an offer or contract. Which estimate should govern the screen, and what assumptions need checking?

## Round two

Before any staffing commitment, the founders check the centres’ written procurement conditions. Forty of the 80 require a completed supplier-onboarding approval that ClearPage cannot obtain before this year’s purchasing deadline. The other 40 permit its participation. This is a fictional buyer-specific eligibility condition, not a claim about Chinese law or all community centres.

A reviewed calendar gives the existing sales team 300 hours for complete qualified opportunities this year. Each requires ten hours, including contact, demonstration, follow-up and procurement work, whether won or lost. The team can therefore complete at most 30 distinct opportunities, all selected from the 40 eligible centres.

No time is spent on the excluded 40 in the revised plan. Each included centre counts once; several contacts at the same centre are not extra opportunities. The 50% planning probability applies after eligibility and completion of this sales work. Do not multiply by the 50% eligibility fraction again.

Prices, ten-stand order size, costs, delivery capacity and the assumed order probability stay unchanged. No extra sales hire, reseller, exemption, purchasing deadline extension or outside-territory demand is available. The proposed coordinator does not add sales hours. Social need has not disappeared because the first-year route to buyers is smaller.

**Student question:** What are the revised reachable opportunities, forecast orders, revenue and contribution? Would you recommend the annual hire under the stated screen? Distinguish an eligible-market ceiling from a forecast and identify the order probability needed to cover the hire with 30 opportunities.

## Debrief · Start with the right buyer

I would use B’s organisation-based model for the first-year screen, after checking its assumptions. A’s arithmetic is CNY2 billion, but people are not this venture’s purchasing accounts. Shared stands also break the assumed one-person/one-device link. No annual purchase rate makes that population calculation an annual revenue forecast.

A broad total addressable market describes a defined opportunity at full penetration, not what this team will sell next year. A serviceable market narrows the scope to buyers and offers the venture can serve. Obtainable sales must then allow for eligibility, selling capacity, timing and actual purchasing probability.

## Debrief · The provisional hiring case

B initially projects 80 × 50% = 40 orders, or 400 stands. Revenue is 400 × CNY2,000 = CNY800,000. Unit contribution is CNY2,000 − CNY1,200 = CNY800, giving CNY320,000 total contribution. That exceeds the proposed CNY180,000 commitment by CNY140,000 under the provisional assumptions.

This passes the numerical screen, not a hiring mandate. The 50% rate is unvalidated, the opportunity count is unverified, and the coordinator’s workload benefit has not been valued. I would resolve the access and capacity checks before recommending the commitment, rather than present CNY800,000 as secured sales.

## Debrief · Eligibility is not conversion

The 40 eligible centres could each buy ten stands, giving a CNY800,000 one-order-per-centre revenue ceiling for that eligible pool. That assumes every eligible centre buys; it is not expected revenue. It also ignores the team’s tighter limit of 300 ÷ 10 = 30 completed opportunities.

Reachable opportunities are min(40 eligible centres, 30 capacity) = 30. At 50%, forecast orders are 15, giving 150 stands and CNY300,000 revenue. The 30-opportunity all-win ceiling would be CNY600,000 revenue. Neither the eligible-pool ceiling nor the all-win ceiling should replace the conditional forecast.

## Debrief · Recalculate the commitment

Revised forecast contribution is 150 × CNY800 = CNY120,000, CNY60,000 below the hire’s annual cost. I would defer the additional hire under the stated screen. This does not require dismissing anyone, delaying owed wages or withholding a promised service. No employment offer exists and existing delivery capacity is funded.

The CNY2 billion population arithmetic and underlying social need have not changed. What changed is the reachable first-year sales process. A large long-term opportunity cannot itself pay a near-term salary. CNY120,000 is contribution before the proposed hire and common overheads, not whole-company profit or guaranteed cash.

## Debrief · A sensitivity boundary

With 30 opportunities and ten stands per order, expected contribution is 30 × q × 10 × CNY800 = CNY240,000 × q. Covering CNY180,000 requires q ≥ 75%. At exactly 75%, the expected screen just passes with no cushion; the supplied 50% assumption does not.

This threshold is not evidence that 75% conversion is achievable. Expected orders at that boundary are 22.5; an average need not be a whole realised order. In an actual year, at least 23 complete ten-stand orders would contribute CNY184,000 and cover the hire; 22 would contribute only CNY176,000.

## Debrief · What evidence would change the recommendation?

Record the buyer, current eligibility, decision date and complete sales effort for each centre. Test the assumed order rate and ten-unit order size through authorised purchasing evidence, including refusals. Revise the forecast when evidence changes; do not count demonstrations as orders or remove a procurement safeguard to reach a target.

A future approved route or additional sales capacity could change the model, but would bring its own timing and costs. Report those separately rather than silently enlarging this year’s pipeline. The chosen first segment is unchanged; the result is a smaller executable plan, not a judgment that unmet need lacks value.
