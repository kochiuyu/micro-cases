# One room. Two check-ins?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 527 -->

HSP-43 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Day-use hotel stays · Room turnover · Capacity scheduling · Contribution

![A hotel desk supervisor and housekeeping lead compare a blank shift board beside a room key and cleaning cart.](../one-room-two-check-ins-scene.jpg)

## Hotels can sell rooms by the hour

The Kowloon Hotel advertised a day-use offer valid through 31 August 2026, with four-, six- and eight-hour options. A day-use offer promises a defined span of access rather than a room for the night. The hotel, bookings, turnover and earnings in this exercise are fictional.

## Opening decision

Suki manages eight identical rooms that are ready by noon at a fictional Kowloon hotel. Eight confirmed overnight guests are promised those rooms by 17:00. None has agreed to wait or move, and there are no spare rooms. To earn extra contribution, Suki can publish one day-use package before reservations open, or offer none.

Under Early, six customers are ready to book and leave at 14:30. Under Late, eight are ready to book and leave at 16:00. Suki can cap sales at any whole number. Guests under each package leave at its stated time; no departures or evening bookings change.

Each used room takes one worker 30 uninterrupted minutes to clean and inspect after checkout. One worker cannot do two rooms at once. Both workers are available from 14:30 to 17:00, and tasks can finish exactly at 17:00. Contribution is incremental and already deducts routine extra costs. Rooms not sold in the day remain ready.

## Evidence

| Package | Checkout | Day demand | Contribution per sale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 14:30 | 6 rooms | HK$200 |
| Late | 16:00 | 8 rooms | HK$380 |

**Student question:** Which package and sales cap earn the most without failing the evening promise? Schedule the turnarounds rather than assuming that eight available rooms means eight sellable day stays.

## Round two

Before advertising any day rooms, Suki learns that one worker cannot clean from 16:00 to 16:30. Both are still available before 16:00 and from 16:30 to 17:00. Changing the room-reset standard, moving the evening arrivals or dividing one task between workers is not an option.

**Student question:** Which package and cap now work best? At what contribution per Late sale would the reduced Late plan tie Early?

## Debrief · Time starts when the guest leaves

Early turns six rooms in three half-hour rounds from 14:30 to 16:00. Late has only two half-hour rounds before 17:00, with two workers in each, so it can sell four rooms, not eight.

## Debrief · Compare only feasible sales

Early adds 6 × HK$200 = HK$1,200. Late adds 4 × HK$380 = HK$1,520. Select Late but make only four rooms available for that package. The four other rooms remain ready for the confirmed night arrivals.

## Debrief · Recount the slots after the staffing change

Early can still finish by 16:00. For Late, one worker can reset a room by 16:30, then both can finish a further two rooms by 17:00. Late yields 3 × HK$380 = HK$1,140, so offer Early. Three Late sales tie at HK$400 each.

## Debrief · Protect the promised check-in

A capacity count is not a service buffer. The initial Late plan depends on the last two inspections finishing exactly at 17:00. If cleanup or checkout becomes uncertain, retest a smaller cap or earlier cutoff before selling, not after accepting bookings.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to schedule, 4 to adjust coverage and 5 to discuss promises. Ask students to mark every job on a two-worker timeline. What changes if management needs all rooms ready by 16:45 instead of 17:00?
