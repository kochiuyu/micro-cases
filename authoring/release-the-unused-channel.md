# An exclusive deal. What if a channel stays idle?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 533 -->

ENT-44 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Channel exclusivity · Licensing scope · Performance triggers · Opportunity cost

![A product founder and a distributor discuss two blank licence sheets beside an unbranded prototype dispenser.](../release-the-unused-channel-scene.jpg)

## A licence can separate channels without selling the rights

Hong Kong’s Intellectual Property Department lists distribution channels, exclusivity, duration and audit rights among terms parties can consider in a licence. The dispenser venture and offers below are fictional. The exercise values the stated business terms and does not determine whether a real agreement is enforceable.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong startup has developed a compact refill dispenser. It owns the rights it proposes to license and is choosing between two one-year offers from a distributor. Retail and institutional buyers do not overlap. The sales estimates and probabilities below are teaching inputs.

Broad grants the distributor exclusive sales rights in both channels. Scope grants exclusive rights in retail only; the startup can independently serve institutional buyers if that route is worth the HK$12,000 launch cost. Through its own route the startup earns HK$10 per unit contribution before launch cost. It decides after observing institutional demand.

Retail forecasts are 9,000 distributor sales under either offer. The institutional forecast is low with probability 65% or high with probability 35%. Institutional quantities in the table apply to the distributor under Broad or to the startup’s separate route under Scope. Count payments and net contributions over one year, with no discounting or other differing costs.

## Evidence

| Term | Broad | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial licence fee | HK$42,000 | HK$30,000 |
| Royalty per distributor sale | HK$4 | HK$5 |
| Institutional units, low demand | 1,000 | 1,000 via own route |
| Institutional units, high demand | 8,000 | 8,000 via own route |

**Student question:** Which offer is stronger under low and high demand? What expected value does each give the startup at the stated 35% chance of high demand?

## Round two

The distributor now says it can sell just 1,000 institutional units in the first six months under either demand outcome. Both parties can agree that institutional exclusivity ends if the first six months record fewer than 3,000 verified paid institutional units. Retail exclusivity and fee stay unchanged.

In the scenario, ending exclusivity at month six lets the founder start its separate institutional route with the same launch cost and margin. The distributor makes no further institutional sales. The founder can sell zero additional units if demand is low, or 6,500 if high. Assume all first-half distributor sales still pay the stated HK$4 royalty.

**Student question:** Which offer should the founder favour now? Calculate the revised Broad offer’s value and identify what the contract must measure to trigger release without cancelling retail rights.

## Debrief · Paying for more scope benefits both sides differently

Broad pays HK$42,000 + 9,000 × HK$4 before institutional royalties. Its low and high values are HK$82,000 and HK$110,000. Scope pays HK$30,000 + 9,000 × HK$5 before its own route. In low demand the route would lose HK$2,000, so do not launch and keep HK$75,000.

## Debrief · Protect the upside when it pays

With Scope and high demand, the founder’s route adds 8,000 × HK$10 − HK$12,000 = HK$68,000, giving HK$143,000. At 35% high demand, Broad is worth HK$91,800 and Scope HK$98,800. The larger upfront Broad fee does not outweigh the extra high-demand gain from Scope.

## Debrief · Price the clause and the lost time

Under revised Broad, verified sales of 1,000 miss the threshold in both states. Initial fee, retail royalties and first-half institutional royalties give HK$82,000. In the high state, the startup adds 6,500 × HK$10 − HK$12,000 = HK$53,000; in the low state it does not launch. Expected value is HK$100,550, ahead of Scope by HK$1,750.

## Debrief · Make the release clear enough to verify

Define which institutional sales count, what evidence establishes a paid unit, the measurement date, and what changes if the target is missed. Only institutional exclusivity ends in this exercise. Verify the remaining demand and launch cost before accepting a narrow expected lead.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 for the initial low/high comparison, 5 to value the revised clause, and 4 to discuss evidence. Ask why the founder keeps the original intellectual property yet may still lose the ability to serve a channel during exclusivity.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All offers, forecasts, tiers and contractual outcomes are fictional. The cited guides are used for concepts, not advice on contract drafting, compliance, rights or enforceability in any jurisdiction.

- [Hong Kong Intellectual Property Department, Practical Guide for IP Managers](https://www.ipd.gov.hk/filemanager/ipd/en/share/publications/Practical-Guide-to-IP-Managers-e.pdf). Section 4.1 identifies exclusivity, scope, channels and audit rights as negotiated licence matters.
- [Hong Kong Intellectual Property Department, Intellectual Property Licensing booklet](https://www.ipd.gov.hk/filemanager/ipd/en/share/publications/IP-Licensing-Booklet_2023-e.pdf). Shows licences can define scope and conditions, with parties weighing different interests.
- [World Intellectual Property Organization, Earn](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/ip-business-moments/earn). Explains payment structures and the possible loss of exclusivity if rights are underused.
- [WIPO and ITC, Exchanging Value: Negotiating Technology Licensing Agreements](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/licensing/906/wipo_pub_906.pdf). The training manual distinguishes milestone/performance-linked terms and fields of use.
- [World Intellectual Property Organization, IP Licensing Strategies for Ventures](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/ip-commercialization/w/blog/ip-licensing-strategies-for-ventures). Separates what rights licensees receive from whether the grant is exclusive; not a valuation of these offers.
