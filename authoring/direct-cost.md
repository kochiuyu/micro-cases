# No commission. No acquisition cost?

Case 54 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Hospitality · Distribution channels · Acquisition costs

![A hotel manager and colleague discuss two booking folders at a reception desk with a laptop and room key.](../direct-cost-scene.jpg)

## In the news

In a Legislative Council reply on 10 September 2025, the Hong Kong Government distinguished hotel room tariffs, accommodation tax and online travel agency commissions. It said tax collected on a hotel’s behalf should not enter the commission calculation and reported that mainstream agencies had followed its appeal.

The discussion shows why a booking’s headline price and the hotel’s receipts need separating. It does not establish that direct booking is always cheaper. Our hotel and campaign below are fictional; tax is excluded from their simplified comparison.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small Hong Kong hotel receives HK$1,000 in room revenue for a completed one-night stay before channel costs. An online travel agency charges 18% commission.

The manager proposes a new paid campaign sending guests to the hotel’s own website instead: “Without the commission, we keep the whole HK$1,000.” No campaign money has yet been committed.

The team is comparing prospective routes for equivalent stays, not assuming existing agency guests will automatically move to the website. It has not yet reviewed the direct campaign’s advertising and processing costs.

## Evidence

| Agency-route measure | Per completed one-night stay |
|---|---:|
| Room revenue before channel costs | HK$1,000 |
| Agency commission | 18% |

Taxes, service charges and common hotel operating costs are excluded from both routes.

**Student question:** Would you shift the next campaign toward direct bookings, or retain the agency route while investigating? What costs and benefits would you compare?

## Round two

The proposed direct campaign has a HK$20,000 advertising budget and forecasts 100 completed, paid one-night stays. Booking and payment processing would cost another HK$30 per completed stay.

For this exercise, these are all additional direct-channel costs. The agency’s 18% commission includes all its channel-specific costs. Both routes use the same HK$1,000 room rate, room type, dates and guest service cost.

The forecast is hypothetical, not guaranteed or a causal estimate of extra demand. Existing organic or repeat direct bookings are outside this campaign comparison. Future repeat revenue is not supplied.

**Student question:** Under this forecast, how much remains per completed stay through each route? Would you approve the proposed campaign as it stands, revise it, or test it on a smaller scale?

## Count the full route cost

Agency commission is 18% × HK$1,000 = HK$180, leaving HK$820 per completed stay before common hotel costs.

At 100 completed stays, the proposed campaign spreads HK$20,000 of advertising over 100: HK$200 per stay. Add HK$30 processing, leaving HK$1,000 − HK$200 − HK$30 = HK$770.

Under these assumptions, direct leaves HK$50 less per stay. That does not mean every direct booking is more expensive. An existing repeat guest may not need this paid campaign.

## What would change the comparison?

To match the agency’s HK$820, advertising cost per completed direct stay must be HK$150: HK$1,000 − HK$30 − HK$820.

If spending stays HK$20,000 and all other assumptions hold, HK$20,000 / HK$150 = 133⅓ stays. Therefore 134 whole completed one-night stays would exceed the agency comparator per stay.

This is a conditional channel-cost threshold, not whole-hotel break-even, proof that 134 stays are attainable or a reason to treat clicks and reservations as completed stays. More advertising spend would change the threshold.

## Choose a mix, not a slogan

Before shifting spend, check the forecast, cancellations and attribution: which completed stays would the campaign actually add, and which would happen anyway? Compare like room types and dates, and keep the cost boundary explicit.

Direct relationships may offer future benefits, but do not invent their value. Agency visibility may also support discovery through other channels. Removing a listing need not simply transfer its guests to the hotel website.

A limited, measured campaign may be defensible if its learning or strategic benefits justify the cost. Permanent reliance on either route is not established by this exercise. Record acquisition spending, completed stays, net channel receipts and any displacement.

## Instructor note

Take an initial vote before revealing the campaign budget. Ask students why “no commission” is not the same as “no acquisition cost.” Keep completed, paid one-night stays as the denominator throughout.

The HK$770 and HK$820 figures are after channel costs but before common operating costs, not profit. The threshold assumes the same fixed campaign spend and comparable room revenue; it does not estimate demand, incrementality or an optimal channel mix.
