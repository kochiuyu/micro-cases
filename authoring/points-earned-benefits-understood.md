# Points earned. Benefits understood?

MKT-20 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Loyalty design · Reward comprehension · Redemption friction

![Two café colleagues consider plain reward-card samples beside a cup and planner.](../points-earned-benefits-understood-scene.jpg)

## A reward must be usable, not merely earned

On 15 February 2024, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council discussed complaints about reward availability, redemption arrangements and changes to scheme terms, and called for clearer information and attention to less digitally confident consumers. These complaint accounts are not a prevalence survey or a finding about our fictional café.

## Opening decision

A fictional café is choosing a new optional reward scheme for future enrolments. Existing customers’ balances, redemption rights and agreed terms will be honoured separately without loss or forced migration. Both proposals are lawful, fully disclosed before joining, free to join and usable through a physical card with staff help as well as an optional digital display. Joining does not require consent to unrelated marketing, extra purchases or unnecessary personal-data collection.

Under A, a visit with at least HK$40 of eligible food or drink earns one stamp, with at most one stamp per person per day. Six stamps earn one standard drink, normally priced HK$40. The card expires 180 days after its first stamp. Under B, each HK$1 of eligible net spending earns one point with no daily limit; 240 points earn the same drink. Each point expires 180 days after it was earned. Points are spent oldest first. No bonus multipliers or tier status apply. All dates are displayed, and staff can explain them.

Under either scheme, once earned, the free-drink voucher is issued immediately, can be used from the next visit and remains valid 30 days. No extra purchase is required to redeem it. Reward supply is budgeted and reserved; no surprise stock restriction applies. The planner forecasts HK$6,000 actual reward cost for each scheme during the pilot, not HK$6,000 retail value. This equal forecast is an assumption requiring later validation, not a claim that their individual benefits are equal. Each initial design costs HK$2,000 to set up within a separate HK$4,000 setup allowance. No scheme has launched and no repeat-purchase effect has been measured.

## Evidence

| Scheme | Reward requirement | Progress validity |
|---|---|---|
| A visits | Six qualifying visits, at most one stamp daily | 180 days from first stamp |
| B points | 240 points; 1 per HK$1 eligible spend | Each point valid 180 days |

**Student question:** Would you pilot the visit-based reward or spend-based points first? Explain which purchasing behaviour you want to recognise and how you would make the remaining steps and expiry understandable without promising more repeat purchases.

## Round two

Twenty consenting customers complete hypothetical tasks with both draft explanations; presentation order is alternated. Eighteen correctly identify both the next reward requirement and the relevant expiry under A, compared with eleven under B. The B mistakes mainly concern the 240-point threshold and the difference between point expiry and the later 30-day voucher validity. Nobody earns or loses real rewards in these tasks. This small convenience group is not a random customer sample.

For an additional HK$2,000, B can show a plain receipt panel with eligible points remaining, points still needed for the next drink, the earliest expiry date and amount, and a separate voucher expiry once earned. Terms and earning requirements stay unchanged; staff and physical-card access remain. In a repeat task, eighteen of the same twenty answer both B questions correctly. Practice and prior explanation may contribute; this is not a controlled estimate of the panel’s effect. Clarified B costs HK$4,000 setup in total, versus A’s HK$2,000; both fit the allowance. Reward-cost forecasts remain HK$6,000 each, but actual redemption and purchasing still need observation.

**Student question:** Would you choose the simple visit scheme or pay for the clarified points version before a pilot? Distinguish demonstrated comprehension from future behaviour, and state what you would measure while protecting existing rewards.

## Recognise visits or recognise spending

A recognises six qualifying days, irrespective of extra spend above the minimum on those days. B recognises cumulative eligible spend. Six HK$40 visits can earn the same drink under either scheme if progress is still valid. A single HK$240 eligible purchase yields one A stamp but 240 B points and a voucher usable on the next visit. The reward structures therefore favour different patterns; equal forecast business cost does not make them equivalent for every customer.

## Make the path and the clocks legible

A has a card clock; B has expiry dates for separate point amounts. Both then have a separate 30-day voucher clock after earning. Show what remains, what expires next and when redemption is possible. Do not create fake earned progress, quietly extend the target, remove existing balances or count misunderstanding-driven non-redemption as a success. Disclosed terms can still need better explanation.

## Comprehension is an intermediate result

Eighteen of twenty is 90%; eleven is 55%. B’s repeat task rises by seven correct respondents, or 35 percentage points, in this selected group. It does not establish that the panel caused the change, that all customers understand, or that people will buy more. A is simpler and cheaper to set up; clarified B can still be preferable if recognising varied basket spending fits the café’s purpose. Test actual understanding, redemption, complaints, reward costs and purchasing over time without pressuring customers to spend unnecessarily.

## Instructor note

MKT-01 studies fare self-selection; MKT-10 studies finding a suitable item in a catalogue. Here the mechanism is understanding a future reward and the progress needed to earn it. Original loyalty research provides context that progress framing can matter in particular settings, not a promised effect of this café’s scheme. Do not copy deceptive experimental procedures or generalise study percentages to a new population. All case rules, budgets and comprehension counts are original fiction.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original optional future-enrolment designs; existing rights are protected separately. A awards one stamp per day for at least HK$40 eligible spend, with six stamps required. B awards one point per HK$1, with 240 points required. The same drink has a HK$40 retail price; actual reward cost of HK$6,000 per scheme is independently forecast, not established. A expires 180 days from the first stamp; B points expire individually after 180 days and are used oldest first. Vouchers expire 30 days after earning and can be redeemed from the next visit without extra spending; supply is reserved. Setup costs are HK$2,000 for A and HK$2,000 + HK$2,000 clarification = HK$4,000 for B, within a separate HK$4,000 allowance. Twenty convenience participants see alternating presentation order: 18 correct for A, 11 for B and 18 for repeated B. The repeated task confounds learning; 90%, 55% and a 35-percentage-point rise are not causal or behavioural estimates. The two Consumer Council reports are related complaint context; the two original research papers do not predict café performance.

- [Consumer Council: Reward points disputes, 15 February 2024](https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/p-568-reward-point-schemes-complaints). Actual author-hosted manuscript describes field and scenario experiments on framed progress.
