# Would you cross the harbour early to save HK$20?

Case 4 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Congestion pricing, opportunity cost

![Drivers approach a Hong Kong harbour tunnel at dawn.](../tunnel-scene.jpg)

## Opening decision

You drive through Hong Kong’s Cross-Harbour Tunnel to work on a weekday. You can cross in the early, low-toll period or during the morning peak. Your shift starts at the same time either way. Arriving early costs you HK$24 in lost time at home. For the first vote, assume both trips take equally long.

## Evidence

| Cost per private car (HK$) | Early | Peak |
|---|---:|---:|
| Toll | 20 | 40 |
| Early arrival | 24 | 0 |

**Student question** Which crossing time would you choose? How much would the early arrival have to cost for you to switch?

## Round two

Now learn that the peak queue adds ten minutes to your journey. You value each minute in the queue at HK$1. Keep the early-arrival cost and both tolls fixed.

**Student question** Vote again. If many other drivers make the same switch, what happens to the reason you switched?

## Debrief

With equal journey times, early costs HK$44 including the inconvenience, versus HK$40 at peak. You choose peak. The break-even early-arrival cost is HK$20. With the ten-minute queue, peak costs HK$50, so early saves HK$6. At HK$1 a minute, a four-minute peak delay makes you indifferent.

One driver who leaves the peak can save time for others in the queue. That benefit lies outside the driver’s own calculation. As drivers shift, the peak queue may shrink and the early queue may grow. To assess the policy, compare travel times and vehicle counts across adjacent periods and all three crossings. A shorter peak queue at one entrance alone cannot establish how much delay was saved.

## Instructor note

0–3 minutes. Take a vote before revealing the queue. Ask one student to put a dollar value on the lost time at home. 3–8 minutes. Reveal the delay and ask two students to defend their choices. 8–12 minutes. Ask how a driver with an inflexible school drop-off might respond and which queue the class would measure next.

## Sources and case assumptions

All information needed for the classroom decisions is provided in the case. The sources below are optional background, not assigned reading.


1. [Transport Department, Toll Plans for Road Harbour Crossings](https://www.td.gov.hk/en/transport_in_hong_kong/tunnels_and_bridges_n/tvt/index.html). Gives the operating schedule and confirms the 17 December 2023 start.
2. [Government toll announcement, 11 October 2023](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202310/11/P2023101100477.htm). Gives the HK$20–40 private-car range used in the exhibit.
3. [Transport Department, first working day, 18 December 2023](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202312/18/P2023121800569.htm). Reports about 64,000 crossings from 7 to 11 am across all three tunnels. This is a vehicle count, not a journey-time measure.
4. [Legislative Council written reply, 13 November 2024](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202411/13/P2024111300234.htm). Discusses how traffic shifted between crossings and into the periods beside peak hours.
5. [Transport Panel paper, June 2025, Appendix 1 and traffic tables](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2025/english/panels/tp/papers/tp20250620cb3-956-1-e.pdf). Provides a later comparison of weekday flows and explains how queues are treated in the measurements.

The private-car tolls are the low and peak levels for the Cross-Harbour Tunnel on a weekday excluding general holidays. The two choices omit transition periods. The commuter, equal initial travel times, HK$24 inconvenience, ten-minute delay and HK$1 per minute are classroom assumptions.
