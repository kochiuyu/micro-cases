# Earn more interest. Available on which day?

## Deposit rates move; payment dates still matter

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority’s September 2025 stability report described lower retail Hong Kong-dollar time-deposit rates in the first half of 2025 as liquidity conditions eased. This is historical banking context, not a current offer or a prediction. Our studio’s rates, terms, payment dates and early-withdrawal charge are entirely fictional.

## Opening decision

A fictional design studio has HK$300,000 reserved for three supplier payments: HK$60,000 on day 30, HK$90,000 on day 60 and HK$150,000 on day 90. All other obligations and emergency needs are funded separately. No borrowing or additional receipts are available within this envelope. Banks, payment systems, authorised signatories and suppliers operate normally. Compare policies; do not open accounts or move real money.

A maturity ladder places each payment amount in a deposit maturing on its due day. A liquid-first plan keeps the first HK$60,000 on demand and uses the same deposits as the ladder for the later two payments. Both meet every currently known payment. Keeping all HK$300,000 until day 90 is not a permissible third policy: it would fail the earlier commitments.

Fictional simple annual rates are 2.4% for 30 days, 3.0% for 60 days and 3.6% for 90 days; on-demand cash earns 0.6%. Use a 360-day year and interest = principal × annual rate × days/360, with no compounding or tax in this classroom comparison. Maturity cash is available before each payment cutoff, with no automatic renewal. Interest is retained separately without further interest through day 90. Fees and early-exit permission must be checked if a payment date changes.

**Student question:** Which policy would you choose for the known payment schedule, and how much interest would you give up to keep the first payment liquid?

## Round two

Now examine each policy as if it had been put in place on day 0; do not pretend to switch retrospectively. On day 15, the first supplier offers an optional early delivery with payment of HK$59,700 that day instead of HK$60,000 on day 30. Quality, service and warranties are unchanged, and the studio can receive and use the delivery safely. Declining leaves the original delivery and day-30 payment intact, with no penalty. There are no extra receiving, storage or operating costs.

Under A, the bank confirms it will permit early withdrawal of the entire HK$60,000 30-day deposit today, pay no interest on it and deduct a HK$200 fee from principal. The remaining HK$59,800 is available before the supplier cutoff. Partial withdrawal is not allowed. Under B, the HK$60,000 on-demand balance is available without a withdrawal fee and earns 15 days of interest, paid separately. Other deposits and payments do not change.

Any money left after making the first supplier payment, including discount savings and interest, is parked without further interest through day 90. Thus there is no hidden reinvestment assumption. Do not assume another bank’s early-exit policy or that time deposits are always withdrawable. The supplied approval and charge apply only to this fictional branch.

**Student question:** Under each policy, would you accept the early-delivery offer? Reconcile the discount with lost interest and fees, then explain what this one opportunity says about liquidity.

## Debrief: fund the dates first

For the ladder, interest is HK$60,000 × 2.4% × 30/360 = HK$120; HK$90,000 × 3.0% × 60/360 = HK$450; and HK$150,000 × 3.6% × 90/360 = HK$1,350. Total is HK$1,920. B earns HK$30 on its first cash balance instead of HK$120, so total interest is HK$1,830. The initial price of keeping HK$60,000 liquid is HK$90, not the whole interest on the reserve.

## Price the actual early-exit branch

The discount is HK$300. A gives up HK$120 of interest and pays HK$200, making early payment HK$20 worse than keeping its original schedule. Equivalently, the broken deposit returns HK$59,800; after the HK$59,700 supplier payment, HK$100 remains, compared with HK$120 interest under the original route. A should decline this optional offer under the stated cash objective.

B earns HK$15 for the first 15 days rather than HK$30 over 30 days. It gives up HK$15 of interest but saves HK$300, a net HK$285 improvement. After early payment it retains HK$300 of principal plus HK$15 interest, compared with HK$30 originally. B should accept if the operational and contractual facts remain as stated.

## Liquidity has a price and a use

With the best choice in each branch, A retains its original HK$1,920 cash benefit; B has HK$1,830 + HK$285 = HK$2,115, or HK$195 more. That realised opportunity does not prove B was always optimal at day 0: no frequency or probability of such offers was supplied. Keep a maturity calendar and deliberate liquidity buffer rather than infer a universal rule from one favourable event.

## Instructor note

Have students calculate interest for each actual holding period, not treat an annual percentage as the return for 30 days. State the round-two branch reset clearly: deposits are already placed, and early exit is specifically approved at a cost. Declining is legitimate because the original supplier arrangement remains available. The lesson is contractual maturity matching, not bank failure, deposit-protection limits, personal investment advice or an instruction to undertake a real transaction.
