# A lower rate. A less certain payment?

## In the news

On 9 June 2025, Reuters reported abrupt movements in Hong Kong interest rates following capital inflows and currency-market interventions. Lower borrowing costs were welcome for some businesses, but a low rate today did not guarantee low costs throughout a loan.

The fictional workshop below faces that planning question. Its quotes, cash figures and future rate paths are invented, not forecasts of Hong Kong rates or offers from any lender.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A Hong Kong furniture-repair workshop has approved an equipment purchase and needs HK$240,000. You must provisionally choose between two otherwise comparable 12-month financing quotes. F fixes the annual rate at 6%. V starts at 4% for month 1 and resets monthly thereafter.

Both repay HK$20,000 principal at each month-end, plus interest on that month’s opening principal. Interest equals opening principal × annual rate ÷ 12. The floating rates in the exhibit already include the lender’s fixed margin: do not add it again.

There are no extra fees, caps, floors, early repayment or refinancing in this exercise. Security and other terms are identical and assumed acceptable solely to isolate rate exposure. Ignore taxes, day-count differences and interest earned on retained cash. Neither quote has been accepted.

All rates are annual. F remains 6% throughout every path. These are illustrations, not forecasts or an exhaustive set of possibilities. No probabilities are supplied. Opening principal is HK$240,000, HK$220,000 and HK$200,000 in months 1–3, then falls HK$20,000 per month to HK$20,000 in month 12. The sum of all 12 opening balances is HK$1,560,000; months 2–12 sum to HK$1,320,000. These are calculation aids, not additional borrowing.

**Student question:** Which quote would you provisionally favour, and why? Compare financing costs across the three supplied paths. What cash evidence would you need before choosing?

## Round two

The bookkeeper now supplies the first three months’ cash available for interest **after operating bills, scheduled principal and the agreed minimum cash buffer**:

| Month | New cash for the interest-payment pot |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | HK$1,200 |
| 2 | HK$1,100 |

These are new monthly contributions, not cumulative balances. There is no initial spare pot. Retain every unused dollar for later months, including savings from V’s lower starting rate. The cash figures and principal timetable are the same under both quotes. No extra credit is approved.

Months 4–12 can meet principal and either quote’s interest under each illustrated path, provided this early bottleneck is crossed. The owners require the choice to pass all three supplied paths without breaching their buffer. The rising path is a planning test, not a predicted outcome.

**Student question:** Does your choice pass the first three months under the rising path, even after retaining earlier savings? Recommend F, or state the confirmed change needed before V could meet the owners’ rule.

## Same principal, different interest exposure

F costs HK$1,560,000 × 6% ÷ 12 = **HK$7,800** in interest. V starts with HK$240,000 × 4% ÷ 12 = HK$800. The remaining interest uses the HK$1,320,000 sum of opening balances for months 2–12.

| Quote and path | Interest | Total repayments, including HK$240,000 principal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F, every supplied path | HK$7,800 | HK$247,800 |
| V, falling | HK$3,000 | HK$243,000 |
| V, unchanged | HK$5,200 | HK$245,200 |
| V, rising stress | HK$9,600 | HK$249,600 |

These are undiscounted totals, not annual percentage rates or present values. No probabilities are given, so an expected cost cannot be calculated.

## Carry the earlier savings forward

Under the rising path, V’s first three interest bills are HK$800, HK$1,466.67 and HK$1,333.33. The pot starts empty. After month 1 it holds HK$400. After month 2 it holds about HK$33.33. Month 3 then has about HK$1,133.33 available against HK$1,333.33 due: a **HK$200 shortfall**.

Equivalently, cumulative contributions are HK$3,400 and cumulative V interest is HK$3,600. A negative projected pot identifies an unfunded payment, not an overdraft the workshop can use. Displayed monthly figures are rounded; the cumulative calculation uses exact values.

F’s first three interest bills are HK$1,200, HK$1,100 and HK$1,000. Its pot ends those months at HK$0, HK$0 and HK$100. Both quotes also repay the same HK$60,000 principal over these months; that is already funded separately in the cash plan.

## A rule about resilience, not a rate prediction

F passes the owners’ stated test. V does not yet. To pass this specific early stress test, V needs at least HK$200 of additional usable cash confirmed by month 3, without weakening the minimum buffer or creating another unfunded obligation. Later income, hoped-for credit or an unagreed lender concession is not available cash.

That HK$200 is not protection against every possible rate or business shock. In a real review, inspect the full payment calendar and the terms and consequences of any added funding. Deferring the commitment while arranging a credible change is different from assuming the change exists.

## Certainty has a scenario-dependent cost

In the unchanged path, F costs HK$2,600 more than V; in the falling path it costs HK$4,800 more. In the rising path it costs HK$1,800 less. A fixed quote is neither automatically cheapest nor free protection.

Certainty means knowing the scheduled payments, not paying an equal amount every month. Here F’s total payment declines as principal reduces. Fixing the rate does not protect against weak sales, operating losses or default. With greater verified cash capacity, choosing V may be defensible without claiming an ability to forecast rates.

## Back to the Hong Kong context

The reported rate movements explain why today’s attractive borrowing cost need not settle a financing decision. The sources do not establish this workshop’s rates or cash capacity.

For actual quotes, check the benchmark, reset dates, margin, fees, calculation method, security, covenants and early-repayment conditions. The exercise isolates exposure and affordability; it is not a recommendation to take a real loan or use a particular lender.

## Instructor note

Allow two minutes for the news and role, four for the first decision and path comparison, three for the cash reveal, and three for the discussion. Keep the debrief hidden until the second vote. Supply completed annual totals if arithmetic would distract beginners from the decision.

Ask V supporters to carry its initial saving forward, then identify a confirmed resource that would close the stress gap. Ask F supporters to explain the cost they accept if rates stay low. Neither group should invent probabilities or treat the three paths as exhaustive.

Unlike FIN-06, both quotes use the same principal repayment schedule. Unlike FIN-03, customer payment timing does not change. The new mechanism is uncertain interest expense meeting a dated cash constraint. An alternative conclusion requires different verified cash capacity, quote terms or risk tolerance, not simply a confident rate forecast.
