# Rates fell. Why did the loan cost more?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 488 -->

ECO-44 · Kwun Tong, Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Monetary transmission · Credit spreads · Interest coverage

![Two colleagues at a Kwun Tong sign workshop compare loan proposals beside rolled print material.](../rate-cut-credit-spread-scene.jpg)

## Reported context

In December 2024 the US Federal Reserve cut its target range by a quarter point and the HKMA set its Base Rate at 4.75%. In the same month the HKMA’s measure of banks’ average funding cost rose from 2.20% to 2.24%. The Base Rate, average funding cost and one borrower’s loan rate are different measures.

## Opening decision

Kwun Tong signmaker Blue Arc needs to renew a fictional HK$10 million facility for one year. Owner Bo’s lender offers a new rate despite a lower reference benchmark. The other lender’s offer carries a one-time fee rather than a wider spread.

All options below use constant annual rates for the comparison; there is no amortisation. The old facility cannot be renewed. Current annual earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) are HK$1.4 million. Blue Arc wants coverage of at least 2.0 times annual financing cost, including any renewal fee, as an internal screen.

## Evidence

| Facility | Benchmark | Spread | One-time fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old loan, for comparison | 4.0% | 2.0% | HK$0 |
| A: incumbent | 3.5% | 3.0% | HK$0 |
| B: challenger | 3.5% | 2.0% | HK$120,000 |

**Student question:** Which offer is cheaper for the year, and how can the lower benchmark leave this workshop paying more than before?

## Round two

A major customer postpones new signage; annual EBIT now looks like HK$1.2 million. Assume the offers are still available and the proposed loan would remain fully drawn for the year.

Bo may use unrestricted cash to repay part of the old balance before renewing the remainder under A, provided at least HK$500,000 remains. He has HK$1.5 million of unrestricted cash before repayment. Under A the rate and spread apply equally to any remaining balance.

**Student question:** How much principal must Bo repay to meet the coverage screen under A, and can he do it while preserving the reserve?

## Debrief

Old cost was 6.0% × HK$10 million = HK$600,000. A costs 6.5%, or HK$650,000. A half-point benchmark cut is outweighed by a one-point spread increase. B costs 5.5% × HK$10 million plus HK$120,000 = HK$670,000. A is cheaper, and both clear 2.0 times at HK$1.4 million EBIT.

At HK$1.2 million EBIT, annual financing cost cannot exceed HK$600,000. Under A, loan principal can be at most HK$600,000 ÷ 0.065, about HK$9.231 million, so Bo must repay about HK$769,231, rounding repayment up to whole dollars.

That leaves about HK$730,769 of unrestricted cash, above the HK$500,000 reserve. Verify the downside cash forecast and payment terms before committing. A smaller spread or a steadier order book could also restore coverage.

## Instructor note

Ask pairs to separate benchmark, credit spread and fee first. Then let them calculate the largest sustainable balance and check the separate liquidity constraint.

What would convince the credit committee that a weak order book, rather than benchmark repricing, is the main reason the coverage screen fails?

## Boundary of the evidence

This case uses annual financing cost including a fee as an internal screen, not as a contractual covenant definition or a financing recommendation. The public rates and averages do not establish why any real lender changed a quote.
