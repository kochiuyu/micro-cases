# A business price. A price for the shares?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 386 -->

FIN-33 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Enterprise and equity value · Cash and debt bridge · Restricted cash

![Two fictional adult equipment-maintenance colleagues review a plain sale worksheet beside a tool cabinet, with separate folders for company balances and lender payoff.](../business-price-share-price-scene.jpg)

## One headline, different recipients

An enterprise-value headline and a shareholder’s cash receipt answer different questions. Valuation teaching separates operating-business value from equity, while purchase agreements define which cash and debt enter a price adjustment. Those definitions need checking before treating a bank balance as additional sale proceeds.

## Opening decision

You prepare a sale worksheet for the sole founder of fictional Hong Kong equipment-maintenance business Harbour Bench. A prospective buyer proposes HK$10 million enterprise value for all ordinary shares. This is a supplied negotiated headline, not an independently assessed fair value. Nothing has been accepted or signed.

The proposed price formula is: cash paid for all shares equals enterprise value, plus eligible surplus cash, minus the debt payoff amount. The founder owns 100%; there are no other shareholders, preferred claims, options or conversion rights. The buyer pays the resulting share price entirely in cash at closing.

The worksheet shows HK$3 million debt payoff and HK$1.4 million in company bank balances. The payoff includes all principal, interest and lender charges. For the first calculation, provisionally treat every bank dollar as eligible surplus cash. This classification has not yet been verified; it is not a contractual assurance.

All bank balances remain in the acquired company. The buyer separately funds the lender’s HK$3 million payoff, without using company cash. The subtraction in the share-price formula already accounts for that debt. The founder does not receive company bank balances separately, and no pre-closing dividend or withdrawal is permitted.

For this simplified exercise, the working-capital adjustment is zero and there are no transaction fees, taxes, escrow holdbacks, deferred payments, earnouts or other price adjustments. No other debt or debt-like item applies. All figures refer to the same closing instant; there is no intervening cash movement.

The founder’s stated minimum immediate share-sale receipt is HK$8 million. Your task is to say whether the worksheet supports that minimum, not whether selling is wise. There is no alternative offer, forecast, valuation multiple or information about future personal needs. Do not send an acceptance or arrange any transaction.

**Student question:** Under the provisional cash assumption, what would the founder receive for the shares? Does it meet the HK$8 million minimum, and which classification must be checked before relying on that answer?

## Round two

The closing review identifies HK$500,000 of the bank balances in a restricted deposit supporting an ongoing premises agreement. It cannot be withdrawn, distributed or used for debt repayment at closing. It stays with the acquired company after the share sale. The remaining HK$900,000 is verified eligible surplus cash.

The proposed contract expressly excludes that restricted deposit from the cash add-back. Its benefit and the associated operating arrangement are already covered by the unchanged HK$10 million enterprise-value headline. Neither creates a further debt or working-capital adjustment. There is no later seller payment or retained founder claim when restrictions end.

These are explicit fictional bargain terms, not a rule that every restricted deposit has no value or must always be excluded. The accounting label on the bank-balance schedule does not override this purchase-price definition. Debt payoff, payment mechanics and all other assumptions remain unchanged.

Update the immediate receipt and the gap to the founder’s minimum. Holding eligible cash and debt fixed, calculate the enterprise-value headline that would just meet the minimum. You may identify a negotiation requirement, but no buyer has agreed to a higher price and no release of the deposit may be assumed.

**Student question:** What does the founder now receive, and does the proposal meet the minimum? Reconcile shareholder and lender payments without counting debt or cash twice, then find the headline needed to deliver HK$8 million under the same terms.

## Debrief · Start with a conditional bridge

The first worksheet gives HK$10.0m + HK$1.4m − HK$3.0m = HK$8.4m for the shares. That is HK$0.4m above the founder’s minimum. The result is conditional on all HK$1.4m qualifying for the add-back. It does not turn the enterprise-value headline itself into the founder’s receipt.

## Debrief · Classify before adding

After review, eligible cash is HK$1.4m − HK$0.5m = HK$0.9m. The share price becomes HK$10.0m + HK$0.9m − HK$3.0m = HK$7.9m. The founder’s immediate receipt falls HK$0.5m from the provisional estimate and misses the HK$8m minimum by HK$0.1m.

The restricted deposit has not vanished. It remains an asset within the acquired company, subject to its restriction. Under these particular terms its treatment is already within the headline, with no extra add-back or separate deduction. Subtracting HK$0.5m again from HK$7.9m would count the exclusion twice.

## Debrief · Follow the two recipients

The buyer pays the founder HK$7.9m and the lender HK$3.0m: total external closing payments HK$10.9m. That equals the HK$10.0m headline plus HK$0.9m eligible cash retained in the acquired company. The bank balances are not a third payment to the founder.

Do not subtract the loan again from the founder’s HK$7.9m: its effect is already in the bridge. Conversely, the buyer’s HK$10.9m total includes lender payment and is not shareholder proceeds. Buyer funding needs, company assets and seller receipts are separate quantities, even though one worksheet reconciles them.

## Debrief · A minimum, not an accepted offer

Let H be the required headline in HK$ million. H + 0.9 − 3.0 = 8.0 gives H = 10.1. A HK$0.1m increase would exactly meet the stated minimum if every other term stayed fixed. There is no evidence that the buyer will agree.

Report that the current proposal fails the founder’s minimum; do not recommend accepting it on the old HK$8.4m worksheet. The founder could seek a changed proposal or decline this one. Releasing or extracting the deposit is not an available shortcut, and a future contingent payment would not meet an immediate-cash objective.

## Debrief · Definitions are part of the calculation

Before using a real bridge, reconcile the balance date, cash eligibility, payoff statement and overlap with other adjustments. Here those facts are supplied. A restricted balance’s accounting presentation does not by itself decide its negotiated price treatment. Different agreed terms could produce a different bridge without changing the recorded bank balance.

This exercise establishes a contractual amount for all ordinary shares. It does not value the business from forecasts, certify legal enforceability or estimate after-tax personal wealth. Unlike FIN-29, there is no division among preferred and ordinary claims: determining the equity amount comes before any such distribution problem.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: calculate the provisional bridge and mark its unverified assumption. Minutes 4–8: reveal the deposit terms and replace only eligible cash. Minutes 8–12: draw separate arrows to founder and lender. Minutes 12–16: derive the required headline and challenge double deductions or an invented deposit release.

FIN-30 distinguishes a reserve from repayment of a maturing loan; FIN-32 changes leverage and equity returns. Here closing debt is given and the issue is the price boundary between enterprise and equity. Keep the restricted deposit’s specified treatment visible: neither a general valuation discount nor a share-class waterfall is being taught.
