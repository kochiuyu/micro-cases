# A smaller price tag. A cheaper quantity?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 315 -->

MKT-27 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Unit pricing · Pack-size comparison · Customer usage requirements

![A shopper compares two sizes of identical chilled sauce at a shelf with plain label holders.](../smaller-pack-cheaper-quantity-scene.jpg)

## Common units improve comparison, not every purchase decision

On 15 March 2021, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council reported that inconsistent quantity units complicated supermarket price comparisons and recommended common unit-price displays. Its survey also found that larger packs were not invariably cheaper per unit. Those historical findings do not establish today’s prices or label law.

The packs and shopping requirements below are fictional. They illustrate why a standard unit price and the amount a customer actually needs answer different questions.

## Opening decision

You manage shelf information for a fictional Hong Kong grocery shop. It sells the same chilled fresh sauce in two sealed pack sizes. Ingredients, quality, suitability and storage instructions are identical, with no promotions, membership prices or hidden charges. The assortment, prices and available stock will not change. Customers may buy any whole-number combination of packs; loose portions are not sold.

Choose one truthful label layout for both sizes within an already funded redesign. Ticket-led gives the total pack price the strongest visual emphasis, with net weight and price per 100 g clearly readable beside it. Paired makes total price and price per 100 g equally prominent in aligned blocks, with net weight beside the product name. Both meet all applicable requirements and accessibility checks; neither hides the total, quantity or unit price. The printing cost and label area are the same.

The goal is to help shoppers compare immediate spend and quantity quickly, not to steer everyone into one pack. Before seeing shoppers’ usage plans, calculate both unit prices and choose a provisional layout. Each layout has room for the same optional short reminder without reducing required text.

## Round two

Two shoppers explain their requirements for tonight. Shopper A needs exactly 250 g; Shopper B needs exactly 600 g. Neither has sauce at home. Both can afford any relevant pack combination, but each wants the lowest total outlay that supplies the required amount. For these shoppers, any surplus has no further usable value: they cannot share, freeze or use it before its stated safe-use deadline. They will not consume extra sauce merely to avoid leftovers.

All purchased sauce needed tonight can be stored and used safely according to the same product instructions. Do not extend a safety deadline or invent later use. Pack prices remain HK$12 and HK$24. Explain each shopper’s least-cost purchase and any surplus, then decide whether your layout needs the optional reminder. Do not label either pack universally cheapest or best value.

**Student question:** What should A and B buy at the lowest outlay for their stated needs, and how would you keep the label helpful without treating the lowest unit price as the cheapest purchase for everyone?

## The first label needs two kinds of price

Small: HK$12 for 250 g, or HK$4.80 per 100 g. Large: HK$24 for 600 g, or HK$4.00 per 100 g. The small pack has the lower ticket price; the large pack has the lower common-unit price. Neither statement determines every shopper’s total purchase cost.

## For 250 g, the smaller ticket wins

A buys one small pack for HK$12 and uses all 250 g. One large pack would cost HK$24 and leave 350 g unusable. Buying two small packs also costs HK$24 and leaves 250 g. A saves HK$12 by choosing the single small pack despite its higher unit price.

## For 600 g, count whole packs

B buys one large pack for HK$24 and uses all 600 g. Two small packs cost HK$24 but supply only 500 g, so they do not meet the need. Three small packs cost HK$36, supply 750 g and leave 150 g unusable. The lowest ticket price per pack therefore does not produce B’s lowest relevant outlay.

## Choose transparency rather than a universal winner

Paired is a strong choice because the immediate outlay and comparable quantity price are visible together. Ticket-led can still serve a shopper managing cash if its unit-price information remains clear. Add a neutral reminder such as: Compare price per 100 g, then buy the quantity you can use. Keep price, quantity and safe-use information intact.

Do not redefine the shelf unit price as price per consumed gram: that would vary with the customer. The shelf calculation remains based on purchased net quantity. The shopper separately compares feasible whole-pack combinations and unusable surplus. No evidence here establishes which layout will increase sales or how often either need occurs.

## Instructor note

Give three minutes to calculate the labels and choose a layout. Reveal the two needs, then give four minutes to calculate feasible baskets. Ask why two small packs are not an option for B despite matching the large pack’s price. Require both outlay and surplus, not a unit-price ranking alone.

This holds assortment and prices fixed. It is not reference-price framing or introducing a new pack to study cannibalisation. Both layouts remain lawful and accessible. No unsafe storage, food-waste pressure or hidden information is a legitimate option. Public overseas guidance supports comparison principles, not a declaration of Hong Kong legal requirements.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All products, pack sizes, prices, shoppers and usage constraints are fictional. Both layouts comply with applicable requirements. Surplus is unusable only under the supplied shopper circumstances, not a claim about all households. Public overseas pricing guidance is not a legal opinion for Hong Kong. The two CMA sources are related institutional materials.

- [Hong Kong Consumer Council, Inconsistent Unit Measurement of Products in Supermarkets (15 March 2021)](https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/533-unit-pricing-of-supermarket-products). Primary historical survey release supports common-unit comparison and cautions against assuming bigger means cheaper per unit. No survey price is reused.
- [Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, Unit pricing](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/pricing/unit-pricing). Explains common measurement units and clear, nearby, legible unit-price information. Australian legal coverage is not imposed on the fictional shop.
- [NIST SP 1181-2025, Unit Pricing Guide](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/1181e2025/NIST.SP.1181e2025.html). Primary design guidance discusses alignment, clarity, prominence and keeping total and unit prices visible. No source label image is reproduced.
- [UK Competition and Markets Authority, Summary of consumer research and unit pricing analysis (2024)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unit-pricing-analysis-and-consumer-research/summary-of-consumer-research-and-unit-pricing-analysis). Reports qualitative findings on visibility, inconsistent units and shopper concerns about fresh-food waste. Findings are indicative, not a representative estimate of these fictional shoppers.
- [UK Competition and Markets Authority, Unit pricing: information for consumers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unit-pricing-information-for-consumers/unit-pricing-information-for-consumers). Explains comparing equivalent weight or volume alongside pack prices. The case uses original pack sizes and numbers.
