# Two versions. Two separate groups?

## In the news

On 13 August 2026, HKPC announced an AI and Digital Transformation Zone for the Hong Kong Computer & Communications Festival, scheduled for 21–24 August, with practical tools and guidance.

This was an announced initiative, not an effectiveness study. The ordinary workflow feature below is fictional and unrelated to the exhibitors.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong software company supplies a shared workboard for routine event-preparation administration. It wants to compare a structured handover-note helper with its existing free-text editor. Both permit the same information; the helper guides writers through prompts.

The business question is: would enabling the helper for an entire customer team reduce time preparing and clarifying handovers without reducing completeness? Twenty-four consenting teams of ten adult users have volunteered for a four-week evaluation. Their workspaces and operational assignments are separate; no user belongs to two trial teams.

Both versions have passed basic usability, security and accessibility checks. Staff still verify notes, deadlines and operational decisions. The feature changes no safety decisions, payment rights, access controls or staffing. Participation, data protection and limited measurement are agreed; results will evaluate the product, not rank employees.

Both allocation plans are technically feasible and funded, including analyst support. Outcomes are aggregate staff time on the handovers and completeness under the same rubric. The analysis plan and assessment of whether the trial can answer the question must be completed before launch.

## Evidence

| Plan | A · Assign people | B · Assign whole teams |
|---|---|---|
| Random assignment | Within each team, five users receive each version | Twelve whole teams receive each version |
| Users per version | 24 × 5 = 120 | 12 × 10 = 120 |
| Team experience | Every team contains both versions | Each team uses one version |
| Initial attraction | Compare users working in the same local setting | Keep the team experience consistent |

There are 240 users in total. A assigns versions to people within teams; B makes 24 team-level assignments. Equal user totals do not imply equal precision. Team differences matter under B; more recorded notes do not create more independently assigned teams.

**Student question:** Would you assign people or whole teams? Which choice gives a fair comparison for the business question, and what could make you change your mind?

## Round two

A pre-launch walkthrough confirms that each completed handover note immediately enters the shared team board. A colleague using the old editor can read and act on a structured note written with the helper. That colleague might need less clarification, or experience a different effect. No time saving has yet been measured.

The edit button a person receives does not isolate the information reaching them. This is normal collaboration, not a privacy breach or a software defect.

No notes cross the 24 customer workspaces within the agreed trial scope. Monitor departures from that scope rather than assuming all human contact is impossible. Do not hide useful notes or stop normal teamwork simply to preserve a comparison.

**Student question:** Keep A or choose B for the stated whole-team question? Explain who the apparent control users could be learning from, and one limitation that still remains under your chosen plan.

## Follow the note, not just the button

Under A, an old-editor user can receive a helper-written note from a colleague. Their clarification time may therefore depend on someone else’s assignment. The effect could help or hinder; its direction and size are unknown.

Random assignment is still useful. The problem is treating editor access as if it separated the whole information experience.

## Match the comparison to the question

B better matches the stated question: compare teams where everyone has the helper with teams where everyone has the old editor. Shared notes remain within one assigned condition under the stipulated workflow.

A could instead study access to the writing helper when half the team has it. That is a different question, not automatically the effect of enabling an entire team. Individual assignment is not universally invalid.

## Count assigned groups honestly

B has 12 teams per version and 24 team-level assignments. Its 240 users, or thousands of note records, are not hundreds or thousands of independently assigned teams.

The analyst must account for group assignment and between-team differences. Baseline workload, a consistent completeness rubric and predefined outcomes remain important. No effect size or variability is supplied, so neither four weeks nor 24 teams is established as sufficient.

## A cleaner design is not a successful feature

Monitor whether cross-team contact or workflow changes undermine the intended separation. Preserve normal collaboration and describe limitations rather than engineering an artificial workplace just for tidy results.

If the study cannot distinguish meaningful differences reliably, report that limit. Choosing B does not establish time savings, better handovers, sales, cash savings or a reason to cut jobs.

## Instructor note

Have students trace a note from its writer to its reader before naming the assignment unit. The reveal concerns information reaching the apparent control group, not a measured improvement or a reason to stop colleagues sharing useful work.

Keep the business question fixed when judging A and B. A has a plausible initial within-team comparison; after the reveal, B better represents all-enabled versus all-old teams. Ask what B does not solve rather than awarding it an automatic effectiveness verdict.

Technical extension: this is interference between users, and B uses cluster randomisation. Distinguish the unit assigned from the unit observed; do not equate 24 clusters with a precisely calculated effective sample size. No significance calculation is required.
