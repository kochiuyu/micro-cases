# The subsidiary earned a profit. Why did its net assets rise less in HKD?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 528 -->

ACC-44 · Hong Kong and Singapore · 15 minutes · Foreign currency translation · Consolidation · Other comprehensive income · Monetary items

![A Hong Kong controller compares local subsidiary results and a consolidation worksheet at a shared office table.](../the-subsidiary-earned-where-did-equity-go-scene.jpg)

## Consolidated statements tell two exchange-rate stories

HKAS 21 distinguishes translating a foreign operation for group reporting from remeasuring a foreign-currency transaction in the entity’s own records. Hong Kong Monetary Authority exchange-rate data identify the quote direction explicitly. The exchange rates and company below are fictional.

## Opening decision

You prepare a Hong Kong parent’s consolidated accounts. It owns 100% of a Singapore operating subsidiary. The subsidiary’s functional currency, the currency of its main operating environment, is SGD. The parent’s functional currency and the group’s presentation currency are HKD. Neither economy is treated as hyperinflationary.

At the start of the year, the subsidiary had SGD1.0m in net assets. It earned SGD0.2m during the year and ended with SGD1.2m. No dividends, capital changes, local OCI, goodwill, intercompany balances or other adjustments affect this exercise. The income and expense transactions that make up the profit all occurred at the same stipulated exchange rate.

The financial controller sees an increase in local net assets. Another manager expects the translated HKD net assets to rise by exactly the translated HKD profit and proposes putting the shortfall through group operating profit.

## Evidence

| Fictional exchange rate | HKD for SGD1 |
| --- | --- |
| Previous year-end closing rate | 5.80 |
| Transaction date rate for the subsidiary’s income and expenses | 5.60 |
| Current year-end closing rate | 5.20 |

**Student question:** What HKD profit and opening and closing net assets come from the subsidiary? What is the residual, and where should it be reported?

## Round two

Now add a transaction in the Hong Kong parent, not in the subsidiary. On the day the rate was HK$5.60 per SGD1, the parent received an outside vendor’s services and recorded an SGD100,000 invoice. The full amount remains payable at year-end when the rate is HK$5.20. There is no hedge and no tax effect.

**Student question:** Calculate the parent’s initial and year-end payable, and explain where the exchange difference belongs. Should it be netted into the subsidiary translation line?

## Debrief · Translate the activity and the position

Translate the subsidiary’s income and expenses at their transaction-date rate, giving HK$1.12m profit. Opening net assets were SGD1.0m × 5.80 = HK$5.80m. Closing net assets are SGD1.2m × 5.20 = HK$6.24m. Their HKD increase is HK$0.44m, not HK$1.12m.

## Debrief · Put the difference where it belongs

The bridge is HK$5.80m + HK$1.12m − HK$0.68m = HK$6.24m. The HK$0.68m translation loss is in other comprehensive income and accumulates in the separate exchange reserve within equity. It does not reduce group operating profit and does not mean the subsidiary lost HK$0.68m in cash.

## Debrief · Trace the two components

Re-expressing opening net assets at the new closing rate contributes SGD1.0m × (5.20 − 5.80) = −HK$0.60m. Re-expressing current profit from the transaction rate to the closing rate contributes SGD0.2m × (5.20 − 5.60) = −HK$0.08m. Closing net assets still rose compared with opening; translation is the negative component.

## Debrief · A payable is not a foreign operation

The HKD-functional parent initially records a HK$560,000 service expense and payable. Remeasure the monetary payable at 5.20, giving HK$520,000 and a HK$40,000 exchange gain in profit or loss. No cash has yet been paid. This gain stays separate from the subsidiary’s HK$680,000 loss in other comprehensive income.

## Instructor note

Give four minutes to trace the rates, four to bridge net assets, four to handle the payable and three to explain the statement distinction. Ask students to state the rate units before multiplying. Real groups may face other exchange rules for hedges, qualifying net investments and non-controlling interests; none is modelled here.
