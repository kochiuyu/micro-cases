# The coach has seats. Does it have room?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 516 -->

HSP-42 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Hotel shuttle operations · Multiple capacity limits · Service recovery · Reservation design

![A hotel transport coordinator and a coach driver compare a row of passengers’ cases beside the vehicle’s luggage bay.](../the-coach-has-seats-scene.jpg)

## A hotel shuttle needs room for passengers and luggage

A Regal Airport Hotel shuttle booklet revised on 16 December 2025 described first-come, first-served service and separate luggage limits. That archived booklet illustrates why a seat does not settle whether a traveller and their bags can ride. This case uses a fictional hotel and a different booking policy.

## Opening decision

An airport hotel’s transport coordinator must send 32 guests from the airport on one shuttle departure or book them on alternatives. Everyone is confirmed to travel and can depart on either service. The coach has 28 seats and 36 secure luggage units. No luggage can go outside that bay.

Eight guests each have three luggage units. Call them heavy-luggage guests. Twenty-four guests each have one unit. Everyone’s luggage is already within the applicable operator limits, but each guest must travel with all their bags.

The current approach serves guests in arrival order. The eight heavy-luggage guests arrive before the others. Management wants at least 26 guests on the shuttle and seeks the lowest total cost among plans that meet that target. The usual coach costs the same either way; only the listed alternative fares and any coach surcharge change.

## Evidence

| Guest type | Guests | Units per guest | Alternative cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy luggage | 8 | 3 | HK$260 |
| Light luggage | 24 | 1 | HK$80 |

**Student question:** Does first-come, first-served meet the target? If the hotel reserves places by group in advance, which mix meets the target at the lowest alternative-transfer cost?

## Round two

The dispatch company offers a coach with the same 28 seats but 40 secure luggage units for an extra HK$180. The guest mix, transfer costs and service target are unchanged. Advance allocation is allowed with either coach.

**Student question:** Should the coordinator buy the larger luggage bay? Give the lowest-cost manifest with each coach and report the most the hotel should pay for the larger bay.

## Debrief · Count both kinds of capacity

The first eight guests take 24 luggage units. The remaining 12 units carry 12 of the other guests, bringing the total to 20 with eight empty seats. Alternatives cost 12 × HK$80 = HK$960, but the service target is missed.

## Debrief · A fuller coach can cost more

For 26, 27 or 28 guests on the usual coach, start with one luggage unit per guest, then add two for each heavy-luggage guest. The most heavy-luggage places are 5, 4 or 4. Choose those maxima for each total because displaced heavy luggage costs more.

## Debrief · Choose 26 on the usual coach

At 26, send five heavy-luggage and 21 light-luggage guests. Alternatives for three of each cost HK$1,020. The best 27-guest mix costs HK$1,120 and the best 28-guest mix costs HK$1,040. The lowest cost is not the fullest coach.

## Debrief · Price the extra luggage space

With 40 units, the best 26-, 27- and 28-guest mixes give heavy-luggage places of 7, 6 and 6. The respective alternative costs are HK$660, HK$760 and HK$680. Choose seven heavy-luggage and 19 light-luggage guests. Add HK$180 for a total of HK$840. The larger bay saves HK$180 at that quote and ties at a surcharge of HK$360.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to diagnose first-come loading, 5 to compare mixes, and 4 to discuss. Ask how to communicate the reshuffle and handle a new bag count without separating guests from luggage. Treat costs and policy as fictional, not as guidance on actual vehicle loading.
