# Twenty per cent less. Less of what?

Case 45 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Governance · Environmental claims · Measurement scope

![A hotel manager and communications colleague discuss a draft card and report in a warm office overlooking a courtyard.](../less-of-what-scene.jpg)

## In the news

A 1 June 2025 Hong Kong Government feature described the Water-smart Taskforce programme, using meter data and tailored advice to help participants understand water use. A hotel-group representative discussed the value of understanding consumption patterns.

The feature’s programme-wide savings target was not a verified hotel result. Our hotel, dashboard and proposed advertisement below are fictional. The decision is about approving accurate wording, not judging any real organisation’s advertising.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. You are the manager responsible for signing off a Hong Kong hotel’s booking-page claims. After work on water management, your team wants to celebrate a measured improvement.

The checked dashboard reports water use per occupied room-night: total metered property water divided by the number of occupied room-nights. One room occupied for one night counts once, regardless of how many guests share it. This is not a measure of water used only inside guest bedrooms.

The marketing team proposes the headline “Our hotel uses 20% less water.” It would appear without a denominator or comparison period. Nothing has been published yet. You have the intensity figures below but have not yet seen total use or occupancy counts.

## Evidence

| Dashboard measure | Previous year | Current year |
|---|---|---|
| Litres per occupied room-night | 500 | 400 |

Verified fictional intensity reduction: (500 − 400) ÷ 500 = 20%.

**Student question:** Would you approve “Our hotel uses 20% less water”, rewrite it, or hold it for more information? What comparison would a guest reasonably understand?

## Round two

The property recorded 10,000 occupied room-nights in the previous year and 15,000 in the current year. The figures cover the same property, equal 12-month periods and an unchanged measurement boundary.

No premises have been added or removed, and neither meter coverage nor outsourcing of operations has changed. The intensity figures are exact for this exercise, so multiplying them by occupied room-nights recovers total metered water use.

The dashboard does not establish which particular action caused the intensity change, or measure carbon, waste or overall environmental performance.

**Student question:** What exact wording would you approve now? Calculate total water use in both years and explain how you would communicate the genuine improvement without overstating it.

## Reconstruct the totals

Previous year: 500 litres × 10,000 occupied room-nights = 5 million litres.

Current year: 400 litres × 15,000 occupied room-nights = 6 million litres.

Intensity fell 20%, but total metered use rose (6 − 5) ÷ 5 = 20%. Both statements can be true. Occupied room-nights rose 50%, more than offsetting the lower intensity in this arithmetic.

## Approve a claim with its basis visible

A defensible version is: “Water use per occupied room-night fell 20% from the previous year, from 500 to 400 litres. Total metered property use rose from 5 million to 6 million litres while occupied room-nights increased.”

Put the denominator, comparison period and total-use context where readers see the claim, not only behind a small link. In an actual publication, name the reporting years and property boundary and retain the evidence behind the calculation.

You need not suppress the real intensity improvement. But the original unqualified headline could be understood as a fall in total consumption, which the supplied evidence does not support.

## Do not extend the claim beyond the evidence

The figures alone do not prove that a particular device or campaign caused the improvement. For example, changes in occupancy can affect water per room-night when some property water use does not vary proportionally with occupancy.

They also do not substantiate “20% greener overall”. Water, energy, emissions and waste are different measures. Approve only the claim supported by the measured scope; review broader claims separately.

Key idea: specify less of what, per what, compared with when, and across which boundary.

## Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the headline vote, 3 for reconstructing totals and 5–7 for drafting a precise replacement. Ask students to preserve the genuine improvement while correcting its scope. Keep this a claim-approval exercise, not an allegation of deliberate deception or a causal evaluation of the real programme. UK source guidance supplies communication principles, not a legal verdict on a Hong Kong advertisement.
