# Open first. Be ready first?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 150 -->

STR-13 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Entry timing · First-mover advantage · Complementary services

![Two studio founders discuss opening plans beside a printmaking press and botanical samples in a ready courtyard workshop.](../open-before-the-network-scene.jpg)

## Industry context

M+ opened to the public on 12 November 2021. Its release distinguished advance reservations from the opening itself; a separate 19 November update reported visits and revised admission arrangements after operation began.

These are different kinds of evidence: an announced date, a reservation and an actual visit. They do not prove that early entry causes success. The small commercial studio and transport uncertainty below are fictional, not an account of M+’s operations.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong creative studio is ready to offer a complete two-hour printmaking workshop at a new cultural cluster. Safety, permissions, accessibility, instruction and ordinary public-transport access are already satisfactory. The narrower early offer is not an unfinished or unsafe service. A separate operator plans an optional direct shuttle from a distant district; some target visitors would find that much more convenient.

Choose A: open in week 1 for an initial four-week programme without promising the shuttle, or B: defer those four weeks and prepare a broader launch around the shuttle’s target start in week 5. A requires HK$24,000 of additional fixed opening costs for weeks 1–4; B avoids that entire amount. Existing rent and already committed staff pay are the same, funded separately and excluded. Either route remains affordable. Each paid visit contributes HK$200 after all visit-variable costs, before the HK$24,000 opening cost. A has capacity for 160 visits over those four weeks. No other incremental costs or revenues for this period are assumed.

There are 80 free, cancellable expressions of interest for the initial four weeks, not paid bookings. No customer has been promised an opening date. A rival has announced a week-3 opening in the same cluster. The founders hope opening first could help them build local relationships and learn how to run sessions; they do not know that customers would stay exclusive. Both routes can adopt the shuttle later if it operates, on the same terms. Neither has a protected location, exclusive supplier or contractual customer lock-in.

## Evidence

| First four weeks only | A · Open | B · Defer |
|---|---|---|
| Additional fixed opening cost | HK$24,000 | HK$0 |
| Contribution per paid visit | HK$200 | No visits in this period |
| Available capacity | 160 visits | No operating programme yet |
| Potential strategic benefit | Operating learning and relationships | Readiness information before launch |

**Student question:** Open in week 1 or defer the first four weeks? Identify the concrete advantage early operation could build, and calculate how many paid visits would cover its extra opening cost.

## Round two

A follow-up with all 80 interested people finds 30 say they would consider visiting using existing transport; 50 say they would consider it only if the direct shuttle operates. All remain free to decline, and nobody pays or reserves a paid place. The groups are not a representative market sample. There is no evidence about additional walk-in demand, and 30 is neither a sales forecast nor a hard ceiling on total attendance.

The shuttle operator has assigned vehicles and completed a route trial, but final scheduling approval is still pending. It gives week 5 as its target and week 7 as a possible later start, with no probability or guarantee for either. It cannot run in weeks 1–4. The rival’s week-3 announcement is still a plan: no opening has occurred or been independently verified. The studio has not committed under either option.

A still buys four weeks of operations and learning without the shuttle, with the same cost and capacity. B avoids those four weeks of opening costs but gains no operating experience or visitor relationships during them. Waiting does not guarantee a shuttle-ready week-5 launch. Before announcing any later date, the studio must check what service it can actually offer and either open truthfully without the shuttle, wait further or revise its plan. Later-period financial outcomes are not supplied and must not be treated as equal or known.

**Student question:** Keep or change your entry-timing choice? Explain what the 30/50 interest split does and does not establish, and set a specific readiness check before announcing the customer offer.

## Being first is a date; advantage is a mechanism

Opening first could develop repeatable session routines and relationships with visitors and nearby partners. Those capabilities might persist after a rival opens, but the date alone does not create them. No exclusive route, protected site or customer switching cost is supplied. Explain what staff would learn and retain, rather than assuming the first sign above a door secures the market.

A later entrant can observe publicly visible operating arrangements and prepare with clearer transport information. It does not automatically acquire the early studio’s internal skills or customer relationships. Early operation and later preparation each offer a different learning opportunity, with no guaranteed winner.

## Put a price on the head start

HK$24,000 ÷ HK$200 = 120 paid visits are needed to cover the extra first-four-week opening cost. That is 120 ÷ 160 = 75% of available capacity. At full capacity, contribution less that cost would be HK$32,000 − HK$24,000 = HK$8,000. These are period cash-contribution calculations, not whole-business profit.

If exactly 30 visits were paid, the period result would be HK$6,000 − HK$24,000 = −HK$18,000. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a forecast based on the survey. Even all 80 interested people paying once would yield −HK$8,000; neither response counts nor capacity establish how many will actually buy. Do not value early learning at zero, but do not invent a monetary return for it either.

## The offer works, but access shapes its audience

The workshop itself is ready and reachable. The proposed shuttle is a complement that could make it more useful or convenient for a particular group. Fifty people expressing a shuttle condition is a reason to examine that dependence, not proof that fifty sales arrive when the shuttle starts.

Assigned vehicles and a route trial provide more information than a vague aspiration, but the unresolved approval still prevents a firm start promise. Waiting until week 5 on a calendar does not resolve the dependency. Likewise, a rival’s planned opening is not an observed first-mover advantage.

## Choose a route with a readiness condition

A is defensible if the founders deliberately value four weeks of operating learning and local relationships enough to bear the possible cash shortfall. Describe the workshop and existing transport accurately, set a paid-booking and learning review, and never advertise an unavailable shuttle. B is defensible if they prioritise launching to the broader intended audience with better readiness information and avoiding the initial HK$24,000 exposure.

For B, set a check before any week-5 announcement: obtain the operator’s confirmed start, timetable and booking terms, then verify that customers can complete the journey. If that is not ready, decide explicitly between a disclosed narrower opening and further deferral using updated costs and demand evidence. Do not keep silently moving a promised date. The supplied first-period arithmetic cannot determine lifetime value or a globally optimal entry date.

## Instructor note

Use 3 minutes for the initial timing vote, 3 for the 120-visit threshold and 5 for the readiness reveal. Ask students to name a durable capability or relationship, not just publicity. Challenge both “all reservations are sales” and “nothing can open without a shuttle.”

The early service is complete and lawful; the complement broadens convenience, not basic safety or accessibility. Accept either route with a coherent opportunity-cost and learning argument. Do not assign probabilities to week 5 versus week 7, treat the interest split as market conversion or assume later cash flows cancel. The sources provide contextual distinctions and strategy reasoning, not a demand forecast for this fictional studio.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

The studio, cluster, rival, shuttle, participants and all costs are original fiction. A opens a complete, lawful workshop programme in weeks 1–4; B defers that period. Existing access and accessibility are satisfactory without the optional shuttle. A incurs HK$24,000 additional fixed costs and earns HK$200 contribution per paid visit, with capacity 160. B has no visits or additional opening costs in that period. Common rent and committed pay are excluded and funded under either route. Break-even is 120 visits, or 75% of capacity. Full-capacity period contribution net of opening cost is HK$8,000; 30 paid visits would yield −HK$18,000 and 80 would yield −HK$8,000. These are sensitivity calculations, not forecasts. The 80 interest responses divide into 30 willing to consider existing transport and 50 conditional on the shuttle; none has paid or made a binding reservation. The shuttle cannot start in weeks 1–4, targets week 5 and may start in week 7, with no supplied probabilities. The rival has only announced a plan. Later-period outcomes are unspecified and not assumed equal. There is no legal or transport approval claimed for an actual service.
