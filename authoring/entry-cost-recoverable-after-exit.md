# Many enquiries. A credible threat of entry?

## Entry needs more than interest

A prospective operator’s interest is not the same as an economically attractive entry plan. Initial funding, operating receipts and recoverable assets answer different questions. Competition authorities examine entry incentives and the extent of irrecoverable expenditure rather than treating every enquiry as evidence that effective new competition will arrive.

## Opening decision

You advise the adult investors of fictional HarbourLink in Hong Kong. They can fund one three-month ferry season or decline with zero incremental cash result. No money has been spent or committed. Several people have enquired about investing; these enquiries are neither funding commitments nor evidence of customer demand.

Both choices are affordable without touching protected reserves. All licensing, docking rights, passenger safety, accessibility and properly paid staffing requirements are stipulated satisfied and fully funded if entry occurs. Existing operators’ prices, schedules and responses are fixed. This is not an actual route proposal or a recommendation to bypass approvals.

Entry requires HK$1,300,000 initially: HK$1,000,000 for a vessel, HK$200,000 for terminal installation and HK$100,000 for startup administration and training. The entire season must be completed; there is no early-exit option. The investor then sells available assets and meets all remaining obligations.

After every voyage, staffing, safety and other operating cost, the season’s operating cash margin is HK$500,000 in the high-demand state or HK$100,000 in the low-demand state. Each state has supplied probability 50%. These margins exclude the initial outlay and asset recoveries. All amounts and probabilities are fictional.

At season end, the vessel yields HK$900,000 net of all selling and disposal costs in either demand state. The initial planning sheet also assumes HK$150,000 net recovery from the installation. Startup administration and training recover nothing. No other receipts, costs, tax, interest, discounting, alternative investment returns or later business value enter this bounded comparison.

The investors’ stipulated rule is to maximise expected incremental net cash, accepting the specified demand risk. Expected net cash equals expected operating margin plus net recoveries minus the initial outlay. Funding needs and possible losses must still be reported. Do not substitute an unstated worst-case or risk-aversion rule.

## Round two

Before any commitment, a technical assessment establishes that the installation is route-specific. It cannot be resold or redeployed after the season: net recovery is zero, not HK$150,000. There is no refund, alternative use or compensation. Its HK$200,000 initial price is unchanged; the vessel’s HK$900,000 net recovery remains certain.

All demand probabilities, operating margins, safety arrangements and other costs remain unchanged. Neither new competition nor a reaction by the existing operator caused this revision. The entry decision is still entirely avoidable. No speculative later seasons or strategic benefits may be added to rescue the investment.

**Student question:** Does entry still maximise expected net cash? Calculate the revised state results and the expected operating margin required for strictly positive entry value. Why is “ignore sunk costs” the wrong instruction before these payments have been committed?

## Debrief · Separate funding from net exposure

Expected operating margin is 0.5 × HK$500,000 + 0.5 × HK$100,000 = HK$300,000. Initially, total recovery is HK$900,000 + HK$150,000 = HK$1,050,000. The initial outlay less recovery is HK$250,000, so expected net cash is HK$50,000. Entry beats declining under the stated rule.

High demand yields HK$500,000 − HK$250,000 = HK$250,000; low demand yields HK$100,000 − HK$250,000 = −HK$150,000. Positive expected value does not guarantee a gain. The business must still fund HK$1,300,000 initially: future resale proceeds cannot be silently counted as cash already available.

## Debrief · Recompute after the reveal

Revised recovery is HK$900,000 only. Outlay less recovery is HK$400,000. Expected net cash becomes HK$300,000 − HK$400,000 = −HK$100,000, so decline beats entry. High demand yields HK$100,000; low demand yields −HK$300,000. The lost recovery reduces every state’s result by HK$150,000.

With revised recovery fixed, expected operating margin must exceed HK$400,000 for strictly positive entry value; equality gives indifference with declining. Initially the boundary was HK$250,000. At the unchanged HK$300,000 expected margin, installation recovery would have to exceed HK$100,000; exactly HK$100,000 would merely break even.

## Debrief · Fixed does not mean sunk

These initial investments do not vary with passenger volume during the season, but recoverability differs. Most vessel expenditure returns through resale. The installation becomes unrecoverable once installed, and startup spending cannot be recovered once incurred. Fixed describes a relationship with output; sunk describes an inability to recover expenditure after commitment.

The revised HK$400,000 net exposure comprises HK$100,000 vessel purchase-to-resale loss, HK$200,000 installation and HK$100,000 startup expenditure. This is not a claim that the entire amount becomes sunk instantly upon purchasing the vessel. Nor is the vessel’s accounting depreciation an additional cash charge in this calculation.

## Debrief · Put the decision on the right date

At both classroom decisions, every initial payment is still avoidable. Anticipated irrecoverability matters when deciding whether to enter. The rule against letting past sunk expenditure distort a future choice does not instruct investors to omit payments they have not yet committed.

After irreversible spending, a genuinely available continuation or exit decision would compare its own remaining cash flows and obligations. That is a different problem. Here the complete season is the chosen package; do not invent an early shutdown option or treat protected passenger and employment obligations as expendable.

## Debrief · What this establishes

The revised package lacks an entry incentive for these risk-neutral investors under the supplied assumptions, despite positive operating margins and investor enquiries. It does not establish that all possible entrants would decline, that a market is legally protected from competition, or that existing operators can safely raise prices.

Entry assessment also needs evidence about timing, scale, permissions, financing and competitive effects. Those are held fixed or left outside this exercise. The case isolates the effect of a recovery correction; it is neither a complete market-contestability test nor financial or competition-law advice.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–5: distinguish funding from recoverability and calculate the initial state results. Minutes 5–10: reveal the installation assessment and recalculate. Minutes 10–15: establish the strict thresholds. Minutes 15–20: distinguish anticipated irrecoverable spending from already-sunk spending and discuss the limits of an entry conclusion.

ECO-11 compares an additional operating hour with normal closing using incremental costs. STR-34 models an incumbent’s capacity commitment and a rival’s response. Here a prospective entrant values recoverable assets before spending, with incumbent behaviour fixed. Do not turn the exercise into a deterrence strategy or a repeated-season investment appraisal.
