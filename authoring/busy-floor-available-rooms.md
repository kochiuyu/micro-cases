# The building is busy. Are the rooms available?

HSP-36 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Flexible workspace · Service entitlements · Peak amenity capacity

## Membership is not a room reservation

Workspace operators distinguish membership access, booking allowances and room reservations. Workplace-planning guidance also distinguishes average attendance from peak demand and the particular spaces people need. The fictional property below asks whether a proposed membership promise can actually be delivered, not whether a real operator oversells its rooms.

## Opening decision

You manage fictional Harbour Work, a Hong Kong coworking floor. For a new one-month offer, compare U: forty individual memberships at HK$2,000 each; and R: thirty memberships at HK$2,400 each. Nobody has signed or paid. Existing users and bookings are outside this new floor, so no established rights are changed.

Both include desk access and meeting-room use without a per-booking charge. U’s draft advert says unlimited room bookings at members’ chosen published times. R includes one pre-agreed room block per member each week. No extra room use is sold under R during this test. The U promise still requires a capacity review.

The floor has forty desks and two identical enclosed meeting rooms, each suitable for one private meeting of up to four people. Each room offers eight one-hour service blocks per weekday over five days: forty blocks per room, eighty across both. Each block includes forty-five meeting minutes and fifteen minutes for turnaround.

All safety, fire, accessibility and building-occupancy limits remain fixed. Staff, maintenance and turnaround are funded. The floor cannot convert desks into private rooms, shorten turnaround, expand rooms or use another venue during this month. Management requires the chosen package to accommodate every request in the supplied planning week before launch.

The initial model supplies forty room-block requests weekly under U and thirty under R, with all stipulated memberships sold and billed for the month. Maximum simultaneous desk demand is twenty-four under U and eighteen under R. The timing of room requests has not yet been examined. These are fictional planning inputs, not demand forecasts.

## Round two

For this planning week, ten peak time blocks are identified: 10–11 a.m. and 11 a.m.–noon on each weekday. With two rooms, these provide twenty room-blocks of peak supply. Meetings must occur in their requested time block; clients cannot move them. Each requested meeting needs a separate enclosed room.

U has three simultaneous room requests in each of those ten peak blocks: thirty peak requests. Its remaining ten requests occur outside those periods, never exceeding two simultaneously. R has two simultaneous requests in each peak block: twenty peak requests, plus ten off-peak requests that also fit the available rooms.

All requested meetings have at most four attendees. A checked people-count schedule, including guests and staff, remains within the fixed building limit under either proposal; desks and safety occupancy are not the bottleneck. Meetings cannot share a room or move to open desks because the stated private-room requirement would not be met.

These ledgers are complete, deterministic classroom scenarios, not observations proving future behaviour. R’s thirty weekly blocks are allocated one per member at mutually accepted times before enrolment. U’s quoted revenue assumes all forty buy the draft promise; adding an availability disclaimer or booking cap would create a changed offer with unmeasured demand.

No extra room, compensated relocation or willingness to reschedule is supplied. No package is already contracted. The team can launch a deliverable R on the stated model, or withhold U while designing and testing different terms. It cannot book three meetings into two rooms or sell unavailable access as assured.

**Student question:** Can either package meet every supplied request? Calculate the peak shortfall and compare it with the weekly average. Recommend a launch decision, separating billed revenue, delivered access and profitability. What would a changed U offer need to establish?

## Debrief · Count the right service unit

The monthly revenue worksheet gives U: 40 × HK$2,000 = HK$80,000; R: 30 × HK$2,400 = HK$72,000. U’s apparent premium is HK$8,000. These are proposed membership billings, not lease profit, collected cash or contribution after operating costs. No complete cost or collection comparison is supplied.

Weekly room supply is 2 × 8 × 5 = 80 room-blocks. U requests 40/80 = 50%; R requests 30/80 = 37.5%. These ratios describe demand against total weekly supply, not realised occupancy or the probability that a preferred booking will be available.

## Debrief · Locate the peak

Peak supply is 2 rooms × 2 daily blocks × 5 days = 20 room-blocks. U requests 30 there, or 150% of peak supply. One request fails in each of ten peak blocks: ten unmet requests, even though forty of the eighty weekly room-blocks appear unused in the aggregate comparison.

Only twenty peak requests and all ten off-peak requests can be served under U: thirty of forty requests, or 75%. The ten unserved requests cannot use spare capacity at another time under the supplied constraints. Empty desks and unneeded afternoon room-blocks are not substitutes for a private room at 10 a.m.

R uses all twenty peak room-blocks and ten off-peak blocks. All thirty supplied requests fit, but peak capacity has no spare block. This deterministic feasibility result does not establish resilience to overruns, outages or additional demand. The fifteen-minute turnaround is already included, not spare meeting time.

## Debrief · Choose a deliverable promise

R meets management’s stated launch test; unchanged U does not. Choose R on this evidence rather than treating higher proposed billings as revenue that can be earned while fulfilling U’s promise. A future disruption plan still needs explicit provision. No safety limit or existing commitment may be relaxed to improve the spreadsheet.

Unlimited booking quantity is different from assured access at any chosen time. A clearly disclosed availability-based product could be legitimate, but it is not the quoted U offer. Changing its wording, allocation rules or cap may change who buys and at what price. Do not retain the HK$80,000 assumption without retesting.

## Debrief · Measure fulfilment, not just busyness

For a redesigned offer, record requested room type and time, accepted bookings, rejected requests, cancellations and actual attendance using proportionate, authorised data. Counting only successful bookings hides unmet demand. Compare peak periods separately from daily averages and keep room demand separate from desk attendance.

Test whether members accept specified slots, a smaller allowance or genuine alternative space, then cost the resulting service and support. No such redesign is free or automatically accepted here. A package with fewer members can fit a constrained amenity better without proving that fewer members always produce more profit.

## Instructor note

Ask students to draw two rooms across ten peak blocks, then place three requests in each under U. Contrast this with the tempting 50% weekly ratio. Require the 10-request shortfall and 75% fulfilment calculation before discussing revenue. R’s 100% peak use is feasible in the supplied week, not a reliability guarantee.

All packages, customers, requests, capacities and permissions are fictional. HSP-18 compares compatible property uses and ECO-14 models participation and usage under prices. Here the binding issue is simultaneous demand for a promised shared amenity. Sources supply operating and transparency distinctions, not local legal advice or validation of these numbers.
