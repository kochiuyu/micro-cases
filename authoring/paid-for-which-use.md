# We bought the photograph. May we reuse it?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 177 -->

GVR-15 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Copyright permissions · Licence scope · Creative procurement

![Two tour-business colleagues consider a harbour photograph and plain paperwork beside a camera.](../paid-for-which-use-scene.jpg)

## Industry context

In a written Legislative Council reply on 20 May 2026, Hong Kong’s Government described support for commercialising local creative intellectual property, including an enhanced Asia IP Exchange Portal that began operating on 31 March 2026. This is context for trading rights, not evidence about any particular photographer or dispute.

The tour operator, images, licence wording, quotes and deadlines below are original classroom fiction. The exercise distinguishes paying for creative work from agreeing the scope of its use. It is not legal advice or a finding of infringement.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong walking-tour operator needs an image for its own four-week paid social-media campaign, launching in five working days. It previously paid a freelance photographer HK$3,000 for a harbour photograph used in one print brochure. The file and paid invoice are available, but the signed usage schedule is still being retrieved. The invoice says “photography and brochure image”; that description alone does not establish the new campaign’s permissions.

A is to prepare reuse of the harbour image, conditional on checking the existing agreement and obtaining any additional written permission needed. The photographer is reachable and gives a provisional HK$1,200 estimate for the proposed own-channel campaign. B is to commission a fresh photograph for HK$2,000, with the required campaign uses agreed before work starts. Both can deliver approved files and confirmed permissions before launch. Neither is ordered, paid or published yet. No image may be released on the strength of an assumption or silence.

The old image visually matches the brochure; the new one offers a fresh composition. Both meet the campaign’s creative brief, and no test establishes better bookings from either. The budget is HK$2,000 for all additional image and permission costs, separate from identical advertising costs. The old HK$3,000 is already spent and cannot be recovered under either option. The manager is choosing a conditional procurement route, not choosing between compliance and knowingly unauthorised use. No identifiable people, private interiors or third-party artwork appear in either scene; the exercise assumes other necessary clearances are satisfied.

## Round two

The signed schedule is retrieved. Its complete classroom text is: “The photographer retains copyright. The operator may reproduce this image in one edition of its printed tour brochure distributed in Hong Kong. The agreed credit must appear. No digital campaign, onward licence or transfer of copyright is included. Other uses require a separate written agreement.” This explicit agreement, not a guessed default for commissioned works, governs the exercise.

A prospective hotel partner now offers to run the same campaign image on its own paid social account during the same four weeks. Participation is optional; the operator’s own campaign remains feasible without it. There is no promised reach, booking uplift or payment from the partner. Merely sending it the file is not the same as giving it permission to advertise with the image.

The photographer confirms A at HK$1,200 for the operator’s named social account for the four-week campaign. A further HK$800 includes the named hotel’s account for those same dates. Both permits allow the agreed resize and crop and require the stated credit. Neither permits resale, transfer of copyright or further onward use. B’s HK$2,000 quote includes the new photograph and both named accounts for the same period, with the same crop, credit and no-resale conditions. The new photographer also retains copyright. These are complete additional quotes with no differing costs omitted. Written permissions and delivery can still be completed before launch under either route.

Thus three feasible packages remain: A for the operator alone at HK$1,200; A for both accounts at HK$2,000; or B for both accounts at HK$2,000. B may also be used only on the operator’s account, but its fee remains HK$2,000. Both photographers agree to the stated terms if selected; no further right is implied. The approved campaign manager will retain the final signed scope and provide the partner only the authorised file and usage instructions. No publication has occurred.

**Student question:** Which image and channel package would you authorise now? Specify the parties, media, dates and permitted edits to record, and explain whether the optional partner use is worth including without inventing a booking uplift.

## Separate the file, payment and permission

The operator possesses a digital file and has paid for earlier work. The supplied schedule nevertheless retains copyright with the photographer and limits the first permission to one printed brochure edition. The new social campaign is outside that express scope. A credit is a condition of authorised use, not a substitute for the missing permission. This is not a claim that every commissioned photograph has the same ownership rule.

## Compare packages on the same basis

A for one account costs HK$1,200, leaving HK$800 of the image budget unused. Adding the hotel costs HK$800 more, bringing A to HK$2,000. B for both accounts also costs HK$2,000. The old HK$3,000 payment is common and unrecoverable; adding it to only A would distort the comparison. Unused budget is not a measured profit. There is no evidence that the hotel’s possible exposure produces enough additional bookings to justify its inclusion.

## Make a commercial choice within permission boundaries

A alone is defensible if continuity and the own-channel campaign meet the need and the hotel opportunity is not compelling. A with the hotel buys broader specified use while retaining continuity. B offers the fresh composition and the same two-account scope for the same total price as that broader A package. Neither image is proved more persuasive. Broader permission has value only if the planned use warrants it; it is not ownership of every future use.

## A small rights register prevents a large assumption

Record the image identity, copyright owner, licensee and named partner, authorised accounts and campaign dates, paid-ad permission, crop/resize limits, credit, price and no-onward-use condition. Obtain the agreed written scope before release, give the hotel the same limits, and schedule campaign removal or renewal before expiry. Keep the original brochure permission separate. A later brochure edition or another partner needs its own scope check; do not silently stretch this permission.

## Instructor note

Ask students first for a provisional route and release gate, then reveal the complete fictional schedule and optional partner. The mandatory boundary is no unsupported reuse; the live business decision is which lawful package to buy. Accept either image and optional partner inclusion with a reason grounded in the stated needs. Do not reward assertions that payment transfers all rights, credit cures missing permission, commercial use always requires ownership, or silence grants permission. This is not a real contract template or a legal opinion; a real transaction requires review of its actual agreement and applicable rights. The factual sources support the conceptual distinction, not these fictional prices or terms.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All parties, images, costs, contract text, quotes and deadlines are original fiction. The licence schedule is fully supplied and explicitly retains copyright; no general commissioned-work ownership default is inferred. No real dispute or wrongdoing is alleged. Initial reuse is conditional procurement, never unauthorised publication. All options fit the five-working-day launch and HK$2,000 additional budget. Prior HK$3,000 is sunk and common. A one-account 1,200 + hotel extension 800 = 2,000; B both accounts = 2,000. No tax, production, legal or licence cost differs beyond the complete quotes. Both providers retain copyright; both named-account packages have matching four-week, crop and credit scope. Own-channel-only B retains its full price. Partner is optional with no measured commercial result. Other clearances are stipulated satisfied; do not use this simplification to conclude photographs never involve privacy, trade marks or other rights.
