# A benefit far away. Less valuable to whom?

## A calculation across generations needs a stated viewpoint

Public appraisal guidance uses discounting to compare effects occurring at different dates. The UK Green Book and the US EPA’s economic-analysis guidance distinguish the calculation from the broader appraisal of distribution and effects not captured in a monetary total.

UK supplementary discounting guidance and an independent report commissioned by New Zealand’s Treasury discuss the assumptions behind rate choices. Defra’s natural-capital guidance calls for physical and qualitative evidence as well as monetary estimates. The case uses its own constant 1% and 5% classroom scenarios, not a prescribed rate from any jurisdiction.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A community public-benefit fund can spend HK$100,000 now on one indivisible optional project. Near retrofits a community building component. Later establishes a long-lived planted environmental corridor. Both satisfy every mandatory safety, accessibility, land-use and ecological safeguard. Neither replaces an obligation, removes existing habitat or displaces residents. Essential services and protected reserves are funded separately.

For a deliberately simplified comparison, each project uses HK$100,000 of real resources at year zero. Near avoids a one-off HK$140,000 resource cost at the end of year five. Later avoids a one-off HK$300,000 resource cost at the end of year forty. These are invented incremental social-benefit amounts at those actual dates, not sums of annual flows moved to a convenient endpoint, nature’s market price or a cash payment to residents.

The stated amounts include all differences in monetisable costs and benefits within this classroom model. Other maintenance, delivery and end-of-life resource requirements are equal to the funded baseline; no additional differential monetisable flow or terminal receipt is assumed. Later’s continuing habitat and amenity effects are not priced into HK$300,000. They must be reported separately rather than called zero. The complete appraisal considers effects through year sixty, including those after year forty.

All figures are constant today’s HK dollars. Hold outcomes certain solely to isolate time weighting; actual forecasts would need separate uncertainty tests. At the initially supplied constant **5% real social discount rate**, use present benefit = benefit / (1 + rate) raised to its year; subtract HK$100,000 to get the monetised net present value. Use factors 0.783526 for year five and 0.142046 for year forty. The rate is an appraisal weight, not interest earned in a fund account.

## Round two

The board requests a second scenario using a constant **1% real social discount rate for both projects**. Use factors 0.951466 at year five and 0.671653 at year forty. Do not give one project 1% and the other 5%, and do not change the physical benefits, their dates or their real amounts. This is a sensitivity comparison, not permission to select a rate after seeing which project it favours.

The cohort account adds context. Under either project, today’s community bears the HK$100,000 opportunity cost. Near’s year-five saving mainly benefits today’s building users and the public services using the released resources. Later’s year-forty saving mainly benefits the community then, including people not yet born, though some current residents may still be present. No transfer compensating today’s payers is promised. The same people can belong to more than one time cohort; these are incidence descriptions, not additive headcounts.

Later also maintains a habitat connection and opportunities for future nature contact through year sixty. The local evidence supports describing these effects but not a defensible monetary estimate. Neither a number of species saved nor a value per future resident is supplied. Near improves current users’ experience in a way also left unpriced. Consultation must include current residents’ views and an explicit representation of future interests without pretending to know future people’s preferences.

The board’s remit is to balance present and future public benefit within the same fixed budget, not mechanically maximise a partial money score. It has not selected a single binding rate. It must explain any recommendation across both scenarios and the unpriced effects, while keeping all rights and ecological safeguards non-negotiable.

**Student question:** Recalculate both projects at 1%. Explain the ranking reversal, identify who bears costs and receives benefits, and recommend a transparent decision process rather than declaring one rate the scientifically correct moral answer.

## Answer the first comparison

At 5%, Near has present benefit HK$140,000 × 0.783526 = HK$109,693.64, so net present value is **HK$9,693.64**. Later has HK$300,000 × 0.142046 = HK$42,613.80, so its net present value is **−HK$57,386.20**. Near leads the monetised comparison by HK$67,079.84 using the supplied rounded factors.

That is not proof that Later has negative total social value. The calculation omits deliberately unpriced effects, and the rate expresses a temporal valuation framework. Near is the provisional answer to the specified 5% money-score question, not an automatic final award.

## Answer the sensitivity comparison

At 1%, Near’s present benefit is HK$133,205.24 and its net present value **HK$33,205.24**. Later’s present benefit is HK$201,495.90 and its net present value **HK$101,495.90**. Later now leads by HK$68,290.66. Small rounding differences from full exponential calculations do not change either ranking.

The later benefit is larger but forty years away. A higher discount rate reduces its present weight much more than the five-year benefit. Nothing physical has improved when the rate changes. Nor has the fund acquired more money: both projects still require HK$100,000 today. The reversal exposes sensitivity to how dates are weighted, not an arithmetic error or an inflation mismatch.

## Make the value judgment visible

Do not label today’s community selfish for preferring Near, or call Later automatically fair because its benefits arrive later. Current people can have urgent legitimate needs; future people have interests without a present vote. Identify actual burdens, distribution within each cohort and the opportunity cost of the rejected project. A larger aggregate benefit does not mean every payer gains.

A defensible process publishes both rate scenarios and the undiscounted dates, separates monetised and unpriced effects, and explains the governing intergenerational principle before selection. Here, a provisional preference for **Later** can be justified by an explicit long-horizon stewardship priority and habitat connection, while acknowledging Near’s stronger 5% score and current-user benefits. Near remains defensible under a reasoned priority for nearer benefits. Neither choice is uniquely dictated by the exhibit.

## Keep constraints outside the score

No positive present value permits a breach of rights or ecological safeguards. Do not add an invented price for habitat to force a preferred ranking, and do not count its unpriced effect again inside the stated monetary benefit. Describe duration, beneficiaries, evidence quality and monitoring separately.

Before a real commitment, test delivery risk, maintenance assumptions, relative environmental values and distribution, not only discount rates. Seek the applicable public appraisal rules for the actual jurisdiction; the two constant classroom scenarios are not substitutes. A longer-lived asset is not necessarily better, and a lower discount rate is not a complete theory of justice.

## Instructor note

Make students calculate the 5% comparison before revealing 1% and the cohort account. Require the same rate and present date for both projects in each scenario. Emphasise that a one-off year-forty benefit is not a forty-year accumulated flow. Present-value factors are provided so no external calculator or source reading is necessary.

FIN-14 concerns matching real and nominal units; here both scenarios already use consistent real units. SUS-25 compares equivalent service lives and replacement costs; here the central issue is social weighting across generations and a partial money score. Accept either final project only with explicit principles and recognition of what the table cannot decide.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original fiction; no actual environmental valuation, procurement or investment recommendation. Budget and cost HK$100,000 now for either indivisible option. One-off real incremental benefits HK$140,000 at year five or HK$300,000 at year forty; other differential monetisable flows stipulated absent. Habitat/amenity and current-user experience remain unpriced, not zero. Common appraisal horizon through year sixty. Constant 5% and 1% are alternative classroom social-rate scenarios applied equally to both options, not official rates or interest earned. Rounded factors govern displayed arithmetic. Cohort descriptions can overlap and are not population estimates. Rights, mandatory services and ecological safeguards remain binding.

Chapter 6 explains consistent timing, rate sensitivity and intergenerational ethical issues. Historical methodological reference, not a claim of current US regulatory requirements.

- [Abelson and Dalton · Declining Discount Rates in Cost Benefit Analysis · 2024](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2024-09/cr-declining-discount-rates-cba.pdf). Treasury-commissioned report discusses conceptual approaches and sensitivity analysis. Authors’ analysis, not a universal endorsed discount schedule.
- [Defra · Enabling a Natural Capital Approach](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/enabling-a-natural-capital-approach-enca-guidance/enabling-a-natural-capital-approach-guidance). Guidance on physical context, unmonetised effects and biodiversity constraints supports a broader account than a partial monetary total. UK provisions are not transplanted as local law.
