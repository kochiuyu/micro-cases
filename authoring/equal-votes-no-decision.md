# Equal votes. No decision?

ENT-15 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Founder governance · Deadlock · Bounded decision authority

## In the news

On 26 January 2026, Hong Kong’s government reported that its 2025 Startup Survey counted 5,221 startups. The growing ecosystem includes many firms whose founders must organise how decisions are made. The survey does not measure founder conflict or establish that equal ownership causes failure.

The founders and all operating facts below are original fiction, not survey respondents or legal case facts.

## Opening decision

Two fictional Hong Kong founders, Sam and Lee, each own 50% of a small scheduling-software company. They remain committed to working together. Sam handles day-to-day operations; Lee leads product development. Both have relevant experience and neither has a personal financial interest in the suppliers being considered. They disagree over which of two service providers should support a four-week internal workflow trial. Both providers meet the agreed minimum security, service and budget requirements; there is no evidence establishing one as the correct commercial choice.

The booking decision is due in two working days. Either provider costs HK$8,000 in total for four weeks, with no renewal, exit fee or further commitment. The company can stop using it and return to its existing process, but any paid trial fee is not refundable. Current customer commitments remain supported by the existing process. No safety, data-rights or mandatory approval requirement is waived. A separate HK$1,000 governance-advice budget is already approved.

The founders are willing to agree on a narrow process before selecting the provider, though neither has conceded the provider choice. A gives Sam the final operating choice after a documented discussion with Lee; there is no additional fee. B refers the same evidence to an independent adviser whom both have already selected and checked for conflicts. The adviser can decide within one working day for HK$1,000, paid from the separate budget. Both agree to follow that decision within the stated trial scope. This is not merely mediation: a mediator would help them negotiate rather than choose for them.

## Evidence

| Process | Who decides? | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| A · designated operating authority | Sam decides after Lee’s input | No extra fee; clear internal accountability, but Lee must accept a bounded decision they may oppose. |
| B · agreed independent decider | Selected adviser decides within one working day | HK$1,000; neutral perspective, but less day-to-day context and an additional handoff. |

**Student question:** Which process would you jointly adopt for this operating disagreement: bounded authority for the operating founder or the independent adviser’s decision? Explain the speed, accountability and trust trade-off, rather than choosing who should own more shares.

## Round two

The founders now complete a decision map for this exercise. Ordinary reversible operating trials may be delegated only if the total commitment is at most HK$10,000, duration at most four weeks, within an approved budget and with no reserved matter involved. New shares, borrowing, transfer of core intellectual property, related-party deals and commitments longer than four weeks remain reserved for joint approval. A small payment does not override these category restrictions. Neither a delegated founder nor the adviser can change the map.

The current HK$8,000 four-week trial meets every ordinary-decision condition. A supplier also offers a separate HK$24,000 annual service package; that is not the trial and is not approved. It is reserved because of its duration and exceeds the delegation limit. There is no requirement to accept it to obtain the trial. Both founders confirm that either narrow process can be properly authorised through the company’s existing arrangements in time; students are choosing management design, not drafting or signing legal terms. Their 50/50 ownership remains unchanged.

**Student question:** With the decision map in hand, would you keep or change your chosen process? Specify its scope, consultation deadline, spending limit and fallback, and explain why it cannot decide the proposed annual commitment or change ownership.

## Separate the percentage from the process

Equal ownership describes economic and voting interests under the applicable arrangements; it does not, by itself, answer who chooses this trial provider. The founders can agree a limited operating process without redistributing shares. They must jointly authorise that process through the proper company arrangements. Sam cannot simply announce a casting vote, and the adviser acquires no general power over the company.

## Two workable ways to end this particular tie

A uses operating knowledge and preserves clear internal accountability without the referral fee. Its weakness is reliance on both founders accepting the delegated boundary when they dislike the result. B buys a neutral decision for HK$1,000 and still meets the deadline, but the adviser needs a shared evidence brief and is not infallible. Both can be defended. The trial costs HK$8,000 under A and HK$9,000 including advice under B; the separate advice allocation does not make its cost disappear. The adviser’s role here is an agreed choice, not mediation, arbitration or a statutory casting vote.

## Apply every boundary, not just the price

The trial is within HK$10,000, four weeks and approved resources, with no reserved matter. The annual offer fails both the monetary and duration conditions: HK$24,000 exceeds the cap by HK$14,000 and twelve months exceeds four weeks. It cannot be relabelled a trial, split into monthly invoices or approved by the narrow decider. A low-priced transfer of core intellectual property would still be reserved. Delegation is permission within limits, not a general override of joint approval.

## Make a procedure that can actually run

One defensible design is a shared provider comparison delivered today, a fixed consultation window for both founders and a decision tomorrow by the chosen decider. Record the choice, reason, HK$8,000 trial limit and four-week end date. If the adviser is unavailable, or the facts show the trial falls outside the map, do not silently hand authority to the preferred founder. Return to joint decision; if no valid decision exists by the deadline, defer the trial and keep the current process. This loses the immediate trial opportunity, not an existing customer service.

## Review the trial without rewriting the bargain

At the end, compare the trial with the agreed service and security requirements and review whether the decision process worked. Any extension or annual package is a new decision under the map. The founders may jointly revisit the process through appropriate formal arrangements, but one unhappy outcome does not authorise unilateral share changes or ignoring a reserved matter. A bounded process can resolve an operating tie; it does not cure a fundamental breakdown of trust.

## Instructor note

Ask students to choose a mechanism, not guess which provider will win. Reveal the map and test the annual offer and a hypothetical inexpensive core-IP transfer against it. Require an explicit missed-deadline fallback. Challenge claims that 50/50 equity necessarily means permanent paralysis, that a mediator automatically decides, or that “reversible” means the fee is refundable. This fictional governance design is not a legal clause, enforceability opinion or instruction to amend a real company’s documents.
