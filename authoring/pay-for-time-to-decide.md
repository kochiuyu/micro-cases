## A later choice can have value

Investment research distinguishes committing money now from preserving the right to invest after learning more. That flexibility is not free: a reservation may cost money and expire before uncertainty is resolved. A decision tree makes the timing visible.

This original fictional case isolates that mechanism. The supplier terms, probabilities and cash amounts are invented, not market quotations or investment advice.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** You manage a small display-making workshop considering a specialist finishing unit for one seasonal contract opportunity. The firm can afford either route without borrowing or using its protected operating reserve. It can also decline, earning zero incremental cash from this opportunity.

Today, day 0, you may **commit HK$100,000** to the unit, or pay a **HK$10,000 non-refundable reservation fee**. The fee gives you the right, not the obligation, to pay a further HK$100,000 by day 30. It is not credited against that payment. Both routes secure the same unit and day-50 start if exercised on time. Without either commitment today, the supplier slot is unavailable this season.

Initially, assume a perfectly informative demand signal arrives on day 20: it tells you whether this season is high or low demand. Each state has probability 50%. Waiting until day 30 loses no operating receipts. Once purchased, the unit cannot be cancelled or resold in this model; its specialised seasonal use has no later residual value.

The table gives total day-180 cash receipts after all operating costs and taxes, but before equipment and reservation payments. Management ranks policies by **expected incremental net cash**. Ignore interest and the time value of money for this short, simplified comparison. This is not a market option price or a risk-adjusted NPV. A loss is possible despite a positive average.

## Round two

**Still day 0: nothing has been signed or paid.** The customer corrects the timetable. There is now a 25% chance the perfectly informative signal arrives on day 20, and a 75% chance it arrives on day 40. Signal timing is independent of demand. No signal by day 30 therefore leaves high and low demand equally likely; silence is not bad news.

The reservation still expires on day 30, with no extension or refund. If no signal has arrived, you must either pay the HK$100,000 then, without knowing demand, or let the right expire. A day-40 signal is too late to secure this season’s slot. All other amounts, start dates and assumptions remain unchanged.

**Student question:** With the revised signal timing, which route would you choose today? Show the reservation policy if no signal arrives before expiry, and calculate the largest reservation fee that would match committing now.

## Work backward from the later choice

With a timely signal, exercise in the high state: HK$160,000 − HK$100,000 = HK$60,000 from that later decision. In the low state, exercise would produce HK$60,000 − HK$100,000 = −HK$40,000, so let the right expire. The reservation fee is already sunk at that later decision; do not pay another HK$100,000 merely to justify it.

## Round 1: compare whole policies

Committing now gives HK$60,000 in high demand and −HK$40,000 in low demand: expected net cash is HK$10,000. Reserving gives HK$50,000 in high demand, after both payments, and −HK$10,000 in low demand, after only the fee. Its expected net cash is HK$20,000. Declining gives zero. Reserving leads under the stated objective, but pays away HK$10,000 of the high-state upside to avoid committing in the low state.

The HK$30,000 expected payoff from the later exercise decision is not the gain over the best alternative. With certain timely information, the maximum fee that matches committing now is HK$30,000 − HK$10,000 = HK$20,000. A larger fee would erase that advantage.

## Round 2: information must arrive in time

If no signal has arrived by expiry, exercising has expected incremental cash HK$10,000 versus zero from allowing expiry. Exercise under the supplied objective. Do not subtract the already-paid fee again when choosing at day 30. Do include it once when comparing entire policies today.

A timely signal gives expected exercise payoff HK$30,000 before the fee. A late signal gives HK$10,000 because you commit without information. Reservation therefore gives 0.25 × HK$30,000 + 0.75 × HK$10,000 − HK$10,000 = **HK$5,000**. Committing now remains HK$10,000; declining remains zero. Commit now under the stated criterion.

The revised right has HK$15,000 expected payoff before its fee. To match committing now, its fee must be at most HK$15,000 − HK$10,000 = **HK$5,000**. This is the firm’s model-specific indifference fee, not a fair market quote. No negotiation or extension is promised.

## A check on the decision tree

For the revised reservation policy, early-high and early-low each have probability 12.5%; late-high and late-low each have probability 37.5%. Their whole-policy net cash amounts are HK$50,000, −HK$10,000, HK$50,000 and −HK$50,000 respectively. The weighted average is HK$5,000. In the late-low branch you paid both the fee and the equipment price before discovering low demand.

More generally, with timely-signal probability q and the HK$10,000 fee, reservation expected net cash is HK$20,000 × q. It matches committing at q = 50%, under all the other assumptions. This threshold is a sensitivity calculation, not a forecast of customer behaviour.

## What travels beyond this example

An option is valuable because a future action can depend on new information, not merely because payment happens later. Check what can be learned, when it arrives, what the right costs and when it expires. A delayed signal can leave a costly right with little advantage over commitment.

Expected cash is not a guarantee or a complete real investment appraisal. Actual decisions may need risk preferences, financing and discounting, imperfect signals, competition, resale and alternative uses. Preserve those questions rather than treating this simplified tree as universal investment advice. Properly specified NPV can incorporate contingent choices; real options do not make NPV inherently invalid.

## Instructor note

Allow 15–20 minutes: three for terms and the first vote, four for backward reasoning, five for the timing reveal, and three to eight for comparison and discussion. Keep round 2 hidden until the first policy is recorded. Draw the day-0 choice before the demand branches; for the reveal, put the early/late timing branch before the conditional exercise choice.

Ask which payment is sunk at day 30, then which costs belong in the day-0 comparison. Check that students do not treat the fee as a purchase credit, late information as usable, or absence of a signal as evidence of low demand. Round 2 is a revision before commitment, not recovery of a payment already made.

FIN-08 compares fixed cash-flow timing; FIN-19 selects compatible projects within a budget; FIN-12 compares ownership with a contractual lease exit. Here the new mechanism is paying for the right to make an initial investment after information, then valuing the effect of expiry. The arithmetic does not require option-pricing formulas or a recommended financial transaction.
