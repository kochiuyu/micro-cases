# A birthday offer. A full birth date?

Case 56 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Governance · Data minimisation · Purpose limitation

![Two stationery-shop colleagues consider a blank clipboard and an abstract membership form beside a wrapped notebook and calendar.](../birthday-fields-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 11 November 2026, the Hong Kong Productivity Council announced a collaboration with Hong Kong’s privacy regulator and partner organisations on privacy and data security. Its first initiatives included three free training seminars for SMEs and NGOs.

The announcement concerns business practices, not a finding against a retailer. Our fictional shop turns that broad issue into a small design decision: which customer details should a promotion form request?

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong stationery shop plans a birthday-month voucher for existing loyalty-account holders. Its ready-made promotion form asks for full date of birth, home address, mobile number and email address.

The manager favours leaving the template unchanged: it is quick to launch and could provide richer profiles for future campaigns. A colleague wants to check which fields the birthday offer actually requires before collecting them. Changing the form takes some setup work, and the team has not yet mapped the information needed to deliver the offer.

No new delivery service or additional campaign has been approved. The team must choose whether to launch the template now or first review its fields. These are invented choices, not the practices of any named retailer.

## Round two

The system team confirms that the voucher can appear inside the existing account during a customer-selected birthday month. The existing account identifier, chosen month and redemption record can enforce one voucher per account per year. The reduced form is technically feasible.

The offer does not require proof of age, identity verification, delivery, an email or a phone message. The shop accepts self-declared months and the risk of multiple accounts; it is not promising one voucher per verified person. There is no approved age-based or address-based campaign.

The full birth date, address and extra contact fields are therefore not needed to operate this particular offer. Setup work still has to be scheduled; no cost or sales uplift is supplied.

**Student question:** Would you still collect the extra fields for possible future campaigns? Specify the information you would use now and one safeguard that remains necessary.

## Start with the job, then the field

For this offer, the account identifies where to place the voucher, the chosen month controls timing, and the redemption record controls repeat use within that account. Day and year of birth, home address and extra contact details do not perform those jobs under the stated design.

Prefer the reduced form, or delay launch until the review and setup are done. The lesson is not that dates of birth or addresses can never be justified. A different service might need them; its purpose and requirements would need their own assessment.

## Less data is not anonymous data

An account-linked month and redemption history can still relate to a person. Limit access, explain the offer and information use clearly, and set a justified retention and deletion process. Do not invent a universal retention period.

Self-declared months do not verify age or identity. One voucher per account is not one per person. If that fraud tolerance changes, reconsider the design rather than claiming the present controls do more than they do.

## Security and permission answer different questions

Encryption helps protect information that is held; it does not explain why an unnecessary field should be collected. A broad permission statement is not a substitute for a specific purpose-and-necessity review. “It might be useful someday” leaves that review unfinished.

Assess any later campaign separately, including appropriate notices, permissions, information requirements and current local rules. This case teaches a design principle, not a legal-compliance certificate or a claim about a real retailer.

## Instructor note

Allow two minutes for the opening vote, three for field-by-field reasons, two for the reveal and five for redesign. Ask template supporters to name a specific approved use; ask minimalists how the offer controls duplicate redemption.

A strong answer retains enough information to deliver the agreed service, rejects speculative collection, and acknowledges the account-level fraud limitation. Discuss a delivery or age-restricted service as a changed assumption, not an existing need. No monetary benefit or legal verdict can be calculated from this case.
