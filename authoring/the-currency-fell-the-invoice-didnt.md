# The currency fell. Why didn’t the invoice?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 580 -->

ECO-50 · Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area · 15 minutes · Invoice currency · Exchange rate pass-through · Contract renewal · Retail pricing

![Two shop staff compare an invoice and a shelf card beside wooden desk organisers.](../the-currency-fell-the-invoice-didnt-scene.jpg)

## The price on the invoice can stick

Research using Chilean import records found that prices at the border respond first to the currency used on invoices, which was often the US dollar. The exporting country’s currency gained importance as contracts reset. Separately, Hong Kong research distinguishes changes in import prices from the later effect on local prices. The store and figures below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Jan buys the next batch of desk organisers from a mainland supplier for a fictional Hong Kong shop. The two-period supplier contract fixes the invoice at US$10 per unit. Use HK$7.80 per US dollar in both periods as a classroom assumption, not a fixed private transaction rate. Hong Kong dollars per yuan fall from 1.10 to 1.00. The supplier has not reset the contract, and local handling is HK$12 for every unit sold.

Jan considers keeping the local shelf price at HK$130 or cutting it to HK$120. The forecasts are 100 and 128 sales respectively for the same period, even after a supply cost change. The shop buys only units it sells, with no unsold inventory, other unit cost or capacity limit. Compare total contribution. Also consider what the shop’s unit cost would have been under a different contract fixed in yuan at RMB70, with the same handling cost.

## Evidence

| Rate or choice | Before movement | After movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HK dollars per US dollar | 7.80 | 7.80 |
| HK dollars per yuan | 1.10 | 1.00 |

**Student question:** What happens to unit cost under the actual US dollar invoice and under the yuan counterfactual? Which shelf-price plan earns more on the actual invoice before renewal?

## Round two

At renewal, the supplier offers a new fixed quote of US$9 per unit. The exchange rates, handling costs and sales forecasts remain at their post-movement values. This quote is new evidence, not an automatic result of the supplier’s weaker currency.

**Student question:** Which shelf price now earns more? What is the smallest whole number of units the shop would need to sell at HK$120 to earn at least as much as the HK$130 plan?

## Debrief · Read the currency on the invoice

The actual unit cost is US$10 × 7.80 + HK$12 = HK$90 in both periods. A fixed RMB70 quote would instead go from 70 × 1.10 + 12 = HK$89 to 70 × 1.00 + 12 = HK$82. The exporter’s location or own costs do not rewrite a contract expressed in another currency.

## Debrief · Compare total contribution, not unit cost alone

At HK$90 unit cost, the higher price earns 100 × HK$40 = HK$4,000 and the discount earns 128 × HK$30 = HK$3,840. Keeping HK$130 is better at those forecasts.

## Debrief · Revisit the price when the quote resets

At US$9, local unit cost is HK$82.20. Keeping HK$130 earns 100 × HK$47.80 = HK$4,780. Cutting to HK$120 earns 128 × HK$37.80 = HK$4,838.40. Break-even discounted volume is HK$4,780 ÷ HK$37.80, about 126.46 units, so at least 127 whole units are needed.

## Instructor note

Ask students where two separate pass-through decisions occur: the supplier’s invoice reprice and the shop’s shelf-price response. The contract and volumes are stipulated; real pass-through is not a universal fraction and the example does not call for a currency trade.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original research and official sources provide the concepts and broad setting for the fictional Hong Kong retailer. No actual currency quotes, hedging or purchase advice. All model rates, prices, and sales estimates are invented.

- [BIS Working Paper 784 · Import prices and invoice currency](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-784-import-prices-and-invoice-currency-evidence-chile). Chile customs evidence distinguishes short-run invoicing currency from the greater role of exporter currency as contracts reset.
- [BIS Working Paper 1266 · Exchange rate effects on firm performance](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1266-exchange-rate-effects-firm-performance-nicer-approach). Thai firm data show why invoice currency, rather than trade geography alone, can matter for profit exposure.
- [HKMA Working Paper 02/2008 · Exchange rate pass-through in Hong Kong](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/publication-and-research/research/working-papers/HKMAWP08_02_full.pdf). The executive summary distinguishes historical estimates on import prices from domestic price responses; it does not set the model rates.
- [HKMA · How does the Linked Exchange Rate System work?](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/money/linked-exchange-rate-system/how-does-the-lers-work). Describes the system and the two convertibility undertakings. It does not fix the exchange rate a retailer gets at HK$7.80.
- [IMF Staff Discussion Note 20/05 · Dominant Currencies and External Adjustment](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/sdn/2020/english/sdnea2020005.pdf). Paragraphs 7–8 and Figure 4 estimate price responses using bilateral manufacturer trade; the timing and importance of invoice currencies vary by setting.
