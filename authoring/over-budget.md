# Over budget. Out of control?

ACC-05 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accounting · Flexible budgets · Performance evaluation

![A lunch-kitchen owner and cook discuss a blank ledger beside lunch boxes and fresh ingredients.](../over-budget-scene.jpg)

## In the news

In its annual results announced on 30 June 2025, Fairwood reported food and packaging costs of HK$750.1 million, or 24.2% of revenue, for the year ended 31 March 2025.

The disclosure puts spending beside an activity-related denominator. It is not a flexible budget or evidence of the fictional management problem below. Our independent lunch kitchen is unrelated to Fairwood.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong lunch kitchen plans to make and sell 1,000 identical lunch boxes this month. Its agreed ingredient standard is HK$20 per box, giving an original ingredient budget of HK$20,000.

The month-end report shows HK$23,000 of ingredients consumed. The owner sees HK$3,000 above the original budget and proposes a tighter ingredient cap next month. The manager says the comparison is incomplete.

Only ingredient cost is under discussion. Rent, labour, packaging and other costs are excluded. The report has not yet shown actual output.

## Evidence

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Planned lunch boxes | 1,000 |
| Ingredient standard per box | HK$20 |
| Original ingredient budget | HK$20,000 |
| Actual ingredients consumed | HK$23,000 |

**Student question:** Would you impose a tighter ingredient cap immediately, accept the extra cost, or investigate first? What comparison is missing?

## Round two

The kitchen actually made and sold 1,200 lunch boxes. The additional orders were approved. Recipe, portion size and required quality were unchanged.

HK$23,000 is the cost of ingredients consumed for that output, not purchases affected by stock timing. For this comparison, the original HK$20 per box remains the agreed ingredient benchmark across this output range. There are no other products or ingredient-cost categories.

The records do not yet separate ingredient prices, quantities used or waste. Sales revenue and other costs are not supplied.

**Student question:** At the actual output, is ingredient cost above or below the original standard? What should the owner investigate before judging the kitchen manager?

## Compare the same amount of work

A flexible budget asks what the original cost rule would allow for the output actually produced. Here that is 1,200 × HK$20 = HK$24,000.

Actual ingredient cost of HK$23,000 is HK$1,000 below that allowance: a favourable difference relative to the benchmark. It remains HK$3,000 above the original HK$20,000 plan.

Both statements can be true. The additional 200 boxes add HK$4,000 at the standard rate; the remaining difference is HK$1,000 favourable. Thus HK$4,000 − HK$1,000 = HK$3,000 above the original plan.

## Favourable is not a performance verdict

The HK$1,000 difference does not prove that the manager reduced waste or improved efficiency. Lower purchase prices, ingredient use and an easy or outdated standard could contribute. The next review needs purchase prices, quantities consumed, waste records and the basis for the standard.

Do not infer higher overall profit: selling prices, labour, rent and other costs are missing. Nor should a favourable cost difference justify reducing food quality or safety. The required recipe, portions and quality remain unchanged here.

## A comparison is not spending permission

Use the activity-adjusted comparison before blaming the manager or imposing an arbitrary cap. Investigate the remaining difference and plan next month around expected orders, appropriate standards and available cash.

Flexing a benchmark does not retrospectively authorise spending, guarantee funding or erase approval rules. The extra orders are explicitly approved in this exercise; a real business must check those controls separately.

Only the stipulated variable ingredient allowance changes with boxes here. Do not automatically increase fixed rent or every other budget line by the same percentage.

## Instructor note

Vote before revealing output. Ask students to name the missing denominator, calculate the HK$24,000 allowance, and reconcile the HK$3,000 excess in two parts.

Finish with a short managerial message: “We spent more than originally planned, but less than the ingredient benchmark for the approved output. Let us investigate why before judging performance.” No price-versus-usage decomposition can be calculated from the supplied totals.
