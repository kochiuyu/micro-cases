# Keep improving it. Or harvest what remains?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 126 -->

STR-11 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Strategy · Product portfolio · Managed harvest

![Three photo-products colleagues compare an open refillable album and modular picture-display boards around a studio worktable.](../what-remains-scene.jpg)

## Hong Kong company context · Different directions within a portfolio

In its 14 May 2025 annual-results announcement, Hong Kong-listed VTech reported further contraction in the US residential-phone market while also describing new phone launches and growth in hotel phones and headsets.

These are company-reported developments, not proof of any particular investment rule. The small photo-products business below is unrelated and fictional.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong photo-products business sells **refillable albums** and **modular display boards**. Album sales have fallen for three years; board sales have grown. Both lines still generate positive cash after their own routine costs. Those facts alone do not establish next year’s returns.

For the coming year, management can fund one of two complete development programmes from a **HK$120,000 discretionary budget**. Both are deliverable with the available paid staff time. A half-sized version of either programme has not been costed.

Separately, a protected HK$30,000 operating allocation covers album quality checks, replacement parts and all existing customer-support promises for the year under either route. Neither route closes the album line, cuts that support or requires redundancies.

The combined development-and-support envelope is HK$150,000. Other routine operating costs are outside that envelope.

## Evidence

| Programme | Use of the HK$120,000 development budget | What it postpones |
|---|---|---|
| A · Renew the album niche | HK$90,000: stronger hinge and compatible additional page packs; HK$30,000: limited board colour extension | Larger board accessory and distribution programme |
| B · Maintain albums, develop boards | HK$0: new album development; HK$120,000: board accessories and distributor demonstration kits | Album improvements |

Under B, current albums and protected support continue. Mature-line cash need not automatically be reinvested in that line. Neither programme guarantees additional orders.

**Student question:** Choose programme A or B for the coming year. What role should each product line play in the business, and what evidence could change your allocation?

## Round two

The sales team separates last year’s album figures by customer use. The decline is concentrated in occasional gift purchases; clubs maintaining physical archives have bought the same number for two years.

| Album units sold by this business | Previous year | Latest year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occasional gift buyers | 800 | 500 |
| Clubs maintaining physical archives | 200 | 200 |
| Total | 1,000 | 700 |

In follow-up conversations, six existing club customers emphasise the unchanged page format and reliable refills. Four welcome the proposed stronger hinge and extra page-pack options, but none commits to buying more or paying a higher price. These selected conversations do not represent all clubs.

A technical check confirms A preserves the existing page format. B can also supply the current album and standard refills throughout the year using its funded operating arrangements. The board opportunity remains credible, but its recent sales growth is not a forecast of returns from the larger programme.

**Student question:** Keep or change your allocation? Explain whether the stable album niche deserves new development or responsible maintenance, and name one result that would trigger a review.

## Separate the decline from the niche

Total album units fell by 300, from 1,000 to 700: a 30% decline. Gift units fell by 300, while recorded club units stayed at 200. Every lost unit in this comparison came from the gift category.

That makes the club segment worth examining, not automatically worth expanding. Stable purchases and favourable comments do not prove that the proposed improvements create enough additional value to justify HK$90,000.

## Give each line a job

A treats albums as a niche worth renewing while boards receive a smaller extension. It preserves an established capability and a specific customer use, but postpones a larger opportunity elsewhere.

B treats albums as an existing source of cash to maintain responsibly while concentrating discretionary development on boards. This is a managed-harvest direction, not abandonment: current products, quality and promised support continue. The risk is underinvesting in a niche that could remain valuable.

## The trade-off survives the reveal

A’s development total is HK$90,000 + HK$30,000 = HK$120,000. B also spends HK$120,000. The separate HK$30,000 album operating allocation means HK$150,000 is committed across these stated budget envelopes under either programme; it is not the company’s total cost or a profitability comparison.

The reveal strengthens the reason to retain albums, but both routes already retain them. The unresolved question is where additional development earns its place. Neither “old products are finished” nor “loyal customers deserve every improvement” answers that question.

## Choose a review signal

An A recommendation could specify evidence that club buyers actually adopt the compatible improvements at acceptable terms. A B recommendation could track whether current-form albums still meet club needs while board distributors use the new kits to generate worthwhile business.

Set the date and evidence before spending, rather than changing the success measure afterwards. Keep support promises separate from discretionary investment. No market-share data are provided to assign automatic portfolio labels, and no uncosted third programme is a free solution.

## Instructor note

Start with an allocation vote, then reveal the customer split. Ask students whether they changed the role assigned to albums, rather than merely changing a spreadsheet total. Both programmes can be defended with a clear opportunity cost and a credible reversal signal.

A supporter must explain why new album development is better than maintaining the existing offer. A B supporter must explain how a managed harvest avoids starving quality or neglecting obligations. The protected support allocation prevents an ethical shortcut from masquerading as strategy.

This is portfolio renewal across two existing product lines, not STR-09’s alignment of activities behind one positioning promise, STR-10’s dependence on one customer, or ACC-02’s closure-cost arithmetic. Do not infer an industry life-cycle stage from one firm’s unit history alone. No future return is supplied to force a unique winner.
