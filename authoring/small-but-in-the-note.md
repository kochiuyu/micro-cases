# A small payment. An important correction?

ACC-16 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Qualitative materiality · Classification errors · Related-party disclosure

![Two arts-club colleagues review plain paperwork and an open ledger among art supplies.](../small-but-in-the-note-scene.jpg)

## A Hong Kong reporting context

In its April 2021 technical news, HKICPA reported the issuance of HKFRS Practice Statement 2 on making materiality judgements. Its later staff guidance discusses assessing both amount and nature, not only a numerical cutoff.

The club, reporting policy, supplier relationship and figures below are original fiction. This is a reporting-judgement exercise, not an allegation about a real organisation or a determination of the accounting framework any club must use.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong adult arts club is preparing its annual member accounts. Draft expenses total HK$1,200,000 and the annual surplus is HK$50,000. The accounts have not been approved or circulated. Members use them to decide next year’s programme funding and review how the committee uses club money.

The treasurer finds an HK$2,400 payment recorded under printing. The invoice shows that it paid for event-room hire instead. The service was received, the amount and payment date are correct, and there is no additional expense or missing cash. Printing is currently HK$20,000; event-room hire is HK$80,000. The error log contains no other unresolved differences.

A colleague suggests leaving this small reclassification in the error log because it is only a small fraction of annual spending and changes no surplus. The treasurer can correct the draft and explain the review before issue, without missing the deadline. All identified errors must be assessed; there is no automatic permitted-error amount. Before deciding whether a separate explanation matters, the treasurer can examine the supplier record and the club’s reporting policy.

**Student question:** Would you correct and explain the item now, or first assess it with the error log before deciding its reporting significance? Calculate its share of total expenses and identify what information could make that percentage an inadequate answer.

## Round two

The supplier record shows that the room-hire company is controlled by the club chair. That relationship was declared before booking; an unconnected committee member approved the service and payment under the club’s purchasing procedure. Supporting documents show the agreed service was delivered. No improper price, hidden benefit or dishonest intent has been established.

Under the club’s supplied reporting policy, transactions with committee-controlled suppliers belong in a separate related-party note. Members specifically review that note when considering continued use of such suppliers. It reports the relationship, type of service and annual amount. The existing draft total is HK$8,000 and excludes this payment because the reporting extract omitted its relationship flag. The HK$2,400 is not already in the note; all other amounts and classifications are confirmed.

The policy requires a correction before issue when an error could affect members’ understanding of that separate information, even if total surplus is unchanged. It does not declare every small connected transaction automatically material in every organisation. Here the nature, omission and members’ stated use must be assessed together. The treasurer can correct both the expense category and note, retain a change record and obtain the normal independent review. The accounts remain unissued; no public retraction or prior-year restatement is involved.

**Student question:** What should the corrected expense lines and related-party total show? Explain why you would correct the draft before issue, what remains unchanged, and how to describe the correction without alleging misconduct.

## Start with size, but do not stop there

HK$2,400 ÷ HK$1,200,000 = 0.2% of annual expenses. That describes one comparison, not a safe harbour. A payment can be small beside total activity yet alter a separate account of relationships that members use. An unchanged surplus alone cannot establish that the presentation is unimportant.

## Reclassify once; disclose without adding twice

Printing becomes HK$17,600 and event-room hire HK$82,400. Total expenses remain HK$1,200,000 and surplus HK$50,000. The related-party note increases from HK$8,000 to HK$10,400. That note analyses transactions already within expenses; adding HK$2,400 again to total expenses would double count the payment.

## Explain what the omitted information changes

The note had excluded a room-hire transaction with a committee-controlled supplier. The additional HK$2,400 is 30% of the previously displayed HK$8,000, although that percentage is not another universal threshold. Members’ stated review purpose and the relationship make the omission important in this scenario. The correction is supported by these facts, not merely by suspicion whenever someone knows a supplier.

## A correction is not a misconduct finding

Record the category change and complete the relationship note, then have the unconnected reviewer check both against the invoice and supplier record. A suitable explanation says that a draft classification and related-party extract were corrected before approval; it does not say money disappeared or that the chair acted dishonestly. Purchasing approval and reporting completeness answer different questions.

## Instructor note

Keep the supplier relationship hidden until the first question. Accept correcting the known classification immediately; the exercise does not require students to defend a knowingly misleading draft. Challenge the claim that 0.2% alone settles significance. After the reveal, the supplied policy and user purpose support correction, not an unrestricted preference vote. Ask for four reconciled numbers and a neutral one-sentence explanation. This differs from procurement splitting: no new purchase or delegation decision is being made. Actual reporting requirements and materiality depend on the applicable framework and circumstances; this fictional member-reporting policy is not a legal threshold.
