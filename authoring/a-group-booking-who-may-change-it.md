# A group booking. Who may change it?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 468 -->

HSP-39 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Group booking authority · Rooming-list changes · Permission and commitment

![An adult tour coordinator and hotel reservations colleague compare a plain room-allocation sheet with three simple twin-room diagrams, without personal names, prices or an indicated decision.](../a-group-booking-who-may-change-it-scene.jpg)

## One contact does not mean one kind of permission

Travel operators distinguish booking contacts, amendments and permission to provide other travellers’ information. Hotel group procedures also identify who may change a room block. The details vary by contract. This fictional case separates authority over a paid allocation from an individual’s room preference and permission to communicate that preference.

## Opening decision

Fictional Harbour Visits organises a two-night Hong Kong stay for six adults. Three twin rooms are confirmed and fully paid at HK$1,000 per room per night, including all charges. Each room has two beds. All six initially agreed their pairings; Lin and Jo share Room 1.

The accepted fictional contract names organiser Mei as the contact authorised to amend the group’s paid room allocation and commit group funds. This authority does not permit Mei to compel anyone to share a room, disclose private reasons or impose a new personal charge on another traveller.

Lin privately asks the coordinator to make Room 1 sole-use for Lin. Lin permits the coordinator to tell Mei about this request, but supplies no reason and authorises no payment. The latest instruction from Mei is to keep the existing three-room allocation and avoid additional group expenditure.

The coordinator can acknowledge preferences and obtain quotes without changing the booking. Changing a paid allocation requires Mei’s specific approval; any affected adult must agree to a new personal arrangement. Individual purchases require that buyer’s separate approval. These agreed operating rules are fictional, not a statement of universal agency or privacy law.

## Round two

The hotel confirms that making Room 1 sole-use would remove Jo’s allocated bed. The other two twins are fully occupied. No traveller has agreed to a different pairing. The hotel will not move Jo or mark a changed allocation confirmed without the required approvals.

One additional room is available for both nights at HK$1,000 per night, all charges included. A free, non-binding hold lasts until 16:00 today. There is no amendment fee, but no credit for leaving a bed unused in the original three rooms. No booking or charge occurs automatically at expiry.

Mei confirms that the group will not pay extra. Mei would approve a list-only update if Lin voluntarily buys the fourth room, Jo keeps Room 1, and everyone else’s room, price and rights remain unchanged. Jo agrees to remain in Room 1 alone without any new charge.

Lin has not yet agreed to buy the extra room. The coordinator can present its exact dates, total and cancellation terms for Lin’s separate decision before 16:00. For this fictional quote, payment would be due on confirmation and non-refundable thereafter. Existing privacy controls and verified communication channels remain in place.

**Student question:** What is the extra cost, and who has authorised it? Specify the minimum outstanding confirmation for the fourth-room route, the records to update after approval, and what to do if approval does not arrive by 16:00. Do not treat a preference as a payment instruction.

## Debrief · Separate the permissions

Lin has authorised communication of the request to Mei, not a room conversion or a charge. Mei’s booking role concerns the agreed group allocation and budget. Jo’s existing bed is a separate affected commitment. Neither being the payer nor expressing a preference settles all three issues.

The coordinator can explain the conflict, quote a feasible alternative and ask specific questions. There is no need to ask why Lin wants a separate room or circulate a private explanation. Share the necessary proposed allocation and price only with the people responsible for the relevant decision.

## Debrief · Account for every adult and every room

Three rooms × two nights × HK$1,000 = HK$6,000 already paid. The fourth room adds two nights × HK$1,000 = HK$2,000. If purchased, total accommodation expenditure becomes HK$8,000: HK$6,000 from the group and HK$2,000 from Lin. There is no unused-bed refund.

Simply relabelling Room 1 sole-use for Lin would leave Jo without the agreed bed. The workable quoted route instead puts Lin in the fourth room and leaves Jo in Room 1. The remaining four adults retain their two twins. A rooming-list change is not merely cosmetic when it changes someone’s access.

## Debrief · Obtain a specific choice

Mei’s conditional list approval and Jo’s agreement are supplied. What remains is Lin’s explicit acceptance of the fourth room for the stated two nights, the HK$2,000 personal charge, payment on confirmation and non-refundable condition. Confirm the hotel still honours the hold before processing the separately approved purchase.

Only after the approvals and hotel confirmation should the coordinator issue the revised allocation and the correct separate payment record. Confirm the same final room assignment with Lin, Jo, Mei and the hotel through appropriate channels, without sending everyone another traveller’s payment information or private reasons.

## Debrief · Pending is not confirmed

If Lin declines or does not approve by 16:00, let the free hold expire without a charge. Do not silently book a fourth room, move Jo or change the paid group allocation. Tell Lin promptly that the requested separate room is not confirmed; later availability or price is not guaranteed.

The original allocation remains booked, but that is not an instruction forcing Lin to share. Discuss any further voluntary alternative and its actual availability and terms before commitment. No cancellation, refund, compensating payment or alternative accommodation has been authorised or promised in this case.

## Debrief · Record authority, not a story

A useful change record identifies the requested action, affected rooms and travellers, payer, total, terms, specific approvals and hotel confirmation. Keep the original and revised versions distinguishable. A checked identity and a message from a genuine traveller still do not establish authority over another person’s bed or money.

Privacy permission and commercial authority answer different questions. Disclosing a room preference does not buy a room; booking-contact status does not give unlimited access to personal explanations. Follow the actual contract and applicable safeguards rather than assume one operator’s terms apply everywhere.

## Instructor note

Ask students to draw the six adults into the rooms before and after each proposal. Then identify which approval changes accommodation, which accepts payment and which only permits communication. The round-two alternative is conditional, not a completed purchase or a reason to pressure Lin.
