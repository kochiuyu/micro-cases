# More tea sold. Did the tasting credit pay?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 582 -->

MKT-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Product sampling · Redemption economics · Purchase timing

![Two people compare three small tasting cups across a tea shop counter.](../a-tasting-flight-for-whom-scene.jpg)

## A tasting can be a product and a sales prompt

The Hong Kong Trade Development Council described exhibitors offering visitors a range of teas to taste at its inaugural Tea Fair. A fictional Hong Kong tea shop now tests a paid tasting flight. The question is not which tea people like best; it is whether crediting the tasting fee generates enough additional full-pack contribution.

## Opening decision

A tasting flight is three small pours. Each visitor in this hypothetical pilot has already chosen the identical HK$20 tasting, costing HK$14 to serve. After serving it, the shop randomly assigns 100 visitors to each offer. F is the standard full-pack offer; C gives a clearly disclosed HK$16 credit valid for one full pack that week. Guests can decline without losing access to the tasting.

Each full pack has HK$42 contribution before the credit, and its price and variable cost are otherwise unchanged. All full packs bought that week under C redeem the credit, and no packs under F do. A visitor can buy at most one full pack over the two-week exercise. There are no returns, fixed-cost differences or other changes.

## Evidence

| Results after one week | F: no credit | C: credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tastings served | 100 | 100 |
| Full packs bought | 38 | 50 |

**Student question:** Would you extend the credit on these first-week numbers? Calculate the full-pack contribution for each offer and the minimum C sales needed to match or beat F at the same credit.

## Round two

During the following week, eight more F visitors and three more C visitors buy their first full pack. No credit is valid then; each late pack earns HK$42. There are no remaining commitments. The shop still does not know which individual purchases were newly created or brought forward.

The tea flight remains available at HK$20 either way. The owner can run another approved test with a lower credit. The next pilot would predefine a longer measurement window and keep flight access, buying terms and customer privacy the same.

**Student question:** Which offer would you carry forward now? Calculate two-week contribution including the tastings, and explain what can and cannot be inferred from the extra seven packs.

## Debrief · Redemption is a cost on every credited pack

F earns 38 × HK$42 = HK$1,596 from packs in week one; C earns 50 × (HK$42 − HK$16) = HK$1,300. C would need 62 credited sales to at least match F: 61 × HK$26 is HK$1,586, while 62 × HK$26 is HK$1,612. Identical tasting contribution cancels in the comparison.

## Debrief · Check the time window

Each arm earns 100 × (HK$20 − HK$14) = HK$600 from tastings. F earns HK$1,596 + HK$336 + HK$600 = HK$2,532. C earns HK$1,300 + HK$126 + HK$600 = HK$2,026, HK$506 less. Its first-week advantage of twelve packs becomes seven over two weeks: 53 versus 46.

## Debrief · Keep the flight; test the credit

Prefer F on these figures. Some of the difference may be purchase timing, but aggregate counts cannot prove which individual would have bought without a credit or what happens after two weeks. For a smaller-credit test, count all visitors assigned, purchases in both periods and contribution after credit. A higher conversion fraction alone is not the objective.

## Instructor note

Reveal the second week after the initial arithmetic. Ask where to book the credit, whether a more generous offer might earn less, and why repeated same-person buying is excluded from this simple comparison.
