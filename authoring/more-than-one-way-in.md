## In the news

On 2 February 2024, the Hong Kong Museum of Art opened Small Small World Panorama, describing tactile, audio, simple-language and wheelchair-friendly features.

This is a historical example, not a current attraction recommendation. Harbour Stories and the choices below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. Harbour Stories is a small Hong Kong visitor attraction. Its new exhibit explains an old harbour crane. Visitors should be able to describe the same three-part story: what it lifted, how the mechanism moved the cargo, and whose work made that possible.

Physical access arrangements, basic readable labels and necessary visitor assistance are already funded and will remain. The manager has a separate HK$24,000 budget for a six-month enhancement, with two fully scoped fixed-price offers.

Both improve interpretation of this one exhibit, not the number of exhibits. No ticket-income increase is assumed. The decision is how to add meaningful access to the story, not whether to retain required provision.

## Evidence

| Six-month package · HK$24,000 each | Strength and limit |
|---|---|
| A · Scheduled facilitated sessions with a handling replica | A trained facilitator adapts explanations and answers questions. Visitors need to attend advertised sessions; preparation and paid delivery are included. |
| B · Visitor-controlled interpretation at the exhibit | A tactile replica, short audio description and matching plain-language large-print text offer independent choice whenever open. They cannot answer a new question like a facilitator. |

B includes setup, agreed maintenance, staff orientation and ongoing content checks. The venue supplies the audio equipment and a quiet listening option; no smartphone, account or personal headphones are needed. Visitors select their modes rather than receiving everything at once.

Existing interpretation and staff assistance remain with either choice, including outside A’s scheduled sessions. Neither package promises to meet every individual’s needs.

**Student question:** Which enhancement would you fund first, A or B? Explain how it serves the exhibit’s story and name specific evidence that would reverse your provisional choice.

## Round two

A paid access-design workshop with visitors who have different access preferences has revised B’s prototype. Its original replica conveyed the crane’s shape but did not help explain how cargo moved. The revised safe, purpose-built model lets a visitor follow the cargo route by touch; matching audio and text explain the same three story points.

During an observed walk-through, a visitor using touch and a companion using text could discuss that story together without joining a separate activity. Another visitor who preferred spoken questions still wanted a facilitator.

The supplier confirms that the paid workshop, revised model and aligned audio/text content are all included within B’s HK$24,000 six-month package. Audio remains visitor-controlled. No additional programme or budget is included. These invented qualitative observations support this prototype’s coherence, not a representative finding, universal understanding or access certification.

**Student question:** Does the revised prototype change your choice? What visitor outcome would you check during the six months to decide whether to retain your approach or change it later?

## Look for routes to meaning, not a feature count

A replica can be touchable yet say little about the exhibit’s subject. The revision connects touch with the cargo route, while audio and text explain that same route and the people doing the work.

The goal is not to add as many sensations as possible. It is to make the same three story points usable in different ways, with visitors choosing how to explore them. Physical admission alone does not establish that this has happened.

## Recognise what improves, and what remains different

The new evidence makes B a stronger option for flexible independent visits and shared exploration. The revised content allows companions using different modes to discuss the same story rather than being separated into unrelated activities.

A still offers responsive conversation and questions that the station cannot answer. Choosing A remains defensible if dialogue is the main unmet need and visitors can attend its sessions. Choosing B means accepting that independent availability does not replace every benefit of a facilitator.

## Observe visitor outcomes without assuming identities

With consent, invite visitors to explain or show the cargo route in their chosen way. Ask whether companions can use the exhibit together and where they want further explanation. Do not infer someone’s access needs or diagnosis from appearance.

Requests for assistance are useful information, not automatically failures. The walk-through shows a possibility worth developing, not what all visitors will prefer or understand. Include varied perspectives in the review rather than treating people with disabilities as one audience with one preference.

## Keep interpretation usable after opening

Check that the model, audio and text remain available, consistent and working. Tell visitors accurately in advance what is offered and when facilitated sessions run. Staff should know how to help without taking over a visitor’s choices.

At the six-month review, use visitor feedback and observed understanding to decide what to retain or improve. Facilitated sessions might be added later if further resources are agreed; they are not a free extra in B’s present package. Baseline assistance and required provision stay in either case.

## Instructor note

Take a provisional A-or-B vote before the revised prototype. Both offers cost the same and serve the same exhibit; the debate concerns interpretation, independence and responsive dialogue, not cheapest access or more exhibits per dollar.

Ask what the revision actually changes: a meaningful cargo route and aligned explanations, not merely an extra tactile feature. The model is a safe replica, not permission to handle a historical collection object or a specification for constructing one.

No uptake rate, population inference, learning-effect estimate, revenue forecast or legal certification is supplied. If visitors chiefly need live answers, A’s advantage grows; if they need flexible self-directed visits, B’s advantage may matter more. These are conditional choices to review, not a universal hierarchy of access modes.
