# A neighbourhood improved. Who can still stay?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 334 -->

SUS-28 · Fictional neighbourhood shopping street · 15–20 minutes · Distribution of place benefits · Displacement risk · Timing of tenant support

![A neighbourhood coordinator and a shop tenant discuss plain project folders beside modest shopfronts and public benches.](../better-place-same-people-scene.jpg)

## Follow the people as well as the place

EPA’s equitable-development guidance encourages considering displacement before redevelopment rather than waiting for it to happen. Urban Institute case studies distinguish new affordable provision from retaining existing occupants. These housing and redevelopment sources inform a question about benefit distribution; they do not establish commercial tenancy rules.

Evidence is not uniformly a story of inevitable harm. Brummet and Reed’s longitudinal study finds benefits for many original residents as well as some additional out-migration, with unobserved moving costs remaining a limitation. San Francisco Fed research examines both retention and exclusion, and HUD distinguishes improvement from affordability. The trading street and every forecast below are fictional, not extrapolated effect estimates.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A voluntary neighbourhood partnership has HK$600,000 for an optional public-realm programme. Existing safety, accessibility, drainage and maintenance duties are fully funded outside it. Neither option involves demolition, compulsory relocation or weakening anyone’s legal or contractual rights. Participation in tenant support is voluntary, and tenants may obtain independent advice. Twenty existing businesses use the street: twelve commercial tenants and eight owner-operators.

Rapid spends HK$600,000 on a larger planting, seating and wayfinding upgrade, opening at the start of month 6. Supported spends HK$420,000 on a smaller upgrade, opening at the start of month 9, and reserves HK$180,000 for tenant assistance beginning before renewal. Both have the necessary permissions and separately funded upkeep. The larger scope and three-month earlier opening of Rapid are real benefits, not unsafe luxuries.

Supported’s HK$180,000 comprises HK$36,000 for independent advice and administration plus HK$144,000 in grants: HK$1,000 per month for each of the twelve tenants for twelve months. All twelve meet the published eligibility criteria and wish to remain; there are no other eligible applicants or unbudgeted programme costs. Advice includes reviewing renewal offers and helping plan after support ends; it cannot compel a landlord to renew or change terms.

The partnership’s stated aims are both a more useful public space and an opportunity for existing traders to share its benefits. No spending is committed. It has not yet checked the tenants’ renewal dates or offers. No reliable extra-sales forecast, property-value estimate or complete money valuation of either upgrade is available. The programme must stay within HK$600,000; the larger upgrade and funded support cannot simply be combined.

## Round two

The twelve tenant leases all renew at the start of month 3, before either upgrade opens. Each tenant currently pays HK$10,000 monthly. Each has a lawful, still-open offer of a twenty-four-month renewal at HK$12,000 per month, fixed throughout months 3–26. Independent review confirms the offers preserve applicable rights and contain no displacement during that term beyond ordinary lawful contractual conditions. This is a classroom stipulation, not legal advice.

Each business can sustainably allocate at most HK$11,000 a month to rent from current trading, without taking money from wages, essential household needs or existing commitments. No spare funds, affordable credit or alternative bridge are available for the remaining HK$1,000. Under Rapid, this gap begins in month 3 and is not funded by the programme. Higher future footfall cannot pay that earlier bill, and no extra sales are guaranteed.

Supported’s grant can start in month 3 and continue through month 14 inclusive. The renewal offers allow these grants to be paid monthly alongside the tenant’s own payment, with no upfront payment of twenty-four months’ rent. No landlord commitment or tenant signature has yet been made. With informed voluntary acceptance, the grants can cover the stated current gap for twelve months; they do not lower the contractual rent.

At the start of month 15, the grants end while the renewal term continues through month 26. No new fund, increased trading income or affordable alternative premises is assured. The partnership has no evidence that its proposed upgrade caused the higher renewal offers; market pressures may have other causes. It must distinguish a temporary bridge from durable affordability and cannot promise that all twelve tenants will remain.

**Student question:** Would you retain or change your package choice? Calculate the funded rent gap and its end date, explain the risk that remains, and propose how to evaluate benefits for the original traders rather than only the upgraded street.

## Answer the provisional choice

Rapid buys a larger improvement three months earlier. Supported gives up HK$180,000 of physical scope and opens later in exchange for advice and a funded bridge. A provisional choice should ask who uses the place, their tenure and renewal dates, affordability, whether support reaches them in time and what happens when it ends. A rising average turnover or property value would not answer those questions.

Either initial preference can be reasoned before the renewal review. The partnership should not assume every tenant will be displaced or that every owner gains equally. Nor should it claim that equal access to the completed space means equal ability to remain nearby and use it.

## Answer the reveal: support, with an honest limit

Prefer **Supported** given the partnership’s aim of including existing traders and the newly confirmed early gap. HK$12,000 rent minus HK$11,000 sustainable payment leaves **HK$1,000 monthly per tenant**. Twelve tenants × HK$1,000 × twelve months gives **HK$144,000**; add HK$36,000 advice and HK$420,000 works to reach HK$600,000.

The grants cover months 3–14 inclusive, beginning before both openings. They create a financed opportunity to remain through Supported’s month-9 opening, subject to voluntary, informed lease decisions and ordinary contractual performance. They do not prove remaining is best for every trader or make the twenty-four-month commitment harmless. Tenants need independent advice on the unsupported portion before deciding.

## A bridge is not permanent affordability

Months 15–26 remain unsupported: twelve more months within the offered lease. If the same gap persists for all twelve tenants, another HK$144,000 would be needed just to cover it. No such money is budgeted. If trading does not improve sufficiently, tenants may face an unaffordable ongoing commitment; advice alone does not fill the gap.

Before committing programme funds, explain this limit clearly, examine sustainable terms and alternatives with willing tenants and landlords, and set an early review before month 15. Do not treat hoped-for concessions, income or future grants as secured. If informed review finds the bridge cannot support a viable choice, revisit programme design rather than use temporary assistance to pressure tenants into signing.

## Measure a cohort, not just a postcode

Track the original twelve tenants and eight owner-operators separately, with consent and appropriate privacy. Ask whether tenants obtained workable terms, remain by choice, face higher rent burdens or moved, and why. Follow movers where they agree: a pleasant street photograph cannot reveal their outcome. Record support take-up and expiry as well as physical completion.

Also assess public-space use, access, upkeep and trader experience, including newcomers. Comparing a street’s average sales before and after may mix different businesses. Without a suitable comparison or causal design, do not attribute every rent increase, departure or gain to the programme. Remaining alone is not success if it requires unsustainable sacrifice; moving is not automatically harm if freely chosen and beneficial.

## Instructor note

Require the renewal date before either opening, the monthly affordability gap rather than the entire rent rise, and the inclusive twelve-month count. Current rent rises by HK$2,000, but tenants can absorb HK$1,000; programme support covers the remaining HK$1,000. Month 15 is the risk point, not month 14.

Unlike SUS-18, this is not about access to agenda setting. It tests who can capture place benefits and the timing/limits of a retention measure. All rents, terms, budgets and preferences are fictional. Housing studies provide conceptual and measurement insights, not evidence that these grants guarantee commercial retention. Do not infer a right to renewal, a project-caused rent increase or a universal anti-improvement policy.
