## Rail procurement can depend on supplier capability

Rail operators publish procurement results and criteria for screening suppliers. For example, MTR’s October 2024 awards list distinguished the procedures used for rolling stock and signalling contracts. That does not show MTR buying bargaining power; the negotiation and prices below are entirely fictional.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong railway refurbishment contractor buys non-structural interior panels. Its incumbent offers a first-year volume deal if the contractor does not fund qualification of a second supplier during that year. If the contractor declines, it pays the standard first-year cost and can fund qualification.

The table gives whole-order costs in HK$ thousands on the same present-value basis. If the second supplier passes, the contractor can use it in year two; if it fails, only the incumbent can supply. In the first forecast, a qualified second supplier can cover the entire order and the incumbent is not expected to change its renewal quote. Qualification succeeds with probability 75%. Choose the lower expected cost.

## Evidence

| Cost or offer | HK$ thousands |
| --- | --- |
| Incumbent first year with volume deal | 90 |
| Incumbent first year without volume deal | 100 |
| Qualification programme | 22 |
| Incumbent renewal without a credible alternative | 150 |
| Alternative for the full renewal order if qualified | 105 |

**Student question:** Would you take the incumbent’s volume deal or fund qualification under the first forecast? How much would qualification save, after counting the lost discount and both renewal outcomes?

## Round two

The second supplier can in fact deliver only 60% of the year-two order. Its per-unit rate still corresponds to 105 for a full order. The incumbent would charge its usual rate, equivalent to 150 for a full order, on any remainder.

However, the incumbent says it would offer the entire renewal for 112 if the second supplier passes and can credibly deliver its 60%. Treat this as a firm conditional offer. If qualification fails, the renewal is still 150. No costs or outcome chances otherwise change.

**Student question:** If qualification succeeds, which renewal deal is better? With the revised terms, would you fund qualification today, and how high would its success probability have to be for the two initial options to tie?

## Debrief · Put the discount and qualification on the same ledger

The volume deal costs 90 + 150 = 240. Qualification initially costs 100 + 22 + 0.75 × 105 + 0.25 × 150 = 238.25. Its expected saving is only 1.75, even though a successful full switch would save 45 on renewal.

## Debrief · The outside option can change the incumbent’s offer

A split renewal costs 0.60 × 105 + 0.40 × 150 = 123 when the alternative is qualified. The incumbent’s offer of 112 beats that split. In this scenario the contractor can benefit from a qualified supplier without buying from it.

## Debrief · Recalculate the chance needed

New expected qualification cost is 100 + 22 + 0.75 × 112 + 0.25 × 150 = 243.5, so take the volume deal on the stated figures. At success probability p the qualification total is 272 − 38p. The two options tie when p = 32 ÷ 38, or about 84.2%.

## Takeaway · Test whether the alternative can win actual work

A supplier name does not create an outside option. Ask what capacity or proof makes the offer credible and whether the second supplier would stay available after a year with no orders. The case assumes it stays available and values no benefit after renewal.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 3 for the first-year terms, 4 for expected renewal, 5 for the split and renegotiated offer, and 3 to discuss maintaining credibility. Ask each group to separate present savings from later bargaining.
