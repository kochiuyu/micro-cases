# One signed letter. Which milestone is real?

ENT-41 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Commercial milestones · Working capital · Customer acceptance · Conditional orders

![A sensor start-up founder compares two documents and a box of unfinished components in a workshop.](../one-signed-letter-scene.jpg)

## Business matching is a step towards sales, not a sale

On 7 September 2026, Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation opened its first Co-Development and Investment Conference. The programme brought together ventures, investors and industrial partners for activities such as pitching and business matching. The fictional sensor maker in this exercise must decide what to do after two such conversations.

## Opening decision

A Kwun Tong firm has promising feedback on its building-condition sensors. Co-founder Yan must decide whether to commit to a fixed-term technician for its first customer installation or wait and keep negotiating. The firm has HK$120,000 in cash and will not go below a HK$80,000 cash floor at any point.

A distributor signs a letter expressing interest in HK$240,000 of sensors but commits to no order or payment. Separately, a building operator offers a HK$160,000 order, conditional on a review of its site. It pays HK$35,000 on signing and the remaining HK$125,000 on completed installation, after acceptance. The initial payment is credited to the price or refunded in full if the site is rejected.

To meet the installation date on these terms, Yan must pay the technician and buy site-specific materials before the review. If the site is rejected, the business refunds the initial payment and sells the materials for the recovery amount in the exhibit at the same time. No other money arrives or leaves during this decision window.

## Evidence

| Item | Cash amount |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed technician commitment | HK$48,000 |
| Site-specific materials | HK$32,000 |
| Recovery if the site is rejected | HK$10,000 |
| Minimum cash balance | HK$80,000 |

**Student question:** What does each document establish about customer demand and committed cash? Calculate cash after commitment and after rejection for the conditional order. Would you hire the technician for either document?

## Round two

Before the venture signs, the operator offers to pay HK$45,000 up front, of which exactly HK$20,000 is refunded if the site is rejected. It agrees to review the site early enough for Yan to wait for approval before buying materials. The technician must still be paid before the review.

If accepted, the full HK$45,000 credits the HK$160,000 order price and the balance is paid after installation. The HK$80,000 cash floor still applies. The distributor’s letter has not changed.

**Student question:** Do the revised terms justify committing to the technician for this installation? Compare the two cash paths and name one further milestone you would require before staffing a second installation.

## Debrief · A signature does not assign cash

The distributor letter indicates interest and an opening for follow-up, not committed revenue or finance. It contributes zero to the cash available to hire. Yan should keep negotiating rather than commit on the letter’s strength.

## Debrief · Face value does not pay bills early

On the original order, cash after the advance and both commitments is HK$120,000 + HK$35,000 − HK$80,000 = HK$75,000. If the site is rejected, refunding HK$35,000 and recovering HK$10,000 leaves HK$50,000. Even successful delivery would require crossing the cash floor earlier.

## Debrief · Change payment and spending together

The revised offer leaves HK$117,000 after collecting the advance and paying the technician. Rejection leaves HK$97,000 after the partial refund and no purchase of materials. Approval means paying for materials next, taking cash to HK$85,000 before the final payment, then to HK$200,000 after it. Both paths preserve the floor.

## Debrief · Hire for the job, not projected scale

The terms now allow the hire for one project under the model. Rejection still costs HK$23,000 net: HK$48,000 less the retained HK$25,000. Do not extrapolate repeat demand. For a second installation, require evidence such as the first customer’s accepted and paid installation plus a funded next order.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to trace the initial cash, 5 for the revised branches and 4 to discuss a repeatable milestone. Have groups point to the condition that changed a dated cash line, not just the higher initial payment.
