# Start a business. Or buy one to run?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 223 -->

ENT-19 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Acquisition entrepreneurship · Transferable operating platform · Owner dependence

![An aspiring business owner and studio proprietor discuss a plain folder beside printing equipment.](../start-a-business-buy-one-to-run-scene.jpg)

## More than one route into ownership

A Hong Kong government reply on 17 June 2026 discussed support for individuals starting businesses, including one-member companies. One member refers to ownership, not necessarily a business with no employees. Our question concerns a different choice within entrepreneurship: build an operation or buy one to manage. The service studio and financial figures below are fictional.

## Opening decision

An aspiring owner with relevant service-management experience wants to run a small Hong Kong document-layout studio. She has HK$900,000 available for the business, with personal living costs funded separately and no borrowing required. She does not already own another business. She can build a new operation for HK$300,000, including equipment, premises setup and recruitment, or buy an operating studio for a complete upfront outlay of HK$600,000, including transaction fees. Both paths are affordable.

Building gives her control over the initial workflow but no existing orders or established team. Buying brings equipment, documented production routines, two experienced employees and current service commitments. The existing studio recorded HK$1.2 million sales in the last twelve months. Sales are not profit. No comparable future earnings forecasts or independent valuation are supplied, and she has not signed or paid for either route.

For this classroom decision, qualified advisers have confirmed that the proposed acquisition structure preserves employee rights, customer obligations, required permissions and privacy safeguards; no layoffs or contract avoidance are assumed. Staff have indicated willingness to remain, without surrendering future rights. Either route has an affordable six-month operating plan after its initial outlay, even with no new sales; the residual cash is working capital, not a promised return. The seller offers a defined four-week paid handover included in the price. Due diligence must still clarify which commercial relationships carry forward.

## Round two

The authorised review separates last year’s HK$1.2 million sales: HK$720,000 came through documented recurring company accounts serviced by the team, while HK$480,000 came from one-off projects introduced personally by the departing owner. The buyer would receive the operating records and permitted account information, but no person’s loyalty can be transferred by contract.

Two company accounts have confirmed valid continuing service agreements totalling HK$360,000 in fees for the next six months, subject to the studio delivering the agreed work. All necessary consents are in place and corresponding delivery costs are included in the affordable operating plan. These fees are not net profit or a guarantee of collection. No repeat order is committed for the seller-introduced projects. The seller will make agreed introductions during the four-week handover but gives no sales guarantee. Price, staff, equipment, routines, rights and both affordable entry plans remain unchanged.

**Student question:** After separating the transferable platform from owner-dependent sales, which entry path would you choose? Identify what you are paying for and what you still cannot treat as assured future revenue.

## Buy a platform, not a sales total

The additional HK$300,000 initial outlay buys an operating organisation rather than a blank setup. Equipment, working routines, an experienced team and continuing agreements can matter to an owner who wants to manage rather than assemble everything. They also bring real duties. The existence of a platform does not establish that its price is attractive.

## Separate history from commitments

HK$720,000 + HK$480,000 = HK$1.2 million historical sales. The owner-introduced portion is 40% of that history, not a prediction that revenue will fall by 40%. The HK$360,000 forward agreements are a different period and must not be added to last year’s total. Delivery costs, collection risk and later renewals still matter.

## Choose the entry work you want to undertake

Buying remains defensible if the transferable operating base and a managed handover justify the extra outlay despite uncommitted referrals. Building remains defensible if control over design and the larger cash buffer matter more than an existing platform. Neither route avoids acquiring customers. A real purchase would require valuation and further diligence; this exercise supplies no profit forecast from which to calculate a uniquely correct price or payback.

## Instructor note

STR-05 asks whether an established buyer gains incremental reach from combining client lists. This first-time owner has no existing firm to combine: the comparison is building versus acquiring an operating platform. Keep staff and customer rights separate from a commercial retention forecast. Research on investor-backed search funds illustrates one acquisition model, not expected returns for this self-funded microbusiness. No real transaction is being recommended.
