# Flexible for the business. Predictable for the team?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 317 -->

PPL-27 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Schedule predictability · Employment quality · Mutual adjustment

![A retail supervisor and two adult colleagues discuss a blank four-week roster.](../flexible-business-predictable-team-scene.jpg)

## Hours and notice are different dimensions

Research on retail scheduling and international working-time guidance distinguish the amount of work from its timing, predictability and employee influence. The following shop and availability exercise are fictional. No research result is used as a guaranteed local business benefit.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong homewares shop is choosing a four-week scheduling trial. Every employee’s agreed paid weekly hours, pay, rest periods and availability protections remain unchanged. All proposed shifts and changes must be lawful and voluntarily agreed, with no penalty for declining a change. No unpaid standby or last-minute cancellation is allowed. A previously agreed base roster remains the fallback; approved paid cover protects essential service if a proposed change cannot be filled.

Plan A publishes the full four-week roster 28 days before it starts. It retains agreed swaps and voluntary adjustments, but normally leaves published shifts intact. Plan B confirms each week seven days before it starts, using more recent demand information to propose placements within agreed availability. Employees receive their protected hours even if they decline a proposed rearrangement; the base roster and funded cover still apply. Both plans fit the staffing budget, including protected hours and cover. Neither assumes that workers must keep every unconfirmed time free.

The manager expects B to put more staff where customer demand turns out to be strongest. A may make personal planning and early swaps easier but commits earlier against an uncertain forecast. Demand estimates and service promises are identical today. The team must choose the notice arrangement, not reduce hours or determine who is committed to the job.

## Evidence

A: four-week roster published 28 days before the period; agreed swaps remain possible. B: weekly confirmation seven days ahead using updated demand information. Both retain identical paid hours, protected availability, base-roster fallback and funded service cover. No forecast-improvement value is quantified.

**Student question:** Would you choose the four-week roster or weekly rolling confirmation for the trial? Explain what flexibility means for the shop and for employees, and what availability information you would check first.

## Round two

Before either trial begins, staff complete a planning exercise using the same twelve proposed four-hour rearrangements in the first week of the four-week period, the same people and the same demand calendar. Notice is measured before that first week starts, not before each individual shift. These are alternative placements of existing paid hours, not twelve extra shifts. Employees say ten arrangements would be workable if confirmed 28 days ahead, but only six if confirmed seven days ahead. The six short-notice arrangements are among the ten workable with earlier notice. The other arrangements revert to the agreed base roster and approved cover; no one loses pay or must accept them.

Employees explain that some course bookings, shared transport and family arrangements need more than a week to coordinate. Some colleagues genuinely prefer choosing nearer the date. Staff do not have to disclose private details to justify declining a change. The exercise records stated feasibility, not actual attendance or a randomised experiment, and cannot guarantee future availability.

The demand forecast has not changed. There is still no quantified gain from the later information B might receive. Both plans and the funded fallback remain available. The manager must now decide how to test the roster while preserving choice and checking whether apparent scheduling flexibility becomes usable coverage.

**Student question:** With demand estimates unchanged, which roster would you trial and why? Compare the stated workable rearrangements, then specify a notice-and-swap rule and measures that would show whether the arrangement works for both sides.

## Answer the opening decision

A is a reasonable provisional choice when predictability supports employees’ planning; B is reasonable if the team values later choice and better demand information is likely to matter. Before choosing, ask when employees can commit, which placements are genuinely optional, what notice different arrangements need and how swaps affect service. Employer discretion over timing is not the same as employee control. Equal paid hours do not settle that distinction.

## What the reveal changes

The exercise makes A the stronger trial for this team: ten of twelve rearrangements are declared workable with early confirmation versus six of twelve with shorter notice, or about 83.3% versus 50%. The difference is four four-hour placements, representing 16 hours of differently placeable work, not 16 extra paid hours, saved wages or lost sales. The baseline staffing and cover remain. Availability depends partly on when the request is made; it is not a fixed pool waiting for the forecast.

## A stable commitment with a usable adjustment route

Publish A as promised and keep a simple, voluntary swap route. Check replacement skills, rest, agreed availability and service coverage before confirming a swap. Keep the original assignment until both parties and the manager agree the change; do not pressure colleagues or make employees solve unfilled business demand themselves. Allow workers who prefer later choices to volunteer for suitable adjustments without making that preference compulsory for everyone.

## Test the relationship, not only the forecast

Review the four-week trial with the team. Record actual notice, requested and accepted changes, unresolved placements, paid cover use, service queues and voluntary feedback on ability to plan. Compare these with demand forecast errors and staff preferences; do not infer cause from one month or treat private reasons as performance data. Retain B as a possible later trial if usable short-notice capacity or the value of updated forecasts proves greater. A is not a promise never to adjust, and B is not inherently abusive under the stated protections.

## Instructor note

Keep hours and pay constant so students cannot answer by assuming fewer hours or unpaid risk transfer. The reveal supplies no new demand shock: it changes what the manager knows about coordination with employees. The twelve-item exercise is a paired planning comparison, not observed shifts or a causal estimate. The Gap study used a package of practices; both study groups already had advance notice and no on-calls. It therefore does not identify the isolated effect of four-week notice. This case is about employment-quality and mutual adjustment, not hybrid meeting overlap or aggregate production quantities.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All shop details, notice periods, protections, availability declarations and figures are original fiction, not statements of Hong Kong scheduling law. Both plans preserve agreed paid weekly hours, rest, voluntary changes and base-roster fallback; essential service has funded paid cover. Twelve four-hour rearrangements concern existing hours. Early feasible 10/12 = 83.3%; late feasible 6/12 = 50%; difference four placements × four hours = 16 differently placeable hours, not extra work or wage savings. Stated feasibility is not an attendance forecast or causal finding. Demand is unchanged and later-information value unquantified. International guidance is context, not a local legal rule. The twelve rearrangements concern the first week; both notice intervals are measured before that week starts, not before each shift.

- [Stable Scheduling Study, 2018: original report](https://worklifelaw.org/publications/Stable-Scheduling-Study-Report.pdf). Describes the multi-component Gap experiment, advance-notice practices shared by both groups and additional treatment practices. Not an estimate of four-week notice alone.
- [Schneider and Harknett: research brief on schedule instability, 2019](https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/files/2019/01/Consequences-of-Routine-Work-Schedule-Instability-for-Worker-Health-and-Wellbeing.pdf). The authors report associations between unstable service-sector schedules and wellbeing in their survey data. These are not local causal effects.
- [ILO: Working Time and Work-Life Balance Around the World](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_protect/@protrav/@travail/documents/publication/wcms_864222.pdf). Distinguishes hours from arrangements and discusses how unpredictable schedules interfere with planning non-work time.
- [ILO: Guide to developing balanced working time arrangements, 2019](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_protect/@protrav/@travail/documents/publication/wcms_706159.pdf). Provides principles for balancing enterprise needs, employee influence and working-time arrangements; no specific notice rule is imported.
- [OECD Employment Outlook 2021: working-time arrangements](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-employment-outlook-2021_5a700c4b-en/full-report/working-time-and-its-regulation-in-oecd-countries-how-much-do-we-work-and-how_bfff25bb.html). Distinguishes employee-controlled flexible hours from variable scheduling over which employees have little control. The fictional B plan retains stronger voluntary protections.
