# A larger limit. The same gap?

## In the news

On 4 December 2023, Hong Kong’s Insurance Authority released claims information following Typhoon Saola and the September black rainstorm, including property-damage and business-interruption claims.

Aggregate claims do not establish any particular shop’s coverage. The studio, quotations and simplified policy terms below are invented for teaching, not a real insurance recommendation or claim determination.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong craft-workshop studio reviews its weather protection before the next storm season. The owner remembers losing business when customers could not attend.

Its current interruption policy pays verified covered interruption loss only following insured physical damage at its own premises, up to HK$100,000. For this exercise there are no non-damage, access-denial, utility or supplier extensions. Ignore deductibles and waiting periods solely to isolate the issue.

For an additional HK$2,000 annual premium, the studio can increase that limit to HK$200,000. Nothing else changes. The owner says: “Twice the protection should make us ready.”

## Evidence

| Option | Maximum payment | What starts coverage? |
|---|---:|---|
| Current policy | HK$100,000 | Insured physical damage at this studio’s premises |
| Higher limit | HK$200,000 | The same condition |

Higher limit costs HK$2,000 more annually; other stated conditions unchanged.

**Student question:** Would you approve the higher limit now, keep the current limit, or investigate first? What must you know before calling it twice the protection?

## Round two

The owner identifies the planning scenario: a storm warning prevents that day’s booked workshops from operating. No physical damage occurs at the studio.

The studio incurs HK$30,000 of net interruption loss, after deducting expenses it does not incur. No other stated coverage trigger applies. This is a hypothetical scenario, not a forecast of its probability or an actual claim.

**Student question:** Does the higher limit address this scenario? Explain the payment under each limit, then identify what the owner should investigate next.

## Check the entry condition before the ceiling

Under the supplied terms, both options pay HK$0 in this scenario. The required physical damage has not occurred. Raising the maximum payment does not change that condition.

The HK$30,000 loss remains with the studio under either option; the higher-limit option also costs HK$2,000 more in annual premium. Do not confuse a coverage limit with a promise to pay every business loss.

## Does that make the higher limit useless?

No. A different scenario involving insured physical damage could produce a covered interruption loss above HK$100,000. A higher ceiling could then matter, subject to all terms.

One uncovered scenario is not enough to decide the best policy. Map the loss mechanisms and sizes, compare actual terms and premiums, and consider the studio’s ability to retain losses. No probabilities or complete alternatives are supplied, so an optimal insurance purchase cannot be calculated here.

## Optional extension · A different trigger

Consider a separate fictional warning-trigger arrangement. It pays a fixed HK$20,000 if a specified official warning persists throughout a defined operating-time window, without requiring physical damage. Assume all other eligibility conditions are met for this example; its premium is not supplied.

If that precise condition is met while the studio loses HK$30,000, the stated payment is HK$20,000 and HK$10,000 of the loss remains, before considering premiums. If the condition is not met, the payment is zero even if the studio still loses HK$30,000.

This mismatch between a measurable trigger and actual loss is called basis risk. It changes the gap rather than eliminating every gap. Do not treat this arrangement as a replacement for all existing property protection.

## Make the next review concrete

Ask a qualified adviser to compare the actual contract’s insured events, locations, extensions, exclusions, limits, waiting periods, deductibles and loss definitions with the studio’s exposures. Real policies differ from our simplified terms.

Consider affordable reserves and safe continuity measures alongside insurance. Never operate during unsafe weather merely to reduce a loss. The question is how to finance and reduce disruption, not whether to ignore safety guidance.

## Instructor note

Take an opening vote. After the reveal, ask students to point to the exact condition that fails before discussing amounts. Separate “this upgrade does not cover this scenario” from “this upgrade has no value.”

Use the optional warning-trigger example only after the main distinction is clear. It illustrates a tradeoff, not a real quotation, advice to buy or cancel cover, or a statement about Hong Kong insurance law.
