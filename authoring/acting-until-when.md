# Acting lead. Until when?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 531 -->

PPL-44 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Temporary leadership · Credible return rights · Backfill commitments

![Two exhibit-design colleagues discuss a folder and blank calendar grid beside miniature display stands.](../acting-until-when-scene.jpg)

## An acting title is a dated appointment

In September 2020, HKEX announced an interim chief executive and specified when the appointment would take effect. The interim appointment took effect on 1 January 2021, the day after the outgoing chief executive retired. This fictional small firm instead needs temporary cover during an agreed leave; it borrows the idea of an explicit transition, not HKEX’s terms or governance rules.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong exhibit-design firm needs a lead for twelve weeks while its regular operations lead is on approved leave. A qualified internal colleague volunteers for a temporary move, provided the firm documents the right to return to their usual coordinator post at week twelve. The original leave, usual employment rights and agreed hours will not change.

I appoints the internal acting lead, with a paid acting allowance and qualified fixed-term backfill for the coordinator work. E hires a qualified outside interim lead; the coordinator stays put. Under either plan the interim lead has written authority on routine work within the usual limits, and unusual commitments remain with the owner. No candidate is owed a permanent promotion.

The owner has HK$120,000 for the twelve weeks. Listed costs are hypothetical and include applicable on-costs and handover in the initial period. Both plans meet the same essential service and competence requirements. The internal move offers practice leading the team, while outside cover avoids a second staffing transition.

## Evidence

| Plan | Weekly extra cost | What the cost buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I: internal acting lead | HK$8,000 | HK$3,000 allowance + HK$5,000 coordinator backfill |
| E: external interim lead | HK$10,000 | Qualified external lead; coordinator stays |

**Student question:** Which cover plan would you recommend before the leave begins? Compare the twelve-week costs and state two commitments the firm should write down so an acting appointment is genuinely temporary.

## Round two

Assume the firm chose I with a written twelve-week end and return date. During week ten, a properly approved extension brings the regular lead’s absence to eighteen weeks. The acting lead confirms they will return to the coordinator role as agreed at week twelve; their choice and the regular lead’s leave are not up for negotiation. The coordinator backfill contract ends as agreed with no renewal obligation or new loss of work assumed.

The owner approves a separate HK$70,000 budget for weeks thirteen to eighteen, after allowing for all first-period spending. The vetted external lead is available for those six weeks at HK$10,000 per week, plus HK$8,000 to prepare the extra transition. That amount includes funded handover before week thirteen, with no extra unpaid hours. A protected reduced-service fallback remains if the external lead does not clear final checks.

**Student question:** What would you recommend for weeks thirteen to eighteen? Show the extension budget and describe who tells staff when authority changes, what job the acting lead returns to, and how you would recognize the trial without promising a promotion.

## Debrief · Price the two roles, not just the title

I costs 12 × HK$8,000 = HK$96,000; E costs HK$120,000. I leaves HK$24,000 unspent in the original envelope. Its defensibility depends on qualified cover, the colleague’s voluntary agreement, and a written end and return to the coordinator post, not simply the saving. E is defensible if the extra handoff is a material operational concern.

## Debrief · A lower paper cost cannot extend consent

External cover costs 6 × HK$10,000 + HK$8,000 = HK$68,000, leaving HK$2,000 in the separate extension budget. The two funded phases together cost HK$164,000 if this option proceeds. The old balance is not needed or used as extension authority. An internal extension at the old weekly rate is not an available choice once the colleague declines.

## Debrief · Make the return normal

Have the owner document the end of the internal authority, the handover and the external start and expiry. Explain the change to relevant staff without disclosing private leave reasons. Restore the usual coordinator post as promised. Give formative feedback and recognize specific work done in the assignment; neither the return nor the extension is a verdict about readiness. Any future permanent role needs its own explicit process.

## Instructor note

Ask students to write one announced handover sentence, then reveal the extension. Discuss why a merely informal return promise could discourage future volunteers. This exercise neither negotiates anyone’s protected leave nor recommends an actual staffing or employment decision.
