# Split the surplus. Compare which alternative?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 287 -->

ECO-24 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Bargaining surplus · Outside options · Distribution

![A fictional gallery manager and ensemble organiser discuss a plain event folder beside empty chairs and a closed instrument case.](../split-surplus-compare-alternative-scene.jpg)

## A deal is compared with another possible day

Negotiation analysis separates the payoff from an agreement from the value of the best available alternative. A larger combined payoff leaves room for both parties to gain, but it does not by itself decide how they should divide that gain.

The event and amounts below are original fiction. Equal incremental gains is an explicit classroom benchmark, not a universal fairness rule, legal entitlement or prediction of what either business will accept.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong gallery and an independent music ensemble are discussing one Saturday event. Their services are complementary: neither can deliver this event alone. All production, musician pay, venue staffing, accessibility, safety, insurance and other event costs are already deducted from the stipulated HK$24,000 net amount available to divide. The gallery would collect this amount and pay an agreed net fee F to the ensemble; final project payoffs are HK$24,000 minus F and F. Do not deduct those included costs again.

If they do not agree, the gallery can take a verified separate booking yielding HK$8,000 net, while the ensemble can perform elsewhere for HK$4,000 net. These alternatives can both happen without the joint event, but each party can do only one engagement that Saturday. Each alternative is its best available one. Amounts are certain for this classroom exercise, measured for the same day, with equal payment timing and no omitted taxes, fees or financing differences. No prior obligation is being broken.

You advise the gallery. No fee has been proposed or accepted and no binding agreement exists. Both partners want a voluntary, defensible arrangement and may decline. Consider both equal final payoffs and equal gains relative to the separate alternatives. Actual relationship value and preferences about fairness have not been priced; the monetary comparison is a benchmark, not a complete utility model. Negotiation concerns only this complementary project, not unrelated prices or market sharing.

## Evidence

| Arrangement | Gallery net payoff | Ensemble net payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Joint event | HK$24,000 − fee | Fee |
| Separate alternatives | HK$8,000 | HK$4,000 |

**Student question:** What fee would you propose for the ensemble? Compare splitting the event payoff equally with splitting the gains over each alternative equally, and explain why a feasible fee is not automatically an accepted one.

## Round two

Before any proposal is accepted, the ensemble’s alternative organiser cancels its invitation for reasons unrelated to this negotiation. The ensemble now has a verified smaller engagement yielding HK$1,000 net, its best remaining alternative. The gallery’s HK$8,000 alternative is unchanged. Both alternatives remain mutually exclusive with the joint event. The joint event’s net HK$24,000, resources, costs and quality are unchanged; nobody has produced an extra service or reduced a production cost.

You may keep the fee you proposed in round one, or suggest a different fee before agreement. If you used the equal-incremental-gain benchmark initially, that earlier proposal was HK$10,000. The partners have not committed to that benchmark as a contractual formula. The ensemble may consider a reduced proposal unfair despite earning more than its new alternative; acceptance is not guaranteed. There is no evidence about the value of future business or either side’s minimum acceptable fairness standard. Do not invent a probability of agreement or treat the alternative’s loss as misconduct by either partner.

**Student question:** After the ensemble’s alternative changes, would you keep the earlier proposal or revise it? Recalculate the feasible range and equal-gain benchmark, and distinguish a changed fallback from new value created by the event.

## First find the room for agreement

Initially the gallery is no worse off than its alternative when HK$24,000 − F is at least HK$8,000, so F is at most HK$16,000. The ensemble is no worse off when F is at least HK$4,000. The monetary range is therefore HK$4,000 to HK$16,000 inclusive. At either endpoint one party is indifferent in money; both strictly gain only inside the range.

The event payoff is HK$24,000. The sum of fallback payoffs is HK$12,000. The incremental surplus relative to disagreement is HK$12,000. These are different quantities, not two names for the same pot.

## Equal money is not equal gain

An equal split of final payoffs gives HK$12,000 each. Gallery gain is HK$4,000 and ensemble gain is HK$8,000. Splitting the incremental HK$12,000 equally instead gives each a HK$6,000 gain: gallery payoff HK$14,000 and ensemble fee HK$10,000.

The symmetric bargaining benchmark can be illustrated by maximising the product of monetary gains: (HK$16,000 − F) × (F − HK$4,000). For this fixed-sum, linear-money example it is largest at F = HK$10,000. The assumptions matter; money is not necessarily utility, bargaining weights need not be equal, and the formula does not establish what fairness requires.

## A fallback change is not new production

After the update, the monetary range is HK$1,000 to HK$16,000. Combined fallback payoff falls to HK$9,000, so incremental surplus relative to disagreement rises to HK$15,000. The event still produces the same HK$24,000. Its unchanged payoff is now compared with a worse alternative.

Equal incremental gains now mean HK$7,500 each: gallery payoff HK$15,500 and ensemble fee HK$8,500. Compared with the earlier benchmark, the ensemble gets HK$1,500 less money while its gain over the new fallback is HK$1,500 greater. Keeping the earlier HK$10,000 fee instead gives gallery payoff HK$14,000; their respective gains are HK$6,000 and HK$9,000. Both proposals remain strictly inside the new monetary range.

## Recommend a proposal, not a supposed law of fairness

Keeping HK$10,000 can protect the original basis of the discussion and avoid appearing to exploit an unrelated setback. Proposing HK$8,500 can apply the explicitly chosen equal-gain benchmark to current alternatives. Neither choice creates extra event revenue. Explain the benchmark honestly and listen to the partner; do not claim that a positive monetary gain forces acceptance.

Laboratory bargaining studies show that acceptance can depend on expectations and perceived fairness. Those controlled games differ from this continuing business relationship, so they justify caution about prediction, not a rejection probability or diagnosis of irrationality here.

## Instructor note

Allow 3 minutes for the initial fee range, 3 for the two equality ideas and 5 for the update. Ask which number changed before asking who should receive more. Keep three columns: final payoff, fallback payoff and gain. A strong answer distinguishes a voluntary proposal from a binding commitment, monetary feasibility from acceptance, and increased surplus relative to a worse fallback from increased production. No real negotiation, competitor information exchange or legal conclusion is required.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All actors, engagements, net amounts, cancellations and timing are hypothetical. Initially, 24,000 − 8,000 − 4,000 = 12,000 incremental surplus; an equal gain of 6,000 implies an ensemble fee of 10,000 and gallery payoff of 14,000. Equal final payoffs of 12,000 imply gains of 4,000 and 8,000. Later, 24,000 − 8,000 − 1,000 = 15,000; an equal gain of 7,500 implies a fee of 8,500 and gallery payoff of 15,500. Holding the fee at 10,000 yields gains of 6,000 and 9,000. All monetary amounts are HK dollars. Inclusive monetary ranges allow indifference at endpoints, not guaranteed acceptance; strict gains require interior fees. All event delivery costs including paid work are already deducted, so the fee is a transfer of net payoff, not additional production cost. The remaining alternative is exogenous and verified; no threat, deception, agreed-fee breach or strategy to destroy an alternative is proposed.
