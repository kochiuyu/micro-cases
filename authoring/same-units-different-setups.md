# Same units. Same overhead?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 121 -->

ACC-11 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Activity-based costing · Cost drivers · Quotations

![Two adult workshop staff compare a large tray of matching badges with smaller trays of varied designs beside a setup checklist.](../same-units-different-setups-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 8 July 2024, the Hong Kong Productivity Council opened its Future Manufacturing Hall, showcasing manufacturing approaches supporting customisation and smaller batches. In September, it released a manufacturing study with Deloitte China highlighting the shift towards more variety in smaller quantities.

Flexibility raises a practical question: if two customers buy the same number of items, do they use the same support resources? The workshop and all figures below are fictional, not accounts from the demonstrations or surveyed businesses.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. You prepare repeat-order quotations for a Hong Kong badge workshop. Two customers each want 1,200 badges next month. One needs a repeated emblem; the other needs several artwork versions. Both use the same badge blanks and production-running time.

Sales favours one simple price per badge. The production supervisor thinks design-heavy orders may need a different quotation. You must choose the cost evidence for those quotations, not approve an immediate sale at any price.

The summary assigns each order half the setup-support pool because their quantities match. This is transparent and easy to explain. Setup-level costing could better reflect the work, but only if the records measure resource use. No quote has been sent and no production has started.

The HK$14,000 setup-support pool covers only the technicians and checking resources used to prepare and approve these runs; direct cost does not already include them. No other orders use the pool. **Student question:** Which costing basis would you provisionally adopt for repeat quotations: equal support cost per badge, or support cost based on each order’s setup work? Choose one, defend it and name the record that would validate your choice.

## Round two

A checked setup plan shows that the repeated-emblem order requires two setups and the artwork collection twelve. Each setup includes artwork loading, adjustment and required approval checks before its run.

For this exercise, every setup uses the same technician time and checking resources. There is no idle setup capacity or unrecorded activity in the pool; all 14 required setups are included. The HK$14,000 pool, volumes and production costs remain unchanged.

These are the customers’ specified orders. Combining or removing artwork versions has not been agreed. The reveal is new evidence about existing requirements, not a changed order. All required checks remain mandatory.

**Student question:** How much setup-support cost would you assign to each order now? Would you retain equal quotations, investigate a setup charge or offer a differently specified order for agreement? Explain why the cost evidence informs, but does not settle, your commercial choice.

## Count the work behind the units

The unit rule divides HK$14,000 across 2,400 badges: HK$5.8333 per badge, or HK$7,000 per order. Keep the exact order totals rather than multiplying a rounded unit rate.

The activity rate is HK$14,000 ÷ 14 = HK$1,000 per setup. Two setups assign HK$2,000 to the repeated emblem; twelve assign HK$12,000 to the artwork collection. Equal production-running time did not capture equal preparation work.

## Reconcile before recommending

Under setup costing, the repeated emblem carries HK$12,000 direct cost + HK$2,000 support = HK$14,000, or about HK$11.67 per badge. The artwork collection carries HK$12,000 + HK$12,000 = HK$24,000, or HK$20 per badge.

Both methods allocate the same total: HK$24,000 direct + HK$14,000 support = HK$38,000. Compared with setup costing, the unit method assigns HK$5,000 more to the repeated-emblem order and HK$5,000 less to the artwork collection. Changing the report saves no cash.

## A driver needs a reason

Setup count is defensible here because the checked record confirms equal resources per setup. If setup durations or checking demands varied, technician time or another activity measure might be better. The label “setup” alone is not enough.

This pool excludes direct materials, production-running costs and other business costs. Do not use setup count to distribute every expense merely because it works for this activity.

## Better cost evidence is not a price instruction

A separate setup charge or differentiated quotation is worth investigating. Neither customer’s willingness to pay is supplied, however. Keeping a single price may still be a deliberate commercial choice, provided management recognises the different support demands.

A differently specified order requires the customer’s agreement. Reducing setups might release staff time rather than reduce next month’s payroll. An assigned cost is not automatically an avoidable payment, a minimum acceptable price or proof an order should be rejected.

## Back to the news

The HKPC announcements supply a real small-batch manufacturing context, not evidence that surveyed companies use this costing method or misprice their products. The fictional comparison separates equal quantities from equal resource use.

Activity-based costing can improve a repeat-quotation discussion. Its value still depends on credible records and whether better information justifies collection effort.

## Instructor note

Allow two minutes for the situation and first vote, three for requested records, three for the reveal and calculations, and four for quotation choices and limits. Keep the debrief hidden until the second decision.

Ask equal-price supporters whether simplicity is worth the unequal support demands. Ask differentiated-price supporters what customer evidence is missing. Neither answer should invent demand, automatically cancel an order or skip required checking.

Unlike ACC-02’s branch-closure decision, this case traces a defined resource pool rather than identifying costs avoided on exit. Unlike OPS-04’s batching question, no production schedule or customer specification changes. Check that students reconcile HK$38,000 under both methods.
