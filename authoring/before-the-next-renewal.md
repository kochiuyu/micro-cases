# More renewals. More people who meant to stay?

MKT-45 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Renewal reminders · Informed retention · Reversal costs

![Two designers in a drawing studio consider a phone mock-up beside two blank planner cards.](../before-the-next-renewal-scene.jpg)

## A reminder has to arrive while there is still a choice

Hong Kong’s Consumer Council has documented disputes over mobile-app renewals and urged clear charges, cancellation steps and reminders. The fictional service below is comparing when to send a supplemental reminder, not whether to make cancellation difficult.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong drawing-practice subscription is deciding when to send an extra reminder about the next paid term. Its existing terms and required notices are clear, compliant in the exercise and identical under either option. Subscribers explicitly opted into renewal, can stop it until the same disclosed cutoff, and retain paid access until expiry.

A sends the extra reminder seven days before the cutoff. B sends it one day before. Same visible price, cutoff, direct route to turn off renewal, and refund protection. Members can decline the extra message. The service may not introduce another step or count silence as a survey response.

A fictional randomized pilot assigns 100 consenting subscriptions due to renew to each arm. The first reading counts successful renewal charges. A kept renewal produces HK$100 contribution after ordinary variable costs; a fully reversed charge produces no contribution and costs another HK$20 in handling. Other costs are equal and ignored.

## Evidence

| Preliminary reading | A: seven days | B: one day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscriptions assigned | 100 | 100 |
| Successful renewal charges | 72 | 80 |

**Student question:** Would you keep A or B as your provisional preference? Explain what the charge count establishes and what it does not show about whether people wanted to stay.

## Round two

The completed pilot finds five of A’s 72 charges and twenty of B’s 80 charges were fully reversed by the same end date. All others remain paid. These are observed reversals, not an estimate of all unwanted renewals.

Of the renewed subscriptions that stayed paid, 61 in A and 45 in B had independently recorded a current, explicit intention to continue before the charge in an optional prompt. They had correctly identified the charge and cutoff. Others have no verified preference by this measure; they are not assumed to be unhappy. The prompt and recording process were the same in both arms.

**Student question:** Which timing would you carry forward? Calculate contribution and confirmed intended continuation per assigned subscription. What would you monitor about the cancellation route before the next test?

## Debrief · Gross billing can reverse the ranking

A retains 72 − 5 = 67 paid renewals; B retains 80 − 20 = 60. Contribution is HK$6,700 − HK$100 = HK$6,600 for A, and HK$6,000 − HK$400 = HK$5,600 for B. Per assigned subscription, that is HK$66 versus HK$56. The later extra reminder charges eight more accounts but produces HK$1,000 less contribution in the observed window.

## Debrief · Keep confirmed intent and billing separate

Confirmed intended continuation per 100 assigned is 61% for A and 45% for B. Six kept renewals in A and fifteen in B do not meet this measurement definition. A missing voluntary response does not prove an unwanted charge. Recommend A here based on net contribution and the higher verified count, not a rule that seven days is universally best.

## Debrief · Test the route, not just delivery

In an approved follow-up, check whether reminders arrived before the actual cutoff in the account’s local time and whether a person could turn off renewal and receive confirmation without going through retention offers. Measure complaints, refunds and later paid continuation separately. Keep access, refunds and price fixed; review nonresponse before interpreting intent rates.

## Instructor note

Take a vote on the first dashboard before revealing reversals. Have students reconcile the 100 assigned accounts within each arm and explain why verified intent, net billing and satisfaction are three different outcomes. The exercise is not legal advice or a test of restricted cancellation.
