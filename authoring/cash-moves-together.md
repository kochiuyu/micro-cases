# Two income streams. One bad month?

## A Hong Kong financial-education context

On 27 September 2024, Hong Kong’s Investor and Financial Education Council explained that diversification depends on how investments move together, not only on holding different names. The case adapts that principle to operating cash streams, not purchases of financial securities.

The business, service portfolios, cash amounts, scenario weights and stress event below are original teaching assumptions. They are not estimates for any actual firm or an investment recommendation.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong business provides event-preparation support. It can add one of two contract portfolios with existing funded capacity. A adds event-display preparation; B adds document digitisation work. Both can be served competently and lawfully, require the same setup outlay and management time, and fit the same budget. Choosing either does not change cash from the existing line. Contracts have not been accepted; no securities, loans or financial accounts are involved.

The owner prioritises steadier total monthly operating cash rather than the largest single good month. The planning team supplies four ordinary monthly scenarios, weighted equally at one quarter each within this ordinary-scenario model. Each amount is cash received that month less that line’s directly attributable cash payments. Common monthly cash commitments of HK$100,000 are excluded from the table and must be paid from the combined total. No receivable timing, double-counted expense or unpaid invoice adjustment is needed. These are constructed scenarios, not a random sample or guaranteed forecasts.

The same row must be used for the existing line and the chosen addition: it describes one common operating environment. The existing line and A rely more on event activity; B’s digitisation workflow is scheduled differently in this model. A and B each average HK$35,000 and have the identical standalone set of values: HK$20,000, HK$30,000, HK$40,000 and HK$50,000. Their order against the existing line differs. A funded, unrestricted contingency reserve is available; choosing a portfolio is not permission to release it.

**Student question:** Which addition better serves the owner’s ordinary-month cash-stability objective, A or B? Compare the combined cash in each row and explain why equal standalone averages and variability do not make the additions equivalent.

## Round two

A separate stress workshop identifies a prolonged building-access disruption affecting all three workflows. In the supplied one-month stress scenario, cash from the existing line is HK$20,000 and from either addition is HK$10,000, after each line’s direct payments. The common HK$100,000 commitment is unchanged. This is a stress assumption, not an instruction to work in an unsafe building; all service and safety obligations remain protected.

The owner has HK$70,000 of unrestricted cash already ring-fenced outside operating needs. It is available before payments fall due, is not borrowed and has no competing committed use. That reserve is the only funded contingency in the exercise. No extra credit, insurance recovery or delayed obligation is promised. Both additions remain operationally feasible; the stress scenario does not establish a new advantage for A.

No probability or duration beyond that single stress month is supplied. Do not add this scenario as an equally likely fifth row or claim that the four ordinary rows cover every possible month. The owner asks whether B’s smoother ordinary cash warrants describing the business as protected against bad months, and what to do with the reserve. A multi-month disruption requires further funding and planning; no such funding is assumed.

**Student question:** Would you retain your portfolio choice after the common-shock test? Calculate the one-month cash gap, state what the funded reserve can and cannot cover, and give a more accurate claim than “two income streams protect us from a bad month”.

## Combine matched scenarios

Existing plus A yields HK$60,000, HK$90,000, HK$120,000 and HK$150,000. Existing plus B yields HK$90,000, HK$100,000, HK$110,000 and HK$120,000. Both averages are HK$105,000, but the ordinary range is HK$90,000 for A versus HK$30,000 for B. The narrower spread favours B for the stated stability objective, not because it has higher expected standalone cash.

## Co-movement supplies the missing information

A rises and falls with the existing line; B moves against it in the four constructed ordinary rows. Co-movement means asking whether weak cash happens at the same time. Equal individual variability does not determine variability of their sum. These rows deliberately make the pattern clear; real operating streams rarely offset so neatly, and four hypothetical rows do not estimate a lasting correlation.

## Cash stability is not enough cash in every month

Subtract the HK$100,000 common commitment: A leaves −HK$40,000, −HK$10,000, HK$20,000 and HK$50,000; B leaves −HK$10,000, HK$0, HK$10,000 and HK$20,000. B still needs contingency cash in its weakest ordinary row. Both have average surplus HK$5,000 within the ordinary model. An average surplus is not cash available in every month.

## Stress the shared exposure

Under the separate disruption, either combination produces HK$20,000 + HK$10,000 = HK$30,000. Against HK$100,000 due, the gap is HK$70,000. The funded reserve covers exactly that one supplied month, leaving nothing in the ring-fenced reserve afterward. It does not cover an indefinite closure or every unmodelled cost. B remains preferable for ordinary stability on the given facts, but diversification does not replace contingency funding.

## Instructor note

Ask students to add rows before using any formula. Do not let them pair B’s best cash with the existing line’s best row: outcomes must be matched. Optional technical extension: ordinary population variances in (HK$000)² are 500 for existing cash and 125 for either addition; covariances are +250 for A and −250 for B. Combined variances are 1,125 and 125 respectively, using the supplied quarter weights rather than a sample n−1 divisor. No stress probability is given, so no unconditional expected return, insolvency probability or optimal reserve can be inferred. This is a hypothetical operating-policy comparison, not investment advice or an instruction to transact.
