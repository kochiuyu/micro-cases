# The supplier promises to pay. Is the money there when needed?

GVR-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Contract protection · Accessible funds · Continuity funding

![Two managers review a sheet and folders with lift doors visible behind them.](../the-promise-isnt-the-protection-scene.jpg)

## Contract protection needs to work under stress

Hong Kong’s Development Bureau publishes public works contract conditions that contemplate contract security. The United Kingdom Cabinet Office also publishes guidance on monitoring suppliers’ financial standing. These sources provide context for comparing ways to support continuity; the service contract and remedies below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Chan needs a replacement provider ready if the main lift-maintenance supplier fails. A separate funded safety plan keeps equipment out of service until professionals approve it. The continuity team models one failure in which a qualified replacement provider must be paid HK$90,000 by Day 5. The organisation has HK$30,000 of its own cash for this bill; ordinary services cannot supply more.

Promise is an unfunded supplier indemnity. Reserve is a separately funded amount already held by an independent agent. Both cover this failure by their stipulated fictional terms. The table gives the cash the planner assumes is available by Day 5 in that one scenario, not merely what can ultimately be recovered. No overdraft, supplier extension or other funding exists unless stated.

Choose the lowest annual fee among arrangements that cover the Day 5 bill together with the existing HK$30,000. Fees come from a different budget with a HK$9,000 annual cap. Both arrangements are unsigned. The continuity plan measures timing as well as amounts.

## Evidence

| Fictional protection option | Promise | Reserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominal recovery amount | HK$80,000 | HK$65,000 |
| Usable by Day 5 in the stated failure | HK$60,000 | HK$65,000 |
| Annual fee | HK$2,000 | HK$8,000 |

**Student question:** Does each arrangement pass the Day 5 funding screen? Which is initially preferred, and why is the nominal amount not the number to use?

## Round two

Verification reveals that Promise supplies only HK$40,000 by Day 5 in the stipulated failure, though its eventual HK$80,000 remains contractually due. Reserve’s HK$65,000 remains available by Day 5 as confirmed directly by the agent. Both fees stay unchanged.

**Student question:** Which option now passes? For Promise, how much separate assured financing available by Day 5 would close the gap? What evidence should Chan obtain before relying on either option?

## Debrief · Count spendable recovery at the deadline

Initially Promise provides HK$30,000 + HK$60,000 = HK$90,000, exactly sufficient. Reserve provides HK$95,000 and also fits. Under the lowest-fee rule, choose Promise provisionally at HK$2,000; it leaves no Day 5 buffer.

## Debrief · Eventual entitlement cannot pay tomorrow’s bill

Revised Promise provides only HK$30,000 + HK$40,000 = HK$70,000 by Day 5, a HK$20,000 shortfall regardless of what remains due. Reserve still supplies HK$95,000 and its HK$8,000 fee fits, so select it under the stated assumptions. Promise would need HK$20,000 in separate assured financing available before payment.

## Debrief · Verify the path to usable funds

Verify coverage of the stipulated failure, required documents, release authority, claimant access, available amount and time to receive cash. For Reserve, confirm independently that the funds really exist and the stated trigger and deadline can work. An accounting entry or nominal cap does not prove availability.

## Instructor note

Use 2 minutes reading, 4 to build the Day 5 bridge, 4 for the changed recovery and 5 to rehearse evidence questions. Do not draw conclusions about a real party’s solvency, claim enforcement or maintenance safety.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All amounts, contracts, remedies and timing are invented. The two examples are abstract protection structures, not advice on insurance, guarantees, banking or contract wording.

- [Hong Kong Development Bureau, General Conditions of Contract for Building Works](https://www.devb.gov.hk/filemanager/en/content_188/gf541a.pdf). Published public works terms include a security-deposit mechanism.
- [Hong Kong Development Bureau, General Conditions of Contract for Civil Engineering Works](https://www.devb.gov.hk/filemanager/en/content_188/gf548.pdf). A separate standard form describes its own security arrangements.
- [Hong Kong Development Bureau, NEC4 ECC Practice Notes](https://www.devb.gov.hk/filemanager/en/content_1002/01_NEC4%20ECC%20PN_(12-2024)(edit)%20v1.pdf). Discusses the use of performance bonds in public works projects.
- [UK Cabinet Office, assessing and monitoring suppliers’ financial standing](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-sourcing-and-consultancy-playbooks/assessing-and-monitoring-the-economic-and-financial-standing-of-suppliers-guidance-note-html--2). Explains assessing suppliers and using appropriate mitigation in sourcing and ongoing contracts.
- [UK Cabinet Office, The Sourcing Playbook](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-sourcing-and-consultancy-playbooks/the-sourcing-playbook-html). Discusses resolution planning and managing service continuity where a supplier fails.
