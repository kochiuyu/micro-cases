# The pass brought visitors. What did it pay?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 590 -->

HSP-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Attraction passes · Redemption payments · Channel cannibalisation · Incremental contribution

![Two staff compare blank tickets at a waterfront exhibition entrance.](../the-pass-brought-whom-scene.jpg)

## One pass can bundle several attractions

The Hong Kong terms for Go City describe attraction admission through an eligible pass. Other providers use multi-attraction products too. These are examples of bundling; the fictional contract below states its own payments to the venue and makes no claim about what pass operators actually pay.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong waterfront exhibit is choosing whether to join a city pass for one month. It has spare capacity on every day in this exercise, so no visitor is turned away. Without the pass, the exhibit keeps HK$90 for each direct ticket. Every actual admission costs HK$15 to serve, whichever channel the visitor uses.

Under the proposal, the pass operator remits HK$45 for every accepted redemption after its deductions; the exhibit pays a fixed HK$3,600 promotion charge. There is no further commission. The operator forecasts 300 redemptions. The initial estimate is that 50 of those visitors would otherwise have bought a direct ticket and the remaining 250 would not have visited. Other direct sales and costs stay constant.

## Evidence

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cash received from direct ticket | HK$90 |
| Net cash from a valid redemption | HK$45 |
| Cost per admission | HK$15 |
| Fixed promotion charge | HK$3,600 |

**Student question:** Would you join under the initial forecast? Calculate the incremental contribution and the greatest number of displaced direct sales that can occur without making the deal worse than staying out.

## Round two

A review of comparable previous campaign windows revises the displacement estimate to 90 of the same 300 expected redemptions. These are now the planning inputs, not confirmed causal effects. The payment terms, service costs and capacity are unchanged.

**Student question:** Should the exhibit still join? Calculate the minimum net payment per redemption that would make it indifferent at the revised displacement estimate.

## Debrief · Compare cash relative to staying out

A pass visit contributes HK$45 − HK$15 = HK$30 and a direct visit contributes HK$90 − HK$15 = HK$75. Joining at first adds 300 × HK$30, removes 50 × HK$75 from direct channel contribution, and costs HK$3,600. The net change is +HK$1,650.

## Debrief · Find how much switching the price can bear

After the fixed charge, the 300 redemptions leave HK$5,400 before lost direct sales. At HK$75 per lost sale, 72 lost direct sales exactly exhaust it. With spare capacity, losing more than 72 is not a crowding cost; it is the cost of visitors switching how they pay.

## Debrief · Renegotiate the net remittance

At 90 switched visits, the change is 9,000 − 6,750 − 3,600 = −HK$1,350. Decline on current terms. Adding HK$1,350 ÷ 300 = HK$4.50 per accepted redemption gives a tie at HK$49.50 net, holding volume and displacement fixed.

## Debrief · Treat displacement as an estimate

Track actual validated redemptions, direct tickets and operator remittances. Use a credible comparable period or trial to estimate which direct sales would have happened anyway. A bigger redemption count alone cannot verify a contribution gain.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to calculate the baseline, 4 to price the revision, and 5 to discuss what must be tested. As a cross-check, ask students to compute 250 new visits at HK$30 and a HK$45 contribution loss for each of the 50 switched visits.
