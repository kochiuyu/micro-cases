# A minimum spend. A banquet worth accepting?

## The meal is not the whole booking

Function-space research considers contribution over the time a room is unavailable to others, rather than food revenue alone. Venue terms often distinguish the public event from setup and dismantling access. This fictional booking decision uses that distinction; no real venue’s prices, policies or contract rights are imposed on its guests.

## Opening decision

You manage fictional Harbour Room, a Hong Kong venue with one indivisible banquet room. A confirmed Monday breakfast occupies it from 07:00 to 10:00, including setup, service and reset. That booking must be honoured. The room is ready for another use at 10:00; no alternative room or shared occupancy is available.

Two new enquiries await offers. A business lunch would need Monday 10:00–16:00: setup 10:00–12:00, event 12:00–15:00 and reset 15:00–16:00. A small wedding initially requests Monday 16:00–23:00: setup 16:00–18:00, banquet 18:00–22:00 and reset 22:00–23:00. All stated transitions and required checks fit those periods.

Neither enquiry is confirmed and neither customer has paid. For this exercise, the lunch will proceed if offered its specified Monday slot, and the couple will proceed if offered their complete requirements on either Monday or Tuesday. Tuesday is otherwise empty and has no other forecast demand. These are fictional demand assumptions, not real commitments.

The wedding package has a HK$20,000 food-and-beverage minimum. The couple’s stated order is exactly that amount; no extra consumption is assumed. At this minimum, food and beverage costs are HK$8,000 and all other attributable venue costs are HK$3,000, including staffing, setup support, reset and payment charges.

The lunch brings HK$20,000 revenue and HK$8,000 total attributable costs. All amounts use the same net-revenue basis; there are no additional customer fees or taxes in this exercise. Unchanged overheads and breakfast contribution are excluded because they are identical under every option. Both prospective events are operationally fundable.

Before sending a final offer, confirm the entire access requirement. Provisionally offer Monday under the package, or offer Tuesday at the same HK$20,000 minimum with explicit access hours. No discount, involuntary date change or later hidden surcharge is available. Safe staffing, breaks, accessibility, food controls and required room resets remain protected.

## Round two

The couple’s decorator supplies the final access specification before any offer is accepted: the wedding needs exclusive room possession from 11:00, not 16:00. Setup is 11:00–18:00, banquet 18:00–22:00 and complete removal/reset 22:00–23:00. The couple will not reduce or share that access. It is necessary for this agreed design, not a universal wedding standard.

This longer possession adds no cash cost beyond the quoted HK$3,000 other attributable costs in this exercise. Required staffing and breaks are already funded; not every exclusive hour requires continuous venue staff. Food costs remain HK$8,000. The change concerns room availability, not a newly discovered labour bill or permission to cut required work.

Monday’s lunch still needs 10:00–16:00 and cannot move dates or shorten. Its enquiry is not a confirmed booking and may be declined before acceptance. The confirmed breakfast remains fully protected. Tuesday can accommodate the wedding’s complete 11:00–23:00 requirement at the same price and costs, with no competing use.

The minimum is a food-and-beverage revenue floor, not payment for unlimited access or a guarantee of profit. Decide what complete offer to make now. A final written offer must specify the date, exclusive setup, event and reset periods, inclusions and total price; no customer has agreed to an unspecified access restriction.

**Student question:** Would you offer the wedding Monday or Tuesday now? Compare room occupancy and total incremental contribution across both dates. Explain why meeting the minimum does not settle the choice, and how you would communicate the alternative without changing an existing booking.

## Debrief · The initial schedule fits, conditionally

On the original specification, breakfast finishes its reset at 10:00; lunch occupies 10:00–16:00; wedding occupies 16:00–23:00. No room periods overlap. The wedding contributes HK$20,000 − HK$8,000 − HK$3,000 = HK$9,000. Together with lunch’s HK$12,000, the two enquiries contribute HK$21,000 if they proceed.

Monday is therefore provisionally feasible, not already confirmed. Tuesday would initially give the same two-date contribution under the supplied demand assumptions. The important pre-offer check is exclusive possession from first access through final clearance, not only guest arrival and departure. A positive food margin cannot certify a workable calendar.

## Debrief · The reveal occupies a different product

The wedding originally requested seven hours, 16:00–23:00. Its complete requirement is now twelve hours, 11:00–23:00, five additional exclusive hours. It overlaps five hours of the lunch’s six-hour possession period, 11:00–16:00. The lunch cannot be split, so the entire HK$12,000 contribution is lost if Monday is sold to the wedding.

The breakfast is unaffected: its full possession ends at 10:00. Protect that confirmed obligation under either choice. Do not solve the clash by making the lunch leave before reset, giving decorators access during another party’s booking or promising two customers exclusive use of the same room.

## Debrief · Compare the whole available schedule

Monday wedding alone contributes HK$9,000 across the available choices; Tuesday then remains empty. Keeping Monday lunch and offering Tuesday wedding contributes HK$12,000 + HK$9,000 = HK$21,000 under the stated acceptance assumptions. Offer Tuesday with the complete access period and the unchanged HK$20,000 minimum.

The alternative preserves HK$12,000 of contribution. Even if Tuesday were unavailable, Monday lunch alone contributes HK$12,000 versus HK$9,000 for Monday wedding, a HK$3,000 difference. That separate comparison does not justify cancelling a confirmed wedding; here both enquiries are still unaccepted and the breakfast remains untouched.

## Debrief · Minimum revenue is not opportunity cost

A food minimum answers what food-and-beverage revenue is required under the package. Contribution subtracts attributable costs. Opportunity cost is the best alternative business prevented by the promised room rights. They are different quantities: HK$20,000, HK$9,000 and, for the Monday wedding, HK$12,000 respectively.

Do not treat the five additional exclusive hours as free merely because this case adds no staff cost. Equally, do not invent a universal hourly surcharge from the lost lunch. A different price or shorter-access design would be a new offer requiring agreement; neither is supplied as an available solution.

## Debrief · Make the offer clear before commitment

Explain that the full design requires exclusive 11:00–23:00 possession and offer Tuesday at the stated minimum, with all listed costs included. Record setup, banquet, removal and room-ready times separately. Confirm the complete proposal before taking payment or promising access. The couple retains the choice to accept the offered date.

The exercise stipulates demand for comparison. Outside it, a flexible-date enquiry is not secured revenue; acceptance, cancellation terms and other demand would need checking. Never hide access conditions until after booking, move an existing guest to improve contribution, or suggest a minimum spend overrides agreed service and safety obligations.
