# One ticket. Can we promise a return?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 600 -->

HSP-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Re-entry guarantees · Live occupancy · Capacity buffers · Guest communication

![A gallery attendant checks a paper ticket beside a visitor and an entrance gate.](../one-ticket-two-entries-scene.jpg)

## An entry and a right to return are separate

M+ publishes ticket terms that distinguish gallery entry from rules for leaving and returning during an event. Hong Kong Palace Museum’s event ticket terms also qualify permission to re-enter. The fictional gallery below deliberately offers a stricter service promise than these examples.

## Opening decision

You run admission for a fictional Hong Kong design gallery with an occupancy limit of 80 visitors at any instant. You have issued some free return slips. Each promises one same-morning re-entry by the expiry time printed on it. The desk must allow every holder who arrives with a valid slip to enter, even if they return together. No other visitor has a prior booking.

At 10:00, there are 50 visitors inside and 14 valid slips held by visitors currently outside. These are disjoint groups. A cumulative gate tally shows 68 entries so far today, counting repeat entries. Thirty new visitors are waiting. Assume any person admitted now could remain until all guaranteed returns have arrived. No walk-up admission comes with a return slip.

## Evidence

| 10:00 observation | Visitors or passes |
| --- | --- |
| Gallery occupancy limit | 80 |
| Currently inside | 50 |
| Valid guaranteed returns still outside | 14 |
| Gate entries logged so far today | 68 |
| Walk-up queue | 30 |

**Student question:** How many new visitors can the desk admit right now and still honour all 14 potential returns? Explain why the gate tally does not determine that number.

## Round two

Before any walk-ups have been admitted, staff verify five permanent exits among the original 50 occupants. Three slip holders return in time and join the occupancy count. Six other slips expire unused, after the guaranteed deadline has passed and no holders are waiting. The other five slips remain valid and outstanding. All 30 walk-ups are still waiting.

**Student question:** How many walk-up admissions can be confirmed now? Reconstruct the inside and outstanding counts. Which change affects both counts but does not free a new place?

## Debrief · Track promises alongside occupancy

The capacity commitment is 50 inside plus up to 14 valid returns. Admit at most 80 − 50 − 14 = 16 new visitors now. The tally of 68 counts events, not how many people are currently inside or have a guaranteed return.

## Debrief · A return moves one person between categories

The three successful returns increase current occupancy by three and reduce outstanding rights by three. They do not create free capacity for a new visitor. Each final exit frees one place; each verified expired right frees a place reserved for someone who is outside.

## Debrief · Recompute before inviting more visitors

Inside is now 50 − 5 + 3 = 48. Outstanding is 14 − 3 − 6 = 5. Capacity for walk-ups is 80 − 48 − 5 = 27. Admit up to 27 and keep three on the wait list. Reconcile the counts again after further confirmed changes.

## Debrief · Records should match the promise

Use a consistent count of inside, permanently departed, valid return pending, returned and expired. Apply both arrival and lapse updates without double counting. Explain guarantees and waiting separately. An unread counter or ambiguous expiry is not a reason to assume a reserved place is free.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to draw the first capacity ledger, 4 to update for returns and lapses, and 5 to discuss uncertain counts. Ask students which change they would verify before letting anyone else enter.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

The return slips, visitors, counts, rules and decisions are fictional. Source venues have different policies. This case does not establish any actual museum’s safety capacity or admissions rules.

- [M+ Museum terms and conditions](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/terms-and-conditions/). Separates gallery entries from leaving and re-entering an event at management discretion.
- [Hong Kong Palace Museum entertainment ticket terms](https://www.hkpm.org.hk/en/ticketing-terms-and-conditions). Clause 14.9 sets out a distinct real event re-entry rule.
- [Hong Kong Palace Museum membership terms](https://www.hkpm.org.hk/en/join-and-support/membership/terms-and-conditions). A specific guest entry code is restricted to single entry, illustrating distinctions by product.
- [Dr Sun Yat-sen Museum group visit application form](https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/forms/sysm_afgv.pdf). Notes describe regulating admission by gallery capacity and say re-entry is not permitted for the arrangement.
- [Hong Kong Disneyland, park visit reservation terms](https://www.hongkongdisneyland.com/static/resources/html/reservation/park-visit/terms-and-conditions/index.html). A separate venue distinguishes reservation from capacity availability, with different rules to those in the case.
