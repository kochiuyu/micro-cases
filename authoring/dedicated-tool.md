# A dedicated tool. A dependable agreement?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 138 -->

STR-12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Asset specificity · Contractual safeguards · Relationship governance

![A workshop owner, production colleague and retailer discuss a display-tray sample and two tooling options at a meeting table.](../dedicated-tool-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 28 October 2026, a Hong Kong Government reply described support for new smart production lines and the upgrading of manufacturing. Investment in equipment also raises a business question: how useful will it be if a particular customer relationship ends?

The workshop and quotations below are fictional. No government funding, eligibility or recommendation is assumed.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A Hong Kong workshop is negotiating a one-year programme of non-safety-critical display trays for a retailer. The retailer forecasts 10,000 units at HK$25 each, but has not yet committed to a minimum quantity. Neither equipment route has been accepted.

Both routes use the workshop’s existing press and staff, meet the same quality and delivery requirements, and are affordable without displacing existing work. Choose a production-and-relationship proposal, not whether to outsource. All figures are incremental, undiscounted cash amounts; common overhead, tax and financing are excluded.

## Evidence

| Route | A · General fixture | B · Dedicated tool |
|---|---|---|
| Setup and tooling | HK$20,000 | HK$80,000 |
| Cost per delivered unit | HK$18 | HK$10 |
| Other uses | Reset for other customers | Retailer’s unique tray shape |
| Cost at 10,000 units | HK$200,000 | HK$180,000 |

Setup includes installation/training; unit costs include all additional production, inspection and delivery costs. Replacement orders are not secured. End-of-year asset values are not estimated; this is first-year cash outlay, not lifetime profit.

**Student question:** Which proposal would you provisionally favour, A or B? Explain the cost advantage you would pursue, the dependence you would accept, and one commitment you would seek before buying the tool.

## Round two

Before either investment is made, an independent equipment assessment confirms that the general fixture could be sold unused for net HK$15,000, while the dedicated tool would recover only net HK$5,000 if this retailer withdrew immediately after setup. These are stipulated values for that specific stress scenario, not year-end valuations.

The retailer now offers a professionally checked B package: agreed prices remain HK$25, and it must purchase at least 8,000 conforming units during the year. The workshop can deliver that quantity. After production starts, ordinary cancellation does not release the minimum obligation. Before production starts, the retailer may instead terminate by paying HK$60,000; the workshop retains the tool. This termination payment replaces, rather than supplements, the minimum purchase obligation.

A remains available on the original forecast-only terms. The retailer will not offer the B safeguards with A, and no other package is currently offered. Neither proposal protects against insolvency or breach, assures renewal, or guarantees sales above the minimum. No such probabilities are supplied. Both parties still need to accept the arrangement before it takes effect.

**Student question:** Would you now choose A or the safeguarded B package? Explain how the minimum purchase and pre-production termination payment change the dependence, and identify one important exposure that remains.

## A lower unit cost can create a narrower exit

At 10,000 units, A costs 20,000 + 18 × 10,000 = HK$200,000. B costs 80,000 + 10 × 10,000 = HK$180,000. B saves HK$20,000 in first-year cash outlay at that forecast volume.

The dedicated tool needs more money upfront and has much less value outside this relationship. This is asset specificity: productive value tied to a particular use or partner. It is not simply an expensive machine, or proof that the retailer will behave badly.

## Match the safeguard to the investment

The minimum purchases, if performed and paid, support a base volume rather than turning the entire forecast into an order. At 8,000 units, B’s specified cash outlay is HK$160,000 and receipts are HK$200,000. The HK$40,000 difference is before excluded common costs and is not a guaranteed profit.

At that same quantity, A would cost HK$164,000, but A has no commitment to that volume. A same-volume calculation is not evidence that both contracts offer equal demand security.

## Test the exit separately

If the customer uses the stated termination right after setup but before any production, B’s unrecovered setup cash is 80,000 − 60,000 − 5,000 = HK$15,000, assuming payment and the stipulated net sale. Without that termination payment it would be HK$75,000. Do not also count 8,000 unit sales in this branch.

For comparison, A’s unused-fixture loss in that immediate-withdrawal scenario is 20,000 − 15,000 = HK$5,000. The safeguard reduces B’s exposure; it does not make B as easy to redeploy as A or eliminate collection risk.

## Choose a relationship, not only a machine

B is defensible if lower production cost and the limited commitment justify more upfront exposure and dependence. A remains defensible for wider potential uses, lower initial commitment and a smaller loss in the specified early-exit scenario, despite its higher production cost.

A contract can allocate responsibilities without guaranteeing cooperation or payment. Before a real investment, verify who can commit, credit, product acceptance, change procedures, payment security and practical dispute handling. This classroom package is not a legal template or permission to compel purchases outside agreed terms.

## Instructor note

Allow three minutes for the forecast-cost vote, four for the commitment reveal and four for groups to defend their choice. Ask: what becomes harder to change after the tool is bought, and which promise addresses that difficulty? Use the exit arithmetic only after discussing that relationship question.

Keep the before-investment decision distinct from a sunk-cost decision after purchase. Hold-up is a possible vulnerability when alternatives become weak, not an allegation of misconduct. STR-03 concerns retaining prototyping capability; STR-10 explicitly uses reusable assets and concerns customer concentration. Here both routes produce internally, and the central issue is governing a relationship-specific investment. Do not turn this into FIN-12’s lease-versus-buy exercise or infer a universal best contract.
