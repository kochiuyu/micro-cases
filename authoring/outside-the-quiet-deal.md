# A better private deal. A cost outside the contract?

ECO-30 · Fictional commercial courtyard · 15–20 minutes · Negative externalities · Coasean bargaining · Transaction costs

![Three neighbouring small businesses face a shared courtyard, with two owners comparing a work schedule.](../outside-the-quiet-deal-scene.jpg)

## The boundary of the bargain matters

Coase’s original analysis asks how institutional arrangements change the total cost of harmful effects, explicitly including the expense of finding parties, bargaining and enforcing an agreement. CORE’s economics text and EPA’s economic-analysis guidance explain why information, affected parties and transaction costs matter. An IMF explainer similarly distinguishes private and wider costs.

Official UK noise guidance illustrates a separate point: public protections and complaint routes do not vanish because businesses prefer an arrangement. No UK rule is transplanted into this fictional courtyard, and the economic calculations are not a legal permission to cause a nuisance.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** Workshop A and recording studio B share a commercial courtyard. Both are considering additional controls for lawful, non-hazardous operating noise during one specified month. All mandatory noise, worker-health, building and other protections are satisfied under the professionally assessed options. These additional arrangements cannot waive anyone’s legal rights or authorise a nuisance. Neither option has been implemented.

Separate installs A’s partial source-control package for HK$6,000 and B’s receiver-side isolation for HK$8,000. Together these remove the modelled disturbance to B. Bargain instead shifts A’s noisy work to hours when B is closed, imposing HK$9,000 of opportunity cost on A and no remaining loss on B. B pays A HK$5,000, and they split a HK$1,000 real negotiation/monitoring cost equally. No extra equipment is purchased under Bargain.

All figures are complete incremental economic costs for this same month, including installation/removal where relevant. There is no later residual value, financing effect or unpaid labour omitted. Opportunity cost is net surplus forgone after avoided costs, not lost revenue. The HK$5,000 payment is an internal transfer between A and B, not a new resource consumed. Relevant business output and other benefits are otherwise held constant.

A and B have confirmed these private costs but have not mapped everyone affected by the changed hours. No assertion that the two-party cost is the whole neighbourhood cost is justified. The decision is whether to pursue the proposal subject to that check, not to sign away someone else’s interest. All parties retain complaint and lawful remedy routes.

**Student question:** Would you pursue Bargain rather than Separate? Calculate A’s and B’s private costs and the combined resource cost, and state what must be checked before implementation.

## Round two

A site-wide check identifies editing business C, which works during the proposed shifted hours and was not party to the negotiation. Under Bargain, C would lose HK$6,000 of net operating surplus during the same month. Under Separate, A’s partial source control prevents this loss to C; B still needs its own isolation. This is a stipulated technical difference between source control and moving the noise, not a measurement claim about real buildings.

A third option, Full Control, uses a source package costing HK$13,000 that eliminates the modelled disturbance to both B and C, so B’s isolation is unnecessary. A complete three-party cost-sharing agreement would consume a further HK$2,000 in information gathering, negotiation and monitoring. This is the complete alternative transaction cost, replacing rather than adding to Bargain’s HK$1,000. All costs are prospective and avoidable; none is sunk.

C’s HK$6,000 loss is real forgone net surplus, not a payment to A or B or business merely diverted to another local firm. No other differential harms or benefits enter this deliberately bounded monetary model. Non-monetary concerns and legal rights remain separate constraints, not assumed to be purchasable. Compensating C would redistribute money but would not remove that production loss.

Compare Separate, the original Bargain with C’s loss included, and Full Control with its stated transaction cost. A lower-cost mediation route might reduce the latter cost, but there is no quotation below HK$2,000 yet. Nobody has authority to charge C merely because it would benefit from quieter operations.

**Student question:** Which option now has the lowest total modelled cost? Explain why compensation is not abatement, and find the transaction-cost ceiling at which Full Control would match or beat Separate.

## Answer the bilateral calculation

Separate costs A HK$6,000 and B HK$8,000, total HK$14,000. Under Bargain, A bears HK$9,000 − HK$5,000 + HK$500 = **HK$4,500**; B bears HK$5,000 + HK$500 = **HK$5,500**. They save HK$1,500 and HK$2,500 respectively. Combined cost is HK$10,000, a private joint gain of HK$4,000.

The transfer cancels: HK$9,000 real opportunity cost + HK$1,000 transaction cost = HK$10,000. Do not add HK$5,000 again. Pursue the proposal conditionally, but map affected parties, verify effects and preserve all mandatory standards and rights before implementing. A bargain that benefits its signatories is not automatically a social improvement.

## Answer the wider comparison

Including C, original Bargain costs HK$9,000 + HK$1,000 + HK$6,000 = **HK$16,000**. Separate remains HK$6,000 + HK$8,000 = **HK$14,000**, with no modelled C loss. Full Control costs HK$13,000 + HK$2,000 = **HK$15,000**. Choose Separate among the three quoted feasible alternatives.

Full Control is technically cheaper before bargaining expenses, but not after them. This is not a theorem that separate investment is always best, or that government must never intervene. It is a bounded comparison of supplied alternatives. Unknown external effects, enforcement difficulty or new quotations could alter the ranking.

## Transfers do not make the loss disappear

If A and B compensate C HK$6,000, C’s private net position can be restored in this simplified money model. The lost surplus still occurred; adding the payment to one side and subtracting it from the other cancels in total. Original Bargain still costs HK$16,000 before any extra compensation-negotiation cost. Nor can compensation buy permission to violate mandatory protections or another person’s rights.

Relative to Separate, A and B together save only HK$4,000 while C loses HK$6,000. Their gain cannot fund full compensation and leave all three no worse off under the stated costs. Distribution and voluntary participation therefore matter alongside the total. Avoid treating C’s exclusion as a mere administrative inconvenience.

## The transaction-cost boundary

Let T be Full Control’s complete transaction cost. It matches Separate when HK$13,000 + T = HK$14,000, so **T = HK$1,000**. Below HK$1,000 it is strictly cheaper; at HK$1,000 it ties; above that it is dearer. The quoted HK$2,000 does not meet this ceiling. Do not assume a free mediator or ignore participants’ time.

At T = HK$1,000, A and B could in principle share the HK$14,000 total as HK$6,000 and HK$8,000 while C pays nothing, matching Separate’s private positions. That arithmetic shows feasibility under voluntary agreement, not a predicted bargaining outcome. A saving below the ceiling opens a possible surplus to share, subject to evidence, rights and agreement.

## Instructor note

Write three ledgers: real control costs, real lost surplus and transfers. Only the first two plus real transaction costs belong in the aggregate. The costs are a one-month common-horizon model, not capital purchase prices compared with annual losses. C’s loss is not a health valuation, and legal compliance does not prove an absence of economic spillovers.

SUS-04 asks who funds a non-excludable shared benefit. Here the original contract relocates an adverse effect and omits an affected business. Coase’s zero-transaction-cost benchmark is a diagnostic, not a claim that actual bargaining automatically covers everyone or produces a fair distribution.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All businesses, technical effects and HK$ amounts invented for one common month. Mandatory protections satisfied and rights retained; no legal nuisance authorised. Separate eliminates C loss through A source control; Bargain moves operating hours and causes C loss; Full Control removes both affected firms’ disturbance. Complete prospective costs, no sunk costs/residual values. C loss is net surplus not revenue or diverted trade. Transfer HK$5,000 cancels; original Bargain resource total HK$16,000 including C. Full Control replaces the HK$1,000 negotiation cost with HK$2,000, not both. No broader social optimum claimed.
