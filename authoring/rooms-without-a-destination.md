# A busy season. A destination all year?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 360 -->

HSP-30 · Fictional waterfront destination · 15–20 minutes · Seasonality · Destination management · Resident value

![A quiet waterfront hotel stands beside a closed attraction and a small transport stop.](../rooms-without-a-destination-scene.jpg)

## Seasonality extends beyond the hotel

UN Tourism’s indicators guide treats seasonal opening, infrastructure and employment as part of destination performance, not just occupancy. OECD’s 2026 tourism analysis describes uneven demand across seasons and cautions that national figures can conceal local variation. Its separate 2025 destination report links resilience to transport, community support and co-ordinated services.

Fáilte Ireland’s Kilkenny plan includes opening-hour extension and stakeholder delivery as destination-development tasks. Germany’s Environment Agency explains what seasonal bed-night indicators measure and their limits. These are frameworks and plans, not evidence that a specific off-season festival produces benefits. The destination and every figure below are invented.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A waterfront destination partnership has HK$120,000 for one discretionary programme this year. Its board values both local year-round usefulness and visitor business, without assigning monetary weights between them. Existing safety, accessibility, environmental protections, resident services and worker pay remain funded and unchanged. No bookings or contracts for the new programme have been made.

Peak is four visitor-facing cultural events during the busy season. The complete HK$30,000 per-event price includes content, staff, venue, access arrangements, waste handling and programme administration. Each has 100 attendance places. Event slots, public transport and operating hours are verified as sufficient; resident access and crowd limits are protected. These are capacities, not forecasts of attendance or new tourism spending.

Shoulder is a resident-co-designed programme of six afternoon craft and local-history sessions in quieter months, open to residents and visitors. The quoted core programme costs HK$20,000 per session and offers 40 attendance places each. Core staff, content, venue use, access within the venue and administration are included. The proposal assumes ordinary public transport and attraction opening hours are sufficient; no extra transport or opening costs are included, and those assumptions are not yet checked.

Hotels report sixty unbooked rooms on each proposed Shoulder date. This is neither a commitment of visitors nor a reason residents need to stay overnight. The partnership cannot convert rooms into attendance, jobs or community benefit by arithmetic alone. Both proposals must become complete, deliverable programmes within the same cap; a mixed programme has not been quoted. The board can pause rather than promote an undeliverable offer.

**Student question:** Which programme would you provisionally favour: Peak or Shoulder? State whose benefit you prioritise and what must be verified before treating spare rooms and a programme quotation as a usable destination experience.

## Round two

The operating calendar reveals that Shoulder sessions run 15:00–18:00, but the gallery site normally closes at 15:00 and the last public return service leaves at 16:00. The core quotation had assumed routine opening and adequate return travel. Visitors and residents who depend on that connection could not complete the advertised experience. Neither a cheaper room nor extra advertising solves those gaps.

A verified revised Shoulder package is now available for four, not six, dates: core content and paid delivery at HK$14,000 per date; HK$24,000 total for an additional accessible return service; HK$18,000 total for gallery staffing/opening until 18:30; and HK$12,000 total for co-ordination, accessible booking and outcome evaluation. All are incremental package costs with no overlap. The remaining budget can be retained as a contingency, not counted as delivered activity.

The revised core price reflects a reduced, redesigned offer, not an assumed linear discount from the original. Each date still provides 40 attendance places. Twenty additional round-trip transport places are secured per date, with return at 18:45. The remaining twenty attendance places are for people able and choosing to reach the venue independently by assessed accessible local routes; no one is assigned a travel mode on a demographic assumption. Allocate transport places at booking to actual needs and cap dependent bookings at twenty. No taxi subsidy or extra capacity is assumed.

A safety/access check confirms the revised schedule, waiting arrangements, paid staff, venue capacity and environmental limits. Provider commitments can be secured before promotion; participation remains unproven. Peak remains available at its original complete HK$120,000. The partnership must monitor residents’ experience, service reliability, paid work and environmental pressures, not simply celebrate room occupancy. No claim is made that four dates establish a year-round destination.

**Student question:** Would you fund the revised Shoulder offer, choose Peak or pause? Calculate the revised budget and attendance/transport capacities, then set a delivery gate and measures that distinguish resident usefulness from hotel occupancy.

## Answer the initial choice

Peak’s four × HK$30,000 = HK$120,000 is a complete verified delivery quotation, with 400 attendance places. Shoulder’s six × HK$20,000 = HK$120,000 covers only the stated core under unverified access/opening assumptions, with 240 nominal places. Do not treat equally sized quotations as equally complete programmes. Neither capacity is a demand forecast.

Provisionally favour Shoulder if the board prioritises useful quieter-month services and community participation, but condition that preference on transport, venue hours, paid staffing, access and environmental checks. Peak is defensible if near-term visitor programming is the priority within verified capacity. Vacant hotel rooms supply one component, not an entire experience or a demonstrated resident benefit.

## Answer the revised operating choice

Revised Shoulder costs four × HK$14,000 + HK$24,000 + HK$18,000 + HK$12,000 = **HK$110,000**. The HK$120,000 cap leaves **HK$10,000** contingency. Four × 40 gives **160 attendance places**; four × 20 gives **80 round-trip transport places**. These are programme-date capacities, not unique residents, tourists, room-nights or jobs.

Prefer the revised Shoulder pilot if the board accepts a smaller, genuinely usable offer aligned with resident-facing objectives. Do not advertise the original six-session experience. Peak remains defensible for the other stated priority; pausing is warranted if commitments or accessible operations cannot be secured. The figures do not establish that Shoulder has higher social return or that spending all available money is better.

## Make the connected offer real

Before promotion, secure dated provider commitments for core delivery, gallery opening, accessible return travel, waiting arrangements and staffing. Confirm who owns updates, cancellations, accessible communication and refunds if any paid element is later introduced. This programme has no assumed admission receipts, so no ticket income offsets the budget.

Match bookings to transport needs within the twenty dependent participants per date and forty total places. Preserve independent-arrival choice rather than forcing people to walk or buy an overnight room. If a provider withdraws or safe/access arrangements fail, adapt or cancel with clear notice instead of quietly selling an incomplete itinerary. Contingency does not authorise unspecified services beyond the cap.

## Measure a destination, not a bedroom

Track whether advertised sessions and return services actually operate, place use and unmet transport demand, resident and visitor participation separately, accessible participation and resident feedback. Count repeat attendances separately from unique people. Record paid hours and staff experience, but do not label temporary sessions as permanent jobs.

Monitor noise, waste, travel burden and displacement of ordinary community activity; quieter months do not justify spreading pressure everywhere. Hotel occupancy may be one contextual indicator, not the primary resident-usefulness measure. Compare with an appropriate baseline and note weather, other events and selection effects before attributing change to the programme. Use the pilot to decide whether and how further seasons should be supported.

## Instructor note

Ask students to draw the full experience from origin to return: transport, operating attraction, staff, access and information. A hotel vacancy is not a usable itinerary. The reveal changes feasibility and the number of dates, not just the headline marketing message. The revised price is explicitly a new offer, not an algebraic scaling of the original.

HSP-03 optimises one hotel’s stay-pattern displacement; ECO-17 coordinates two complementary suppliers’ commitments. Here the unit is destination service across seasons and its residents as well as visitors. Require a stated objective, a bounded budget and service-delivery evidence; no causal employment, spending multiplier or year-round transformation claim is supported.
