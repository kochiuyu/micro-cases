# A price cap. Which shortage remains?

## Books bring readers together; stock is still finite

On 15 July 2026, Hong Kong’s Cultural and Creative Industries Development Agency described the opening of the 36th Hong Kong Book Fair and its support for publishing and reading events. The following small book sale is entirely fictional. Its price limit is an organiser’s voluntary condition, not a rule of that fair or a Hong Kong statutory price control.

## Opening decision

You manage a fictional one-day sale of an ordinary illustrated leisure book. Exactly 100 identical copies are available, all are offered, and no replenishment is possible that day. An agreed organiser condition sets a maximum posted price of HK$60; the seller chooses to charge that maximum. The objective is affordable access, not maximising revenue. The procurement and event budget already covers either allocation method, even at a lower price. No sale or customer promise has yet been made.

For this exercise, the exhibit gives exact numbers of distinct eligible people willing and able to buy one copy at each price, not survey estimates. Every person wants only one copy. Ignore resale, duplicate entries, no-shows and changes in book quality. All successful buyers collect and pay. The HK$80 row is a comparison showing where 100 buyers match 100 copies; it is not an authorised price option.

Choose an orderly first-come queue or a transparent ballot with one free entry per eligible person. Both have funded, safe and accessible arrangements, suitable assisted participation, advance notice and privacy protection. The queue gives an immediate result when a person reaches the desk but involves waiting. The ballot accepts entries during a published window and avoids ranking people by arrival, but leaves them uncertain until the draw. Neither guarantees everybody a book.

If a ballot is chosen, every willing eligible buyer enters exactly once.

## Round two

Before any announcement, the organiser lowers the agreed maximum to HK$40, and the seller will charge HK$40. At that price, exactly 200 distinct eligible buyers want one copy. The stock remains 100; none is withheld, and production cannot respond during this sale. All earlier participation, cost and collection assumptions remain unchanged.

You may retain or change the allocation method before publishing it. There is no evidence about buyers’ incomes, strength of preference or value of waiting time. Do not infer that an early arrival has greater need, or that equal ballot chances identify the poorest buyers. A one-copy limit is already in place and cannot create additional copies.

**Student question:** The maximum price is now HK$40. Would you retain or change your allocation rule, and what would you tell buyers about affordability and availability? Calculate the shortage and, if using a ballot, each eligible buyer’s chance.

## Price and quantity answer different questions

At HK$60, excess demand is 150 − 100 = 50 copies. At HK$40 it is 200 − 100 = 100 copies. The lower price reduces the cash payment for each successful buyer by HK$20, but sales remain 100 under the stipulated fixed-stock and collection assumptions. More people are willing to buy; more people do not necessarily receive a book.

## Specify the non-price rule

An equal-chance ballot gives each of the 150 initial buyers a 100/150, or two-thirds, chance. With 200 buyers, that becomes 100/200, or one-half. A queue allocates to the first 100 eligible arrivals; individual chances cannot be calculated without arrival information. It can offer immediate clarity but makes time and arrival order consequential. A ballot offers equal chances among entrants, not a guarantee or proof of need.

## Do not turn a shortage into a welfare verdict

Both rules can be defended against stated objectives. Announce the total stock, price, method and number who can receive a copy without implying universal access. Receipts fall from HK$6,000 to HK$4,000 if all 100 sell, but that is not a calculation of profit, consumer welfare or social loss. Without individual valuations, costs and waiting burdens, no monetary welfare comparison follows. Long-run supply responses are outside this fixed-day model.

## Instructor note

Unlike SUS-03’s unchanged-price allocation problem, the reveal changes the binding price limit and quantity demanded while stock stays fixed. Unlike ECO-03’s fee incidence, there is no seller repricing response. This voluntary organiser restriction illustrates a price-limit mechanism without claiming a government policy or extrapolating to housing, essentials or emergency rationing.
