# A reward for early repayment. After which fee?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 458 -->

FIN-39 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Early settlement · Incremental cash flows · Opportunity cost

![Two adult small-business finance colleagues compare a plain loan folder and blank dated calendar sheets in a fixtures workshop office, with no visible rates, balances or selected repayment date.](../early-repayment-after-which-fee-scene.jpg)

## A settlement date changes more than interest

Repaying a loan early can avoid interest, but a settlement fee and the return forgone on cash also matter. Compare complete cash flows on a common date. Returning borrowed principal is a payment of an existing obligation, not itself a saving.

## Opening decision

Fictional Harbour Fixtures, a Hong Kong business, has HK$100,000 outstanding on an existing fixed-rate bank loan. Its last interest payment has just cleared on day 0, so no interest is accrued today. The remaining contract requires HK$1,000 interest every thirty days and the full principal on day 180.

The finance manager compares N: settle fully today, with W: pay the next HK$1,000 interest and settle fully on day 30. Both end the loan before its original maturity. Partial repayment, refinancing and settlement on other dates are outside this decision. The loan is performing normally, with no arrears.

Exactly HK$104,000 is available today after protecting operating needs, obligations and reserves. Both choices are affordable. Any amount retained from today to day 30 earns exactly 0.2% over that period in the same available cash account, credited before day-30 settlement. There are no account charges, minimum balances, tax differences or other cash flows.

Today’s full-settlement fee is confirmed at HK$3,000. The manager initially assumes the same fee applies on day 30, but has not checked the dated schedule. Settlement cancels all interest after its effective date. No other settlement charge, rebate clawback, notice cost or release cost applies in this fictional contract.

Compare the remaining cash immediately after all day-30 payments. After that date, both alternatives are debt-free and face identical opportunities. Both settlement dates can be processed exactly as specified. Treat the stated terms and deposit return as certain classroom assumptions, not as a real bank quotation or a legal determination.

**Student question:** Under the initial assumption of a HK$3,000 fee on either date, which choice leaves more cash immediately after day 30? Include the return on cash left after immediate repayment. What exact contractual detail should be verified before acting?

## Round two

The lender’s verified schedule shows a contractual fee reset: the fee falls to HK$500 for full settlement on day 30. The HK$3,000 fee still applies today. This is a fee reset, not a loan-interest-rate reset. Waiting still requires the full next HK$1,000 interest payment.

The fee schedule and settlement quotes confirm that day-30 settlement is effective after that period’s interest is paid, with no further interest or fee. Available cash, protected reserves, deposit return and all other terms remain unchanged. Both options remain feasible; no lender concession or uncertain approval is needed.

**Student question:** Keep or change the settlement date? Recalculate both day-30 balances and the advantage of waiting. Separate the fee reduction, additional loan interest and extra deposit interest. What day-30 fee would make the two choices tie?

## Debrief · Settle now and retain the remainder

N pays HK$100,000 principal plus HK$3,000 fee today, leaving HK$1,000. That remainder earns 1,000 × 0.2% = HK$2. Its day-30 balance is HK$1,002. There is no further loan interest. Do not compare an immediate cash balance with a later balance without carrying both to the same date.

Under the initial assumption, W retains HK$104,000 and earns HK$208. It then pays HK$100,000 principal, HK$1,000 loan interest and HK$3,000 fee: HK$104,000 in total. Its day-30 remainder is HK$208. N initially leaves HK$794 more than W.

## Debrief · Recalculate the waiting choice

With the verified fee, W still has HK$104,208 before payment. Its settlement-related payment is now 100,000 + 1,000 + 500 = HK$101,500. It leaves HK$2,708 after day 30. N is unchanged at HK$1,002, so waiting leaves HK$1,706 more.

Choose W on the supplied financial comparison. Paying one more interest period is worthwhile here because the fee decline is large enough. This does not mean waiting always helps or that a fixed-rate loan necessarily has this fee pattern. The dated contract, not a general slogan about repayment, determines the result.

## Debrief · Reconcile the incremental difference

Waiting reduces the fee by HK$2,500, adds HK$1,000 loan interest and earns HK$206 extra deposit interest: HK$208 rather than HK$2. Its advantage is 2,500 − 1,000 + 206 = HK$1,706. Counting all HK$208 as incremental deposit income would omit the return still available under N.

The principal is HK$100,000 in both alternatives. Waiting does not erase it, and early payment does not save HK$100,000. Its later timing contributes to the additional deposit earnings already counted. Adding a separate principal-saving figure would double-count an obligation that both alternatives fully discharge.

## Debrief · Find the fee boundary

Let F be the day-30 fee in dollars. Waiting leaves 104,208 − 100,000 − 1,000 − F = HK$3,208 − F. Equating this to N’s HK$1,002 gives F = HK$2,206. Waiting is financially better below that fee, ties at it and is worse above it.

Equivalently, the fee must fall by more than HK$794 from today’s HK$3,000 for waiting to win strictly. The verified reduction is HK$2,500. This boundary assumes the same loan-interest period, deposit return, processing dates and protected cash position; a changed assumption requires a fresh comparison.

## Debrief · Verify before implementing

For a real contract, obtain dated settlement quotations and confirm outstanding principal, accrued interest, notice deadlines, fee bands, promotional repayments and when the loan actually closes. Verify cash availability and the return genuinely available without sacrificing liquidity. A quote for one date is not automatically valid for another.

This exercise compares two specified early-settlement dates, not every possible debt strategy or holding the loan to maturity. No transaction is authorised, and no real borrower is advised to use protected reserves. The original future interest schedule explains the existing debt; payments after day 30 cancel under both compared alternatives.

## Instructor note

Ask students to draw day 0 and day 30 first, then carry the cash left under each choice to the shared endpoint. The day-30 balance method and the incremental reconciliation should agree. Avoid treating the 0.2% thirty-day return as an annual percentage rate.
