# The deposits arrived. What can we spend?

ENT-49 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Pre-orders · Refundable deposits · Working capital · Venture launch

![A founder compares paperwork beside a shallow box and stationery samples.](../deposits-are-not-spendable-scene.jpg)

## Customers sometimes pay before receiving a product

A Hong Kong Consumer Council study on prepayment describes consumers paying a deposit or full purchase price before delivery. Pre-order systems can also offer a partial deposit with a later balance. This fictional maker’s refund and cash rules are assumptions, not a description of customers’ legal rights.

## Opening decision

Lung is preparing to launch an adjustable desk organiser in Hong Kong. There are 150 reservations, each accompanied by a HK$100 deposit. Customers can decline the order and receive the entire deposit back. The business has placed these deposits in a separate refund pot and will not spend any of it before conversion.

Lung has HK$16,000 of unrestricted cash and must keep a minimum HK$8,000 operating buffer. Quote Now requires HK$12,000 for irreversible setup today. Quote Wait costs HK$14,000 but can be accepted after conversions, with no money due now. Both buy the same launch capacity and meet the same delivery date. Wait is open at the date of the conversion count.

## Evidence

| Item today | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Unrestricted cash | HK$16,000 |
| Required operating buffer | HK$8,000 |
| 150 refundable deposits | HK$15,000, set aside |
| Irreversible setup, Now / Wait | HK$12,000 now / HK$14,000 after conversions |

**Student question:** Which quote should Lung keep open? Calculate how much can be spent today without touching the refund pot or the buffer, and what the 150 reservations do and do not show.

## Round two

Ninety-two customers actively convert to firm HK$150 orders and pay the HK$50 balance. For this model, the team has verified these receipts are cleared and available subject to protecting HK$70 per converted order for fulfilment and any remaining claims. All 58 other customers get their HK$100 back from the pot. No other cash changes.

The team requires at least 90 converted orders to proceed for commercial reasons. It also retains the HK$8,000 operating buffer after setup and the separate HK$70 per-order provision. These safeguards are fictional policies, not real suggested reserve amounts.

**Student question:** At 92 firm orders, can Lung take Quote Wait without breaking either rule? Find the minimum conversions needed to fund the setup and distinguish that number from the commercial threshold.

## Debrief · A refundable deposit is not available launch cash

Today Lung can spend HK$16,000 − HK$8,000 = HK$8,000 without touching protected funds. The HK$12,000 setup quote is HK$4,000 beyond that. Wait. Paid reservations are a stronger interest signal than a click, but are not firm orders in this contract.

## Debrief · Reconcile the refunds and conversion

Return HK$5,800 for the 58 declined reservations. The 92 converted orders total HK$13,800, including their original deposits and balances. Set aside HK$6,440 for those orders; HK$7,360 is available for setup. Do not add the original deposits again.

## Debrief · Apply both gates

Unrestricted cash after setup is HK$16,000 + HK$7,360 − HK$14,000 = HK$9,360. That is above the HK$8,000 buffer, and 92 exceeds the requirement of 90. Cash alone requires HK$6,000 in extra available funds, or 75 conversions at HK$80 each; the actual gate is the stricter 90.

## Debrief · Keep the promise funded

Track reservations, cleared firm payments, refunds and fulfilment provisions separately. If another refund or a greater fulfilment provision is required, recalculate before spending irreversible setup funds rather than treating all bank cash as free.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to compare the offer timing, 4 to reconcile the funds, and 5 to discuss governance. Ask how the launch changes if 85 strong orders arrive: cash suffices under the model, but the commercial gate is still not met.
