# Affordable interest. A repayable principal?

## The reporting question

The US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency describes refinance risk as the possibility that existing debt cannot be replaced on reasonable terms when needed. Loan schedules must therefore show principal due at maturity, not only affordable interest instalments. A cash reserve is a separate asset until it is actually used to repay debt.

This fictional comparison supplies both loan schedules and the cash constraint. It is not an application, investment, legal recommendation or forecast that any lender will refinance a business. Foreign guidance supplies concepts, not Hong Kong lending rules.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong printing studio needs HK$600,000 for equipment at time zero. Two hypothetical three-year offers provide exactly that amount, all spent on the equipment immediately. Neither has been signed or drawn. Existing operating cash is ring-fenced for normal operations and cannot fund this debt. After all operating costs, tax and essential investment, exactly HK$240,000 becomes available just before each year-end debt payment in years 1, 2 and 3. Treat those cash amounts as fixed for this exercise. There are no other inflows, usable opening reserves, asset-sale proceeds or owner injections.

Offer A amortises principal in three equal HK$200,000 year-end payments. Annual interest is 6% of the principal outstanding at the start of that year. Offer B pays interest only at 4% of HK$600,000 each year, with the full HK$600,000 principal due at the end of year 3 along with that year’s interest. No fees, taxes, rate changes, penalties or other cash costs apply. Quoted total nominal interest over three years is HK$72,000 for either offer; do not rank them solely on the interest rate. The different rates are supplied offers, not market observations.

Under B, the initial management plan is to pay interest, transfer HK$150,000 at each year-end into a separate zero-return cash reserve, and spend the remaining HK$66,000 each year on cancellable, nonessential promotions. Reserve transfers occur before the maturity payment and remain available for repayment. They do not reduce loan principal before maturity. The plan assumes a new loan will cover any principal not met by that reserve; the status of that future funding will be checked before selecting an offer. No promotion commitment has been made.

## Round two

The funding check finds only an informal expression of interest from a lender, subject to fresh credit approval and future terms. There is no committed refinancing facility. For the decision, assign it zero assured cash availability, not a probability of inevitable rejection. The board will approve only a plan that repays the original loan from the stipulated cash resources without relying on that uncommitted funding. Both original offers remain available for this classroom choice.

The promotions are entirely discretionary and can be reduced now without cancellation charges or any change to the supplied HK$240,000 annual cash availability. Management may instead put the same larger amount into B’s reserve each year. Alternatively it can choose A and use the remainder after each scheduled payment for promotions. Compare both feasible self-funded plans, state the maximum equal annual promotion budget under B, and identify any timing trade-off. No early loan prepayment, reserve return, new borrowing or additional cash source may be assumed.

**Student question:** With refinancing uncommitted, which self-funded plan would you choose, what equal annual reserve and promotion amounts make B feasible, and how does A’s cash pattern compare?

## First answer: A retires principal gradually

A’s opening principal is HK$600,000, HK$400,000 and HK$200,000 in years 1–3. Interest is therefore HK$36,000, HK$24,000 and HK$12,000. Adding HK$200,000 principal each year gives debt payments HK$236,000, HK$224,000 and HK$212,000. Remaining principal after those payments is HK$400,000, HK$200,000 and zero. All fit the HK$240,000 annual cash constraint.

## First answer: B’s small instalments leave a maturity amount

B’s interest is HK$24,000 every year. Its debt payments are HK$24,000, HK$24,000 and HK$624,000, with principal remaining HK$600,000 after years 1 and 2 and zero only after the full maturity payment. Both offers pay HK$72,000 total nominal interest and HK$672,000 total to the lender. Equal totals do not make the timing equal.

The initial reserve accumulates HK$150,000, HK$300,000 and HK$450,000 after the three transfers. It falls short of B’s HK$600,000 principal by HK$150,000. Year 3 interest is already funded separately from that year’s cash. The HK$66,000 annual promotion outlay consumes the rest, so no spare cash can be counted again to close the gap.

## Second answer: fund B’s maturity rather than assume it away

To repay B without refinancing, reserve HK$600,000/3 = HK$200,000 each year. With HK$24,000 interest, this leaves HK$16,000 for promotions from each HK$240,000 inflow. Compared with the initial plan, reserve transfers rise HK$50,000 and promotions fall HK$50,000 each year. After the third transfer the reserve is HK$600,000, used once to pay principal; the reserve and debt then both end at zero.

At the start of year 3’s payment sequence, earlier reserves total HK$400,000. Add the HK$240,000 current inflow: total available HK$640,000. Pay HK$24,000 interest and HK$600,000 principal, leaving HK$16,000 for promotions. The HK$200,000 third transfer is an internal earmarking within that cash, not an additional external expense to add to HK$624,000.

## A is also feasible, with a different discretionary pattern

A leaves HK$4,000, HK$16,000 and HK$28,000 for promotions in years 1–3. That totals HK$48,000, the same as fully reserved B’s three HK$16,000 budgets. Choose fully reserved B if smoothing the discretionary spending is valuable and the reserve discipline is credible. Choose A if gradual contractual principal reduction is preferred. Both satisfy the stated repayment condition; neither is uniquely best without that further preference.

Reject B’s original HK$150,000 reserve plus HK$66,000 spending plan as an assured self-funded plan. Affordable annual interest does not supply the missing principal. Uncommitted refinancing may eventually become available, but it cannot fill this decision’s assured cash column. The original loan must still be retired even if a new one could replace it.

## Instructor note

Draw lender payments and separate reserve balances on two lines. Before the reveal, require all principal and interest, then identify the HK$150,000 assumption rather than calling B affordable from its interest alone. After the reveal, require a feasible annual allocation within HK$240,000 and a year 3 reconciliation that avoids double-counting the reserve transfer.

This is maturity concentration and refinancing risk, not a competition to calculate a lower total loan cost: nominal total interest is intentionally equal. No discounted-value ranking, taxes or variable rates are supplied. The sources discuss different loan settings; no mortgage foreclosure rules or US bank duties apply by implication. Fixed future operating cash is a classroom assumption, not assurance that a real business faces no other risk.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Fictional teaching case with fixed cash inflows and supplied loan terms. No borrowing or financial transaction occurs. Equal nominal interest does not imply equal timing or present value. Foreign regulatory and educational sources provide concepts, not local legal advice.

- [US OCC Bulletin 2024-29, Commercial Lending: Refinance Risk](https://www.comptrollerofthecurrency.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2024/bulletin-2024-29.html). Primary supervisory guidance distinguishes residual principal at maturity and reliance on replacement debt. US bank requirements are background only.
- [US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, What is a balloon payment?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-balloon-payment-when-is-one-allowed-en-104/). Explains that lower early payments can leave a large final amount and refinancing may be unavailable. Its consumer mortgage rules and foreclosure context are not applied here.
- [P. H. Gutierrez and N. L. Dalsted, Colorado State University Extension: Long Term Loan Repayment Methods](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/long-term-loan-repayment-methods/). Original extension teaching distinguishes equal-principal and balloon schedules and interest on unpaid balances. The fictional rates and amounts are independent.
- [OpenStax, Principles of Finance, 8.3 Loan Amortization](https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/8-3-loan-amortization). Original textbook discussion separates payments, interest, principal and remaining balances. Its level-payment examples are not copied; A uses equal principal instead.
- [Iowa State University Extension, Loan Amortization](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/AGDM/wholefarm/html/c5-93.html). Original extension explanation distinguishes amortisation schedules from a balloon maturity that brings the remaining balance due. Agricultural examples and tax conclusions are not imported.
