# A venture plan. Which partners help shape it?

![A photography-workshop founder and venue colleague discuss plans beside sample photographs.](../partners-change-the-opportunity-scene.jpg)

## Partners bring more than money

On 7 April 2025, Microsoft Hong Kong described its partnership programme with Cyberport, including technical guidance and business-matching opportunities for local startups. This is a dated example of partners contributing different resources, not proof that a partnership guarantees demand. The workshop venture and all terms below are original fiction.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong founder can competently teach basic photography and initially imagines four weekend hobby workshops for adults. Two routes fit the same funded HK$12,000 launch budget. S is a standalone offer: the founder selects four available weekend sessions and controls the syllabus. P is a co-created offer: a venue and two specialist instructors are willing to make written, paid commitments to four sessions, but the precise calendar and customer use must be settled together. No route has been accepted and no customers have booked.

Both routes include safe accessible premises, insurance, paid preparation and delivery, lawful image permissions and clear refund terms. They each permit ten adult participants per session, charge an all-inclusive HK$400 per seat, and fund all materials and administration. P’s venue and instructors are independent businesses, not unpaid volunteers or equity investors. The founder can afford either complete programme without relying on customer prepayments. A loss-limit rule does not select between them.

The founder must provisionally choose whether to preserve the original offer’s control and calendar flexibility or develop an opportunity jointly around partners’ resources. P’s lower venue cost is offset by paid specialists. The question is not who provides the biggest subsidy: what service and customer purpose can these parties deliver together, and which choices would the founder give up?

## Round two

The partners now provide specific written offers, open for acceptance until Friday. The venue will reserve four Tuesday sessions, 10 a.m. to noon, for HK$2,000 total; these are otherwise unused slots, not weekends. Each specialist commits paid preparation and delivery of two sessions for HK$3,000, making four sessions for HK$6,000. Their complementary expertise is product lighting and catalogue-photo workflows. Together with the founder, they propose a practical programme for small retailers producing their own product photographs, rather than a general hobby course.

The offers commit the specified capacity at those prices only if accepted together. They do not supply customers, guarantee income or promise future dates. The venue will circulate one permission-based announcement to its business contacts but provides no contact list or sales guarantee. The partners must jointly approve this programme’s scope and any date change; neither may expand it unilaterally. The founder keeps the brand and existing materials, grants only the limited delivery permission needed for these sessions, and makes no exclusive or equity commitment. If a party cannot perform, the written arrangement provides a funded substitute or customer refund; no unsafe delivery or unpaid replacement work is allowed.

S remains available at its original cost, with four weekend slots selected from the venue’s available list and the founder’s broader hobby syllabus. P supplies dependable specialist delivery for a more specific weekday business use, but less calendar and syllabus freedom. No evidence yet establishes demand for either use. The founder can decline P without penalty before acceptance; neither route has a hidden financial advantage that resolves the customer-purpose choice.

**Student question:** Would you keep your provisional route after the partners’ specific commitments? Describe the venture P would now become, distinguish committed supply from customer demand, and name one boundary the parties must preserve.

## Resources can change the goal

The venue’s unused weekday capacity and the instructors’ complementary skills make a retailer-focused service possible. They do not simply finance the founder’s unchanged weekend hobby idea. The potential customer, learning outcome and schedule emerge partly from what the partners are prepared to do together.

## Separate a commitment from encouragement

The written offers specify who provides which dates, preparation and delivery for what payment, conditional on joint acceptance. That is more concrete than praise or a vague introduction. It still guarantees neither attendance nor future expansion. The announcement is a marketing activity, not an order or permission to obtain a contact database.

## Count the constraints as well as the means

P’s HK$2,000 venue plus HK$6,000 instruction, HK$2,000 materials and HK$2,000 administration totals HK$12,000, the same as S. Its advantages are specialist capabilities and specific committed slots, not free resources. The weekday timetable and joint scope approval narrow the opportunity. S preserves broader content control and available weekend choice, but the founder must deliver the original offer independently.

## Choose the service you want to build

P is defensible if the founder wants to develop the retailer-use opportunity with these partners and will test that customer need. S is defensible if the hobby direction and control matter more. Neither is made correct by the effectuation label. Forty available seats at HK$400 would produce HK$16,000 only if all were sold; capacity is not a demand forecast. Customer discovery and voluntary paid bookings remain separate work.

## Instructor note

Ask what changed besides the cost before allowing a vote. A strong answer names the new customer purpose, each party’s concrete commitment, its conditions, and the choices surrendered. Do not turn the case into a contest for the largest grant, an affordable-loss calculation or a claim that an interested venue validates demand. No learner must contact partners, accept an agreement or read an external source. All contract, safety, pay, privacy and intellectual-property boundaries are supplied in the fiction.
