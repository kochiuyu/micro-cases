## Digital support in the community

On 13 December 2024, Hong Kong’s SIE Fund launched the Smart Silver Digital Inclusion Programme for Elders, describing community help desks and practical support. This is a service-design context, not evidence that one channel works best. The community programme, budget, options and walkthrough below are original fiction, not an evaluation of Smart Silver.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong community programme offers free, optional household repair-advice appointments to adults. Existing telephone and staffed-desk booking remain funded, with required accessibility, language support, privacy protections and reasonable adjustments preserved. Nobody must own a smartphone, share a password or accept help from a relative. Both options below meet the programme’s requirements; the decision concerns an additional HK$24,000 six-week improvement budget, not whether to deny access.

Option A adds a self-service online booking system with evening availability, accessible design and confirmation reminders. The full budget covers setup, maintenance and staff training. Option B keeps existing booking technology and buys extra trained staff-supported booking windows, including two evenings each week. The full budget covers staff, cover and equipment. Both use the same appointment inventory, eligibility rules and fair allocation process; online users receive no priority. Advice capacity does not increase under either option.

Existing support can complete every eligible request within the programme’s current service standard. A could improve convenience for independent users; B could make help easier to reach without waiting for existing office hours. No comparative completion rate, demand forecast or cost saving is supplied. Choose which improvement to fund, not a free combination of both.

## Evidence

| Option | Additional improvement | Retained baseline |
|---|---|---|
| A · Online convenience | Self-service booking and reminders outside office hours | Existing assisted routes remain |
| B · More assisted windows | Extra trained help, including two evenings weekly | Existing self-service arrangements unchanged |

**Student question:** Would you fund online convenience (A) or extra assisted booking windows (B) for these six weeks? State whose access would improve and what a successful booking must include.

## Round two

Before commitment, 12 volunteers with varied booking-support needs try a demonstration of A using fictional accounts and no personal documents. All can navigate a smartphone. Eight complete the booking independently; four reach the contact-verification screen but cannot finish without guidance on switching back from a demonstration message to the booking page. With a trained facilitator guiding rather than taking credentials, all four finish. The interface permits accessible alternatives and copying; the difficulty is an observed task sequence, not proof of a standards violation or inability to use technology generally.

The same four say existing daytime help is inconvenient because of caring or work commitments; they can use B’s added evening windows. The eight independent completers value booking at any time. These are selected volunteers and expressed preferences, not a representative demand estimate or a controlled comparison of A and B. Their bookings did not reserve real appointments.

Under A, a clearly visible help route and the existing assisted service remain available, but the quoted package does not include extra evening staff or a redesigned verification sequence. Under B, evening staff can guide the complete booking, confirm the appointment and explain cancellation, with user consent and without storing credentials. Both preserve the funded baseline; neither guarantees attendance. A redesign or additional staffing needs a separately costed proposal, not an assumed free fix.

**Student question:** Does the walkthrough change your six-week choice? Specify where support belongs in the booking journey and two measures that would show whether people can actually complete it.

## A channel is not a completed service

The relevant endpoint is a valid booking that the user understands and can change or cancel, not opening a webpage. Smartphone navigation did not remove the verification-step barrier for four volunteers. Avoid treating age, device ownership or a confidence score as a complete assessment of support needs.

## Make the priority explicit

B is defensible if removing the observed time-and-guidance barrier is the immediate priority. A remains defensible if round-the-clock independent convenience is the chosen additional benefit and the existing supported route remains genuinely usable. The evidence identifies a problem for particular volunteers; it does not establish which option benefits more residents overall.

## Place help at the point of difficulty

Explain how to obtain help before and at verification, offer a complete assisted route, and confirm the next step at the end. Staff guide with consent and preserve user control; assistance does not mean collecting passwords, bypassing identity checks or requiring unpaid family support. Do not call an online-only help button sufficient for everyone.

## Measure the whole journey

Track completed valid bookings relative to attempted bookings separately for independent and assisted routes, along with abandonment points and waits for help. Seek feedback from people who stop as well as those who finish, using accessible channels. Later attendance and ability to cancel are useful downstream checks but should not be confused with booking completion.

## Do not extrapolate the demonstration

Eight of 12 completed independently and four used guidance in this selected walkthrough. Those counts neither forecast population uptake nor prove B’s causal effect. Test the funded service with intended users and staff, examine evening demand and privacy-safe process data, and cost any redesign before the next funding decision.

## Instructor note

Ask students to name the end of the service journey before revealing the walkthrough. Require an actual A-or-B budget choice and a stated beneficiary, not merely a recommendation to research further. Existing compliant access stays funded in both options, so this is not a vote on whether to meet obligations. The distinctive issue is locating an end-to-end access barrier, not training breadth versus depth, software rollout speed or general impact attribution.
