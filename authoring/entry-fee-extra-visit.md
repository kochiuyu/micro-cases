# A low visit price. A high-use member?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 167 -->

ECO-14 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Two-part tariffs · Participation and usage · Marginal contribution

![Two shared-studio colleagues discuss a blank membership card and visit tokens beside art supplies and empty worktables.](../entry-fee-extra-visit-scene.jpg)

## Hong Kong context

On 6 August 2024, LCSD announced changes to fitness-room monthly-ticket purchasing and booking arrangements, including advance booking for monthly-ticket holders from 1 November 2024. Monthly access and individual usage are separate practical decisions.

LCSD’s public-service ticket is not the commercial tariff below. Our shared art studio, customers and prices are original fiction. Published Hong Kong pottery-studio memberships also illustrate that access charges and additional usage-related charges can coexist; they do not establish how our customers will behave.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong shared art studio is choosing one price schedule for a new one-month offer. A charges HK$40 per visit with no entry fee. B charges a clearly disclosed HK$90 monthly access fee plus HK$10 for each visit. The studio offers either A or B to everyone, not both simultaneously. Membership is individual and non-transferable, lasts only that month, and has no automatic renewal, hidden charge or fixed number of prepaid visits. No existing contract is changed.

There are exactly 20 potential adult users: ten light users and ten heavy users. Under the existing HK$40 visit price, each light user makes two visits and each heavy user four: 60 visits total. Nobody else enters the model. Every visit has the same quality and complete incremental cost of HK$15. Fixed rent and existing staffing are unchanged. The studio can safely accommodate at least 90 visits without crowding, added staffing or displacement of other business.

The owner’s first worksheet assumes that all 20 users would join B and keep their old visit counts. On that provisional assumption, a light user would pay HK$110 and a heavy user HK$130. This is a deliberately unverified worksheet, not evidence of willingness to join or unchanged demand. The owner has not announced either offer. The goal is to compare contribution after visit costs, before unchanged fixed costs, while checking participation and usage rather than assuming them.

## Round two

Use the following complete fictional values to replace the fixed-usage assumption. Each row lists one person’s value, in HK$, for successive visits during this month. Light users value visits 1–6 at 70, 50, 20, 0, 0, 0. Heavy users value them at 80, 70, 60, 50, 30, 20. All later visits have zero value. These are incremental values, not cumulative totals, and apply to all ten people of each type.

For this model, each person knows these values and chooses the number of visits that maximises total value minus the full bill. They join only if that best surplus is positive; not joining gives zero. No travel, time, liquidity constraint, uncertainty or separate community benefit is omitted: these are already accounted for in the supplied values. Users cannot resell access. At either candidate per-visit price, there are no ties for an additional visit. Values and service quality do not change with the tariff; only the prices facing the customer do.

The studio still has ten of each type, HK$15 cost per visit, capacity for 90 or more visits and unchanged fixed costs. Calculate each type’s preferred usage and whether its total value covers B’s access fee. This is a deterministic classroom demand model, not a survey or a claim about actual Hong Kong members.

**Student question:** Using the supplied values, who joins and how often under each schedule? Recalculate revenue, visit cost and contribution, then explain why more visits or more revenue need not make B the better contribution choice.

## The provisional worksheet hides the behavioural question

At the old usage counts, A receives 60 × HK$40 = HK$2,400. Visit cost is 60 × HK$15 = HK$900, leaving HK$1,500 contribution. For B, the worksheet gives 20 × HK$90 + 60 × HK$10 = HK$2,400 revenue and the same HK$1,500 contribution. This numerical tie rests on assuming both unchanged participation and unchanged usage. It is not evidence that the schedules are equivalent.

## Choose visits, then check the access fee

Under A, light users buy two visits: values 70 and 50 exceed 40, while the third value 20 does not. Their total value is 120, payment 80 and surplus 40. Heavy users buy four: total value 260, payment 160 and surplus 100. Under B, a joining light user buys three visits because the third value 20 exceeds the visit price 10. Total value 140 minus visit charges 30 leaves 110 to compare with the access fee 90. Surplus is 20, so they join. A heavy user buys six: total value 310 minus visit charges 60 minus entry fee 90 leaves surplus 160, so they also join.

## The fee and the next visit answer different questions

The access fee determines whether joining is worthwhile after considering the best usage plan. Once paid, it does not make the next visit cost HK$40: the next visit costs HK$10. B therefore produces three light-user visits and six heavy-user visits, or 90 in total. The two decisions must be evaluated together before joining. This is not a prediction that people attend merely to recover a sunk fee.

## Reconcile the changed contribution

B’s revenue is 20 × HK$90 + 90 × HK$10 = HK$2,700. Its visit cost is 90 × HK$15 = HK$1,350, leaving HK$1,350 contribution. A remains HK$1,500. B adds HK$300 revenue but HK$450 cost, reducing contribution by HK$150. Each of the 30 extra visits brings HK$10 and costs HK$15; the access-fee revenue does not grow when existing members add visits. A wins this contribution comparison between these two candidates, not every possible tariff.

## Do not confuse contribution with everyone’s benefit

Light users’ surplus falls from HK$40 under A to HK$20 under B; heavy users’ rises from HK$100 to HK$160. Both types still participate in both schedules. The example does not show that every member prefers B or that a lower per-visit price is always harmful. Different entry fees, costs, values or capacity constraints could change the result. We have not optimised a fee or calculated a complete social-welfare assessment. Contribution here is before unchanged fixed costs, not net profit.

## Instructor note

Have students audit the initial worksheet and mark its two demand assumptions. Reveal the visit values, then ask them to circle additional visits worth more than the relevant per-visit price. Only after that should they subtract the access fee and decide participation. Reconcile the 30 extra visits and HK$150 contribution decline. Challenge counting the fee again on every visit, treating a membership as a six-visit bundle, or calling higher revenue higher profit. All figures are supplied; no outside reading or calculus is needed.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All studio conditions, customer counts, valuations, tariffs and costs are invented for this classroom exercise. Values are incremental and already net of non-price participation costs; there is no uncertainty, liquidity constraint, additional customer, crowding, resale or separate membership benefit. Both tariffs are available as alternative firm-wide proposals, not a simultaneous self-selection menu. The initial B worksheet is expressly unverified and holds usage and participation fixed; the second stage supplies a deterministic rational-choice model instead. The monthly fee is charged once to a joining person; usage is charged per actual visit. The offer is new and does not alter existing rights or contracts, renew automatically or hide extra charges. Cost HK$15 includes all incremental visit costs; fixed costs are unchanged and contribution is not net profit. Capacity covers at least the resulting 90 visits with no displaced use. No optimum across all fees, welfare ranking or actual studio demand is asserted. Distinct from ECO-08 combining two services, MKT-01 a finite shareable ferry pass, and ECO-13 quality signalling: this case separates access participation from marginal usage under a two-part tariff.
