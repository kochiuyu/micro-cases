# Did our estate really cut waste by 30%?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 6 -->

SUS-02 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Waste charging, policy evaluation

![An estate manager and a cleaner inspect a refuse bin beside sorted recycling.](../waste-scene.jpg)

## Opening decision

You chair a Hong Kong estate committee. After a one-week trial charging households for each bag of mixed rubbish, the caretaker shows you the collection record. A resident proposes printing “We produced 30% less waste” in the newsletter. Will you sign it?

## Evidence

| Weekly mixed rubbish (kg) | Before fee | Trial week |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estate collection | 1,000 | 700 |

**Student question** Approve the sentence, rewrite it, or ask for another measurement. Which number supports your decision?

## Round two

The recycling contractor sends a second weigh slip. In the week before the trial, the estate sent 100 kg for recycling. During the trial it sent 350 kg. Both slips cover the same households and the same number of days.

**Student question** How many of the missing kilograms can the recycling increase account for? What can you now say about the rest?

## Debrief

Mixed rubbish fell by 300 kg, from 1,000 to 700 kg. Recycling rose by 250 kg. Together, the two recorded streams fell from 1,100 to 1,050 kg. The remaining 50 kg is about 4.5% of the original recorded total. It could reflect prevention, storage until next week, or disposal elsewhere.

Charging per bag makes avoiding an extra bag valuable. Residents might buy less packaging, separate recyclables, or pack each bag more tightly. Weighing collections helps distinguish compaction from a fall in material. Before attributing a change to the fee, compare several weeks with a similar estate facing the same collection schedule and no new charge.

## Instructor note

0–3 minutes. Vote on the newsletter claim. Ask a student to name the 30% denominator. 3–7 minutes. Reveal the weigh slip and have pairs reconcile the 300 kg. 7–12 minutes. Ask for one defensible headline and one observation that could distinguish prevention from displacement.

## Sources and case assumptions

All information needed for the classroom decisions is provided in the case. The sources below are optional background, not assigned reading.


- [Hong Kong Legislative Council, March 2024 planning discussion](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2024/english/panels/ea/minutes/ea20240325.pdf). Records the planned scope of the 14-premises demonstration.
- [Report on the Municipal Solid Waste Charging Demonstration Scheme, May 2024](https://dailyhongkong.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report-on-the-Municipal-Solid-Waste-Charging-Demonstration-Scheme.pdf). Describes collection arrangements and recycling facilities at participating premises.
- [Legislative Council joint panel minutes, 27 May 2024](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2024/english/panels/ea/minutes/eafseh20240527.pdf). Documents discussion of bag use and waste disposal as separate indicators.
- [Hong Kong legislative brief, 31 May 2024](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2024/english/brief/epcr915042_20240531-e.pdf). Explains the decision to defer implementation after the demonstration.
- [Fullerton and Kinnaman, Household Responses for Pricing Garbage by the Bag](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4670). Measures garbage weight, volume and recycling separately in a US programme. It motivates the compaction question.

The estate, charge trial and weights are fictional classroom assumptions. Hong Kong’s 2024 demonstration at 14 premises provides the historical setting. The example does not describe a measured outcome at those sites.
