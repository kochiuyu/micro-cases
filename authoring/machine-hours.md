# A quiet year. Less machine used?

ACC-08 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accounting · Depreciation methods · Consumption estimates

![An embroidery-machine operator gestures toward the equipment while an older colleague considers an open notebook in a sunlit workshop.](../machine-hours-scene.jpg)

## In the news

In its 2025 annual report, with financial statements dated 11 March 2026, Cathay Pacific describes allocating owned-equipment costs over estimated useful lives and reviewing its policy annually.

This is a local reporting example, not a recommendation to change the airline’s method or evidence about embroidery equipment. The workshop and estimates below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong embroidery-service workshop is preparing its first full-year report for a new machine. Its initial recorded cost is already agreed at HK$96,000 and expected residual value at HK$6,000: the estimated net disposal amount at today’s prices for the machine in its expected end-of-use condition. It was paid for and ready for intended use on the first day of the year. No earlier report for this asset has been issued.

The manager proposes equal annual depreciation over an initially estimated six-year life: ageing, support and obsolescence could matter even during quiet periods. The supervisor proposes productive running hours, with an initially estimated 18,000-hour lifetime: workload varies, and operation may consume service capacity more directly. Neither pattern is settled yet. In this simplified exercise, carrying amount means recorded cost minus accumulated depreciation.

This year’s reconciled counter records 1,800 productive running hours, not hours switched on, employee-hours or sales. The classroom accounting basis allocates cost less residual value systematically according to expected consumption of benefits. Choose the method on evidence, not the expense you prefer. All this year’s services are delivered, so no inventory or work in progress absorbs the charge.

## Evidence

| Agreed facts and initial estimates | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Recorded machine cost | HK$96,000 |
| Expected residual value | HK$6,000 |
| Initial service-life estimate | 6 years |
| Initial total productive-use estimate | 18,000 hours |
| This full year’s productive use | 1,800 hours |

No impairment, revaluation, lease, financing, grant, disposal, separate-component or partial-year complication is included. This is financial accounting, not tax accounting or a compliance opinion.

**Student question:** Would you provisionally use equal yearly depreciation or productive hours for this machine? Calculate the first-year charge and name evidence that would make you change your choice.

## Round two

Before this first report is finalised, an independent technical assessment examines this particular machine, its maintenance arrangements, intended work and reliable counter. It supports about 18,000 total productive hours and identifies productive operation as the best available approximation to consumption.

Expected idle deterioration is not material for this purpose. Support availability, technical and commercial obsolescence and use restrictions are not expected to cut off the planned use before those hours. The quiet first year was a planned ramp-up; revised workload planning supports using the remaining 16,200 hours over the next five years. Residual value remains HK$6,000.

This is supplied scenario evidence, not a real engineering report, safety limit or guarantee. The new evidence concerns the consumption pattern and usable lifetime, not merely the existence of a counter.

**Student question:** With that assessment, which method would you recommend for this first report? What charge and carrying amount would it produce, and what change would require the estimate or method to be reviewed?

## Calculate the two proposals on the same base

The amount to allocate is HK$96,000 − HK$6,000 = HK$90,000. Equal annual allocation gives HK$90,000 ÷ 6 = HK$15,000 for the full year, leaving a carrying amount of HK$81,000.

Productive-hour allocation gives HK$90,000 ÷ 18,000 = HK$5 per hour. At 1,800 hours, the first-year charge is HK$9,000 and the carrying amount is HK$96,000 − HK$9,000 = HK$87,000.

Both calculations are internally consistent with their proposed patterns. Arithmetic alone did not establish which pattern represents the machine.

## Let the assessment determine the recommendation

The reveal supports productive hours for this machine’s first report: use-linked consumption is supported, and time-related limits are not expected to prevent the planned service. Recommend HK$9,000 depreciation and HK$87,000 carrying amount on these facts.

A reliable counter measures activity; it cannot by itself establish whether a machine deteriorates while idle or becomes obsolete before its hours are used. Those questions are why the assessment changes the recommendation.

Keeping equal annual depreciation would need a substantive reason to challenge that consumption evidence, not merely a preference for familiar or stable numbers. Equally, the lower first-year charge is not the reason to choose hours.

## Less expense this year is not cash saved

The productive-hour charge is HK$6,000 below the equal-year charge this year. The purchase payment is unchanged. No HK$6,000 cash saving or increase in market sale value follows, and no complete profit figure has been supplied.

Under unchanged estimates, the remaining 16,200 hours allocate HK$81,000 more. Together with the HK$6,000 residual, that reconciles to the HK$87,000 carrying amount. The lifetime depreciable amount remains HK$90,000.

A later year with 3,600 productive hours would carry HK$18,000 depreciation, above HK$15,000. Usage-based allocation is not a permanent route to a smaller expense, and the unchanged schedule does not allocate the asset below its residual.

## Keep the method disciplined as evidence changes

Use a consistent definition of productive hours and preserve the assessment and counter reconciliation. Review the expected life, residual and consumption pattern as required by the exercise’s accounting basis.

Fixed-date support withdrawal, new obsolescence, unexpected idle deterioration or a revised workload that leaves useful hours unreachable could matter. A changed estimate is not permission to rewrite earlier reports simply to improve a result. Actual revisions need the appropriate accounting treatment.

Productive hours are not sales dollars. Price changes or other business inputs can affect revenue without changing the machine’s consumption. Do not replace a physical-use measure with revenue just because revenue is readily available.

## Instructor note

The initial asset cost is agreed. Unlike Case 73’s acquisition-cost question, this decision concerns the pattern used to allocate that cost. Require a provisional method and calculation before revealing the technical assessment.

After the reveal, the hours method has positive support; do not present the two methods as freely interchangeable ways to choose profit. Ask students what evidence counters and assessments each contribute. The first report is still being prepared, so no opportunistic annual switching is involved.

Use the 3,600-hour illustration only if helpful. No closing cash balance, selling price or tax deduction can be inferred. The narrow classroom basis draws on historical IAS 16 principles; it does not certify current reporting requirements for every Hong Kong business.
