# Paid tools. Or pay just to stay listed?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 532 -->

STR-44 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Platform strategy · Seller participation · Catalogue breadth · Pricing design

![A Hong Kong marketplace operator compares a catalogue of craft supplies and an inventory-tool proposal with a merchant.](../paid-tools-or-pay-to-list-scene.jpg)

## Marketplaces can sell tools as well as access

Online marketplaces offer tools to help sellers manage listings or respond to stock changes. For example, Etsy documents a subscription feature that lets shoppers ask to be notified when a seller restocks an item. The case asks whether a fictional Hong Kong marketplace should sell a new inventory tool or require sellers to pay to remain listed.

## Opening decision

Fung runs a Hong Kong online market for small craft suppliers. It earns HK$6 contribution per completed order after variable platform costs and before any new programme. Sellers currently pay no monthly listing charge. The table gives one month’s baseline.

A proposal adds an inventory-tool dashboard and charges every seller HK$40 monthly for continued listing, whether they use the tool or not. The programme costs the platform HK$500 monthly. The initial forecast holds merchant participation and orders fixed. It assumes no seller changes prices.

## Evidence

| Seller group | Number of sellers | Completed orders per seller per month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small suppliers | 30 | 10 |
| Larger suppliers | 10 | 20 |

**Student question:** What monthly contribution does Fung project with the required fee versus keeping the current arrangement? Which participation assumption could make that forecast fail?

## Round two

The revised forecast says 20 small suppliers would leave if charged to stay, taking their expected orders with them. After subtracting those orders, the platform expects a further 10% loss of remaining orders: some shoppers visit mainly to compare the now-narrower range. Larger suppliers all stay. There are no price changes.

An alternative retains ordinary listing with no monthly charge. Sellers can instead opt into the inventory tool for HK$65 monthly. The monthly cost remains HK$500. Twelve sellers would subscribe; no sellers leave and completed orders stay at the baseline. The tool does not affect rank or visibility.

**Student question:** Under the revised forecasts, compare the baseline, required fee and optional tool tier. Which would you pilot and how many paid subscriptions would the optional tier need to beat the baseline?

## Debrief · An access fee can look stronger on paper

The baseline is 30 × 10 + 10 × 20 = 500 orders, for HK$3,000 contribution. If all 40 sellers stay, the mandatory-fee forecast is 3,000 + 40 × 40 − 500 = HK$4,100. It assumes a fixed catalogue and fixed shopper activity.

## Debrief · Follow sellers, then shoppers

Twenty fewer sellers remove 200 orders from the baseline, leaving 300. Apply the further 10% loss to those 300, not to the original 500. That leaves 270 orders. The revised fee plan contributes 270 × 6 + 20 × 40 − 500 = HK$1,920.

## Debrief · Charge for a feature without shrinking the catalogue

With the optional tier, monthly contribution is 3,000 + 12 × 65 − 500 = HK$3,280. The plan strictly beats HK$3,000 if 65 × subscribers exceeds 500. Eight paying subscribers suffice; seven do not.

## Takeaway · Test both sides of the market

Pilot optional tools while separating paid uptake from whether sellers still list and shoppers still complete orders. The model holds merchant prices and rank unchanged. Its feedback comes from loss of catalogue variety, not price pass-through or paid visibility.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 3 for the opening forecast, 4 to map seller exits, 5 to recompute the options, and 3 to describe evidence for the shopper response. Ask what happens if subscriptions are concentrated in one seller cohort.
