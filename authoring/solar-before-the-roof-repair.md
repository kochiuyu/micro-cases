# Solar now, or after the roof repair?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 526 -->

SUS-43 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Solar energy · Project sequencing · Asset maintenance

![A community centre manager and engineer compare plans on a flat roof beside an uninstalled solar panel.](../solar-before-the-roof-repair-scene.jpg)

## Rooftop solar has to coexist with the building

Hong Kong’s Electrical and Mechanical Services Department publishes guidance on rooftop solar installation and maintenance. Its practice handbook includes checking roof watertightness. Hong Kong’s feed-in tariff programme supports distributed renewable generation. The community centre and project figures below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Mei runs a fictional Hong Kong community centre that plans to install solar panels. A survey confirms that qualified contractors can safely install them either now or after a planned roof renewal. If they install now, they must take down and reinstall the panels for the roof work.

Under Install Now, the panels start producing in Month 1. Under Wait, they start in the first month after the roof work. The work occupies one full month, and neither option generates electricity during that month. A roof work window is reserved, but no solar contract has been signed.

For the next 12 months, the board compares net project cash from generation receipts minus any extra removal and reinstallation costs. When generating, both options have the same output and earn the same receipt per kWh. Ignore discounting; the underlying solar installation and roof repair costs are identical under both options and omitted.

## Evidence

| Fictional project inputs | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Scheduled roof work | Month 7 |
| Generation per operating month | 1,000 kWh |
| Receipt per kWh | HK$3 |
| Extra removal and reinstallation under Install Now | HK$12,000 |

**Student question:** How much solar generation and net project cash does each schedule produce in the first 12 months? Which should the board approve under its rule, and what causes the difference?

## Round two

The roof contractor is moving the renewal to Month 3. The work still lasts one month. The contractor confirms that the installation and operating forecasts, the receipt rate and the removal and reinstallation quote do not change.

**Student question:** Which should the board choose now, and what is the highest removal and reinstallation price at which Install Now would at least break even against Wait? Would Wait still be the answer if the board cared only about the first year’s generation?

## Debrief · Count operating months

With Month 7 roof work, Install Now runs for 11 months and generates 11,000 kWh. Wait runs in Months 8–12 and generates 5,000 kWh. Neither option runs in Month 7.

## Debrief · Compare incremental cash

Install Now earns HK$33,000 and costs HK$12,000 extra, leaving HK$21,000. Wait earns HK$15,000 with no extra cost. Install Now leads by HK$6,000 because its six early generating months earn HK$18,000 against HK$12,000 in extra cost.

## Debrief · The earlier roof slot changes the choice

Install Now still generates 11,000 kWh and leaves HK$21,000. Waiting now runs in Months 4–12 and earns HK$27,000 from 9,000 kWh, so Wait leads by HK$6,000. The early two months earn HK$6,000; that is the break-even moving price. Generation alone still favours Install Now by 2,000 kWh.

## Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 for scheduling and cash, 4 for the changed date, and 5 to discuss which generation and maintenance commitments would affect a longer evaluation. A budget comparison is not a carbon calculation or a real building approval.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Site, tariffs used in the example, project dates and costs are fictional. The public materials do not establish that the fictional centre qualifies or imply a fixed rate for every project.

- [EMSD, Guidance Notes for Solar Photovoltaic System Installation](https://re.emsd.gov.hk/english/files/PVGuidanceNotes.pdf). Section 7 discusses maintenance and inspection of panels, inverters and supporting structures.
- [EMSD, Solar Photovoltaic Systems O&M Handbook](https://bestpractice.emsd.gov.hk/en/solar-photovoltaic-systems/introduction/1000). Introduces PV types, maintenance access and roof watertightness considerations.
- [EMSD HK RE Net, Feed-in Tariff introduction](https://re.emsd.gov.hk/english/fit/int/fit_int.html). Describes the Hong Kong scheme and its gross generation payment design.
- [CLP, Feed-in Tariff for business](https://www.clp.com.hk/en/business/low-carbon-solutions/renewable-energy/feed-in-tariff-business). The participating utility explains purchase of clean energy from business customers.
- [HK Electric, Feed-in Tariff application and terms](https://www.hkelectric.com/documents/en/CustomerServices/SmartPowerServices/Documents/FiT%20Scheme%20Application%20Form_EN.pdf). Sections 1.1–1.3 distinguish grid connection and scheme participation and explain that utility requirements apply.
