# The best supplier score. The missing condition.

OPS-38 · Penang · 15–20 minutes · Supplier qualification · Weighted evaluation · Traceability evidence

![Two adult stationery buyers in a Penang office compare blank supplier folders and plain paper notebook samples, with an unmarked evaluation sheet and no scores or approval symbols.](../best-score-missing-condition-scene.jpg)

## Qualify first, compare second

Procurement guidance distinguishes mandatory requirements from attributes used to compare acceptable offers. Traceability systems also distinguish a supplier’s general status from evidence attached to particular products and transactions. The fictional private purchase below uses its own stated material requirement, not a finding about any real supplier or a foreign public-procurement rule.

## Opening decision

You manage purchasing for fictional Meranti Stationery in Penang. Two suppliers, A and B, quote for the same batch of paper notebooks. Both have funded, safe production capacity and meet all ordinary product specifications. Neither has been selected, paid or promised an order. Your team is reviewing an internal recommendation before its release.

The published comparison method uses three ratings from zero to one hundred, with higher always better: delivered cost 50%, delivery performance 30% and product quality 20%. The ratings follow the same pre-issued rubrics for both suppliers. They are stipulated assessment inputs, not percentages of deliveries or defect-free notebooks.

Both complete quotes are affordable and include every differing purchase charge. No currency conversion or cost-rating formula is needed: the agreed ratings are supplied. Management’s rule is to choose the highest weighted score among proposals meeting every mandatory requirement by today’s fixed verification cutoff. The full qualification checklist has not reached the meeting pack.

An analyst’s draft adds a one-point “traceability bonus” to B, which has supplied a material dossier. A has not received that bonus. The analyst says A still wins, so the missing dossier detail cannot change the recommendation. This bonus was not part of the published comparison method; no recommendation has been sent.

## Round two

The original instructions sent equally to both suppliers require verified traceability for the notebook paper before selection. For this fictional purchase, the accepted dossier must link the offered paper lot to its mill and material specification through the relevant supply records, with the independent reviewer’s confirmation. This requirement is mandatory, not an optional environmental preference.

The buyer accepts equivalent evidence meeting that defined test; no particular certification brand is required. B’s complete dossier has been checked and meets it for this batch. A’s mill-link record is still pending, so the reviewer cannot complete verification. A’s general assurance about responsible sourcing does not supply the missing link.

A’s earliest possible complete submission is next week. Today’s cutoff cannot be extended without missing the already-agreed customer delivery; no alternative qualified supplier or substitute batch is available in time. Both suppliers received the same evidence requirements, clarification opportunity and deadline. The buyer can select B today or decline this purchase and miss the delivery.

Missing verification does not prove A’s material is improper or its supplier dishonest. A may become eligible for a later purchase after review. For today’s selection, however, “pending” is not “verified”. All other specifications, capacities, ratings, prices and available funds remain unchanged; management has not authorised a waiver.

**Student question:** Which proposal is eligible now, and which should management select under its stated rule? Repair the evaluation sequence without changing weights after seeing the bids. Explain how you would record A’s status without turning incomplete evidence into an accusation.

## Debrief · The arithmetic is not the gate

A scores 0.50 × 95 + 0.30 × 80 + 0.20 × 80 = 87.5. B scores 0.50 × 80 + 0.30 × 90 + 0.20 × 90 = 85. A leads by 2.5 points under the published weighted method.

The analyst’s unauthorised bonus produces A 87.5 and B 86, leaving A ahead by 1.5 points. Correct arithmetic does not legitimise an extra criterion or show that A qualifies. Pause the recommendation until the original requirements and verification status are checked.

## Debrief · Apply the existing requirement

B is verified against the mandatory material requirement and meets all other conditions. A is not verified by today’s cutoff and cannot become eligible in time. Select B under management’s rule. Its published score remains 85; do not inflate it or pretend it became the higher-scoring proposal.

The decision has two stages: determine which proposals satisfy every mandatory condition, then rank eligible proposals using the published weights. Only B reaches the ranking stage here. The tradeoff is accepting B’s lower cost rating in return for an offer that meets the actual purchase specification, not buying an optional virtue.

## Debrief · A larger bonus would miss the point

Giving B three bonus points would happen to reverse this pair’s ranking, but would still treat mandatory evidence as exchangeable for other strengths. Another supplier with a larger cost advantage could win despite failing the requirement. A gate excludes that substitution; it is not merely a very large weight.

Do not retroactively invent a new gate to favour a preferred bidder. This requirement and its verification deadline were communicated before proposals; the internal worksheet omitted them. Correcting the worksheet restores the stated process. In real purchasing, changes to requirements or unequal clarification opportunities would need separate procedural review.

## Debrief · Record evidence precisely

Record A as “required lot-to-mill link pending; verification incomplete at cutoff”, not “fraudulent” or “unsafe”. Record what B’s reviewer checked, the batch covered and the conclusion. A future complete dossier can support a new decision; it cannot be assumed available today.

A general certificate, logo or supplier statement is not automatically the stipulated batch evidence. Equally, do not demand an unrelated brand certificate when equivalent evidence is accepted. The required records and reviewer test are fictional contract conditions; the cited traceability system illustrates the distinction, not a certification verdict on these notebooks.

## Debrief · Preserve useful competition

Mandatory gates should reflect genuine, proportionate needs, stated clearly in advance. Making every desirable attribute a gate can unnecessarily exclude workable suppliers. Conversely, reducing an essential requirement to points can admit an unacceptable offer. Separate must-have evidence from negotiable performance advantages before collecting quotations.

Keep the same scoring rubrics and documented reasons across eligible suppliers. A score is a structured comparison, not a guarantee of future performance. Retain ordinary delivery, quality and material-receipt controls after selection; today’s eligibility check does not establish permanent supplier reliability.

## Instructor note

Ask students to calculate 87.5 and 85 before showing the checklist. Then ask whether changing the bonus to three points fixes the process. Require a two-stage diagram and an evidence-specific status for A. Do not accept “choose B because it has the highest score”: it does not.

OPS-05 tests whether two distributors share a failure point; OPS-28 prices flexible capacity commitments. This case concerns the architecture of supplier selection.
