## An investment still needs its own case

On 13 December 2024, Hong Kong announced that the Technology Voucher Programme would stop accepting applications after 31 December 2024. The programme had supported technology adoption across industries. This dated change is context for reviewing investment funding, not proof that any firm lost a promised award. The business and project choices below are original fiction and assume no subsidy.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong stationery wholesaler has HK$100,000 available today for discretionary improvements. This is a fixed board-approved investment envelope for this round, not evidence that the firm is insolvent. Existing operations, required safety and privacy work, wages and committed customer services have separate protected funding. There is no additional borrowing, subsidy or budget transfer available within the decision.

Three projects are ready. Each is indivisible: fund the entire outlay or do not start it. They can operate together without overlap or resource conflicts, and their net present values are additive in the initial comparison. No work is committed yet. All figures in the exhibit are HK$000.

The supplied net present values already deduct the initial outlay and all relevant operating, training, support, tax and working-capital cash flows over the same planning horizon at a consistent risk-adjusted discount basis. They are reviewed estimates, not accounting profit or cash received today. There is no later unfunded capital requirement. The objective is the greatest total NPV within the fixed envelope; unspent cash remains available with zero incremental NPV on this basis.

## Round two

Before any project is approved, the team presents a fourth indivisible option: D, a reporting module. Its all-in initial outlay is HK$30,000 and its standalone NPV is HK$3,000. D can operate by itself or alongside any other project. Its timing, risk basis and full-life costing are consistent with the earlier options.

An independent internal review confirms compatibility between A and D and supports an additional HK$20,000 of modeled NPV only when both are implemented. This is an interaction beyond their separate NPVs, net of all integration, training and continuing support costs; it needs no extra initial cash beyond the stated outlays. The benefit is not already counted in A or D. No other pair has an interaction, and no costs, budget or baseline obligations change.

Treat the HK$20,000 as a supplied reviewed forecast for the exercise, not a guaranteed synergy or a supplier’s unsupported promise. No purchase has occurred. The owner can still choose the initial package instead, and wants to see the interaction value at which the recommendation would reverse.

**Student question:** With project D and the reviewed interaction available, which package would you choose now? Show its total outlay and NPV, and identify how small the extra interaction value could become before your recommendation changes.

## Compare feasible sets, not just ranks

A has the highest standalone NPV: HK$35,000. It also has the highest NPV per dollar of outlay: 35 / 70 = 0.50 versus 24 / 50 = 0.48 for B or C. But A leaves only HK$30,000, insufficient for either remaining initial project. B plus C uses HK$100,000 and adds HK$48,000 NPV, exceeding A alone. A plus B or C costs HK$120,000 and is infeasible.

## A complement changes the package

A plus D uses HK$70,000 + HK$30,000 = HK$100,000. Its total NPV is HK$35,000 + HK$3,000 + HK$20,000 = HK$58,000, beating B plus C at HK$48,000. B plus D or C plus D costs HK$80,000 and yields HK$27,000. Every three-project combination exceeds the budget. The best feasible package changes because of the additional option and its scoped interaction, not because A’s original NPV was wrong.

## Test the interaction rather than celebrating it

Without an interaction, A plus D yields HK$38,000, below B plus C. If the additional interaction is x, the tie is HK$38,000 + x = HK$48,000, so x = HK$10,000. Above that value choose A plus D on these assumptions; below it choose B plus C; at equality the model is indifferent. Verify integration, accountable benefit ownership and monitoring before commitment. Do not count the same saving twice.

## Instructor note

FIN-08 compares two mutually exclusive room investments with different timing. Here projects are compatible but lumpy, the budget is fixed and the objective is the best set. A simple ratio ranking does not solve this indivisible problem. We use NPV divided by outlay explicitly; some texts define profitability index as present value of inflows divided by outlay, which adds one in this simple setting. Do not confuse either with accounting ROI. The later interaction is a forecast assumption, not empirical proof or a reason to exceed protected budgets.
