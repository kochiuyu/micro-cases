# One name for two promises?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 219 -->

MKT-19 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Brand architecture · Association transfer · Endorsed services

![Two kitchenware-shop colleagues consider plain presentation boards beside drawer organisers.](../one-name-two-promises-scene.jpg)

## A familiar name enters a different experience

Ryohin Keikaku’s archived announcement, revised on 26 February 2018, describes MUJI HOTEL in Shenzhen and Beijing and how the hotel experience was intended to express the MUJI philosophy. It identifies concept/design and hotel-operation roles separately. This is the company’s stated approach, not evidence that a brand extension raises sales. The independent kitchenware business below is entirely fictional.

## Opening decision

Everyday Table is a fictional Hong Kong kitchenware shop with a familiar name and a reputation for useful everyday products. It plans an optional premium kitchen-organisation service: one consultation and a tailored written storage plan delivered within two weeks, for an all-in fee of HK$1,500. The service does not include products, fitting, electrical work, structural advice or installation. The trained team can deliver the stated offer; customer privacy and safe access procedures are already funded.

The owner must choose an identity for the launch. A uses “Everyday Table Planning”, with the existing parent name dominant. B uses “Roomwise, by Everyday Table”, with a distinct service name dominant and the parent endorsement clear and readily visible. Both state the same operating company, service scope, fee, delivery timing, contact and consumer terms. Neither claims an independent owner or an accreditation it does not possess.

Both routes cost the same HK$20,000 all-in launch budget, including their respective identity work and explanation materials. Both are affordable and legally reviewed for this fictional exercise. The normal shop continues unchanged, existing customer obligations remain funded and neither route has been published. The objective is an understandable specialist offer that can benefit from the parent’s reputation without confusing it with an ordinary shop purchase.

## Evidence

| Identity route | Main emphasis |
|---|---|
| A: Everyday Table Planning | Parent name leads |
| B: Roomwise, by Everyday Table | Distinct service name; clear parent endorsement |

**Student question:** Would you launch the service with the parent name leading, or a distinct name with clear endorsement? Explain which existing associations you want to carry over and which expectations may need to change.

## Round two

Twenty-four adult customers familiar with the parent shop voluntarily join exploratory interviews. Before seeing either identity, 18 mention reliability, 16 mention affordable everyday prices and 14 mention being able to buy something immediately. These mentions overlap: they are not separate customer groups and cannot be added into a market total.

Each then sees both full service presentations, with order alternated, including the identical HK$1,500 fee and two-week delivery. Ten say A best connects the service to trusted practical knowledge. Eight prefer B because it marks a different kind of purchase while retaining a recognisable owner. Six express no strong preference. All 24 can correctly repeat the stated fee, timing and ownership after reading; neither draft conceals those facts.

Some interviewees explain that the parent-led name initially makes them think of an affordable, immediate shop purchase, even though they understand the different terms once they read the offer. Others value that same familiar name. These are qualitative signals, not proof that either route deceives customers or causes a sale. This small volunteer sample is not a representative market survey, a controlled sales experiment or a willingness-to-pay estimate. The service, terms, resources and equal launch costs remain unchanged.

**Student question:** Which identity route would you choose after these interviews? Explain how it can borrow trust without promising the parent shop’s price or speed, and identify evidence you still need before claiming commercial success.

## Trust travels with other associations

The parent can carry reassuring associations about practical knowledge and reliability. It can also suggest a price level and purchase speed that do not match a tailored service. Those associations are not inherently bad: they help the normal shop. The question is whether they help explain this different offer. A respected name is not a substitute for describing what customers actually receive.

## Two legitimate architecture choices

A makes the relationship immediate and can use a precise service descriptor, visible fee and timing to separate the offer from shelf products. B gives the service more room to build its own associations while retaining a clear parent endorsement. B does not make ownership disappear or automatically protect the parent from service problems. Equal launch budgets do not establish equal future recognition or explanation costs.

## Use the interviews to choose a hypothesis

Ten of 24 prefer A, eight prefer B and six have no strong preference. That is not a reliable market-share forecast or evidence of a significant two-person advantage. Either route can be defended if its communication addresses the specific expectation risk. A provisional preference for B emphasises clearer separation; a preference for A emphasises familiar trust with explicit service terms. Test comprehension and qualified enquiries, then paid uptake and delivery satisfaction, without treating interview preference as demand.

## Instructor note

MKT-03 concerns borrowed character attention and attribution; MKT-08 concerns finding a familiar package. This case asks how a firm’s own parent name and service identity should relate. All launch copy is truthful and material terms visible, so the decision is not “hide facts or comply”. No study cited estimates these fictional conversion rates. Keep the opening choice open after the reveal: the research exposes competing associations, not a secretly dominant design.
