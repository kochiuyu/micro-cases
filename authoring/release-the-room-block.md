# Hold the block. Or release it early?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 537 -->

HSP-44 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Room blocks · Inventory release · Expected contribution · Service commitments

![A hotel revenue manager and convention organiser compare two blank room-allocation sheets at a meeting table.](../release-the-room-block-scene.jpg)

## A group rate can have its own inventory

A reservation form for the 2019 IB Global Conference in Hong Kong identified Hong Kong SkyCity Marriott as a conference hotel and distinguished the group-rate inventory from rates after the block sold out. The fictional hotel and organiser below use different terms, including an explicitly negotiated early release.

## Opening decision

The manager of a fictional Wan Chai hotel has 30 unbooked rooms still held for one convention night. Already confirmed rooms are excluded and remain untouched. The organiser will agree in writing either to Hold all 30 until the normal cutoff or Release 12 now. The hotel must still accommodate every further delegate request in the exhibit.

Each room released now can be sold immediately to a non-delegate for HK$520 contribution. At the cutoff, delegates take reserved rooms first. Up to four remaining hotel rooms can then sell to another market for HK$450 each. Unsold rooms and already confirmed bookings add nothing to this comparison.

An unused reservation is not a sale. A delegate hosted at the hotel adds HK$340 contribution. The organiser accepts equivalent nearby overflow rooms arranged by the hotel; each costs the hotel HK$300 net, with no convention-room contribution to the hotel. Initially six overflow places are committed at the same terms and there is no attrition fee.

## Evidence

| Other delegate requests | Probability |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | 70% |
| 24 | 30% |

**Student question:** Which agreement should the hotel choose: Hold or Release 12? For both, check that every delegate can be accommodated and calculate expected contribution.

## Round two

Before signing the early release, the overflow provider can now guarantee only four places. The organiser offers a smaller release of ten instead, on identical per-room terms. All ten will still sell now. The hotel cannot promise a room that is not secured.

**Student question:** Which agreement is now feasible and preferable: Hold, Release 12, or Release 10? Calculate the expected contribution for the strongest feasible alternative to Hold.

## Debrief · Hold protects all requests

Hold houses either 10 or 24 delegates. At least six hotel rooms remain in each state, so four late public bookings fit. Contributions are 10 × 340 + 4 × 450 = HK$5,200 and 24 × 340 + 4 × 450 = HK$9,960. Expected contribution is HK$6,628.

## Debrief · Twelve rooms sold early change exposure

Release 12 locks in HK$6,240 in early sales and retains 18 rooms. With 10 delegates it still sells four late rooms: HK$11,440 total. With 24 delegates it sells no late rooms and places six off-site: 6,240 + 18 × 340 − 6 × 300 = HK$10,560. The initial overflow guarantee holds, so choose its expected HK$11,176 over Hold.

## Debrief · The promise rules out some earnings

With only four overflow rooms, Release 12 can cover 18 + 4 = 22 delegates, two short in the high case. Release 10 retains 20 hotel rooms and can cover all 24 with the four guaranteed places. Its two contributions are HK$10,400 and HK$10,800, so expected contribution is HK$10,520. Choose Release 10 over Hold.

## Debrief · Never sell the same capacity twice

Use the early-release terms only once both parties agree and the alternative rooms are secured. Rooms booked before this decision stay protected; a potential resale after cutoff must not be counted in a state where delegates fill all remaining rooms.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 5 to reconstruct occupancy and sales, 4 to test the reduced guarantee, and 4 to discuss. Ask students why the highest expected contribution cannot excuse an unaccommodated delegate under this service promise.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All quotes, probabilities, obligations, credits and overflow arrangements in the case are fictional. The cited sources describe different group arrangements, not a common policy, legal obligation or actual room quote.

- [International Baccalaureate, 2019 Hong Kong conference reservation form](https://ibo.org/contentassets/a9a6fac90ebd43b79a9e01af4d9d1ca7/reservation-form-hong-kong-marriott-en.pdf). The conference’s own form names the contracted hotel and distinguishes availability in the group block from other rates.
- [Hyatt, Group and Event Sales Agreement terms](https://about.hyatt.com/content/dam/HGP/Group-Sales-Agreement-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf). The hotel group’s clauses reference a cutoff, unused-room resale and attrition charges. Terms differ from the classroom agreement.
- [IEEE, Hotel Room Block Management](https://events.ieee.org/planning-basics/event-planning-logistics/hotel-room-block-management/). The conference organiser’s playbook gives guidance on pickup monitoring and negotiated mitigation.
- [Northwestern Procurement, Reserving Hotel Room Blocks](https://www.northwestern.edu/procurement/about/news-announcements/archive/hotel-room-blocks.html). The venue buyer’s guidance separates a contracted block from its usage and explains why attrition can be negotiated.
- [U.S. Office for Victims of Crime, Negotiating Agreements and Contracts](https://ovc.ojp.gov/sites/ovc/files/pubs/OVC_Archives/reports/confguid/negot.htm). An archival conference planning guide discusses group-rate cutoff and sample block terms; not local legal guidance.
