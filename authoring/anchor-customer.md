# One large customer. Too much dependence?

Case 114 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Strategy · Customer dependence · Renewal

![Two colleagues consider a large catalogue and several smaller sample folders at a catalogue-layout studio desk.](../anchor-customer-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 15 October 2025, Hong Kong’s Commerce and Economic Development Bureau described support for enterprises to develop more diversified markets in a written Legislative Council reply.

That historical policy context does not establish that every business should maximise its customer count. The studio below is fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. Harbour Layout, a Hong Kong studio, prepares nontechnical product-catalogue layouts. Its next-year baseline assumes nine established, unrelated customers each buy HK$100,000 of work: HK$900,000 altogether. These are planning assumptions, not guaranteed renewals.

The owner must prioritise either one new retailer’s anchor programme or six smaller prospects with independent purchasing decisions. Both routes are affordable within the same funded staffing envelope. One account coordinator can support either full scope this cycle, but not both; no additional hire is funded. Existing commitments and quality checks remain protected.

The choice concerns next year’s account-development priority, not signing a contract today.

## Evidence

| Comparison | A · Anchor programme | B · Wider portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| Existing baseline | HK$900,000 | HK$900,000 |
| Additional planned sales | HK$600,000 from one retailer | HK$100,000 each from six prospects |
| Full-plan total | HK$1,500,000 | HK$1,500,000 |
| Largest planned customer share | 40% | About 6.7% |
| Relationship trade-off | One brief and common layout conventions; one major renewal decision | Six briefs and onboarding relationships; more independent buyers |

Totals and shares assume the common baseline and the entire growth plan occur. At this stage, the additional amounts are qualified opportunities, not orders. Common conventions could simplify coordination under A, but no cost saving or profit forecast is supplied.

**Student question:** Which route would you prioritise, A or B? Explain what benefit justifies its dependence or relationship-building effort, and name one detail about commitments or renewal that could change your view.

## Round two

The anchor’s professionally reviewed written proposal includes a first-year minimum purchase of HK$300,000 at agreed rates, provided the studio delivers the specified work to standard. The buyer cannot cancel that minimum merely because it changes its mind. The other HK$300,000 in A’s sales plan remains optional.

The proposal is nonexclusive. It requires neither customer-specific equipment nor an ownership transfer; ordinary skills and equipment remain usable for other clients.

There is no automatic renewal or price guarantee after year one. A discussion three months before expiry is only a discussion, not a further purchase commitment. The minimum is not protection against insolvency, breach or nonpayment.

The six smaller opportunities remain uncommitted, with no minimum volume. Neither route has measured conversion probabilities. Resources are unchanged; these verified fictional terms do not establish either route’s profit.

**Student question:** Does the minimum purchase change your priority? State what the anchor does and does not commit to, then name one action before the renewal discussion that preserves alternatives. If you prefer B, explain why wider relationships are worth forgoing this limited commitment.

## A share measures exposure, not a verdict

Under the full plan, A’s largest account contributes HK$600,000 ÷ HK$1,500,000 = 40%. Under B, each of 15 accounts contributes HK$100,000 ÷ HK$1,500,000, about 6.7%.

Those percentages describe dependence if the plans occur. Neither is a universal safe or unsafe threshold. A large relationship may simplify coordination; a wider portfolio provides more independent buyers but requires more relationship work.

## Separate the plan from the commitment

The reveal strengthens A’s first-year case: HK$300,000 of specified purchases is committed on the stated delivery conditions. It does not turn the entire HK$600,000 plan into an order. Delivering the minimum also costs money, so minimum sales are not minimum profit.

The 40% remains a full-plan share, not a guaranteed fraction or an observed result. Six smaller forecasts do not become six secure revenue streams merely because they are separate.

## Prepare for the next decision before expiry

If choosing A, maintain contact with established clients and monitor a modest prospect pipeline within normal funded business-development time. Review the account’s delivery economics and renewal position before the scheduled discussion.

Nonexclusive terms and reusable resources preserve room to act, not replacement demand. This is not permission to service the anchor and simultaneously onboard all six prospects with the same coordinator.

## Choose the dependence you can justify

A is defensible if the limited first-year commitment and coordination benefits justify reliance on one buyer. B is defensible if wider independent relationships matter more and the owner accepts uncertain conversion and six separate briefs.

Revenue alone cannot rank profit. Costs, customer needs, credit and realistic alternatives would matter before an actual commitment. For this exercise, choose a priority using the given evidence rather than ending with “get more information”.

## Instructor note

Ask students to distinguish three things: full-plan customer share, a bounded first-year purchase commitment and an uncommitted renewal. The reveal should improve the argument for A without making B irrational.

The lesson is strategic customer dependence, not an acquisition, hidden common supplier, channel exclusivity or a credit calculation. Do not introduce a safe-share cutoff, convert sales into profit or invent probabilities to force an expected-value answer.

The sources mix policy, practitioner guidance and a university account of research. Reported associations do not establish that concentration causes better results for this studio.
