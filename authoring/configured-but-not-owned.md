# The clinic paid for setup. What does it own?

## Paying for cloud configuration does not always buy an asset

In its 2021 cloud-configuration agenda decision, the IFRS Interpretations Committee distinguished control of a resource from payment for configuration services. If the customer does not obtain a controlled intangible, when it receives the service matters. The clinic and amounts in this exercise are fictional.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong clinic group using HKFRS Accounting Standards pays a cloud vendor HK$96,000 on 1 January. It receives access to the vendor’s scheduling application from 1 April for exactly twelve months. The supplier controls the underlying software and its updates. The group cannot take delivery of any code or control a new resource.

The vendor configures approval rules and calendars in March. It delivers one combined service: the rules are embedded in the vendor’s application, and the set-up is not separately identifiable from access to the configured application. The combined service is available evenly from 1 April. The bookkeeper proposes capitalising the whole payment as software.

Close the books on 30 June. Ignore tax, interest and any payments not shown. Compare expense and remaining assets, not which option the clinic should buy.

**Student question:** Should the clinic report software at 30 June? What expense and prepaid balance should it report instead?

## Round two

Replay from 1 January. The clinic prepays HK$72,000 to the cloud vendor on the same access dates. It separately prepays HK$24,000 to an independent contractor under a direct contract for configuration, not a subcontract of the cloud vendor.

The contractor performs and completes all of its setup during March. The clinic receives that service in March and still obtains no separate controlled code or other asset. Access starts on 1 April as before.

**Student question:** How much does the clinic expense by 30 June in this version? Compare remaining prepayments and explain why identical cash does not mean identical expense.

## Debrief · Identify the resource

The clinic does not control the vendor’s application or a separate intangible. Neither an invoice line labelled set-up nor a long benefit horizon establishes control. Cash paid before the services are received is initially a prepayment for services, not capitalised software.

## Debrief · Follow the combined service

The first arrangement delivers access to the configured application evenly for twelve months. April through June consumes 3 of 12 months. Expense 3/12 × HK$96,000 = HK$24,000 and carry HK$72,000 prepaid. There is no software intangible in this exercise.

## Debrief · Distinguish the contractor

In the replay, recognise HK$24,000 of directly contracted configuration expense in March, when supplied. Recognise 3/12 × HK$72,000 = HK$18,000 for access by 30 June. Total expense is HK$42,000; only HK$54,000 remains prepaid.

## Debrief · Reconcile the same cash

Both alternatives reconcile to HK$96,000: 24,000 + 72,000 versus 42,000 + 54,000. By June the replay has HK$18,000 more expense and HK$18,000 less prepayment, not a higher lifetime price. Both fully expense the HK$96,000 by the end of access if nothing else changes.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: identify what the clinic controls, draw a timeline for each contract, calculate the June balances, then ask which assumption changes the result. Ask for evidence about scope, separability and rights before using the rule. If separately identifiable code were controlled, assessing recognition would be a different question.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All terms, dates, parties and amounts are fictional. The agenda decision addresses the fact pattern described, not every cloud contract or the supplier’s accounting. This is a classroom case, not reporting advice.

- [IFRS Interpretations Committee, final agenda decision on cloud configuration (2021)](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/supporting-implementation/agenda-decisions/2021/configuration-or-customisation-costs-in-a-cloud-computing-arrangement-mar-21.pdf). Distinguishes combined and distinct configuration services, directly contracted third-party work, and advances.
- [IFRS Foundation, IAS 38 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-38-intangible-assets/). Explains the definition and recognition tests for intangible assets.
- [IFRS Interpretations Committee, access-only cloud arrangement (March 2019)](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2019/ifric-update-march-2019/). Shows the separate access-only question and distinguishes a service from a right to use software.
- [Issuer accounting note filed with the US SEC, cloud configuration](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792045/000114036122011161/R8.htm). An issuer discusses the configuration agenda decision and separate implications for expense, prepayment and asset recognition.
- [IASB staff, Intangible Assets research summary (February 2025)](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2025/february/iasb/ap17e-summary-other-research.pdf). Paragraph 34 contrasts access-only services and additional identifiable code; this staff paper is not a new rule.
