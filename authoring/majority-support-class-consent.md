# Most owners agreed. Which consent was required?

## Voting support is not every required consent

Share rights can concern distributions as well as votes. Governance guidance distinguishes general company decisions from approval by an affected class. This fictional exercise supplies its own strict consent rule. It is not a statement that every new issue changes class rights, or a template for issuing shares in Hong Kong.

## Opening decision

You advise the board of fictional Harbour Fold, a privately held Hong Kong exhibition-fixture company planning an expansion. Holders of seventy of its one hundred ordinary voting shares support raising new equity. This is recorded commercial support, not a completed allotment authority, constitutional amendment or consent by any separate class.

Four existing preference shareholders hold equal numbers of P shares. P has no ordinary meeting votes but has a protected priority for distributions to shareholders. Its holders have separate class-consent rights. New N shares would also have no ordinary meeting votes. The existing ordinary vote count would therefore remain unchanged under either proposal.

The supplied constitutional rule requires written agreement from all four P holders before issuing any shares that rank ahead of P for a shareholder distribution. Shares ranking strictly after P do not trigger that particular rule. Other company approvals, directors’ duties, notices, effective-date checks and filings remain necessary; this is not a complete legal checklist.

Route J revises N to rank strictly after P. The prospective investor would provide HK$1 million, enough for a smaller first expansion stage. Route C seeks the required P consent for N’s proposed priority in return for HK$1.2 million, enough for both planned stages. Both projects are operationally viable; neither financing has been accepted.

Qualified advisers and a funded preparation window are available for either route. Existing operations continue if neither closes. There is no deadline requiring anyone to waive rights. You must recommend a route for further preparation, not execute an issue. The detailed distribution wording and the P holders’ response have not yet reached the board.

## Round two

For this exercise, a year is a twelve-month period from issue or its anniversary. The priority concerns shareholder dividends only, not creditor claims or voting power.

The proposed N wording gives N the first HK$20,000 from any lawfully declared annual shareholder dividend pool, ahead of P, each year. P’s existing priority would follow. The supplied rule expressly treats this senior payment position as affecting P’s protected right. No dividend, profit or repayment is guaranteed, and creditors’ rights are unchanged.

All four P holders say they would accept a narrower amendment: N may receive up to HK$10,000 ahead of P from the first year’s lawfully declared dividend pool only. From the second year onward N must rank strictly after P. Any unpaid first-year amount lapses, with no arrears, extension or later priority claim.

The prospective investor confirms willingness to provide the same HK$1.2 million on that narrowed basis. This is a non-binding negotiating indication, not a signed subscription. The four P holders’ willingness is also not their formal written class agreement. No revised instrument, general authorisation or issue has yet been approved or completed.

Both routes remain available. J avoids changing P’s existing priority and raises less; amended C could fund the larger project but requires the specified class agreement on the exact revised terms. The board has no probability forecasts or valuation evidence to price the priority concession or determine which project creates greater net value.

**Student question:** Does the narrowed C proposal still need separate P consent? Recommend the next authorised steps and describe what must remain on hold. Compare J with amended C without treating willingness as completed consent or HK$200,000 of extra financing as free value.

## Debrief · Count the right electorate

Seventy of one hundred ordinary votes is 70% commercial support. It is not four out of four P consents. P holders can lack ordinary votes while retaining a separate right to agree or refuse a change to their payment priority. The supplied unanimity requirement belongs to this fictional constitution, not a claimed universal statutory threshold.

General corporate authority and class consent answer different questions. The board must establish both where applicable, together with remaining legal and procedural requirements. A record of ordinary support cannot be relabelled P consent, even if the majority believes the expansion benefits everyone.

## Debrief · The protected change is economic

N’s proposed HK$20,000 first position moves it ahead of P. Under the printed rule, that triggers consent even though nobody’s ordinary voting entitlement changes. The narrowed HK$10,000 first-year priority still puts N ahead temporarily, so it still triggers the same rule. Reducing the amount or duration does not remove the category of change.

J places N strictly after P and therefore does not trigger this specific priority-consent rule. That conclusion depends on the supplied terms. It does not mean all junior issues everywhere are exempt from all protections, or that a company may ignore other affected rights, contractual restrictions, approvals or statutory requirements.

## Debrief · Prepare consent, do not presume it

Amended C is a defensible route to prepare if the board values funding both stages and accepts the bounded concession. Circulate one exact revised proposal to all four P holders, allow informed consideration and obtain the required written agreement through the proper process. Record its scope and effective date; preserve each holder’s ability to refuse.

The approved wording must identify the first-year HK$10,000 cap, lapse of any unpaid amount, and strictly junior position from year two. Do not let a later document silently restore HK$20,000, permanence or accumulated arrears. Any changed affected-rights proposal must return through the appropriate approval route rather than borrow consent to different terms.

## Debrief · Separate a viable route from a completed issue

Keep any allotment, subscription commitment or funds transfer on hold until authorised advisers verify the final documents, valid company decisions, required class consent and applicable completion conditions. Classroom endorsement of C is not execution authority. If consent fails or the investor changes its terms, reconsider J or defer; do not issue on majority support alone.

J remains defensible if preserving P’s priority and pursuing the smaller viable stage better serves the company. Amended C offers HK$200,000 more financing, not HK$200,000 of measured profit. Its temporary priority could pay between zero and HK$10,000 depending on lawful dividends, but neither that payment nor a project return is forecast here.

## Instructor note

First distinguish evidence of commercial support from completed authority. Then ask students to identify the changed economic right without calculating an exit waterfall. Require separate statements of the proposed terms, whose consent is needed, what has actually been obtained and what must still happen before implementation.

ENT-15 allocates authority during founder deadlock; FIN-29 computes liquidation distributions; GVR-19 implements an already valid conditional approval. Here majority support exists, but an affected class retains a separate consent gate. All company facts and numerical terms are invented; no real security is recommended, sold, bought or issued.
