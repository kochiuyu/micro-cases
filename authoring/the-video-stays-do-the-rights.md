# The video stays up. Can the campaign keep using it?

MKT-46 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Creator marketing · Reuse rights · Incremental contribution

![A cookware founder and a creator discuss a pan and recipe photos beside a camera on a tripod.](../the-video-stays-do-the-rights-scene.jpg)

## A visible post is not permission for another campaign

TikTok’s Spark Ads documentation describes paid promotion of other creators’ organic posts with their authorisation and says the authorisation can have a defined duration. A fictional Hong Kong cookware brand compares buying a short boost with commissioning a recipe library. No actual platform terms set the fictional fees.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong brand sells an everyday pan. C pays for a creator’s video and permission for one local paid placement. The video may stay publicly visible afterwards, but the brand acquires no edit, repost or second paid-placement rights. L funds a small recipe library whose proposed written agreement would permit the specified local reuse for two more placements.

Contribution in the exhibit uses a hypothetical test against a no-campaign comparison and excludes the campaign costs shown in their own column. Orders are counted once and only if kept. Contribution is in Hong Kong dollars. For later placements use only the supplied incremental forecasts net of the additional media spend; all other effects are excluded.

Two comparable later placements are planned. Reusing L is forecast to add HK$8,000 after media cost each time. C has no reuse rights at the offered fee. Neither has an available renewal quote, and no one assigns any value to simply leaving the creator’s post visible. Compare the whole three-placement window without discounting in this exercise.

## Evidence

| Programme | First-placement contribution before programme cost | Programme cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C: creator | HK$34,000 | HK$16,000 |
| L: recipe library | HK$26,000 | HK$20,000 |

**Student question:** Which programme would you choose for the first placement alone and for the whole window? Show both comparisons and identify a permission you must check before committing.

## Round two

Before any agreement is signed, the production audit finds that the library supplier’s music is cleared for the first placement only. The base agreements and initial forecasts otherwise stand. Both future placements require licensed replacement tracks. Replacing and checking both costs HK$5,000 once in total, with no remaining expected difference in fit or performance.

C does not acquire a transferable audience. The creator has not granted any extension. L’s later contribution remains a forecast after the additional media spend, not guaranteed orders. Both teams still have the option to renegotiate before making a commitment.

**Student question:** With the extra clearance cost, which offer would you favour? Calculate the maximum extra cost at which L still ties C, and explain what should be written down for any future reuse decision.

## Debrief · Start fast or keep using the asset

First placement: C earns HK$34,000 − HK$16,000 = HK$18,000; L earns HK$26,000 − HK$20,000 = HK$6,000. The two cleared repeats would add HK$16,000, taking L to HK$22,000. C stays at HK$18,000 under the offered rights, so L initially leads by HK$4,000 across the window.

## Debrief · Check the soundtrack, not just the file

With replacement clearance, L gives HK$22,000 − HK$5,000 = HK$17,000, below C by HK$1,000. L can bear HK$4,000 of extra clearance cost and tie C under these forecasts. Choose C on these inputs unless a revised compliant L deal changes the comparison. Never run an uncleared placement.

## Debrief · Forecast reuse, not page views

Document the channel, number of placements, dates, local scope, edits, music and other included assets, and who can authorise later use. Check each upcoming placement against permissions before budgeting. Keep first-placement test evidence separate from later forecasts and rerun the comparison if fit, cost or expected lift changes.

## Instructor note

Ask why the brand cannot use the creator’s public video a second time in this exercise and why a rights-cleared library cannot claim a second benefit until it is used. Contrast a content investment with an engagement count or a commission split.
