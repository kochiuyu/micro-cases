# When the lease loses a floor

<!-- legacy-case-id: 591 -->

ACC-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Partial lease termination · Lease modification · Right-of-use remeasurement

![Two people compare floor plans at the entrance to an empty studio.](../when-the-lease-loses-a-room-scene.jpg)

## The lease changes when the rights change

The Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants discusses decreases in lease scope in its quality assurance report. The fictional tenant here first leaves a floor empty, then signs a new agreement surrendering the right to use it.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong training studio leases two equivalent floors under a single contract. Staff move upstairs on 1 July, but the tenant still controls both floors. Payments, term and rights are unchanged; the lessor has agreed nothing. Assume no separate impairment and no contractual early exit option.

The schedule shows a lease liability of HK$840,000 and right-of-use asset of HK$760,000 immediately after normal accounting through the relevant date. The two decisions occur on the same day before any further scheduled movements, so these are also the balances immediately before the agreement. Same-area floors confer equivalent rights, so one floor represents 50% of the scope.

## Evidence

| Controller’s proposed journal on vacancy | HK$ |
| --- | --- |
| Reduce right-of-use asset | 380,000 |
| Reduce lease liability | 420,000 |
| Recognise proposed gain | 40,000 |

**Student question:** Approve or reject the vacancy journal. What contractual evidence would change the decision, and what balances should stay in the ledger now?

## Round two

Both parties now sign an immediately effective amendment that gives one floor back permanently without penalty. The retained floor’s binding new payments are HK$210,000 at the end of each of the next two years. The revised annual discount rate is 5%. There are no other payments, modifications, incentives or separate services.

**Student question:** At the effective date, calculate the partial-termination gain and both final balances. Why does remeasuring the retained floor not create a second gain?

## Nothing surrendered at the first decision

Reject the vacancy journal. Without changed enforceable rights, maintain the existing HK$840,000 liability and HK$760,000 right-of-use asset at that checkpoint and continue normal accounting. Obtain the executed amendment and confirm the effective date before recognising a decrease in scope.

## Remove the surrendered half

At the amendment date, reduce the existing liability by HK$420,000 and the right-of-use asset by HK$380,000. The difference is a HK$40,000 gain. The remaining pre-remeasurement balances are HK$420,000 and HK$380,000 respectively.

## Reprice only the surviving right

Discount the two payments: HK$210,000 / 1.05 + HK$210,000 / 1.05² ≈ HK$390,476. The remaining liability falls by approximately HK$29,524, matched by a reduction in the right-of-use asset. Final asset ≈ HK$350,476; final liability ≈ HK$390,476. The HK$40,000 gain belongs only to the surrendered portion.

## Instructor note

Ask learners what number a one-step model would put in the asset if it booked the whole liability decrease against the existing asset. It would give HK$310,476 and miss the gain. If there were no reduction in scope, the same termination entry would not follow.

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All amounts and rights fictional. Lease amendment is assumed effective and enforceable when signed. Worked example omits tax.
