# HK$60,000 of support. Can we use it?

Case 48 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Entrepreneurship · Resource fit · Startup support

![Two startup founders discuss support folders beside a laptop and interface sketchbook in a shared studio.](../usable-support-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 6 May 2026, HKSTP and Google Hong Kong announced a second co-incubation cohort, with support including cloud credits, funding and expert guidance.

Those are different resources, not interchangeable cash. Advertised ceilings are not guaranteed receipts. Our two awards below are invented, not offers from these organisations or a criticism of their programmes.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. Two Hong Kong founders are building a simple booking tool for craft workshops. They can choose one of two confirmed support awards for the next 12 months, but cannot combine them.

A: HK$60,000 in credits for eligible cloud-service bills during that period.

B: HK$20,000 toward approved development expenses during that period, including cloud bills and interface-design work.

Both allow the intended activities, take no equity and have equal application effort, reporting workload and nonfinancial support. All other material conditions are equal. Assume support is available when the eligible bills fall due; this is not a reimbursement-timing problem.

The founders have enough money to cover either plan’s remaining costs, but want to preserve funds for other needs. They have not yet compared the awards against a costed work plan.

## Round two

The founders cost the same planned product and workload over the 12-month period: HK$12,000 of eligible cloud services and HK$18,000 of necessary interface-design work. These are planning estimates, not guaranteed future costs.

Both options use the same cloud services at the same prices. B can cover either listed cost, up to its combined HK$20,000 limit.

A cannot pay the designer, be sold, converted to cash or carried forward. No additional useful cloud spending is currently planned. There are no other costs in this simplified comparison.

**Student question:** Which award leaves the founders paying less for this plan? Calculate the amount under each, then explain what change in useful resource needs could reverse your choice.

## Compare the expenses actually offset

Planned costs total HK$12,000 + HK$18,000 = HK$30,000.

A: offsets HK$12,000 of cloud bills. The founders still pay HK$18,000 for design.

B: offsets HK$20,000 across the approved costs. The founders pay the remaining HK$10,000.

B preserves HK$8,000 more of their own money for this plan, despite the smaller headline. This compares expenses offset, not the venture’s total value.

## What is the unused credit worth?

A would leave HK$48,000 of its ceiling unused. That is not cash lost from the founders’ bank account; it is unused purchasing capacity for specific services within a limited period.

Buying unneeded computing just to use the credit would not make the venture better off. Start with useful work and resource requirements, not a target to consume the award.

## When could the larger award fit better?

If genuinely useful eligible cloud costs exceeded HK$20,000 within the award period, A could offset more than B’s ceiling, up to A’s HK$60,000 limit. Keep the other assumptions unchanged and recalculate the same spending plan under both options.

In real programmes, mentoring quality, technical access, networks, conditions and future switching costs may also matter. They are held equal here to isolate resource fit, not because they have no value.

Key idea: choose support for the resources the venture can use, not just the largest headline number.

## Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the award vote, 3 for mapping eligible costs and 5–7 for the comparison and reversal condition. Ask which expense each award replaces and whether unused credit can pay for another need. Keep the same product and workload in both comparisons. Do not describe real financial assistance as unrestricted cash or treat forecasts as certain. No application or legal agreement is being accepted in this exercise.
