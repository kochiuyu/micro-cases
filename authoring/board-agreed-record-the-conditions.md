# The board agreed. Did it record the conditions?

![Three company board colleagues discuss plain folders and separate decision cards around a meeting table.](../board-agreed-record-the-conditions-scene.jpg)

## Board effectiveness includes usable decisions

HKEX published corporate-governance consultation conclusions on 19 December 2024, describing measures to strengthen board effectiveness and related governance practices. That announcement concerns listed issuers, not the fictional private company below. This exercise asks how a valid conditional decision reaches the people expected to implement it; it does not claim a template satisfies every legal duty.

## Opening decision

The small board of a fictional Hong Kong service company validly approves a supplier-supported booking project within its agreed budget, subject to three separate conditions. Procurement must evidence an authorised signed contract before supplier kickoff. The data lead must approve the access and privacy checklist before any customer-data transfer. The operations lead must evidence a successful end-to-end rehearsal before customer-facing launch. Each later activity also requires the earlier applicable gates to remain satisfied.

A director supports the project but dissents on the proposed launch timetable, asking that the rehearsal not be rushed. The board does not change the timetable or waive any gate; it asks the secretary to record that concern accurately. The decision is valid, no conflict or missing quorum is alleged, and normal statutory, contractual and security requirements continue independently of these internal conditions.

The secretary can prepare either concise minutes incorporating an identified, version-controlled decision register, or one integrated structured record containing the resolution, conditions, owners, reasons and dissent. Both preserve the complete actual decision and take comparable preparation time. The separate register is convenient for tracking status; the integrated record is easier to read without opening a second document. Neither option permits minutes saying only “approved”. The board approves the record through its normal process; operational recipients receive the relevant instruction and gates, not unnecessary confidential board material.

**Student question:** Would you use concise minutes with an incorporated decision register, or one integrated structured record? Explain how conditions, owners and dissent would survive the handoff to the project team.

## Round two

For the second round, consider a handoff failure that could occur with either format: before the complete controlled instruction arrives, a coordinator emails only “board approved”. The team starts a supplier kickoff call. Procurement promptly pauses the call; no customer data, payment, public announcement or binding new instruction has been sent. Keep an accurate incident record rather than pretending the call never began.

Procurement now supplies the correctly signed contract and verifies the first gate. The data checklist is still awaiting review; the rehearsal has not been completed. The board’s original approval and timetable are unchanged, and the supplier can accommodate a paused sequence without charge or lost rights. There is time to correct the handoff. The question is which activity can resume under the existing decision, not whether to rewrite the minutes as unconditional approval or require a new board vote for evidence the owners were already authorised to verify.

**Student question:** What may the team resume now, and what must remain on hold? Specify the corrective record and evidence needed for each remaining release without changing the board’s original decision.

## The format is not the permission

Both proposed formats can preserve the decision. A separate register must be explicitly identified and incorporated, version-controlled and sent with the applicable instruction. An integrated record needs equally clear status and release ownership. The original approved decision stays fixed; later evidence and status updates are dated additions, not silent alterations to what the board decided.

## Release the right stage

The verified signed contract permits the supplier kickoff to resume under the original first gate. It does not release customer data or authorise launch. Data transfer still requires the data lead’s completed approval, and launch still needs the operations lead’s successful rehearsal evidence plus the applicable earlier conditions. There is no need to treat every activity as either wholly approved or wholly blocked.

## Repair the handoff, preserve the history

Supersede the incomplete email with a controlled operational instruction stating the three gates, current status, owners and evidence locations. Obtain the project lead’s acknowledgement, keep the paused-call incident record, and record the director’s timetable concern accurately in the board record. The project team needs its operational boundaries; it does not need unrelated confidential discussion. If the project needs changed conditions or a changed decision, return to the authorised decision-maker rather than editing the past.

## Instructor note

GVR-06 aggregates commitments against delegated spending limits; GVR-09 selects assurance scope. This case translates an already valid conditional approval into stage-specific execution. The initial choice has two complete record architectures, not lawful records versus missing conditions. The reveal is an explicit common branch so a student’s earlier preferred format is not presumed to have caused the failure. UK corporate and charity guidance supplies recordkeeping principles, not Hong Kong private-company legal advice.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All board, company, conditions, dissent and events are original fiction. Valid approval, budget and authority are stipulated; no fraud, invalid quorum or legal certification claimed. Three sequentially applicable gates: contract before kickoff; data checklist before transfer; rehearsal before launch, with earlier gates still valid. Kickoff began prematurely but paused without data, payment, public statement or binding new instruction. Contract subsequently verified permits kickoff only; two gates remain open. No changed timetable or waiver. Both record formats preserve complete decision including dissent; no terse unqualified approval option. Corrections preserve historical records, confidentiality and access controls. Three UK charity-sector references supply bounded principles, not independent empirical proof or local statutory rules.

- [HKEX: Corporate Governance Code enhancement conclusions, 19 December 2024](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2024/2412192news?sc_lang=en). Actual announcement describes governance and board-effectiveness measures for issuers. It does not establish a specific new minutes rule or apply listing duties to this fictional private company.
- [UK FRC: Corporate Governance Code Guidance](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/corporate-governance/corporate-governance-code-guidance/). Actual guidance discusses recording issues and reasons behind significant board decisions. UK governance context, not a Hong Kong legal checklist.
- [Charity Commission for England and Wales: Charity meetings](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-and-meetings-cc48/charities-and-meetings). Actual minutes guidance covers resolutions, reasons, supporting information and actions without requiring a verbatim transcript. Charity-sector scope remains explicit.
- [Fundraising Regulator: Documenting your fundraising decisions](https://www.fundraisingregulator.org.uk/about-fundraising/resources/documenting-your-fundraising-decisions). Actual guidance covers alternative opinions, delegated authority and intelligible records. Fundraising context; no claim our company is regulated by this body.
- [Scottish Charity Regulator: Governing documents and meetings](https://www.oscr.org.uk/managing-a-charity/managing-charity-trustees/guidance-and-good-practice-for-charity-trustees/governing-documents-and-meetings/). Actual guidance discusses accurate decisions, reasons and clear action owners. Scottish charity duties are not imported into the case.
