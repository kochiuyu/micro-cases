# Enough pledges. Enough capacity to fulfil?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 259 -->

ENT-22 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Reward crowdfunding · Capacity commitments · Campaign design

![A fictional founder and production adviser examine a fold-flat reading stand and plain packing materials in a small studio.](../enough-pledges-enough-capacity-scene.jpg)

## Research connection

Ethan Mollick’s 2015 study of Kickstarter backers distinguished funded projects from delivered rewards. It was historical survey evidence, not a prediction for today’s campaigns. Platform and fulfilment guidance similarly ask creators to plan reward quantities, costs and delivery. Our campaign, quotations and scenarios are entirely invented.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong founder has developed an original fold-flat reading stand. A working sample is ready. She is designing a reward-crowdfunding campaign, not offering shares or financial returns. Each backer can pledge exactly HK$400 for one stand with local delivery included. There are no donations, add-ons or extra reward tiers in this simplified exercise.

The fictional platform collects nothing if the campaign closes below HK$100,000. If it reaches that goal, accepted pledges are collected and each requires one reward. Assume all accepted payments succeed. Fees total 10%; collected net funds arrive before any production payment. No borrowing or additional founder cash is available. These are classroom rules, not a claim about any real platform’s fees or settlement schedule.

Design A limits the only reward tier to 300. Design B leaves its quantity uncapped to avoid turning interested backers away. Both have the same price, goal, product and draft eight-week delivery estimate. Both preserve truthful risk disclosures, the stated refund arrangements and all applicable safeguards. Nothing is public yet. This is a choice of campaign design for review, not permission to launch without a verified fulfilment plan.

The workshop has confirmed the cost and delivery plan for 250–300 stands. The founder expects more interest may be possible but has no verified demand forecast. She has requested a larger-volume quotation. A cap contains the first commitment but forgoes additional pledges; an uncapped design could reach more backers if larger-volume delivery is feasible. Finite production cannot be presumed unlimited.

## Round two

Before launch, the supplier completes its capacity check. More than 300 rewards requires switching the entire order to a different production process, not simply adding another small workshop batch. The existing eight-week slot cannot be split or repeated for this campaign. For 301–600 stands, that process adds a one-time HK$24,000 setup payment and HK$12,000 of paid backer-support capacity. Estimated delivery becomes sixteen weeks for the whole batch.

| Final rewards | Fulfilment arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| 250–300 | HK$240 each + HK$20,000 fixed; eight weeks |
| 301–600 | HK$240 each + HK$20,000 fixed + HK$24,000 setup + HK$12,000 extra support; sixteen weeks |
| Above 600 | No verified quote or delivery capacity |

HK$240 per reward includes manufacturing, packing, local delivery and ordinary fulfilment labour. HK$20,000 covers the common project setup and founder work. The extra HK$12,000 is incremental support, not a second count of ordinary labour. All planned costs and fees are shown, including applicable taxes; no additional quoted costs are omitted. No safety or quality check is waived. Estimates are not delivery guarantees.

Any positive balance must still cover unforeseen problems and promised remedies; it is not distributable profit. For A, the founder’s reviewed risk plan requires at least HK$10,000 left after planned costs, and the supplier confirms feasibility throughout 250–300. At the 250-reward goal, that balance is exactly HK$10,000. A’s refund arrangements and contingency plan have been reviewed for this exercise; do not infer that a generic reserve guarantees success.

Compare 400 and 600 accepted rewards as stress-test scenarios, not forecasts. No new money or faster supplier is confirmed. A can proceed with the reviewed 300 cap. Alternatively, the founder can postpone and redesign the larger campaign’s funding threshold, quantity limit, support and truthful delivery estimate before requesting any pledges. Leaving B uncapped with the current terms is not a defensible launch option. Demand during a delay is unknown.

**Student question:** Launch the 300-reward cap, or postpone and redesign for larger volumes? Use the 400- and 600-reward budgets and delivery estimates to explain why reaching the funding goal is not enough.

## Convert pledges into obligations

A goal of HK$100,000 means 250 rewards at HK$400. At 10% fees, net funds are HK$90,000. Planned cost is 250 × HK$240 + HK$20,000 = HK$80,000, leaving HK$10,000. Below the goal there is no collection and no funded production. Crossing it does not remove the obligation to deliver each accepted reward.

## Check the jump, not only the average

| Rewards | Net funds | Planned costs | Unallocated balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 300 | HK$108,000 | HK$92,000 | HK$16,000 |
| 400 | HK$144,000 | HK$152,000 | −HK$8,000 |
| 600 | HK$216,000 | HK$200,000 | HK$16,000 |

At 400 rewards, gross pledges are HK$160,000, or 160% of the goal. Net receipts are HK$144,000. Costs are HK$96,000 + HK$20,000 + HK$24,000 + HK$12,000 = HK$152,000: an HK$8,000 shortfall before contingency. At 600 rewards, gross pledges are HK$240,000 and net receipts HK$216,000; costs HK$144,000 + HK$20,000 + HK$36,000 = HK$200,000. The remaining HK$16,000 does not repair the unfunded middle range.

## A funded high-volume example is not an uncapped plan

The step cost makes some outcomes above the original goal less fundable than the capped range. At the larger process, 120 × quantity − 56,000 is the balance after planned costs; it becomes nonnegative only at 467 whole rewards. That is not a recommended goal: it leaves almost no contingency, does not validate demand, and does not solve the sixteen-week estimate or unquoted volumes above 600.

## Choose a promise the venture can support

Launching A preserves a verified first-batch plan while declining pledges beyond 300. The founder may collect non-binding interest for later availability, with consent and no implied delivery promise. Postponing can be defended if serving a larger audience is central, accepting delay and uncertain future interest. Before relaunch review, replace the old budget and timeline, set a supported quantity bound, fund support and remedies, and communicate the full terms before anyone pledges. Simply changing eight weeks to sixteen does not fill the 400-reward cash shortfall.

## Do not confuse delay with deception

Creative projects can encounter unexpected problems. The learning point is to avoid making an unsupported promise when the constraint is already known. Keep backers informed and honour the campaign’s stated remedies and applicable obligations; a disclaimer is not a substitute for a delivery plan. The cited US enforcement case concerns alleged deceptive representations and a settlement, not a rule that every late project is fraudulent.
