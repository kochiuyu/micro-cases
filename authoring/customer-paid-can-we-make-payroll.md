## Hong Kong and Thailand link retail QR payments

In December 2023 the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Bank of Thailand launched the FPS x PromptPay link. Eligible travellers can scan a supported merchant’s payment QR code; the joint announcement says merchants can receive their funds immediately through the link. Card authorisation instead confirms approval of a transaction, before the merchant’s payout. The retail contracts in this exercise are fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional Tsim Sha Tsui gift shop Lantern Lane must fund HK$40,000 of weekend payroll from its bank account by 5 p.m. Saturday. It starts Saturday with HK$20,000 available. It expects HK$60,000 in completed sales, of which HK$25,000 can shift from cards to an eligible instant bank-QR channel without changing prices or demand.

Choose cards only or add bank-QR as a payment route for the eligible amount; all other sales stay on cards. Under the shop’s fictional contracts, card receipts carry a 2% fee withheld from the merchant payout on Tuesday. Bank-QR receipts carry a 1% fee withheld as funds reach the bank account. A verified QR receipt credited before 5 p.m. can fund payroll.

Adding bank-QR costs a one-off HK$900 setup fee that is due Tuesday. The shop can draw a prearranged bridge of up to HK$20,000 before payroll. Any positive draw costs HK$800, billed Tuesday, with no other charge. It repays the bridge on Tuesday from settled sales.

Compare incremental fees for this one weekend; ignore interest and other cash movements. Payroll cannot slip. Assume no refunds, chargebacks or final losses in this exercise. Count money only when it reaches the shop’s spendable bank balance.

## Evidence

| Payment route | Fee on gross | Usable at bank | Other fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Card | 2% | Tuesday | None |
| Bank-QR | 1% | At confirmed credit | HK$900 Tuesday |
| Bridge draw | Not a sale | Before 5 p.m. | HK$800 if drawn |

**Student question:** Which route should the shop choose for this weekend if all HK$25,000 of eligible QR sales credit before payroll, and what is its fee advantage?

## Round two

Before committing, you learn that HK$6,000 of the HK$25,000 QR-eligible sales would still be pending confirmation at 5 p.m. under the shop’s provider arrangement. The HK$6,000 credits later that evening at the same 1% fee. All the other assumptions and choices stay the same.

This is a merchant availability scenario, not a description of the official link’s settlement promise. The provider eventually delivers the full net payment; the question is whether the shop can use it by its deadline.

**Student question:** Under the revised credit timing, how much would the shop have to draw with bank-QR, and which route now costs less?

## Debrief · Cards must be bridged

The card terminal can approve HK$60,000 of sales without adding to Saturday’s bank cash. The shop is HK$20,000 short at payroll. Cards plus the bridge cost 2% × HK$60,000 + HK$800 = HK$2,000.

## Debrief · Net the payment fee before payroll

Adding bank-QR gives HK$25,000 × 99% = HK$24,750 of Saturday bank cash. Together with HK$20,000 already available, it covers payroll with HK$4,750 to spare. No bridge fee is incurred.

## Debrief · The fee advantage is HK$150

The bank-QR mix costs HK$700 on HK$35,000 of cards, HK$250 on HK$25,000 of QR sales and HK$900 setup: HK$1,850. It saves HK$150 versus cards alone with the bridge, despite the setup charge.

## Debrief · Now the QR mix needs a draw

By payroll, only HK$19,000 of eligible sales have credited on the QR route. Net proceeds are HK$18,810; available funds are HK$38,810. The draw is HK$1,190 and still triggers the entire HK$800 charge.

## Debrief · Separate fee cost from cash timing

The mixed route now costs HK$2,650 and cards with the bridge still cost HK$2,000. Pick cards for this weekend under the revised facts, a fee saving of HK$650. The later credit eliminates neither the payroll gap at 5 p.m. nor the draw fee.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to frame the decision, 4 for the first calculation and a vote, 4 for the second round, and 5 to defend or revise a recommendation. Ask each group to cite a number and name the assumption that would change its call.

Start by asking groups which receipt they could spend at exactly 5 p.m., then compare fees only among feasible routes. In practice check merchant eligibility, confirmation and payout reporting, reversals, financing terms and holiday cutoffs. The official rail’s speed and the merchant’s contract or actual bank availability are distinct. All case fees and the delayed-credit scenario are invented.
