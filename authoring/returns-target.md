# Target reached. Returns still coming?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 30 -->

ACC-03 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Revenue estimates · Performance measurement

![An accountant and sales manager review campaign records beside a returned parcel in a Hong Kong homewares shop.](../returns-target-scene.jpg)

## In the news

Chow Sang Sang’s 2024 annual report, published on 23 April 2025, explained how product-level return history informs estimates of refunds and why changed circumstances can require an update. It reported a refund liability of HK$3.670 million at 31 December 2024. That is a point-in-time estimate, not the year’s total returns or a loss figure.

This is ordinary disclosed accounting, not evidence of manipulation. The fictional homewares campaign below asks a separate management question: what does “target reached” mean while customers can still return goods? None of its bonus rules or results belongs to Chow Sang Sang.

## Opening decision

You review a fictional Hong Kong online homewares shop’s campaign. It has sold and delivered 1,000 organizers at HK$200 each. The dashboard celebrates HK$200,000 of gross sales. Customers have a clearly disclosed return window, which is still open.

The existing team-bonus rule is sales after actual and expected refunds of at least HK$185,000. Finance must independently review returns evidence before certification; gross sales alone do not qualify. This rule was agreed before the campaign and will not be changed now. The team’s initial forecast uses a 5% total return rate from earlier campaigns. No refunds have yet been processed at this first review.

## Evidence

Fictional initial campaign estimate:

| Measure | Basis | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross delivered sales | 1,000 × HK$200 | HK$200,000 |
| Initial expected total refunds | 5% × HK$200,000 | HK$10,000 |
| Initial sales after expected refunds | HK$200,000 − HK$10,000 | HK$190,000 |
| Existing bonus threshold | After actual and expected refunds | HK$185,000 |

Full refunds are HK$200 per eligible returned item. Net sales are not profit or available cash; cost and stock-recovery details are not supplied.

**Student question:** Would you describe the target as achieved on this evidence? What should finance check before certifying the bonus, and how would you explain the provisional result to the team?

## Round two

At the certification review, refunds for 40 items have now been processed: HK$8,000. Finance also documents comparable completed return-window cohorts from the same product and promotion. Its review supports an estimate of 80 additional returns among this campaign’s remaining items—not 80 returns in total.

The initial 5% rate no longer reflects the best available evidence for this campaign. The 80 additional returns remain an estimate, not completed refunds. The return policy, prices, delivered sales and HK$185,000 bonus threshold are unchanged. Finance must record the evidence and revisit the estimate as the window closes.

**Student question:** What sales figure should the dashboard now show after refunds, and is bonus certification supported? Explain what is known, what is estimated, and what must be checked later.

## Debrief · Replace the estimate, do not double count

Actual refunds are 40 × HK$200 = HK$8,000. Additional expected refunds are 80 × HK$200 = HK$16,000. Total actual plus expected refunds are HK$24,000, so revised sales after refunds are HK$176,000. Do not subtract the original HK$10,000 estimate again: the new estimate replaces it.

HK$176,000 is HK$9,000 below the unchanged threshold. On the supplied reviewed evidence, certification is not currently supported. The prior HK$190,000 was an estimate using earlier information, not a permanently earned net-sales result. A changed estimate does not by itself establish that someone acted dishonestly.

## Keep evidence separate from the bonus

The dashboard should distinguish gross delivered sales, refunds already processed, further expected refunds and the resulting provisional net-sales figure. Keep a dated record of the evidence, reviewer and next update. Check whether the comparison cohorts match the product, promotion, channel and return policy; an old average is not automatically the right rate.

Neither optimism to protect the bonus nor pessimism to avoid paying it is an acceptable estimation method. Use the best available evidence consistently. Explain the existing certification rule to the team and reconcile the estimate to actual outcomes when the return window closes. Do not restrict valid customer returns or rewrite the agreed bonus threshold to obtain a preferred result.

## Net sales are not profit

A refund reduces sales, but it is not automatically an equal reduction in profit: returned products may retain recoverable value. Formal returns accounting also considers a separate recovery asset and associated costs. This short case isolates the sales measure; it does not provide a complete set of journal entries or determine the campaign’s profitability.

For future campaigns, define which dashboard measure is provisional, who reviews it and when a performance claim becomes final. A gross-sales celebration can coexist with a clear warning that refund-adjusted performance is not yet certified. Recognize staff effort without pretending that the measured financial result is settled.

## Back to the evidence

The real annual report illustrates that return estimates use product history and can change with circumstances. Hong Kong accounting guidance and the equivalent IFRS text distinguish revenue expected to be retained, refund obligations and recovery of returned products. The New Zealand standards source is a conceptual cross-check, not Hong Kong legislation.

ACCA’s performance-measure guidance emphasizes clear definitions and reliable review rather than changing targets after the fact. US retail research shows that returns matter operationally, but its industry averages are not evidence for this fictional Hong Kong shop’s return rate. Merchandise returned is also not the same as net economic loss.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–3: identify which of HK$200,000, HK$190,000 and HK$185,000 is a gross observation, an estimate or a threshold. Minutes 3–7: reveal actual and additional expected returns; require a calculation without double counting. Minutes 7–12: draft a brief dashboard explanation for the team, separating the provisional estimate from the certification decision.

Challenge “the cash arrived, so the target is reached” with the agreed measure. Challenge “120 returns have happened” by distinguishing actual 40 from estimated 80. Challenge “refunds are all lost profit” with recoverable stock and missing cost information. This is a management-control illustration, not professional reporting or employment-law advice.
