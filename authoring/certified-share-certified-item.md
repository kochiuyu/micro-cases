# A certified share. A certified item?

## Accounting for an attribute is not tracing every piece

Chain-of-custody systems connect sourcing records to permitted claims. Some preserve physical separation; others allocate supported quantities through controlled accounts. Both can be valid, but they do not necessarily establish the same fact about an individual item. The quotations, supplier records and customer requirement below are fictional.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong furniture buyer needs one hundred finished solid-wood desktop panels for a client. They will be installed unchanged on metal frames; no other wood is added. Both quotations meet the same quality, safety, legal sourcing and delivery requirements. The all-in panel budget is HK$120,000.

A costs HK$120,000 and supplies panels with an FSC 100% claim through a physically segregated transfer route. B costs HK$100,000 and supplies panels with an FSC Mix Credit claim from a certified manufacturer’s credit system. Supplier certificate scope and transaction claims are verified for this exercise. Neither is a counterfeit or uncertified offer.

The buyer values credible forest sourcing and lower cost, but has not yet checked the client’s exact material statement. No order or public statement has been made. Choose a provisional route and describe what supporting evidence you would need before saying every panel’s wood physically comes from certified forests.

Use the following supplied rules. FSC 100% in A covers all wood in the panel. In B, eligible certified inputs create output credits after applying the conversion factor. Selling output with FSC Mix Credit consumes those credits. Credit allocation does not identify the physical certified-forest content of each individual panel.

## Evidence

**Student question:** Which route would you provisionally recommend? Distinguish a valid transaction claim from a physical-content statement about every panel, and identify the client requirement you must check.

## Round two

B’s manufacturer processes one product group at one site this month. Inputs are ten cubic metres carrying FSC 100% and ten cubic metres carrying FSC Controlled Wood. They are the same wood species and quality. No recycled input, other wood component, cross-site transfer or opening credit is involved.

The verified conversion factor is 0.5 cubic metre of finished panels per cubic metre of input, after all processing losses. Twenty cubic metres therefore produce ten cubic metres of panels. Each identical panel contains 0.05 cubic metre of wood, giving two hundred finished panels. This batch is not physically segregated by certified input origin.

For this account, only the ten cubic metres of FSC 100% input contribute credit. The Controlled Wood meets the relevant sourcing controls but does not contribute certified-forest credit. Multiply contributing input by 0.5 to obtain credits in finished-output cubic metres. B proposes allocating all available credit to the buyer’s one hundred panels.

The client’s approved brief is now supplied: all wood in each desktop panel must physically originate from FSC-certified forests. The buyer may not change this requirement unilaterally. A’s verified segregated route meets it; B’s credit claim alone does not establish it. All other certification conditions are stipulated satisfied, and both quotations remain available.

**Student question:** Reconcile B’s input, output and credit quantities. Can its hundred panels validly carry FSC Mix Credit, and does that satisfy this client’s physical-origin requirement? Recommend the purchasing route and an accurate explanation.

## Debrief · Reconcile the credit account

B produces (10 + 10) × 0.5 = 10 cubic metres of panels. At 0.05 cubic metre each, that is two hundred panels. Claim-contributing input creates 10 × 0.5 = 5 cubic metres of output credit, equivalent to one hundred panels. Losses have already been included; do not ignore or deduct them twice.

The buyer’s one hundred panels use 100 × 0.05 = 5 cubic metres of credit. Opening zero plus five created minus five allocated leaves zero. B’s proposed FSC Mix Credit quantity fits the supplied account. The remaining hundred panels cannot use those same credits again or be relabelled FSC 100%.

## Debrief · Valid credit is not a physical map

The credit account controls the quantity carrying the claim; it does not locate certified-origin wood in particular panels. Neither five cubic metres of credit nor a fifty-per-cent input share proves that every panel contains a particular physical percentage. Do not convert FSC Mix Credit into an invented FSC 50% product claim.

Controlled Wood is not simply unknown timber: the exercise stipulates the relevant sourcing controls. It nevertheless differs from input carrying FSC 100%. B’s valid accounting route is not fraudulent merely because it cannot substantiate a stronger physical-content promise. The error would be making that stronger promise without evidence.

## Debrief · Fit the claim to the requirement

Choose A under the existing brief. It costs HK$20,000 more, or HK$200 per panel, and fits the verified physical-origin requirement within budget. This is a requirement-to-evidence decision, not proof that A has lower emissions, better biodiversity outcomes or better overall environmental performance.

For B, an accurate classroom explanation is that the panels are supplied with FSC Mix Credit supported by the manufacturer’s credit account, not a guarantee of each panel’s physical certified-forest content. B could be considered if the client knowingly revised its brief before commitment. No such revision is authorised here.

## Debrief · Keep the records and claims aligned

Retain the supplier’s valid scope, invoice claim, product identity, quantities and the relevant chain-of-custody evidence. In a real transaction, verify applicable current requirements and the authorised claim wording. A certificate logo alone does not replace these checks, and this classroom calculation is not a complete certification audit.

Only FSC rules govern this worked example. Other schemes may use different product groups, attribution methods and allowed statements; do not borrow their terminology to enlarge this claim. Any real customer-facing trademark use needs the applicable approval. No logo, certification endorsement or actual purchase is created by this exercise.

## Instructor note

SUS-11 concerns an end-of-life destination, SUS-24 an emissions reporting boundary, and GVR-09 review scope. Here students must conserve claim quantities through a conversion factor while distinguishing allocation from physical origin. Accept either opening preference with accurate limits; after the brief, require A or an explicit request to revise the requirement before commitment.
