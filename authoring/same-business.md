# A premium offer. The same business?

Case 102 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Strategy · Positioning · Activity fit

![A woman in an indigo apron and a man in a sage shirt discuss closed lunchboxes and a sample place setting at a calm lunch counter.](../same-business-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 8 May 2024, HK Express announced a change from two fare categories to four, with different included services and optional extras.

This historical announcement does not suggest its offers were incompatible or unsuccessful. The lunch business below is unrelated and fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong weekday lunch business serves nearby workers who want a dependable, moderately priced meal without a long stop. It offers three clearly described set lunches, ordered ahead or at a short counter and collected by the customer.

The team prepares the limited menu in batches, follows a common packing sequence and quotes a clear collection window. The owner is choosing next quarter’s development focus: improve this offer or build a higher-priced hosted lunch service for small business groups.

Both development budgets, including training and reorganisation, are affordable. Neither is committed. No reliable comparable demand, margin or profit forecasts are available. Existing customer commitments will be honoured; the choice concerns new future promises.

## Evidence

| Development focus | Customer promise to build |
|---|---|
| A · Strengthen collection | Clearer advance ordering and more dependable pickup for workers wanting a straightforward lunch. |
| B · Build hosted lunches | Consultation and more personalised attention for small business groups willing to consider a higher price. |

There is one site and one operating team. Either route can be developed with the stated resources, but the team cannot promise unchanged quick pickup and unrestricted hosted custom service during the same lunch period. A separate team or site is outside next quarter’s authorised resources.

Required food-safety checks and accurate dietary and allergen information remain under either offer. A limited commercial menu does not remove those obligations.

**Student question:** Which development focus would you choose for next quarter, A or B? State whose needs you want to serve and one fact that would change your choice.

## Round two

To understand the intended hosted-service segment, the owner obtains one prospective corporate organiser’s brief: individual menu changes discussed with a host, table delivery, flexible arrival times during the lunch peak and choices that can be revised after arrival. This is an expression of interest, not a signed order or established market demand.

The team confirms that it can build a coherent hosted service within B’s development budget, including training and reorganisation. It would need to stop advertising the unchanged quick-collection promise during that period, after meeting existing commitments.

The change reaches beyond the menu: batch preparation needs more individual confirmation; predictable collection gives way to varying arrivals and revisions; common packing and customer collection give way to host attention and table delivery. No measured delay or lost-sales estimate is supplied.

The organiser has not accepted narrower terms. There is no funded second team. Both future strategies remain feasible, but an equally resourced “do everything” offer has not been established.

**Student question:** Would you retain the focused collection offer or reposition toward the hosted service? Name two activities you would protect or change, one promise you would not make, and the commercial evidence needed before committing the budget.

## Start with a customer’s reason to choose you

A worker collecting lunch between appointments may value the three-set menu because it reduces decisions and gives a clear handoff. The organiser describes a different need: attention to individual choices and a hosted occasion.

“Premium” does not place both offers on one better-or-worse scale. B can be attractive without being the right answer for the worker who came for simple collection. A need not add hosting to improve ordering and pickup.

## Show how the everyday work supports the promise

For A, a small set menu supports planned batch preparation; advance orders support a collection window; common packing supports a simple handoff. Students should explain those connections, not just list features.

For B, a host needs a way to confirm individual choices, communicate revisions to preparation staff and coordinate table delivery as groups arrive. Training, staffing assignments and customer information must support that routine.

One isolated improvement does not settle which service the business intends to provide.

## Choose the offer, including what it leaves out

A can protect dependable collection and decline to advertise flexible hosted customisation during the same lunch period. B can deliberately reposition, stop promising the unchanged pickup experience to new customers and communicate the new offer clearly.

The reveal makes B a genuine operational alternative, not a trap. It does not establish demand or profit. A also has an opportunity cost: it may leave customers seeking hosted attention to other businesses.

A fixed-menu group order could fit A, but it is not the organiser’s described service. It needs separate acceptance and costing. A new site or team could change the choice later; neither is free or authorised here.

## Test commercial appeal before spending

Having chosen a direction provisionally, check credible demand, willingness to pay and the full costs of delivering the proposed promise. For B, include consultation, individual preparation, hosting and training, rather than treating a higher selling price as a margin forecast.

Also assess how changing the offer affects future demand from current pickup customers. No lost-customer count or financial ranking is provided. Honour existing commitments before any transition and retain food-safety and accurate dietary information throughout.

## Instructor note

Ask each group to draw a short original map: target need → preparation → ordering → handoff or service. Explain how at least three activities support the chosen need. Then ask which advertised promise it would decline to make under the one-team constraint. “More features” and “collect more information” alone are not positioning choices.

This is not a queue calculation or an exercise in accelerating meal stages. Unlike a one-off custom contract, B would become a recurring advertised service. Both routes have feasible, affordable development budgets; business returns remain unknown.

Do not reward an uncosted hybrid as the automatic answer. Students may propose a third offer, but must identify the new customer acceptance, resources and costing it requires. The resource constraint is specific to this fictional quarter, not a universal rule against offering different services.
