# Covered damage. Unchanged behaviour?

Case 119 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Economics · Prevention incentives · Risk allocation

![Two event colleagues discuss plain quotation sheets beside a portable display unit in an intact transport case and a removable padded sleeve.](../covered-damage-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 15 January 2026, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council called for clearer rental charges and deposit arrangements in power-bank rental services.

Its report did not test prevention incentives. The non-electrical display rental below is fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small Hong Kong event organiser rents a portable display unit for a two-day community exhibition. Base rent, quality, delivery, setup, competent operation and required safe handling are identical and already funded under both quotes.

The comparison isolates one accidental cosmetic-restoration event affecting the outer transport case. It costs exactly HK$400 and can occur at most once. It affects neither functionality, safety, the exhibition, injury nor other property. Normal wear, pre-existing damage and supplier fault are not charged to the renter.

Both packages are clearly agreed before hire, with condition records and an accessible dispute process. No statutory right is waived. All amounts are affordable from discretionary funds; other obligations remain protected.

Under required normal handling, the event has a 20% probability in this classroom model. No optional prevention measure has yet been identified. This is an invented assumption, not an observed rental rate.

## Evidence

| Beyond the same base rent | A · Supplier bears specified damage | B · Renter bears specified damage |
|---|---|---|
| Additional fee | HK$70 | HK$20 |
| Who pays the specified HK$400 bill? | Supplier | Renter |
| Refundable security for this risk | None | HK$400 |
| Initial expected additional expense | HK$70 | HK$20 + 20% × HK$400 = HK$100 |

B’s security is promptly returned in full when no qualifying damage occurs, or used only for the documented HK$400 bill. It is not a second charge or an expense merely because it is lodged. There is no financing charge here, but it ties up cash.

A initially has both lower expected expense and certainty for this isolated risk. B might become attractive if credible affordable prevention emerges; do not assume it already exists.

**Student question:** Which package would you choose provisionally, and which evidence about prevention or exposure could change your choice?

## Round two

Before choosing, an approved optional padded outer sleeve and extra handling arrangement becomes available for HK$20 total, including materials, fitting time and all differing costs. It goes over the already safe case during transport; it is not an equipment alteration or substitute for required care.

For this exercise, it reduces the probability of the same HK$400 event from 20% to 5%. Both packages allow it; neither requires it.

The supplier cannot verify its use throughout the hire, and neither quoted package fee changes if it is bought. No future price, reputation, claim-history penalty or non-money benefit is modelled. Actual effectiveness would require evidence; these probabilities remain fictional.

**Student question:** Choose A or B after considering whether to buy the optional measure. Compare expected expense and retained exposure. Why can the same protective action have a different private financial incentive under the two packages? What evidence would change your choice?

## Compare the package and prevention together

A without the optional measure costs HK$70; A with it costs HK$90. The renter pays none of this specified restoration bill either way.

B without it has expected expense HK$20 + 20% × HK$400 = HK$100. B with it has expected expense HK$20 + HK$20 + 5% × HK$400 = HK$60.

For a renter comparing only expected private expense, B with prevention beats A without it by HK$10. This is a benchmark, not a universal choice.

## Who receives the financial benefit of prevention?

Under B, prevention reduces the renter’s expected damage payment from HK$80 to HK$20: HK$60 avoided for a HK$20 cost, a net expected saving of HK$40 compared with B without it.

Under A, the renter’s avoided bill is zero. In this narrow money-only comparison, paying HK$20 brings no private financial saving, although it reduces the modelled restoration risk.

This is the incentive issue called moral hazard: allocating loss can change incentives for costly prevention. It is not proof that protected customers are careless, dishonest or entitled to ignore duties.

## An expected HK$60 is not a certain HK$60

With B and prevention, the renter’s actual additional expense is HK$40 with 95% probability, or HK$440 with 5% probability. The remaining possible restoration bill is HK$400.

A’s HK$70 remains defensible for certainty and avoiding the deposit’s cash tie-up, or if the prevention assumption is not reliable. No risk preference is supplied. Ethics, inconvenience, reputation and future prices may also motivate prevention in reality, though this calculation excludes them.

## Use the mechanism without inventing a new quote

A supplier-funded sleeve or a credible verified-use discount could align financial incentives, but neither is offered here. Do not add a free third package or conclude that all damage costs should always fall on customers.

Required safety, care and rights remain unchanged. The case concerns incentives after responsibility is allocated, not a surprise coverage exclusion or verification of an item’s pre-existing quality.

## Instructor note

Start with who pays the same HK$400 event, then ask who benefits financially from reducing its probability. Do not add the HK$400 deposit to the restoration charge: the security settles that bill, rather than creating another one.

Optional sensitivity: B with prevention ties A when the post-measure probability is (HK$70 − HK$20 − HK$20) ÷ HK$400 = 7.5%, before valuing risk or tied-up cash. Below that, B is cheaper in expectation, not necessarily preferred.

Optional resource distinction: expected physical restoration costs fall from HK$80 to HK$20; adding prevention gives HK$40. Package charges and refundable security are transfers, not additional physical damage. Provider costs, profit and market-equilibrium prices are not supplied.
