# The pilot grant ends. Who maintains the benefit?

## A facility also needs a handover

MTR’s 30 March 2025 project update reported that the re-provisioned GREEN@TUEN MUN facility had been handed to the Environmental Protection Department and opened on 19 March. This illustrates a named handover, not evidence about our maintenance costs or a grant ending. The courtyard project below is original fiction.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong community association has a one-off HK$120,000 grant for a new courtyard growing project. The fictional grant expressly permits installation, three years of maintenance and a ring-fenced reserve. It is not a CIIF or real government scheme. The agreed purpose is accessible, free resident participation for three years, not simply a large opening-day photograph. The association owns the equipment and has written permission to use the site for that period. Existing public services and access are not displaced.

Two professionally designed options meet the same safety and accessibility standards. L installs twelve raised planters for HK$100,000; S installs eight for HK$60,000. Each figure includes safe installation, initial materials and access adjustments. Shared programme staffing, insurance, water, safeguarding and end-of-period safe removal are already separately funded under either option. The association’s named project manager remains accountable. The choice concerns additional routine care, replacement materials and inspections over the three years.

A volunteer group has expressed interest in providing routine care but has not agreed tasks, availability, training or a three-year commitment. Interest is not a resource guarantee, and volunteers may withdraw freely. Before committing the grant, the association can obtain a fixed-price qualified maintenance contract. No orders or promises have yet been made. Provisionally prefer L’s greater simultaneous capacity or S’s larger remaining funding margin, but identify what is missing before either can be approved.

## Round two

Comparable qualified bids now price the complete additional maintenance scope at HK$10,000 per year for L or HK$8,000 per year for S, fixed for three years and including replacement materials, inspections and all labour costs. A separate HK$12,000 reserve must remain uncommitted for unexpected needs; it is not an extra annual cost. The grant can be held and spent across the three years, and the service contract is enforceable with a qualified replacement arrangement. No future grant, fee or donation is assumed.

L therefore needs HK$142,000: HK$100,000 installation, HK$30,000 maintenance and HK$12,000 reserve. It is HK$22,000 short. S needs HK$96,000: HK$60,000 installation, HK$24,000 maintenance and HK$12,000 reserve, leaving HK$24,000 beyond the protected reserve. The organiser may implement S now or defer L while seeking a fully committed additional HK$22,000. The funder permits a four-week decision extension with no penalty, but no further money has been promised. If the gap remains, L cannot proceed under the three-year promise; S remains available on the quoted terms at the extension deadline.

Residents can participate voluntarily in supported gardening sessions, but the contract does not depend on unpaid maintenance. Smaller scale reduces simultaneous places; transparent rotating access can share them, without claiming equal total benefit or forcing participation. The manager must monitor service delivery, review needs with residents and preserve an accessible contact and complaint route. Unexpected costs beyond the reserve require a fresh, funded plan or an orderly agreed scope change, not unsafe operation or quietly abandoning the asset. Existing rights and commitments must be respected.

**Student question:** Would you implement S now or defer L for committed additional funding? Reconcile the three-year budget, explain the decision deadline, and assign responsibility if voluntary help or the maintenance provider becomes unavailable.

## Count the service life

Installation is only one part of the commitment. L totals HK$142,000; S totals HK$96,000. The reserve is included once, not once per year. S’s HK$24,000 unallocated balance is additional to its protected HK$12,000 reserve. Neither a capital asset nor a favourable opening-day response pays future bills.

## Do not book an unconfirmed promise

Volunteer interest and a possible second grant are not committed resources. Paid contracted care supports the service regardless of voluntary participation. Deferring L is a conditional decision: seek HK$22,000 by the permitted deadline, then implement only if the full plan is funded. It is not permission to install L now and hope.

## Name the steward and the exit

The association owns the equipment; its manager monitors the contractor, keeps maintenance records and arranges qualified replacement when needed. Free participation, accessible access and existing promises remain protected. Safe removal at the planned end is funded separately. Before any extension, secure a new purpose, permission, resources and agreement rather than assuming permanence.

## Instructor note

S offers a funded start at smaller scale; a four-week search for L preserves a chance of greater capacity but delays the start and may fail. No quantified social-impact ranking is supplied. Sustainability includes meaningful continued benefit and participation, not merely financial self-sufficiency. CIIF guidance makes that distinction but does not fund this fictional installation. Historical UK funding examples illustrate lifecycle planning, not current eligibility or Hong Kong grant rules.
