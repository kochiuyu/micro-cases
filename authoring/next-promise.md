# Funded. What should we promise next?

ENT-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Entrepreneurship · Crowdfunding · Scope management

![Two adult creators playtest cards in a modest studio with a boxed base game nearby.](../next-promise-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 30 August 2016, Kickstarter announced its opening to creators in Hong Kong and Singapore.

This is historical context, not a new launch or current eligibility advice. The funded game and development decision below are fictional; no platform endorses the proposal.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong two-person creator team has an ongoing campaign that has reached its initial funding goal for a cooperative tabletop card game for two to four players. The promised physical game is fully specified and production-ready. Solo play was not promised.

Some backers now ask for a solo mode so they can play when friends are unavailable. The team could announce a carefully scoped stretch goal, adding a deliverable beyond the original funded goal, or keep this campaign focused and consider solo play later as a separate project.

The original production, fulfilment, fees and contingency budget is ring-fenced. Additional development must have its own budget and capacity; higher pledges are not automatically spare funds. No solo obligation has been announced. The question is whether extra user value warrants another promise, not whether to replace an existing one.

## Evidence

| Add a solo-mode goal | Keep this campaign’s scope |
|---|---|
| May add useful play opportunities | Concentrates attention on the specified physical game |
| Requires design, testing, delivery and support | Leaves solo play for a possible later project |
| No uplift or completion guarantee | No assumed backlash |

**Student question:** Would you add a solo-mode goal now or keep this campaign focused? Name one fact about production or delivery that would change your choice.

## Round two

The designer demonstrates a feasible solo approach using only the existing cards. It needs no new components, artwork, box size, weight or printer change. A digital rule sheet can be delivered after the original physical game ships, removing that manufacturing dependency.

A playtester has reserved separate capacity. A costed development plan can be funded from an additional amount above the fully covered core budget, with its own contingency. These are fictional planning assumptions, not a guarantee that the mode is fun, balanced or finished.

The founders still must choose whether its value warrants taking on the work. If they proceed, the base game must retain its original scope and delivery estimate. The optional-to-use digital rules need a distinct later estimate, clear eligibility and separate progress reporting. No backer should mistake them for a finished feature arriving inside the box.

**Student question:** Does independent digital delivery change your decision? Choose whether to add the goal and write the boundary between the original promise and the new one.

## The enabling fact changes the risk, not the amount of work to zero

The proposed design no longer requires changing the physical production specification. That matters: the team can consider an additional delivery track rather than making every base-game shipment wait for a new component.

Digital still requires design, playtesting, accessibility, distribution and support. Separate testing capacity and funding make the plan more credible, but do not establish finished quality or eliminate the founders’ coordination responsibilities.

## State two promises clearly

If choosing the goal, state that the original two-to-four-player physical game keeps its existing scope and delivery estimate. Explain that the unlocked solo rules would use those cards, arrive digitally later and remain in development.

Assign responsibility for the extra work, specify who qualifies and publish a realistic later estimate before announcing it. If existing backers receive access without extra payment, say so explicitly in the selected offer. Do not invent a funding threshold before accounting for the final pledge mix and reward obligations.

Separate progress updates should make both tracks visible. A later estimate is not permission to abandon the new obligation, and no existing promised feature may silently disappear.

## Either choice still needs a strategy

Adding a bounded digital goal can be defensible when its user value and independently funded capacity justify the work. Keep the core budget protected and confirm the practical separation, including founder attention and ongoing support.

Keeping the campaign focused is also defensible if the mode’s value is too uncertain or the founders prefer not to take on another deliverable. Do not assume that every campaign needs stretch goals, or that declining one necessarily disappoints its audience.

## Change one dependency and reconsider

Suppose solo play instead required a new deck inside every box. The team would need a fresh assessment of artwork, printing, packaging, weight, fulfilment cost and timing. The current separate-digital plan would no longer answer those questions.

This does not mean physical additions are always bad or digital additions always safe. It shows why the architecture of the promise matters: identify what the added feature depends on and what already-promised work depends on it.

## Instructor note

Ask for a provisional choice before the enabling fact. Afterwards, require a decision and a clear promise boundary, not simply “research more”. A useful reversal signal is evidence that the new work cannot stay within its own resources or cannot deliver acceptable play without changing the physical game.

Keep the favourable evidence in view: the design route, reserved playtesting and separate funded plan are supplied. Do not turn the case into an automatic rejection of scope growth. Equally, none proves demand, balance, delivery success or higher pledges.

All team details are fictional. This is product-scope education, not an actual campaign announcement, transaction or legal instruction.
