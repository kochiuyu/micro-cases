# The sales target is met. Are the costs covered?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 385 -->

ACC-33 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Sales mix · Contribution ratio · Multi-product break-even

## Revenue is not contribution

Multi-product break-even analysis depends on what is sold as well as total revenue. Products can contribute different amounts from each sales dollar toward common fixed costs. The kiosk, products, school enquiry and every figure below are fictional; the sources explain the method, not this business’s prospects.

## Opening decision

You manage Paper Corner, a fictional Kowloon stationery kiosk selling only premium notebooks A and basic exercise-book packs B for this planning exercise. The owner wants a monthly dashboard showing green whenever revenue reaches HK$100,000. Decide whether that rule is reliable; you are not accepting a contract or setting a selling price.

A sells for HK$40 per notebook and has HK$20 variable cost. B sells for HK$20 per pack and has HK$16 variable cost. These costs include purchase, packaging, payment and all other costs that vary with units sold. Prices and unit costs remain constant throughout the analysed range.

Monthly fixed operating costs are HK$35,200, covering all remaining operating costs in this model, including paid staffing and rent. Do not allocate them to individual products before calculating contribution. Financing, tax and owner distributions are outside this operating-profit measure. There is no inventory valuation or cash-collection timing issue.

The opening HK$100,000 revenue forecast comprises HK$80,000 from A and HK$20,000 from B: an 80:20 revenue mix. These percentages describe sales dollars, not numbers of units. For an opening break-even sensitivity calculation, assume those revenue proportions remain constant as total revenue changes.

Stock, staffing, space and supplier capacity can support either mix and all calculated break-even quantities without extra fixed costs or lost service quality. There is no bottleneck or product linkage. Forecast demand is uncertain; being able to supply a quantity does not mean customers will buy it.

**Student question:** At the opening revenue mix, what are the weighted contribution ratio, break-even revenue and operating result at HK$100,000? Would you make the green dashboard rule conditional on that mix?

## Round two

The revised forecast includes a proposed school order for B, alongside weaker separately forecast demand for A. It now shows HK$40,000 A and HK$60,000 B, still HK$100,000 total revenue. The 40:60 figures are revenue shares. The school order is unaccepted, and no demand is guaranteed.

This revised forecast replaces the opening forecast; it is not HK$60,000 of extra B revenue added to unchanged A sales. The school proposal has not been shown to cause A’s decline. No sale is rejected because of capacity, and no customer is forced to switch products.

Prices, variable unit costs and HK$35,200 fixed costs remain unchanged. School fulfilment adds no separate setup, delivery, credit or administration cost beyond the stated model. For the revised break-even scenario only, hold the 40:60 revenue mix constant while scaling total sales. That is a sensitivity assumption, not a promise of repeat school orders.

**Student question:** Does HK$100,000 still cover fixed operating costs at the revised mix? Calculate the new break-even revenue, reconcile it to whole product quantities, and recommend what the dashboard must show instead of a revenue-only green light.

## Debrief · Start with each sales dollar

A contributes HK$40 − HK$20 = HK$20 per notebook, or 50% of A revenue. B contributes HK$20 − HK$16 = HK$4 per pack, or 20% of B revenue. Contribution covers common fixed costs before producing operating profit. Neither contribution figure is a product’s final allocated profit.

## Debrief · Weight the opening revenue shares

Opening weighted ratio = 80% × 50% + 20% × 20% = 44%. At HK$100,000, contribution is HK$44,000 and operating profit is HK$44,000 − HK$35,200 = HK$8,800. Break-even revenue is HK$35,200 ÷ 44% = HK$80,000, conditional on the opening mix.

## Debrief · Recalculate after the reveal

Revised weighted ratio = 40% × 50% + 60% × 20% = 32%. At unchanged HK$100,000 revenue, contribution is HK$32,000 and the operating result is a HK$3,200 loss. Revenue did not change, but fewer of its dollars came from the higher-contribution product.

## Debrief · The new break-even point

At the revised mix, break-even revenue is HK$35,200 ÷ 32% = HK$110,000. This is HK$30,000 above the old break-even point and HK$10,000 above the current forecast. These are different comparisons. Selling HK$10,000 more works only if the revised mix and costs remain as assumed.

## Debrief · Check with actual units

At HK$110,000 and 40:60 revenue shares, A revenue is HK$44,000: 1,100 notebooks. B revenue is HK$66,000: 3,300 packs. Contribution is 1,100 × HK$20 + 3,300 × HK$4 = HK$35,200, exactly covering fixed costs. Both quantities are whole units; no rounding adjustment is needed.

## Debrief · Do not mix the weights

The revised unit mix is 1,100:3,300, or 25:75, not 40:60. A unit-weighted contribution is 25% × HK$20 + 75% × HK$4 = HK$8 per average unit; 4,400 units cover HK$35,200. Use revenue weights with contribution percentages, or unit weights with unit contributions, never a mixture.

## Debrief · Repair the decision rule

Show forecast revenue by product, total contribution, common fixed costs and operating result. Display break-even revenue with its explicit mix assumption. The revised HK$100,000 forecast should not be green for operating break-even. A revenue target alone cannot protect a contribution target when the mix changes.

## Debrief · Do not reject a positive contribution blindly

B still adds HK$4 per pack before unchanged fixed costs. This case does not show that rejecting the school order restores A demand or improves profit. Investigate feasible demand, prices and costs separately. Do not prescribe an all-A mix, assume extra sales appear on demand, or infer customer basket effects.

## Instructor note

Allow four minutes for opening ratios, four for the new mix and six for unit reconciliation and dashboard design. Ask why 40:60 revenue shares become 25:75 unit shares. Keep the exercise about total contribution covering common fixed costs, not scarce-resource optimisation or a causal claim that a school customer harms the shop.
