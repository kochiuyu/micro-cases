# Endorsed by whom. Credible to whom?

ENT-26 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · New-venture legitimacy · Audience-specific signals · Credible evidence

![A startup founder and professional exhibition user consider an unmarked application folder and an inventory sample at a shared desk.](../endorsed-by-whom-credible-to-whom-scene.jpg)

## Recognition does not answer every stakeholder’s question

Organisational-legitimacy research asks why audiences regard a venture as an appropriate organisation to support. A young firm may need customers, employees and other partners to take it seriously, but those groups do not necessarily evaluate the same evidence. Recognition can help without proving demand or product performance.

The venture, award, demonstration and stakeholder comments below are original fiction. No real institution endorses this product. The exercise is about choosing a truthful signal for a specific resource need, not manufacturing prestige or disguising a paid relationship.

## Opening decision

You founded a Hong Kong venture building an inventory tool for small exhibition organisers. It helps staff record ordinary display items and export a handover list. It does not value artworks, certify safety or manage regulated collections. The product has no paid customers yet. The current team can deliver a bounded pilot; its existing funded staffing is sufficient for the next three months. A possible additional employee would support later expansion, not unlock this month’s pilot.

This month you have HK$12,000 and two paid development days for one structured credibility-building activity. A prepares an application and presentation for a prestigious general new-venture award. Its independent panel reviews the organisation’s plan, governance and team readiness, not the reliability of the inventory workflow. Entry is permitted, but no shortlist or award is guaranteed. B prepares a supervised demonstration with a respected professional exhibition manager, using synthetic item records to test handover and export. The manager has agreed to participate, not to endorse the venture or buy anything.

Both activities consume the whole allowance, including preparation, fees, staff time and follow-up, and cannot be combined this month. Under B the manager is paid a disclosed fixed fee for time regardless of the result. A factual test summary may be used only after the manager approves its accuracy and permitted attribution. Failure would be recorded too. Neither activity changes the product’s basic scope, employment terms or truthful disclosures. Your immediate priority is to qualify for a buyer’s paid-pilot evaluation, while keeping a credible story for potential employees.

## Evidence

| Choice | Possible credibility source | What is not promised |
|---|---|---|
| A: seek general award | Panel reviews plan, governance and team readiness | Winning, testing, customers or job acceptance |
| B: professional demonstration | Relevant user observes defined workflow | Positive result, endorsement, purchase or certification |

Each uses HK$12,000 and two paid development days. Neither outcome exists yet.

**Student question:** Which activity would you fund first, and for which audience? Identify the concern it might address and one claim you could not honestly make even if the activity went well.

## Round two

Three prospective buyer organisations and two potential employees explain their concerns in exploratory conversations. These are selected contacts, not a representative survey or forecast. All three buyers say the immediate obstacle is whether staff can produce a complete, usable handover file without being locked into the venture’s tool. They require an observed export-and-handover exercise before considering a paid pilot. A recognised exhibition manager’s factual account would help them decide to review that evidence, but would not waive their own evaluation, security checks or procurement process.

Both potential employees recognise the general award and say independent scrutiny of the venture’s governance and readiness would make it look more credible as an organisation to join. They would still examine the role, pay, financing and working arrangements. They do not regard one successful export demonstration as evidence of financial stability. Buyers know the award too, but say its actual criteria do not test their workflow concern.

The proposed demonstration can use 50 synthetic item records, a known reference list and a receiving user who opens and checks the exported file without the venture’s tool. The procedure records completeness, field accuracy, usability and any failure. No result is known. A favourable result would cover only the tested version and conditions. The allowance and one-activity choice are unchanged; the company cannot truthfully describe participation, an application or a private conversation as approval.

**Student question:** Would you keep or change your funding choice after hearing the two audiences? Specify what a truthful demonstration summary or award claim could establish, what each audience must still verify, and what would count as progress beyond attention.

## Name the audience and the resource

The buyers control access to a paid-pilot evaluation; potential employees control whether they consider joining. Their concerns overlap but differ. The buyer asks whether this unfamiliar venture can support an acceptable handover process. Candidates ask whether it appears to be a credible organisation to work for. Calling both concerns trust should not hide the different evidence each requires.

## Prestige can be relevant without being sufficient

A general award is not worthless: independent scrutiny of the organisation’s plan and governance speaks to the candidates’ stated concern. But an application is not a win, and even a win would not prove solvency, good employment terms or the ability to complete the buyer’s export task. The same badge can be meaningful to one audience yet incomplete for another. Do not present award criteria as a technical certification they are not.

## A demonstration supplies evidence and a social signal

B could generate direct evidence about a particular workflow. The respected manager’s involvement could also make buyers willing to examine an unknown venture, provided the account is accurate and its paid nature disclosed. These are distinct functions. A well-known observer does not transform a failed test into a success or make the findings representative of every customer. Approval to attribute a report is separate from approval of the product.

## Prioritise the current bottleneck

B is the stronger first choice for the stated immediate objective because it can address the prerequisite the buyers identify, while the current team can already deliver a pilot. That is not proof B has the greater expected financial return: no outcome probabilities or sales values are supplied. A remains a defensible first choice if the founder explicitly changes the resource priority towards recruitment and accepts postponing buyer evaluation. A different bottleneck could reverse the sequencing.

## Keep claims bounded and next steps observable

Before the demonstration, agree the procedure, reference list, version and reporting permissions. Afterwards, report what actually happened, including failed or missing fields and the fixed-fee relationship. A successful report might say the tested version exported the 50 specified synthetic records accurately under the stated conditions, only if verified. It cannot claim certification, endorsement of all uses or proven customer demand. Ask buyers whether they will proceed to their own evaluation; a separately agreed paid pilot is stronger resource evidence than views or praise, but not proof of recurring demand.

## Do not abandon the other audience

Under B, explain to candidates the real organisation, paid role, financing position and working practices rather than pretending the demonstration guarantees stability. Under A, continue to acknowledge that buyers still need workflow evidence. Existing conversations and accurate baseline disclosures continue under either route, but a second funded activity is not free. Track audience-specific progress: a buyer’s concrete evaluation step, a candidate’s informed continued interest, and eventually separately agreed commitments.

## Instructor note

Use 3 minutes for the first choice, 5 for the reveal and 7 for an audience–concern–evidence map. Require separate rows for buyers and candidates. Reward an explanation of why the award can matter without meeting the buyer’s test, rather than a simplistic contrast between empty publicity and real evidence.

This is not merely making expertise visible to customers or adding unfamiliar contacts to a network. It concerns an emerging organisation’s acceptance by different resource audiences. The professional has agreed only to participate; students must not turn that into an endorsement. No conversion rate, award-win chance or hiring outcome can be calculated from five exploratory conversations. Legal guidance is background on truthful claims, not a conclusion about the law governing the fictional venture.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All venture, award, criteria, budgets, contacts and test details are invented. Each option uses the same HK$12,000 and two paid development days; no free simultaneous activity. There are three prospective buyer organisations and two candidate contacts, selected qualitatively, not a statistical sample. Current staffing can perform the pilot for three months; additional hiring is for later expansion. Award entry is permitted but winning is uncertain. Professional participation is agreed and paid at a disclosed fixed rate independent of outcome; attribution needs separate accuracy approval and permission. Fifty synthetic records and their reference list allow a bounded test, not a general guarantee. No actual endorsement, customer contract or job offer is made. Foreign guidance is not asserted as Hong Kong law.
