# A disagreement over pay. Or over the package?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 257 -->

PPL-22 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Integrative negotiation · Trading across issues · Voluntary agreement

![Two fictional project leads compare blank planning cards beside a calendar in a small office.](../different-priorities-better-package-scene.jpg)

## Research connection

Negotiation research distinguishes dividing a fixed resource from finding trades across issues people value differently. A 2019 study tested multiple equivalent package offers across six experiments. Its results motivate exploring packages, not a promise that every negotiation creates gains. Our people, options and scores below are entirely fictional.

## Opening decision

Ada and Ben are project leads at a fictional Hong Kong design business. They are considering a joint, eight-week internal secondment to improve its booking process. Both must volunteer; either may remain in their current role without penalty. Their normal salary, benefits, pay floors, hours and existing entitlements are protected. The temporary work replaces, rather than adds to, their usual workload.

The sponsor has approved the three negotiable issues below. Both start dates have funded cover and identical project scope. Each colleague receives the same optional course, either before the first start date or after the secondment. It is developmental, not required for safe or competent project work. All combinations fit the sponsor’s budget, with no displaced third-party course entitlement. There are no other negotiable terms in this exercise.

Ada suggests HK$3,000 each, starting the first Monday, with Ada taking the early course seat and Ben the later one. It is simple and divides money evenly. Ben asks to discuss the whole package before agreeing. Neither has explained their priorities. You facilitate their conversation, not impose a settlement. The decision need not be completed today.

## Evidence

| Issue | Available choices |
|---|---|
| One-off allowance | HK$6,000: Ada/Ben 2,000/4,000, 3,000/3,000 or 4,000/2,000 |
| Joint start | First or third Monday next month |
| Optional course | One early and one later seat; one per colleague |

**Student question:** Would you provisionally accept the equal-allowance package, or keep money, timing and training open together? Name two questions you would ask before either colleague commits.

## Round two

After a voluntary discussion, Ada explains that a later start fits a personal commitment; she need not disclose its private details. Ben values taking the course early to explore a future development direction. Each supplies the simple preference scores below. These are invented decision aids, not money, performance ratings or measures of human worth.

| Item | Ada’s points | Ben’s points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each HK$1,000 received | 10 | 10 |
| First-Monday start | 0 | 10 |
| Third-Monday start | 40 | 0 |
| Early course seat | 10 | 40 |
| Later course seat | 0 | 0 |

For this exercise, add the three issue scores for each person separately. The scores capture all stated preferences over these options and have no interaction effects. Each values remaining in their current role at 55 on their own scale, and will consider a secondment package scoring at least 60. Scores do not authorise automatic acceptance: both must confirm their voluntary agreement. No one must disclose a private minimum in real negotiations.

The HK$6,000 allowance is paid only if the joint secondment proceeds. If either declines, both keep their current roles and all existing entitlements; the sponsor postpones the project. All options and protections remain available. Recommend a package, not a threat.

**Student question:** Propose a complete package both colleagues can accept. Show each person’s score, compare it with staying in their current role, and explain why another acceptable package could still be negotiated.

## First ask about priorities, not just positions

Ask which issue matters most and which alternatives are workable. Ask what would make the complete package preferable to remaining in the current role. Equal money can be a defensible starting principle, but it does not establish agreement on timing or training. Do not demand private explanations for a stated preference.

## Calculate one person at a time

Ada’s initial package gives her 30 + 0 + 10 = 40 points. Ben receives 30 + 10 + 0 = 40. Both are below their 55-point alternative and 60-point acceptance floor. Moving only the allowance cannot make both acceptable: it transfers points between them while leaving those two issue choices unchanged.

## Find a trade before splitting the gains

Keep HK$3,000 each, switch the joint start to the third Monday and give Ben the early seat. Ada receives 30 + 40 + 0 = 70; Ben receives 30 + 0 + 40 = 70. Each improves on their own initial score and outside option. The budget has not grown. The improvement comes from matching timing and training to different priorities.

## More than one acceptable settlement

| Third Monday; Ben early | Ada | Ben |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ada HK$2,000; Ben HK$4,000 | 60 | 80 |
| HK$3,000 each | 70 | 70 |
| Ada HK$4,000; Ben HK$2,000 | 80 | 60 |

These three packages meet both individual floors. None dominates the others: moving HK$1,000 from one person to the other helps the recipient and hurts the giver. Equal money is a transparent default here; an unequal split needs a mutually acceptable explanation, not a claim that the table proves entitlement. Do not add the two people’s scores into a welfare total: personal scales need not be comparable.

## Agree the package, not an isolated concession

Record allowance, joint start, early/later course allocation, duties, return arrangements and preserved entitlements together. Confirm both colleagues agree and that the sponsor can deliver. Offering several packages can help discussion, but the three examples are not “equivalent offers” to either person: their individual scores differ. Nothing here implies a universally best tactic or a right to change employment terms unilaterally.

## Instructor note

Give pairs two minutes to choose an opening approach and write questions. Reveal the scores only afterward. Ask them to calculate the initial package, then propose two acceptable alternatives. A useful challenge is to change only the money: can both reach 60? They cannot. Distinguish creating gains through cross-issue trades from distributing gains through the allowance split. Do not role-play pressure, fabricate threats or require personal disclosures. PPL-14 concerns internal pay comparability; PPL-19 coordinates release after an appointment. Here participation and the whole package are still open.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original fictional people, business, project, budget, timing, consent rules and additive scores. Scores rank this exercise’s packages only, not real employee preferences or interpersonal welfare. The 60-point floor is stipulated, with a 55-point current-role alternative; it is not a statutory pay floor. Normal pay and all entitlements are protected separately. Every listed combination is funded and operationally feasible; no employee or third party is coerced or disadvantaged through loss of existing entitlements. This is not legal advice. Acas is UK guidance, not Hong Kong law. PON articles share a publisher; five documents are not five independent empirical studies. Our packages are not MESOs because they are not equal in value to an offerer. No experiment’s numerical effects are imported.

- [Harvard Program on Negotiation · Negotiations and Logrolling](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/mediation/mediation-breaking-a-partial-impasse-in-negotiations/). Professional explanation of trades across issues using different priorities. Supports the mechanism, not our fictional payoff table.
- [OpenStax · Organizational Behavior, 14.4 Negotiation Behavior](https://openstax.org/books/organizational-behavior/pages/14-4-negotiation-behavior). Educational distinction between distributive and integrative bargaining; preparation and identifying goals. No cultural stereotypes or universal tactic recommendations are adopted.
- [Leonardelli and colleagues · Multiple equivalent simultaneous offers · 2019](https://researchmgt.monash.edu/ws/portalfiles/portal/274825846/274619042_oa.pdf). Original six-experiment paper on package choices and economic/relational outcomes. Equivalence means equal value to the offerer; our unequal-score examples do not meet that definition. Results are context-dependent, not guaranteed workplace gains.
- [Harvard Program on Negotiation · Take Your BATNA to the Next Level](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/take-your-batna-to-the-next-level/). Professional guidance on evaluating the alternative outside a negotiation on comparable terms. Supports checking current-role alternatives, not threatening colleagues or treating invented scores as market facts.
- [Acas · Consulting about employment contract changes](https://www.acas.org.uk/changing-an-employment-contract/employer-responsibilities/consulting-about-employment-contract-changes). UK guidance on genuine two-way consultation, considering alternatives and avoiding intimidation. Used for a process safeguard, not a statement of Hong Kong law.
