# A credit limit. How much can we draw?

![A distributor’s finance manager and colleague examine invoice folders beside stocked product bins.](../credit-limit-available-draw-scene.jpg)

## Credit commitments and borrowing bases are different limits

A March 2024 credit-agreement amendment filed by Universal Electronics illustrates that a revolving commitment can be limited by a separate borrowing-base formula and eligibility rules. The public filing is an example of contractual structure, not the contract or pricing used below.

Banks and supervisors describe asset-based borrowing as tied to eligible collateral, not simply total assets on the balance sheet. Every facility, rate and collateral amount in this classroom comparison is fictional.

## Opening decision

You are finance manager of a fictional Hong Kong distributor. Two days before a planned draw, you compare two fully approved offers that remain available through the draw date. Select one facility, not both. Each would refinance HK$200,000 of existing borrowing and provide the new funding. The distributor needs exactly HK$550,000 additional cash on the draw date, making total borrowing HK$750,000 for 90 days.

Fixed offers a committed HK$800,000 limit, independent of the receivables-age movement considered here. Asset offers a HK$900,000 headline ceiling but limits total borrowing to the lower of that ceiling and 80% of eligible receivables plus 50% of eligible inventory. New drawable cash equals that total permitted borrowing less the HK$200,000 refinanced balance. There are no other drawings, letters of credit, reserves or deductions.

Under Asset’s supplied contract, receivables are eligible only while fewer than 90 calendar days have elapsed since invoice issue and they meet all other stated tests. Current eligible receivables are HK$800,000 out of HK$1,000,000 total book receivables. Eligible inventory is HK$400,000 and all its other tests are satisfied. The final certificate must use eligibility at drawdown, not at this preliminary review.

Fixed charges 9% annual simple interest and an all-in HK$2,000 fee. Asset charges 7% and an all-in HK$3,000 fee. Use 90/360 of a year on the full HK$750,000 balance. Fees and interest are paid from a separate sufficient cash reserve, not the HK$550,000 requirement. There are no other cost differences. All non-collateral draw conditions are met, and repayment after 90 days is funded independently. No agreement is signed yet.

**Student question:** On the preliminary certificate, which facility would you prefer, and how much additional cash and 90-day total financing cost would each provide at the required borrowing?

## Round two

The final draw-date certificate arrives before either offer is accepted. HK$300,000 of the previously eligible receivables has moved from 89 to 91 days since invoice issue without being paid. Under the stated less-than-90-day rule it is now ineligible. There are no new invoices, collections, write-offs, changes to other eligibility tests or changes to inventory. Total book receivables remain HK$1,000,000; eligible receivables fall to HK$500,000.

The HK$200,000 existing balance, HK$550,000 additional requirement, prices and Fixed’s committed limit remain unchanged. No waiver, temporary overadvance, additional collateral, second lender or delayed payment is available for this draw. Both offers are still open. Asset’s reduced borrowing capacity is not an accounting write-off or a new leverage-covenant breach; it follows its stated eligibility formula.

**Student question:** After the aging change, how much new cash is drawable under each facility, what shortfall would Asset leave, and which offer can fund the required payment?

## First calculate the base, then subtract usage

Initially, Asset’s borrowing base is 80% × HK$800,000 + 50% × HK$400,000 = HK$840,000. That is below its HK$900,000 ceiling. After refinancing HK$200,000, new availability is HK$640,000. Fixed permits HK$800,000 in total and HK$600,000 new cash. Both initially cover the HK$550,000 need; neither makes the full headline limit available as additional cash.

## Compare prices only for the same feasible draw

For HK$750,000 over 90/360 year, Fixed interest is HK$16,875; with its fee, total cost is HK$18,875. Asset interest is HK$13,125; with its fee, total cost is HK$16,125. Asset is HK$2,750 cheaper on the initial feasible funding plan, conditional on draw-date eligibility. Fixed has greater stability against the specified aging movement.

## The balance sheet did not have to shrink

At drawdown, Asset’s base becomes 80% × HK$500,000 + 50% × HK$400,000 = HK$600,000. Subtract the existing HK$200,000 and only HK$400,000 new cash is available. The HK$550,000 requirement therefore has a HK$150,000 shortfall. The HK$300,000 eligibility loss reduces the base by HK$240,000, even though book assets are unchanged.

## Choose available funding, not a cheaper ceiling

Fixed still provides up to HK$600,000 new cash. Draw only the required HK$550,000 beyond the refinanced balance, bringing total debt to HK$750,000 and leaving HK$50,000 unused. Choose Fixed under the supplied constraints. Asset’s lower rate cannot fund the full payment and no authorised substitute exists. Do not fabricate eligibility, extend invoice dates or treat the advertised ceiling as a waiver.

## Instructor note

Allow four minutes to calculate initial availability and like-for-like cost, then four for the aged certificate. Require the sequence eligible collateral, advance rates, ceiling, existing usage, new availability. Ask why ineligibility need not equal an accounting loss.

This is not a leverage-covenant test after borrowing or a forecast of when a customer order creates a cash gap. The funding date and need are fixed; collateral eligibility changes before drawdown. Real facilities can contain reserves, concentration limits, lender discretion and other conditions. The simplified contract here supplies all operative terms. No real loan, security grant or legal registration is requested.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All facilities, rates, collateral amounts and contractual rules are fictional. Real agreements may have additional eligibility tests, reserves, discretion and legal requirements. This is a classroom financing comparison, not a loan application, security grant or personal financial recommendation. Public US examples do not establish Hong Kong law.

Primary filed agreement distinguishes commitment, eligible accounts, borrowing base and usage limits. Its actual terms and jurisdiction are not the fictional terms.
