# One acquisition. One operating system?

STR-27 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Post-acquisition integration · Strategic interdependence · Organisational autonomy

![Managers from an acquiring company and an acquired studio compare work-order cards beside packing materials.](../one-acquisition-one-operating-system-scene.jpg)

## The deal closes before integration is complete

Acquisition research separates the value of coordinating activities from the disruption that changing organisational boundaries and routines can cause. Integration choices can differ by activity and over time. This case is an original fictional operating decision, not an account of a real merger.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong display-services group has completed its acquisition of a small custom exhibition-label studio. Price, funding, legal approvals and ownership are settled. The group bought an established capability for producing accurate short-run labels against changing client layouts. Existing staff, pay, customer rights and delivery commitments are protected under every option. No layoffs, new acquisition or contract changes are being considered.

The owner must approve a six-week operating arrangement. A immediately moves purchasing, job scheduling and customer-proof approval into the parent’s standard workflow. B protects the studio’s separate operating routines for all six weeks, with required financial and risk oversight but no new shared operational process. C combines selected activities now, keeps others separate and uses a joint review to decide later changes. All three fit the funded integration capacity; there is no external deadline requiring one common workflow. The costs of effort and foregone benefits still matter, even though none is unaffordable.

The parent expects common purchasing and consistent information to help, but has not yet separated those benefits from the value of standardising the studio’s customer-facing work. The studio manager worries about losing the routine that makes short runs reliable. Neither concern proves its preferred structure is right. Required group controls must operate under every plan, and any workflow transfer must pass service checks before live use.

## Evidence

A: immediate common purchasing, scheduling and proof approval after readiness checks. B: six weeks of protected operational separation with mandatory oversight. C: selective integration now, joint investigation and later review. All fit funded capacity and protect existing commitments; no external common-workflow deadline.

**Student question:** Would you choose immediate integration, protected separation or staged selective integration? Identify the benefit that needs coordination and the capability that could need autonomy before choosing boundaries.

## Round two

The joint team identifies one separable procurement opportunity. Buying the same approved blank-label stock through the parent would reduce the studio’s monthly purchase cost from HK$50,000 to HK$48,000 at unchanged volume, quality, lead time and payment terms. The quoted HK$2,000 saving is before any integration or administration cost. It requires shared ordering, not common job scheduling or proof approval. No other financial benefit has been quantified.

A walk-through shows a tightly linked local routine: the client liaison checks a late layout change with the typesetter, the typesetter tests it against the current small print batch, and the dispatch coordinator adjusts the job sequence before release. A proof is approved only after that feedback. The parent’s workflow freezes the sequence before the final customer-proof stage. For these short runs, moving only proof approval could therefore break the feedback loop. The parent has not mapped or tested a way to preserve it. These are supplied operating dependencies, not a claim that the parent’s workflow is generally inferior.

A reporting adapter can produce the parent’s required financial, risk and delivery-status reports from the studio’s existing records without moving scheduling or approval authority. It has passed a reconciliation check for those reports; this is not a test of common workflow. The studio team agrees to explain its routine and participate in a joint six-week review. All three choices are still unimplemented, the same capacity remains available, and live changes must preserve existing service checks.

**Student question:** Which activities would you combine now, preserve for six weeks and reconsider later? Calculate the quoted procurement saving, then give a test and decision gate for any later workflow integration.

## Answer the initial boundary question

C is a sensible provisional choice when the location of value and disruption is still uncertain: identify concrete coordination benefits before imposing a common workflow, and protect a capability while learning how it works. A could be justified if strong interdependence genuinely requires one workflow and safe transfer is demonstrated. B could be justified if coordination benefits are weak or not yet separable. Ownership alone does not answer which operating boundaries should disappear.

## Combine the separable benefit

After the reveal, choose C: use the validated reporting adapter and pursue shared purchasing only if a documented net-benefit check supports it, subject to normal controls and agreed implementation. HK$50,000 minus HK$48,000 gives HK$2,000 per month, or 4% of the previous purchase cost. This is a gross saving on that item, not a net synergy estimate, annual profit forecast or evidence that every operation should merge. Before changing purchasing, compare the gross saving over the relevant review horizon with all incremental implementation and administration costs. If those costs exceed the saving, retain separate purchasing while keeping C’s selective review design. Track actual effort and realised benefit; affordability alone does not establish value.

## Preserve the capability while making it understandable

Keep the studio’s linked liaison, typesetting, sequencing and proof-approval routine together for the six-week review. The need for autonomy lies in the interdependent delivery routine, not in a right to ignore group oversight. Use joint observation, example jobs and exception tracing to make dependencies explicit; do not remove one step merely because it looks duplicative from outside. Shared reporting provides accountability without forcing identical task execution.

## Use a review gate rather than a merger slogan

At six weeks, the owner and studio lead should review a mapped end-to-end workflow, an assessed cost-benefit case and a rehearsal including late layout changes. A common-workflow proposal must preserve the proof feedback loop, all existing quality checks and promised delivery times, with named authority for exceptions and a tested recovery route. If the evidence fails, retain the local workflow and specify what further evidence is needed; the review date is not an automatic absorption deadline. If the parent’s approach meets those conditions and offers meaningful additional value, integration can increase.

## Instructor note

The acquisition decision and price are deliberately closed. Students should separate shared procurement, reporting and the customer-delivery routine rather than choose “integrate everything” or “touch nothing”. Protected separation does not suspend mandatory control. The reporting test establishes report compatibility only, not delivery safety. The proposed selective arrangement is contingent on the supplied boundaries; it is not a universal formula for acquisitions. Research on technology firms, banking and a cross-border airline acquisition motivates questions, not numerical predictions for this studio.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All acquisition, studio, workflow, costs and review conditions are original fiction. The deal is complete and no real transaction or employment change is requested. Procurement is unchanged approved stock at unchanged volume, quality, lead time and terms: HK$50,000 − HK$48,000 = HK$2,000 monthly gross saving; 2,000/50,000 = 4%. No net integration benefit or loss probability is supplied. Reporting adapter validation is limited to reports. Six-week review is an evidence gate, not automatic absorption. Group oversight, staff protections and customer commitments apply to every option. Scholarly sources have different settings and methods; the research primer is a synthesis, not an independent experiment. Shared purchasing is conditional on positive net benefit over the relevant review horizon after all incremental costs; if costs exceed gross saving, keep it separate within the selective design.

- [Puranam, Singh and Chaudhuri, 2009: Integrating Acquired Capabilities](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/Integrated_Acquired_Capabilities.pdf). Original study distinguishes interdependence, structural integration and common ground. Technology-acquisition evidence is not a performance forecast for this studio.
- [Puranam, Singh and Zollo, 2006: Organizing for Innovation](https://www.academia.edu/81052500/Organizing_for_Innovation_Managing_the_Coordination_Autonomy_Dilemma_in_Technology_Acquisitions). Full article discusses coordination-autonomy tradeoffs and how integration effects vary across innovation stages. No universal claim that autonomy is always better.
- [Kroon and colleagues: Hard and Soft Integration](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joms.12766). Open-access longitudinal study examines different unit-level integration processes, task allocation and employee interaction; its single-case limitations are explicit.
- [Singh and Zollo, 1999: Post-acquisition Strategies, Integration Capability, and Economic Performance](https://flora.insead.edu/fichiersti_wp/inseadwp1999/99-42.pdf). Original working paper examines integration and knowledge codification in US banking acquisitions. Preliminary, sector-specific findings motivate deliberate learning rather than copied routines.
- [Bodner and Capron, 2018: Post-merger integration](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41469-018-0027-4). Open-access research primer integrates questions of what to reconfigure and how much structural integration to pursue; presents the autonomy-interdependence framework.
