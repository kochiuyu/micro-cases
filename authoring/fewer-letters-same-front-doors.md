# Fewer letters, the same front doors

ECO-49 · Singapore · 15 minutes · Universal service · Fixed route costs · Cost recovery · Selective entry

![Two postal staff review a route map with a lightly filled mail tray beside them.](../fewer-letters-same-front-doors-scene.jpg)

## A thinner mailbag still needs a route

Singapore’s communications ministry reported in 2023 that domestic letter volume had fallen markedly from FY2015 to FY2022. A 2024 parliamentary answer described SingPost’s obligation to deliver to every addressable place. That combination matters: a thinner mailbag does not remove the addresses from a route. The regulator, contracts and figures below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Maya advises a fictional city that requires weekday letter delivery to every address at the same quality. At S$0.60 per letter, residents and firms send 10 million letters a year. Each letter costs S$0.20 to process, regardless of route. Running the core routes costs S$3.6 million a year and running the outer routes in-house costs another S$1.4 million. Those route costs do not vary with the number of letters.

Option A keeps all routes in-house, raises the price to S$0.80 and permits no subsidy. The carrier expects 8.5 million letters at that price. Option B keeps the price at S$0.60 and tenders outer route coverage. The carrier retains all postage, pays all per-letter processing costs and contributes any surplus after core routes toward outer coverage. The city covers the rest, up to a S$0.95 million budget.

The quoted fixed fees are annual and replace only the outer route costs stated below. Both bids meet the same standards where they cover. Ignore transition costs, taxes and all other income. Neither option permits leaving an address unserved.

## Evidence

| Bid at current postage | Annual fixed fee | Outer route coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full | S$1.30m | Every outer address |
| Partial | S$1.10m | A subset; S$0.40m additional in-house cost for the rest |

**Student question:** What is the current annual shortfall? Is the higher stamp price financially viable at the forecast volume? Under the tender, which bid provides full coverage at the lowest total cost, and how much funding would the city need?

## Round two

A new forecast suggests just 8 million letters per year at S$0.80. Demand at S$0.60, bids and all other costs remain unchanged. The city still requires full service and will not fund Option A.

**Student question:** Which option now passes the funding and coverage tests? How many letters would the higher-price option need each year to break even? Explain why the cheapest quoted bid and the lowest-cost way to cover every address can differ.

## Debrief · Recover what the routes cost

Each letter contributes S$0.40 at the current price, so 10 million letters contribute S$4 million against S$5 million of fixed route costs. The existing shortfall is S$1 million. At S$0.80 the margin rises to S$0.60 and 8.5 million letters contribute S$5.1 million. Option A has a S$0.10 million surplus under that forecast.

## Debrief · Compare equal coverage

The partial bid costs S$1.10m + S$0.40m = S$1.50m with the remaining addresses covered. The full bid costs S$1.30m. Of the S$4m contribution, S$3.60m covers core routes, leaving S$0.40m. The city must pay S$0.90m for the cheapest complete option, within the S$0.95m cap. Both options initially meet the financing tests, but the stamp increase and city funding place the burden differently.

## Debrief · Put a threshold on demand

At 8 million letters, Option A collects S$4.8m in contribution against S$5m in fixed costs, leaving a S$0.20m deficit. It needs S$5m ÷ S$0.60, or about 8.33 million letters; with whole letters the minimum is 8,333,334. Option B still costs the city S$0.90m and meets full coverage.

## Instructor note

A funded option is not necessarily the best policy. Compare who pays, lost benefits from letters no longer sent and any costs of raising public funds. Ask what evidence would make either demand forecast credible. Do not infer welfare or current Singapore postal policy from this simulated threshold.
