# A village tour. Whose story?

Case 120 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Tourism · Community partnership · Destination stewardship

![A professional guide and two adult resident co-hosts discuss a route sketch and photograph backs at a village community-room table.](../whose-story-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 29 July 2026, Hong Kong’s Countryside Conservation Funding Scheme announced six approved projects, including two cultural-revitalisation projects proposing cultural and ecotour activities.

Approval and intentions are not measured outcomes. The operator, village and programme below are entirely fictional.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. Harbour Paths, a small Hong Kong tour operator, is choosing a four-departure heritage-walk programme in an unnamed village. Each walk lasts 90 minutes and offers at most 12 adult visitor places. Either format costs HK$24,000 in total, including guide pay, preparation, booking, materials, venue and all differing costs.

Both routes have the required permissions, safety and access provision, competent guiding and accurate publicly sourced history. Relevant local contacts helped develop the route; local preparation input is paid. Agreed access does not include private homes or obstruct residents.

Nonparticipants need not perform or participate. Individual refusal, privacy and story-use rights remain protected. No association speaks for everyone, and affected neighbours have a feedback route. Existing required provision stays funded.

Both programmes are feasible and affordable. The decision is which offer to develop and book, not whether to market unconsented access. There is no additional departure or free combined programme.

## Evidence

| Same four-walk budget | A · Operator-led | B · Co-hosted |
|---|---|---|
| Programme | Professional guide: researched public history on agreed route | Professional guide for 60 minutes; paid resident encounter for 30 minutes |
| Departure dates | Choose four of eight available dates before publishing bookings | Four fixed dates |
| Local role | Paid preparation advice; no resident attendance required | Paid co-hosts help shape and deliver their contribution |
| Main trade-off | Flexible scheduling and consistent overview; no promised lived-experience conversation | Direct personal voices and shared editorial control; less scheduling freedom |

B has reserved dates and included preparation, venue, host remuneration and contingency cover. Its full hosting arrangement is due before commitment. A’s dates are chosen before bookings; ordinary booking commitments apply afterward.

Either offers 4 × 12 = 48 available places, not guaranteed attendance. HK$24,000 ÷ 4 = HK$6,000 per departure on average. No ticket-price, demand or profit forecast is supplied.

**Student question:** Which programme would you provisionally choose, A or B? Name the specific evidence about guest purposes, host arrangements or scheduling that could change your choice.

## Round two

B’s paid co-design meeting produces a concrete plan within its existing budget and four fixed dates. Two adult residents will rotate as co-hosts, with a third paid, briefed backup. They choose to bring family-photograph copies and a simple route sketch to discuss how journeys between village and market changed across their generations.

They present their own recollections, not a single story for the whole village. The professional guide supplies documented context and distinguishes personal memory from verified fact.

Hosts approve their contribution and promotional description. The encounter is in an authorised community room, not their homes. Visitor recording or reuse of personal photographs is not included; any further permission must be separate and voluntary.

Agreed preparation and delivery boundaries, plus a post-departure review, let hosts adjust future contributions without unlimited access or unpaid work. This is evidence of a developed fictional offer, not a trial proving satisfaction or unanimous village approval. A remains available and accurate; B still has fixed dates.

**Student question:** Does the co-designed hosting plan change your choice? State what you gain and what remains constrained. Name one visitor outcome and one host or community outcome you would review before extending the programme.

## Whose contribution, and whose decisions?

A provides a tour about the village using researched history and paid local advice. B adds an encounter shaped and delivered with specific willing community members. Payment alone is not the same as influence over how a contribution is used.

The developed plan makes B’s role practical without turning everyday life into guaranteed visitor access. Hosts’ voices matter as situated perspectives, not as automatic proof of every historical claim or representation of all neighbours.

## Keep the operator-led choice credible

A can suit guests needing flexible dates, a consistent researched overview or a format making fewer ongoing demands on residents. It is not fake or inherently unethical.

B offers a different encounter, not automatic authenticity, higher learning or greater profit. Its fixed dates, shared editorial control and variable personal accounts remain real constraints after the reveal.

## Promise only the programme being offered

Tell guests whether they are booking a guide-led public-route history walk or a guide-plus-host encounter on specified dates. Neither includes compulsory shopping, home access, a cultural performance, recording rights or a guaranteed village spokesperson.

Extra dates, new recording rights or a mixed programme require resources and fresh agreement. Do not silently expand what hosts or visitors have accepted.

## Review guests and hosts separately

Ask guests whether the experience met its stated purpose and whether they understood the difference between documented history and a personal account.

Ask hosts whether hours, payment and story boundaries worked in practice; listen to affected nonparticipants as well. Respond to concerns rather than treating the lack of complaints as consent. A sold-out programme or happy visitors alone does not establish long-run community benefit.

## Instructor note

Require a provisional programme choice, not indefinite consultation. The reveal strengthens the feasibility of B without removing A’s scheduling and delivery advantages. Neither choice grants rights over residents or their stories.

Keep capacity arithmetic separate from outcomes: both have four departures and at most 48 places. A’s eight possible dates do not mean eight tours; three host people do not create simultaneous sessions. B’s 60 + 30 minutes equals the same 90-minute visit.

The lesson is community agency in a tourism product, not adding sensory formats or maximising attendance. Institutional frameworks support bounded questions, not certification or evidence that this invented programme is sustainable.
