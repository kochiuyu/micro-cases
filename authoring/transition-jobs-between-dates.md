## A net job count can hide a transition gap

The ILO’s just-transition guidelines connect environmental change with worker participation, skills, rights and social protection. OECD research on displacement in high-emission industries examines losses experienced by affected workers, not merely the economy’s eventual employment total. Neither source says that new jobs automatically reach the people whose old jobs disappear.

The World Bank’s review of coal-mine closures stresses preparation before job losses and support during the transition. That sector differs from the fictional depot below. The sources supply questions about distribution and timing, not the depot’s costs, emissions or employment forecasts.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** A community-service transport depot is replacing its fleet with lower-emission vehicles. Two technically feasible schedules meet the same mandatory environmental deadline at the end of month 6. Both preserve safe operations, required transport service, statutory and contractual employment entitlements, and all consultation obligations. No plan has been adopted or redundancy implemented. You advise the joint management and worker planning group.

Fast converts the whole fleet at the start of month 3. Staged converts half then and half at the start of month 7. The end of month 6 and start of month 7 are the same conversion boundary, so Staged meets the deadline. Both reach the same fleet and operating-emissions level from month 7 onward. Their twelve-month budget estimates use the same scope and include equipment, energy, normal staffing, required training, statutory obligations and administration. They exclude only any extra bridging package discussed later.

Twelve existing fuel-system support roles will disappear; twelve new depot roles will be available. The affected workers have expressed interest in the new roles, but individual placement and training arrangements must be confirmed with them. Equal final headcount is not a promise that every individual will move successfully. Mandatory qualifications and safety assessments cannot be waived.

The transition budget ceiling is **HK$3.7 million**. The table’s operational greenhouse-gas estimates include vehicle fuel and purchased electricity generation over the same first twelve months, using stipulated fixed factors and equal transport service. They are not whole-life manufacturing or disposal impacts. Lower emissions are desirable, but no monetary carbon value or weighting against transition burdens has been supplied.

## Round two

The workforce map shows that six new roles start in month 3 and six only in month 7. The later roles depend on a separate facility commissioning and accredited training schedule that cannot be accelerated. All twelve workers can access the required funded training and assessment, but completion and their acceptance still need checking. New roles offer the same regular pay, benefits, location and contracted hours as the old roles.

Under Fast all twelve old roles end after month 2. Six workers can transfer directly; the other six face **four months, months 3–6, without a funded depot post** before the later roles open. Under Staged the six corresponding old posts remain through month 6, allowing a direct transition. Legal payments and notice obligations are already included under either route; those entitlements are not a substitute for continuing employment, and no waiver is proposed. No external job or income replacement is assumed.

A fully costed Fast-plus-bridge option retains those six workers in paid transition duties and training during months 3–6, maintaining pay, benefits and service continuity. The additional all-in employment cost is **HK$25,000 per person per month**; all other training, supervision and administration are already included in the base estimate. The duties are safe and useful, do not displace other workers, and do not change the emissions estimates. Workers can propose changes and use their representatives without retaliation.

Representatives want the plan to record paid training times, confirmed placement dates, individual access needs, and a funded review before each transfer if training or commissioning slips. They have not approved the whole plan. No extra delay cost is quantified; if milestones fail, management must seek a revised funded plan rather than promise indefinite support from the stated budget.

**Student question:** Would you recommend Fast with the bridge, Staged, or Fast without extra bridging? Calculate the budget and emissions trade-off, identify who bears the four-month gap, and specify the worker agreement and review conditions your recommendation needs.

## Follow people through the calendar

Twelve eventual jobs do not replace four months of employment for six particular people. The relevant gap is six workers × four months gives **24 worker-months**, not twelve permanent job losses. Nor can the six earlier transfers be treated as compensating the other six. Ask about individuals’ training access, job acceptance and future conditions rather than assuming a job count is a placement plan.

## Price the bridge honestly

The bridge costs 6 × 4 × HK$25,000 = **HK$600,000**. Fast with the bridge costs **HK$3.6 million**, leaving HK$100,000 below the HK$3.7 million ceiling. Staged costs HK$3.4 million, leaving HK$300,000. Fast without the bridge costs HK$3.0 million but leaves the stated employment gap; its lower budget does not demonstrate lower total social cost.

Relative to Staged, Fast with the bridge uses HK$200,000 more and avoids 60 tonnes of first-year operational CO2e. That is about HK$3,333 per additional tonne avoided within this narrow comparison. It is not a market carbon price, a full cost-benefit ratio or a monetary valuation of workers’ welfare. Both schedules have the same emissions rate from month 7; do not claim a recurring 60-tonne annual saving.

## Make a recommendation across groups

Fast with the bridge is a strong recommendation if the group gives priority to earlier operational reductions and can responsibly commit the extra HK$200,000. It avoids the identified unfunded-post gap without postponing conversion. Staged remains defensible if conserving that budget and the larger contingency matters more, while still meeting the mandatory deadline. No supplied carbon value establishes a single welfare-maximising answer.

Fast without bridging satisfies the stipulated minimum obligations but concentrates the interim burden on six workers. A recommendation for it must explicitly justify that burden and identify credible support; hypothetical future green jobs or someone else’s gains are not actual compensation. Do not characterise consultation as consent or compliance as a complete fairness assessment.

## Commit to dates, voice and checks

Before adoption, use worker dialogue to confirm paid training access, appropriate assessment, voluntary acceptance of new roles and clear written pay, hours, placement and support arrangements. Include relevant contractors and other affected groups in the impact check rather than assuming the twelve-role map is exhaustive. No further quantified impacts are supplied here.

Assign the transition lead and worker representatives to review commissioning and training before month 3 and again before month 7. Track actual placements, interruption months, pay and conditions as well as emissions. If a milestone slips, escalate funding and support before an agreed bridge expires. Do not finance an unapproved extension by silently cutting benefits or required training.

## Instructor note

Take a first vote using costs, emissions and final job counts, then reveal the worker-month timeline. Ask which group benefits when, and who must carry the interval. Require the HK$600,000 bridge calculation and distinguish its employer cost from an estimate of workers’ personal income loss. A clear recommendation must say what it sacrifices.

Unlike PPL-19, this is a decarbonisation programme with different groups bearing transition timing burdens, not a single internal-transfer release date. All schedules, jobs, eligibility assumptions, costs and emissions are fictional. International sources establish broad principles and sector evidence, not local employment law or guaranteed retraining success. Preserve mandatory rights and safety while debating support beyond that floor.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original fictional classroom design. All roles, workforce interests, training dates, costs, funding and emissions are invented. Both schedules meet the mandatory end-month-6 transition deadline and protect existing legal obligations. The six-worker gap is months 3–6, not a permanent loss of twelve jobs. The additional bridge is 6 × 4 × HK$25,000 = HK$600,000, making Fast HK$3.6 million versus Staged HK$3.4 million. Both fit HK$3.7 million. The difference is HK$200,000 for 60 tonnes of first-year operational CO2e, about HK$3,333 per tonne in this narrow comparison. From month 7 the operating emissions rate is the same. Employer cost is not estimated personal income loss. No carbon welfare value, outside employment, guaranteed individual placement, whole-life environmental comparison or uncosted indefinite extension is assumed.

Guidelines on social dialogue, skills development and social protection, particularly paragraphs 24–29. Supports linking training access and employment support, not any invented depot entitlement.

Primary cross-country analysis of displacement and subsequent earnings, using linked employer–employee data. Motivates looking beyond aggregate job counts; its estimates are not applied to the fictional workers.

Explains the role of social protection alongside active labour-market measures in climate transitions. Used for the distinction between training opportunity and income security.
- [ILO · The MNE Declaration and a just transition · 12 December 2024](https://www.ilo.org/mne-declaration-and-just-transition-environmentally-sustainable-economies). Primary responsible-business guidance connects transition planning with rights and effective worker dialogue. It does not establish that consultation alone means agreement.
- [World Bank · Managing Coal Mine Closure: Achieving a Just Transition for All · 12 December 2018](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/extractiveindustries/publication/managing-coal-mine-closure). Institution’s substantive report summary draws on closure experiences and stresses advance preparation and worker support. Coal-sector lessons are background, not a causal forecast for a transport depot.
