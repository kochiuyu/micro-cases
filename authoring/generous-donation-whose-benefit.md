# A donation is generous. Whose interests does it serve?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 381 -->

GVR-32 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Corporate donations · Private benefits · Minority-owner protection

![Three fictional independent company committee members compare community-training proposals beside a plain room-sign mock-up in a calm Hong Kong meeting room.](../generous-donation-whose-benefit-scene.jpg)

## Purpose, benefits and decision rights

HKEX’s May 2025 governance guide assigns independent directors a role in addressing potential conflicts. The G20/OECD Principles also distinguish the company’s interests from private benefits to controlling shareholders. A community project can have genuine value while still requiring scrutiny of who receives the recognition or other benefits.

## Opening decision

You chair an independent committee at fictional Hong Kong-listed family-controlled company Harbour Loom. Its approved community programme supports adult skills training near its workplaces. The committee can recommend one HK$200,000 donation this quarter. The money belongs to the company, not to its controlling shareholder personally.

Two genuine charities propose programmes for 100 adult learners each. Independent checks support comparable training access, delivery capacity and reporting arrangements. The proposals cover the same period and include all stated delivery costs. Learner numbers are funded places, not guaranteed attendance or employment outcomes. Neither charity has received a commitment.

A, Harbour Skills Trust, has the controlling shareholder’s family as its long-standing patron; the shareholder also serves as a trustee. This relationship is already declared. The shareholder is outside your independent committee and takes no part in its recommendation. B, Neighbour Learning Trust, has no identified link to directors or controlling owners.

The company’s fictional donations policy requires documented fit with the community purpose, proportionate due diligence, declared interests and approval by unconflicted directors. A declared relationship alone does not automatically exclude a charity. Your committee must examine the complete donation terms, not merely the charity’s cause or its headline learner count.

An additional fictional policy rule applies whenever a company-funded donation confers a specific personal benefit on a controlling owner: stop the ordinary approval route, obtain independent assessment of the benefit and company rationale, and seek approval by disinterested shareholders. Interested holders cannot vote under this policy. The company secretary must separately establish applicable legal, listing and disclosure requirements.

Both proposals remain open for 20 working days; the required review can finish within ten. No emergency service depends on payment today. No payment, public announcement or naming commitment may be made before the applicable approvals and disclosure steps are complete. The exercise supplies no legal classification, exemption or monetary threshold.

## Round two

The full A schedule grants a ten-year room name honouring the controlling shareholder personally, with launch publicity crediting that individual as the donor. The company pays the entire HK$200,000. No personal money is contributed. The owner confirms that obtaining this recognition matters personally; it was not in the committee’s summary.

A budgets HK$40,000 within its total for the naming installation and launch presentation; the remaining HK$160,000 supports training delivery. The independent programme review still supports 100 comparable places. HK$40,000 is the budgeted activity cost, not a valuation of the owner’s personal benefit or a payment into the owner’s bank account.

B’s full terms provide the same 100-place programme for HK$200,000 with ordinary acknowledgement of Harbour Loom as corporate donor and no identified personal recognition for an owner. Both proposals remain genuine community projects. No bribery, false accounting or misconduct has been established. The committee has authority to pause before recommending either.

Under the supplied policy, A’s personal naming and donor-credit package triggers the additional route even though the owner has already withdrawn from the recommendation. A has not agreed to remove that package. You may request revised terms, but cannot assume they will be accepted. All budgets, review time and community objectives remain unchanged.

**Student question:** Would you recommend B through the ordinary route, pause A for the additional assessment and approvals, or seek revised A terms? Explain why recusal and 100 genuine training places do not by themselves settle the use of company funds.

## Debrief · Separate three questions

Ask whether the programme serves the company’s approved community purpose, what benefits the payment buys, and who has authority to approve those benefits. Comparable training places answer part of the first question. They do not settle personal recognition, fairness to other shareholders or the required decision route.

In round one, compare the complete terms independently. Neither automatic rejection of a linked charity nor automatic preference for the owner’s favourite follows from the policy. In round two, A combines community delivery with a specific personal naming benefit funded by the company. That changes the approval assessment, not the genuineness of the training.

## Debrief · Recusal is necessary, not sufficient

The owner’s withdrawal protects the committee’s discussion from direct participation. It does not remove the naming right, change the donor credit or return the company’s money. Independent assessment must examine the complete package and explain any company rationale rather than assuming that the owner’s preference is the company’s purpose.

Non-controlling shareholders have an interest in how company resources are used even when a majority owner supports the cause. Control is not permission to treat the corporate donation budget as personal philanthropy. This does not mean every donation must produce an immediate financial return or every association is abusive.

## Debrief · Choose a defensible route

B is a defensible recommendation through ordinary due diligence and unconflicted approval because it meets the stated purpose without the identified personal benefit. Alternatively, keep A under consideration but pause commitment while independent assessment, disinterested-shareholder approval and applicable disclosures are addressed. Do not label committee endorsement alone as final permission.

A revised proposal might remove personal naming and correct donor credit, but the charity has not offered that revision. Obtain written terms and reassess remaining benefits and policy requirements. Removing the plaque alone may leave personal publicity intact. Disclosure does not cure an unjustified allocation, and changing the label does not change its substance.

## Debrief · What the figures establish

Both headline budgets are HK$200,000 ÷ 100 = HK$2,000 per funded place. A’s naming-and-launch allocation is 40,000 ÷ 200,000 = 20% of its budget. Neither ratio prices the owner’s personal recognition or proves that one charity delivers better outcomes. No separate market valuation or attendance results are supplied.

Do not call HK$40,000 a proven loss, a cash transfer to the owner or a recoverable receivable. Equally, do not call the benefit immaterial merely because it is non-cash. The fictional escalation trigger concerns a specific personal benefit, not an invented legal threshold or the owner’s percentage shareholding.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: compare purposes and known relationships. Minutes 4–8: reveal the complete recognition package. Minutes 8–13: map company money, community delivery and personal credit separately. Minutes 13–18: specify a decision route and evidence needed. Keep funds uncommitted while approvals remain open.

GVR-03 examines an interested evaluator’s procurement assessment. Here the independent committee already exists and the owner is recused; the problem remains in the resource allocation itself. GVR-31 concerns time and information for board challenge. Here time is adequate, but the benefit and approval route change. Do not turn a governance concern into an unsupported accusation.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

Original fictional company, charities, relationships, budgets, naming terms and learner places. No real charity or owner is accused. The ordinary and additional approval routes are expressly fictional company policy, not statements that Hong Kong law requires a shareholder vote for every donation or that this proposal meets a connected-transaction definition. Qualified advisers must determine classification, thresholds, exemptions, announcements and approvals for actual circumstances. The owner is already recused; naming and personal donor credit remain in A. Programme quality is stipulated comparable, but attendance and later employment are not guaranteed. HK$40,000 is an activity budget, not a private-benefit valuation or proven loss. The sources provide concepts, not a legal or tax opinion. HKEX sources concern different documents from the same institutional group, not independent empirical studies.
