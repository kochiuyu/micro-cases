# Only two left. Left where?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 592 -->

MKT-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Scarcity claims · Stock scope · Marketing integrity

![Two retail colleagues compare a product mockup beside bins of matching items.](../only-two-in-this-basket-scene.jpg)

## Stock can be limited by channel, not by product

A Singapore competition authority market study noted that a travel site’s apparently scarce rooms could reflect only its own allotment. The fictional Hong Kong shop below deals with retail pickup rather than rooms. Students decide what a customer-facing count can safely communicate.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong stationer sells one notebook design at the same price for shop pickup and home delivery. Product page users have not yet selected a channel. The manager proposes showing Only two left beside the notebook name. A competing draft shows channel-specific availability after the customer picks an option. Neither has gone live.

At 10am, 18 notebooks are allocated to the early pickup slot. Sixteen have already been paid and are awaiting pickup; those orders remain protected. A disjoint pool contains 24 hand-checked units available for separate delivery orders. Later pickup can reopen after six additional units, not counted in either current pool, arrive and pass checks.

## Evidence

| 10am ledger | Units |
| --- | --- |
| Allocated to early pickup | 18 |
| Paid orders in that slot | 16 |
| Available in separate delivery pool | 24 |

**Student question:** Would you approve the proposed headline? Find the number a new buyer could order in the early pickup slot and across both channels, then explain the difference a shopper needs to see.

## Round two

At 10:04, both remaining early-pickup units are paid for. The product-page count still displays the 10am figure in its mock feed; the checkout ledger is current. The delivery pool stays at 24. The six incoming pickup units have not arrived, so the store cannot yet open a later pickup slot.

The shop can immediately turn off the cached pickup number. It can show the currently checked status at checkout and later send a stock notice to shoppers who separately opt in. It cannot claim the incoming items are already available or add shoppers to a list by default.

**Student question:** What would you show on the product page and at checkout now? Recalculate availability by channel and describe when a numeric pickup claim would become suitable again.

## Debrief · Specify the relevant pool

At 10am, pickup has 18 − 16 = 2 orderable units. Delivery has 24, so 26 units are orderable across distinct channels. A shopper needing early pickup benefits from a scoped count. A broad Only two left suggests product-wide scarcity and omits a valid alternative.

## Debrief · Remove stale certainty

After the two purchases, there are zero early-pickup units and 24 delivery units. No incoming items are yet available to promise. Hide the stale number, show the early pickup slot as unavailable when its status is verified, and offer checked delivery availability separately. Do not say sold out without qualification.

## Debrief · Match scope at every step

Before enabling a numeric count, test that the channel, slot, variant and new-order definition match between listing and checkout. Verify the current count and fail to a neutral check-availability message if feed freshness cannot be established. Once six replenishment units physically arrive and pass checks, they can support a later slot, not a retrospective promise.

## Instructor note

Draw three distinct boxes for paid pickup, immediately orderable stock and expected replenishment. Ask whether a numerically correct statement can still mislead. Students are comparing disclosure choices, not deciding how much stock to order or giving legal advice.
