# A generous restricted gift. Enough unrestricted money?

## A larger gift can still leave a funding gap

A restricted gift can increase a charity’s income without paying every cost of the work it adds. Full-cost budgeting makes delivery and support needs visible before promises are made. This fictional exercise concerns an explicit donor restriction and a current-year funding choice, not a rule that every grant must cover every cost.

## Opening decision

You advise fictional Harbour Neighbours, a Hong Kong community charity. A donor offers HK$120,000 for 60 additional adult learning places this year. The programme fits the charity’s purpose. Existing services, employment terms, safety standards and promised access must remain intact. No offer has been accepted and no places have been promised.

The proposed agreement permits only tutor time, participant materials and accessible teaching space for this programme. Together these cost HK$2,000 per place. It expressly excludes the programme’s additional safeguarding coordination, administration and audit work, regardless of how those costs might be classified in financial statements. Unspent restricted money must be returned.

The charity has HK$200,000 of unrestricted cash for this year. HK$180,000 is committed to existing services and obligations. A board-approved HK$10,000 minimum contingency must remain untouched in this exercise. The remaining HK$10,000 is available for the new programme. No other usable funds, free staff capacity or future donations are assumed.

A proposes accepting the gift now for all 60 places because it covers their teaching budget. B postpones acceptance while obtaining the complete additional support cost and negotiating a fundable scope if needed. The donor allows two weeks for this discussion without withdrawing the offer. There is no immediate service emergency.

## Round two

The finance and service teams now agree complete incremental support costs: HK$4,000 fixed for programme administration and audit, plus HK$300 per place for additional safeguarding coordination and records. These are necessary paid resources, not allocations of costs already funded elsewhere. Together with the HK$2,000 teaching cost, they cover all additional costs; no later maintenance obligation remains.

The donor confirms that it would fund any agreed whole-number scope from 20 to 60 places at HK$2,000 per place, under the original restriction. Teaching and support quality per place would not be reduced. No place has an existing claimant. Any limited intake must use published, accessible and fair programme rules.

Alternatively, the donor will consider a revised full-cost request for 60 places that expressly includes the additional support work. Approval is not promised. The charity can wait within the two-week window, then accept a fundable smaller offer or decline. Any changed permitted uses require a written agreement before acceptance; relabelling excluded work is not permission.

**Student question:** Calculate the full 60-place cost and its unrestricted funding gap. What is the largest whole-number scope affordable under the original restriction? Compare that option with a revised full-cost request, including the total requested and what must happen before launch.

## Debrief · Available is not the bank balance

The unrestricted amount available is HK$200,000 − HK$180,000 − HK$10,000 = HK$10,000. The protected contingency is counted once and remains cash, but is not available for this decision. The restricted offer cannot pay excluded support work simply because both amounts are held by the same charity.

For 60 places, teaching costs HK$120,000 and support costs HK$4,000 + 60 × HK$300 = HK$22,000. Full additional cost is HK$142,000. The original gift plus HK$10,000 available unrestricted funding totals HK$130,000, leaving a HK$12,000 gap. Accepting now would create an unfunded commitment.

## Debrief · Reduce scope, not safeguards

Under the original restriction, the charity must meet all support costs from its HK$10,000 available balance. Therefore HK$4,000 + HK$300 × places must not exceed HK$10,000. The maximum is 20 places. This is within the donor’s permitted range; ignoring the fixed cost would incorrectly suggest 33 whole places.

Twenty places require a HK$40,000 restricted gift and HK$10,000 unrestricted support funding: HK$50,000 in total. All available unrestricted capacity is used, while existing commitments and the HK$10,000 contingency remain protected. The charity does not receive HK$120,000 and divert the unused HK$80,000 into support.

## Debrief · Make the complete request explicit

A full-cost request for 60 places is HK$142,000: HK$120,000 teaching plus HK$22,000 expressly permitted support. If approved on those terms, it leaves the charity’s HK$10,000 available unrestricted balance unspent. The request is HK$22,000 above the original gift, not merely the HK$12,000 gap after using that balance.

A separately agreed HK$132,000 gift could cover teaching and HK$12,000 of support, with the charity contributing its available HK$10,000. That is a possible co-funded proposal, not the stated full-cost request or an approved offer. Both the extra amount and the permitted uses would need explicit agreement.

## Debrief · Choose a conditional route

I would first submit the transparent HK$142,000 request within the permitted window, while retaining the feasible 20-place fallback. Explain the delivery and support costs, not just an overhead percentage. If approval does not arrive by the deadline, decide openly whether the smaller programme merits committing all HK$10,000 available unrestricted funding, or decline.

Twenty places are financially feasible on the supplied terms, but that does not prove their impact exceeds every other possible use of the money. Sixty places are not authorised by donor interest alone. Do not promise participants the larger scope, spend the gift or transfer money from protected services while awaiting approval.

## Debrief · Cost labels do not amend permission

Support work can be necessary even when a donor excludes it. Accounting categories and grant permissions answer different questions: a project-specific support cost might be directly attributable, yet still be excluded by this agreement. Conversely, some funders permit appropriate support costs. Read the actual terms rather than assuming a universal ban on administration.

Record the authorised scope, permitted spending, cost allocation and unrestricted contribution separately. Assign a manager to monitor delivery, support workload and spending. If costs or scope change, secure an agreed funded revision before expanding; never meet a restriction by disguising costs, removing safeguards or relying on unagreed unpaid work.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–5: separate the unrestricted bank balance from available capacity and vote on accepting now. Minutes 5–10: reveal support costs and solve the maximum scope. Minutes 10–15: distinguish the full-cost request from a co-funded gap request. Finish with the approval condition and the smaller programme’s opportunity cost.

SUS-09 concerns a commercial order’s mission contribution; SUS-20 funds maintenance after installation. Here all costs occur within one year and mission fit is stipulated. The distinctive issue is that additional restricted income requires an explicit unrestricted subsidy unless the donor changes both funding and permission. No Hong Kong legal conclusion is inferred from overseas guidance.
