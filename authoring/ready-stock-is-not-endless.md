# The shared resource is open. Will it last?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 215 -->

ECO-18 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Shared-resource depletion · Intertemporal incentives · Verifiable rules

![Two community-equipment coordinators discuss availability beside stored portable equipment cases.](../ready-stock-is-not-endless-scene.jpg)

## Use and replenishment need different questions

Hong Kong’s trawl ban took effect on 31 December 2012 as part of efforts to protect marine resources. AFCD also describes monitoring and other management measures. This is a dated example of managing use of a shared resource, not evidence for a fixed natural replenishment rate. The equipment pool below is an original, simplified analogy, not a model of fisheries.

## Opening decision

Two authorised operating teams, North and South, belong to the same fictional community equipment service. They draw sealed portable power units from a shared, safely managed pool for optional demonstration jobs. Twenty-four units are fully charged this morning. Each team can undertake either four or six one-unit jobs today; each completed job adds HK$100 net contribution after all variable operating and charging costs. There are no promised jobs that must be fulfilled.

The owner permits either a first-come system with no individual allowance beyond each team’s six-job capacity, or an agreed four-unit allowance for each team today. There is enough ready stock for either team’s maximum today. A four-unit allowance gives up two optional jobs per team now. Both rules preserve equal authorised membership, ownership and all safety checks; no outsiders obtain access. Record-keeping capacity is already funded, but a rule needs actual checks rather than a slogan.

Used units return to the pool depleted and cannot be used again today. A technician will report the overnight recharge capacity and tomorrow’s job requirements before the final rule is set. No extra equipment or charging capacity can be added within this two-day exercise. The teams can afford either route, and required community services use a separate protected reserve. This exercise concerns internal equipment use, not agreement between competing businesses on prices or customers.

**Student question:** Would you initially favour first-come use or a four-unit allowance for each team? Explain what you need to know about replenishment and tomorrow’s use before treating unused stock as wasted capacity.

## Round two

The technician confirms that exactly eight depleted units can be safely recharged overnight under either of the possible total withdrawals. Unused charged units remain ready; no units fail or lose charge. Tomorrow’s ready stock therefore equals 24 minus today’s total use plus 8. All depleted units beyond those eight remain unavailable until after the exercise. This is a stipulated two-day capacity rule, not natural resource growth.

Tomorrow, each team has optional demand for up to twelve one-unit jobs at HK$150 net contribution per job. An existing enforceable allocation rule divides tomorrow’s ready stock equally between North and South. Each team can use its full share; there is no borrowing, resale, additional supply, discounting or value difference after day two. All return, charging and inspection costs are already included. The teams evaluate their own two-day contribution, while the owner also evaluates the sum.

With four units each today, tomorrow starts with twenty-four, twelve per team. With six each, it starts with twenty, ten per team. If only one team takes six, tomorrow has twenty-two, eleven per team. A team can gain from taking two more units today while part of the lost future availability falls on the other team. The owner can adopt and monitor a four-unit rule through an agreed checkout log and daily physical count, with the same funded staff; informal restraint alone does not enforce it.

**Student question:** Which rule would you set now? Compare a team’s temptation to take two extra units with the joint two-day result, then specify how withdrawals, replenishment and exceptions should be checked without compromising safety or existing rights.

## Follow the stock across days

Ready stock is a quantity at a point in time; recharge is a flow between periods. Four plus four uses eight and restores eight, leaving twenty-four ready tomorrow. Six plus six uses twelve but restores only eight, leaving twenty. The equipment still exists: the scarce resource is ready-to-use capacity before tomorrow’s work, not ownership of its casing.

## Individual gain can reduce the joint result

| Today: North / South units | Ready tomorrow | North total | South total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 / 4 | 24 | HK$2,200 | HK$2,200 |
| 6 / 4 | 22 | HK$2,250 | HK$2,050 |
| 4 / 6 | 22 | HK$2,050 | HK$2,250 |
| 6 / 6 | 20 | HK$2,100 | HK$2,100 |

Each total is today’s jobs times HK$100 plus half tomorrow’s ready stock times HK$150. When the other team takes four, taking six raises one team’s total from HK$2,200 to HK$2,250, but reduces the other’s to HK$2,050. When the other takes six, taking six also adds HK$50 relative to taking four. Yet both taking six yields HK$4,200 jointly, below HK$4,400 when both take four.

## A rule must change actual use

Under these exact assumptions, the checked four-unit allowance preserves a higher joint two-day contribution. Log each withdrawal, verify the physical count, record the eight recharged units and name who resolves discrepancies. Agree any exception before extra use and account for its effect on tomorrow, rather than letting the first claimant spend everyone’s future capacity. Do not bypass inspection or charge faster than authorised.

## Instructor note

This controlled pool illustrates subtractable use and a shared future cost, not every defining feature of an open-access commons. Excluding outsiders is easy here; classic common-pool resources often make exclusion costly. Shared ownership alone is not a definition. SUS-04 concerns provision of a non-rival walkway; this case follows a depleted and replenished stock. The two-day assumptions do not establish an optimal long-run quota or justify applying these numbers to fish, water or real batteries.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All service, equipment, demand and contribution figures are original fiction. The two operators are internal teams, not competing firms. Pool starts with 24 charged units. Total use is 8, 10 or 12, so eight depleted units are available for recharge in every branch. Tomorrow stock is 24 − total use + 8 = 24, 22 or 20, equally allocated as 12, 11 or 10. Each today unit earns 100 and each tomorrow unit 150, net of all relevant costs. All jobs optional; baseline services separately protected. Two-day individual payoffs are 2,200 / 2,200; 2,250 / 2,050; 2,050 / 2,250; and 2,100 / 2,100; joint HK$4,400 versus HK$4,200. No discounting or terminal value difference. A funded enforceable owner rule differs from an unenforced promise. This is an analogy for shared depletion, not a claim that all private equipment pools are classic common-pool resources. Source research does not validate the artificial recharge function.

- [Ostrom and Gardner: Coping with Asymmetries in the Commons (1993)](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/pdfplus/10.1257/jep.7.4.93). Actual paper examines incentives, rules and irrigation institutions. No empirical productivity figure or model equation is reused.
