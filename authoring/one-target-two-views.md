# Keep the budget. Update the forecast?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 205 -->

ACC-18 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Budgets and forecasts · Rolling planning · Accountability

![Two business-support colleagues discuss a plain planning sheet beside a binder and planner.](../one-target-two-views-scene.jpg)

## A forecast can change without rewriting history

Fast Retailing’s Hong Kong-filed results announcement of 9 April 2026 revised its full-year outlook, explaining changes in first-half results, sales assumptions and exchange-rate assumptions. It presented previous and new forecasts separately and warned that actual results could differ. This is an example of updating expectations, not evidence about its internal staff targets. Our service firm and figures are fictional.

## Opening decision

You are the finance lead of Harbour Studio, a fictional business-support service. It is the end of March. The approved annual target is 100 completed service jobs each month, or 1,200 for the year. January–March actual jobs total 270 against a target of 300. The original target remains the agreed accountability baseline; no one has authorised changing it or any employee’s pay or appraisal terms.

Operations needs a planning pack for the coming quarter. Keeping the annual schedule as the only planning view is simple and familiar. Alternatively, finance can maintain a separate three-month rolling forecast, updating expected jobs, staffing payments and cash receipts each month while preserving the original target. This uses two funded finance hours monthly that would otherwise improve a nonurgent reporting dashboard. Other controls and deadlines remain covered; there is no new software cost.

The original monthly planning assumptions are 100 jobs, HK$1,000 received per completed job in that same month, and HK$80,000 total core operating cash payments. Existing paid capacity safely delivers 100 jobs monthly. Any additional job needs trained, available cover costing HK$200, fully inclusive. Quality, safety, paid working time and all other obligations are protected. These simplified cash assumptions are original, not revenue-recognition rules. Other cash obligations are separately funded and outside this planning envelope.

**Student question:** Which planning pack would you use: the original budget alone or a separate rolling forecast alongside it? Explain what each number would be used to decide.

## Round two

Before the planning pack is issued, an authorised new customer contract is signed for 30 additional jobs in April, 40 in May and 30 in June. These are additional to the unchanged base expectation of 100 jobs monthly. The same HK$1,000 receipt and HK$200 extra-cover cost per job apply. Delivery slots, qualified cover and payment timing have been checked; the forecast assumes performance and collection as agreed, not a risk-free guarantee.

The expected base remains 100 jobs monthly from July to December; no evidence supports extending this temporary contract into those months. January–March actuals remain 270. The controller can separately approve the required cover from a funded HK$30,000 contingency before bookings are made. Updating a spreadsheet does not itself release spending authority. No extra cover has yet been booked.

The manager proposes replacing the annual target with the latest expected year-end total, saying this would make all reporting consistent. The board instead asks for a truthful planning view and an unchanged, clearly labelled original-target comparison, with reasons for differences. Future targets may be reconsidered through the proper process, but that is a separate decision.

**Student question:** What job and cash figures should the April–June forecast show, what cover approval should be requested, and which original-target comparisons must remain visible?

## Debrief: plan the work that is now expected

Forecast jobs are 130, 140 and 130: 400 for April–June. Expected receipts are HK$130,000, HK$140,000 and HK$130,000. Extra cover costs HK$6,000, HK$8,000 and HK$6,000. Core payments remain HK$80,000 monthly, so total payments are HK$86,000, HK$88,000 and HK$86,000. Forecast net cash within this envelope is HK$44,000, HK$52,000 and HK$44,000, or HK$140,000 for the quarter. Request HK$20,000 of cover approval; do not assume the HK$30,000 contingency is all to be spent.

## Keep the accountability view intact

January–March remains 270 actual jobs against 300 targeted: 30 below. Expected full-year jobs now equal 270 actual + 400 forecast for April–June + 600 forecast for July–December = 1,270. That is 70 above the unchanged 1,200 original target, not 1,270 actual jobs and not a new agreed target. The new contract adds 100 forecast jobs, offsetting the 30-job earlier shortfall in the expected annual total without erasing it.

## Roll forward rather than overwrite

At the end of April, record actual April results, explain differences from the saved April forecast, update May and June assumptions and add July to retain a three-month horizon. Preserve dates, versions and assumptions. A forecast helps plan capacity and cash; a target states an agreed ambition or benchmark. Neither automatically changes spending permission or settles individual performance. For example, the causes of the first-quarter shortfall are not supplied.

## Instructor note

Use three labels throughout: original target, actual result and latest forecast. Require students to reconcile 1,270 without adding the new contract twice. Ask why higher forecast cash does not remove the need to authorise trained cover. Discuss the two-hour opportunity cost of updating the pack, rather than assuming reporting is free. This differs from flexing a past cost benchmark for actual activity: the central task is forward planning while keeping a separate agreed baseline.
