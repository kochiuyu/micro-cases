# Five contracts. How many renewal decisions?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 596 -->

ENT-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Customer concentration · Renewal risk · Contribution · Venture sales

![Two colleagues compare documents and coloured contract folders at a studio table.](../multiple-contracts-one-buyer-scene.jpg)

## Reports can show how much comes from the largest customer

A Hong Kong-listed company’s fiscal 2025 report filed with HKEX separately disclosed the revenue shares of its largest and five largest customers. The fictional venture in this exercise uses a different internal measure: cash contribution exposed to a common renewal decision, not a reporting definition.

## Opening decision

Mei runs a subscription service for shop maintenance teams. Her sales team can pursue one of two retention plans, each with five existing contracts up for renewal next year. It cannot pursue both. Treat the contribution estimates in the exhibit as fixed if all proposed contracts renew into that year. The current proposal contains one annual renewal decision per apparent buyer, and Mei has yet to verify whether these are independent.

To protect a core service team, Mei requires at least HK$70,000 contribution if the single largest decision-making customer group does not renew. She chooses the highest planned contribution among sets that pass. The contribution estimates already deduct costs avoided if each respective contract is lost and exclude core team costs. Do not model simultaneous losses by multiple independent groups.

## Evidence

| Proposal | Contracts | Contribution if all renew |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor | Five at HK$24,000 each | HK$120,000 |
| Spread | Five at HK$22,000 each | HK$110,000 |

**Student question:** On the initial assumption of five independent renewal decisions in each set, which proposal passes the downside screen and which would Mei prefer? What evidence would you verify?

## Round two

A due-diligence review finds that four Anchor account names are operated by subsidiaries whose parent makes one binding annual budget decision for all four. The fifth Anchor account is separate. Spread’s five accounts have distinct renewal authority and funding. Contributions and timing do not change.

**Student question:** What should Mei choose now? Calculate each proposal’s contribution exposed to its largest group and what would remain after losing that group.

## Debrief · Test the assumed independence

Initially the largest assumed Anchor loss is HK$24,000, leaving HK$96,000. Spread loses HK$22,000 and leaves HK$88,000. Both pass HK$70,000; with verified independence, prefer Anchor for the extra HK$10,000 planned contribution.

## Debrief · Group contracts by the decision that can stop them

After verification, four Anchor contracts expose 4 × HK$24,000 = HK$96,000 to one parent decision, or 80% of its planned contribution. Anchor could be left with HK$24,000, below the floor. Spread exposes HK$22,000, or 20%, and leaves HK$88,000. Choose Spread.

## Debrief · A different invoice date is not a new buyer

Check who signs and who can refuse renewal, whose budget pays, and what dependencies contracts share. Four invoice records or four branches need not mean four independent sources of contribution. The single-group stress test is not a forecast of how often cancellation occurs.

## Debrief · Use the right denominator

The ratios are shares of planned contribution, not accounting revenue or counts of customers. Do not understate exposure by counting each of the four contracts separately. Independence for this screen also does not eliminate common sector risk.

## Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to calculate under the assumption, 4 to recalculate after diligence, and 5 to discuss what constitutes a common risk. Ask whether distinct ownership always means budgets are unaffected by the same economic shock.
