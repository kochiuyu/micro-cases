# Same calendar. Same chance to show the work?

## Audit what the assessment is actually measuring

The Hong Kong Privacy Commissioner’s human-resources code says employers should collect no more personal data than promotion planning requires and use assessments relevant to advancement. The following review uses invented files before any real promotion round. No actual candidates or leave reasons are involved.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong exhibition contractor is planning a supervisor recruitment round open to eligible internal colleagues. An HR working group compares S, a draft screen requiring at least eight successful lead assignments in the past twelve calendar weeks, with W, a common paid work sample rated against an approved job-relevant rubric. Neither screen has been adopted.

The group tests two fictional files. An assignment is successful if the worker completes all required handover checks. In these synthetic records the assignments are comparable in difficulty. A work sample tests two skills, plan quality and handover verification, each worth 50 points. To meet the exercise standard requires at least 80 overall and at least 35 in each skill.

Approved leave and ordinary workload protections are not at issue. No one is asked to explain an absence or to make up work while on leave. An independent reviewer will see only work evidence needed for the exercise, not family status, health data or private reasons for availability.

## Evidence

| Synthetic file | Lead assignments offered | Successful assignments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R | 12 | 9 |
| T | 8 | 6 |

**Student question:** What does S do to these two files? Compare raw counts with success as a share of offered work. Would you use S as a gate, and what further evidence would you want?

## Round two

The synthetic calendar audit finds that T had four weeks of agreed leave during which the firm did not offer assignments. R’s file had twelve weeks with assignments. No leave reason is disclosed. The eight offered opportunities for T and twelve for R are not a prediction of their future availability or evidence of commitment.

Both files now have the common paid sample, checked against the same task and scored independently. R earns 47 for plan quality and 34 for handover verification. T earns 41 and 41. The handover floor represents an essential competency, not a preference to be traded for a stronger plan.

**Student question:** Which rule would you recommend for the next promotion process? Work both sample results against the stated standard, and explain how the firm can give comparable opportunity without turning leave history into a ranking.

## Debrief · A calendar total mixes two inputs

S advances R because nine is at least eight, and excludes T because six is not. Both succeed on 75% of the comparable offered assignments: 9/12 and 6/8. That does not prove equal ability or identical evidence strength; the denominators differ and the samples are small. Do not infer commitment from missing opportunities.

## Debrief · An adequate total can hide a critical gap

R scores 47 + 34 = 81 but misses the 35-point handover floor. T scores 41 + 41 = 82 and meets both floors and the total threshold. The simulated pass is evidence from one task, not an automatic promotion or a reason to favour people who took leave. Never adjust scores using leave category or protected information.

## Debrief · Offer the same route to demonstrate readiness

Recommend a pre-announced job-relevant sample open to all eligible applicants on paid time, with approved adjustments and equivalent rescheduling where necessary. Set the rubric and critical floors before reviewing any files. Keep evaluators independent, record reasons and provide a correction route. Recheck whether the sample predicts the specific work before relying on it in a real process.

## Instructor note

Have students calculate the count and rate before revealing the calendar audit. Ask them to distinguish minimum evidence, comparable access to assessment and automatic selection. This fictional process evaluation does not determine anyone’s eligibility, employment status or legal rights.
