# A bigger order. More mission?

Case 106 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Social enterprise · Mission fit · Earned income

![Adult participants and a coach check a fabric folder at a sewing-workshop table with patterns and folded fabric.](../bigger-order-more-mission-scene.jpg)

## In the news

In a 21 May 2025 reply, Hong Kong’s Government described assessing social-enterprise proposals for both social objectives and prospects of becoming self-financing.

This is context, not evidence about the fictional workshop below. Its choices are not governed by that programme’s grant conditions.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong sewing social enterprise helps adult work returnees rebuild confidence and practical experience through paid, supported work. Eight adult trainees are already employed in a part-time, limited-hours programme on agreed pay, hours and support; this is not eight full-time jobs.

Next month, the team has six additional coach half-days beyond its funded core programme. These have not been promised as named lessons. It can retain flexible small-product work and broader coached practice, or replace that work with a commercial order for fabric folders.

Both options fit safe production capacity and available cash. Neither cuts wages, promised core coaching, rest, safety checks or existing customer commitments. Participation in the specific learning activities is voluntary and discussed with the trainees.

## Evidence

| Same-month budget | A · Small-product schedule | B · Folder order |
|---|---:|---:|
| Forecast receipts | HK$20,000 | HK$40,000 |
| Complete budgeted costs | HK$18,000 | HK$32,000 |
| Budgeted surplus | HK$2,000 | HK$8,000 |
| Extra coached-practice capacity | Six half-days | Four half-days |

Costs include materials, wages, core support, coach time, premises and agreed delivery. B replaces work rather than adding to it; two extra coach half-days go to client coordination and final quality review. No unpaid overtime is assumed.

The team can make the folders but has not yet established whether the four coached half-days can involve trainees in useful learning rather than observation. All actual surplus stays in the enterprise for its mission; no future training has been promised from these forecasts.

**Student question:** Would you choose A’s broader practice schedule or B’s larger commercial order? How does your choice advance the mission, and what must you know about the work before treating larger surplus as a better social result?

## Round two

The coach now maps a feasible sequence of fabric measurement, preparation and checking tasks to the trainees’ demonstrated skills and agreed learning goals. Appropriate task supervision and safety training remain.

The customer accepts staged delivery and supervised trainee participation with the same product specification. The four half-days can provide supported participation in fulfilling a customer order, with feedback, rather than observation alone. The budget and deadline still hold.

All eight trainees have been consulted and are willing to participate. The plan still forgoes two optional skill-development half-days from A. Client coordination and quality-review time cannot also be counted as trainee sessions.

No improvement in skills, confidence or subsequent employment has yet been measured. A remains available if broader development is the priority now.

**Student question:** Does the order now fit the mission well enough to choose B, or would you retain A? Name the direct mission contribution, the opportunity cost and one condition that would change your choice. What should happen to any actual surplus?

## Separate the budget gain from the mission claim

A forecasts HK$20,000 − HK$18,000 = HK$2,000 surplus. B forecasts HK$40,000 − HK$32,000 = HK$8,000. The surplus difference is HK$6,000, not the HK$20,000 difference in receipts.

These are budgets, not realised results. Even if achieved, a larger surplus alone would not show what participants learned. All surplus stays with the mission, but a future use still needs a deliberate allocation.

## Recognise two ways trading can support the purpose

B’s mapped tasks now offer supported participation in real customer work. That is a direct connection between this order and the workshop’s stated purpose. Its forecast surplus could also support mission activity later.

Those routes are plausible, not measured outcomes. Funding valuable activity elsewhere can also be legitimate; every commercial task need not itself be training. What matters here is the whole plan, including protected support and the participants’ experience.

## Keep the forgone practice visible

B leaves four extra coached half-days rather than six. The remaining two are used for client coordination and quality review. The workable customer arrangement has not created extra coach capacity.

A protects a wider range of practice and remains financially viable in its supplied budget. Choosing it is defensible when those learning opportunities matter more now. Choosing B is defensible when its task fit and supported participation justify the narrower range.

HK$6,000 divided by two is not a valuation of a learning session or a person. Session counts do not establish proportional social benefit.

## Protect participation as the order proceeds

If choosing B, keep the four structured sessions and named coach, check workload and quality, and ask participants what they are learning. Review the choice if commercial demands begin replacing meaningful participation or promised support.

Safety, wages and rights are not bargaining chips for surplus; concerns need an immediate appropriate response. Record how any realised surplus is used for the mission without promising an employment outcome from the money alone.

Doing A’s six extra sessions as well as B’s client work would need additional coach capacity. That is not a free option in this month’s plan.

## Instructor note

Take a vote before the task-mapping reveal. Ask whether the first recommendation depends on useful work participation, future funding or both. Afterward require one direct mission contribution and one real opportunity cost.

The eight trainees are adult employees with protected terms and voluntary learning participation. Do not infer health conditions, eligibility or uniform needs. Willingness and feasible tasks are enabling evidence, not improved skills or job placement.

This is a trading-versus-mission schedule decision, not accreditation, a grant-eligibility puzzle or a claim that reinvestment excuses poor work. Both routes have credible purposes; no actual employment contract is changed.
