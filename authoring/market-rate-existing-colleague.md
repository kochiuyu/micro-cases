# A market-rate recruit. An overlooked colleague?

## In the news

On 28 September 2026, Hong Kong’s Census and Statistics Department reported a 3.4% year-on-year rise in the nominal wage index for the selected industry sections in June 2026. The index covers employees up to supervisory level, not a salary quotation for every job.

Our fictional firm faces its own hiring and pay-structure decision. Its salaries, candidate and budget below are invented. The aggregate increase does not establish an appropriate offer or an individual employee’s entitlement.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong office-services firm has a vacant experienced account-coordinator role. It has HK$24,000 a month reserved for that vacancy and a separate HK$6,000 recurring monthly pay-improvement pool. The vacancy budget may fund only the recruit; the pool may fund an offer above HK$24,000 or increases for incumbents. The approved recurring ceiling cannot be raised in this exercise. Other employment costs are separately funded and do not change the comparison.

Four current colleagues each earn HK$24,000 monthly and share the coordinator title. Their written duties have not been compared recently. Benefits and contracted hours are comparable. A screened candidate meets the vacancy’s full requirements and will accept HK$30,000, but not less, by Friday. The firm’s role-matched recruitment evidence supports that offer for this vacancy; the general wage index does not. No alternative qualified candidate is currently available at HK$24,000.

A hires at HK$30,000, using the whole HK$6,000 pool. It restores capacity for new client accounts, but leaves incumbents’ pay unchanged. B delays hiring and uses the pool for an initial adjustment to incumbents: before checking roles, the owner considers HK$1,500 each. Both choices preserve current pay, benefits, safe workloads and existing client commitments. Without a recruit, the firm defers new accounts rather than forcing unpaid overtime. Their lost contribution and staff departure probabilities are not supplied, so a precise profit ranking is unavailable.

The owner can explain a review process but cannot promise unfunded later increases. The next funding review is in six weeks; additional money and the candidate’s future availability are uncertain. No offer or adjustment has yet been made. This is a discretionary budget exercise, not permission to postpone a legal or contractual pay obligation. Any identified breach must be separately resolved, not balanced against recruitment.

## Round two

Before Friday, a documented role review checks actual work with the employees, not just job titles. Two incumbents independently manage full client accounts, resolve non-routine problems and carry the same responsibility as the vacancy. Their relevant proficiency and performance are comparable to the screened candidate’s. The other two process standard requests and escalate exceptions; they do not hold the same account responsibility. No overlooked responsibility or different working condition offsets this distinction in the supplied review. This does not prove that their current pay is appropriate; they need their own role-matched comparison.

All four have comparable hours and benefits. The two equivalent-role incumbents each earn HK$6,000 less than the proposed recruit. Bringing both to HK$30,000 requires HK$12,000 recurring monthly, not HK$6,000. B can instead raise each of those two to HK$27,000 now, using the pool and leaving a HK$3,000 gap per person against the recruitment rate. That is a first stage, not a funded promise to close the rest. The other two retain HK$24,000 pending their separate role review. A still uses the entire pool for the recruit, leaving no current adjustment. Combining A with those two HK$3,000 increases exceeds the pool.

No one has threatened to resign, and no retention effect has been measured. Candidate availability, the vacancy restriction and the six-week review remain unchanged. Reconsider the options using the verified job comparison; a common title alone no longer supports treating all four gaps as identical.

**Student question:** Would you hire now or fund the targeted first-stage increases? State the monthly allocation, the remaining comparable-role gap, and what you would explain to both groups of incumbents without promising money you do not have.

## Compare work before comparing numbers

External competitiveness asks what the firm must offer to attract suitable candidates for this job. Internal equity asks whether pay relationships across its own jobs and people are defensible. A common title is a starting clue, not a completed job evaluation. The supplied review identifies two close comparators; it does not rank people’s personal worth or justify ignoring the other two roles.

## Reconcile the recurring budget

A: HK$24,000 vacancy budget + HK$6,000 pool = HK$30,000 recruitment salary. Incumbents stay at HK$24,000. B: 2 × HK$3,000 = HK$6,000 of increases; the two comparable incumbents reach HK$27,000. The unused HK$24,000 vacancy allocation cannot be converted to raises under the stipulated budget rule. Matching both to HK$30,000 costs 2 × HK$6,000 = HK$12,000 a month. Hiring and the staged adjustments together need HK$12,000 of flexible funding, twice the available pool.

## A defensible choice is not a painless choice

A can be defended if restoring new-account capacity now takes priority, but market evidence does not make the comparable-role gap disappear. Acknowledge it and specify a genuine review date and criteria. B can be defended if addressing that relationship first takes priority, accepting deferred growth and possible loss of the candidate. A staged increase does not guarantee retention or establish that HK$27,000 is a final fair rate. Without quantified business and retention consequences, neither option is a proven profit maximum.

## Explain the rule, protect the person

Explain the role factors and funding constraint without publishing identifiable salary records or private performance details. Give employees a confidential way to correct missing duties or an inaccurate comparison. Tell the narrower-role colleagues that their work will be assessed against appropriate comparators, not that they are less valuable people. Do not call a future review a guaranteed raise. Budget scarcity never excuses an established statutory or contractual violation.

## Instructor note

Allow three minutes for an initial choice and identification of missing comparators. Reveal the role review, then ask pairs to reconcile A and B before voting again. Challenge “everyone has the same title” and “the market decides everything”. Accept either bounded priority with an honest allocation and explanation; reject a supposed hybrid that spends the pool twice. Ask what role-matched pay evidence and business consequences would be needed for the next review, without inventing resignations or profits. This is pay-structure reasoning, not individual employment advice or a discrimination adjudication.
