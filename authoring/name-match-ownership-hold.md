## A name alert is not the whole review

Sanctions screening asks more than whether two names look alike. List identity, the relevant regime, ownership and the proposed activity can raise different questions. Official guidance distinguishes these checks. This fictional training exercise supplies an internal hold rule; it does not determine whether any real person or shipment is legally restricted.

## Opening decision

You coordinate a training exercise at fictional Singapore stationery exporter Clearleaf. An unsent order for ordinary paper folders has triggered a screening alert on the buyer’s adult contact. The contact’s displayed name resembles entry L1 in the invented training list. No real person, country designation or restricted-party allegation is used.

The folders remain in Clearleaf’s warehouse. No payment, shipment or contract acceptance occurs during the exercise. Existing legal obligations remain paramount. The compliance lead must determine applicable regimes, goods, destination, end use, parties and any reporting duties before an actual transaction; this classroom record cannot provide that clearance.

Route A gives the trained internal reviewer two paid hours today to examine authorised identity and ownership evidence. Route B uses an approved specialist, available tomorrow, for a fixed S$400 fee. Both preserve the hold, use the same records and document reasons. Tomorrow is within the commercial deadline; no legal deadline may be postponed.

A may close an identity alert only when verified evidence establishes a different person. Any unresolved identity, ownership concern or legal interpretation must go to B. B is a review route, not a waiver or guaranteed permission. A’s two hours displace optional catalogue work; B avoids that diversion but costs S$400.

The exercise’s internal rule also requires specialist review when a training-listed entity directly holds more than half of the buyer’s voting rights. Clearing the contact alert does not cancel that ownership hold. This invented escalation threshold is not Singapore law, a universal sanctions test or a substitute for checking other forms of ownership and control.

## Round two

The authorised document pack is now verified for this exercise. The contact and L1 have distinct teaching identity keys, different birth years and different documented histories. A qualified reviewer confirms they are different people after considering the complete available records. The teaching keys are synthetic labels, not passport or government identifier numbers.

The buyer itself is not named in the training list. However, its verified current voting register shows entity O1 directly holds sixty of its one hundred equal votes. O1 is an exact, independently verified match to training-list entry L2. The remaining forty votes belong to an unrelated, unlisted owner.

These identity and direct voting findings are stipulated correct, not inferred from spelling or one differing address. The sixty-vote holding existed before the order; no restructuring, routing change or concealment is proposed. There are no missing votes, delegated voting arrangements or indirect layers in this simplified register.

The internal majority-ownership escalation rule therefore applies. The specialist has not yet determined the actual legal position or any required action beyond the continuing hold. Both routes still have capacity. The task is to record the distinct findings and assign the next review, not to clear an export or declare a real sanctions violation.

**Student question:** Which alert can be closed, which concern remains open, and who must handle it? Calculate O1’s voting share and write a short internal status note that does not confuse a false name match with transaction clearance.

## Debrief · Close only the question answered

The contact is not L1 under the verified classroom findings. Close that specific identity alert with the evidence, reviewer and date recorded. Do not keep labelling the contact restricted merely because a name resembles an entry. Equally, a single difference in spelling, address or birth year is not a universal clearance rule.

The evidence establishes a different person, not that every participant in the transaction is unrestricted. Keep identity resolution separate from the buyer’s ownership review and from the legal analysis of goods, destination, activity and applicable regime. A list-screen result is one input into that process, not a certificate of lawful trade.

## Debrief · Check the relationship, not just the list row

O1 holds 60 ÷ 100 = 60% of the buyer’s votes. This exceeds the fictional internal rule’s one-half threshold and requires B. The buyer’s absence from the training list does not remove the supplied ownership concern. Do not multiply unrelated shares, aggregate invented owners or substitute an economic stake for the stated voting rights.

The sixty-percent figure establishes the classroom escalation condition, not a general legal finding. Real regimes can use different ownership, aggregation and control tests, and an applicable prohibition may extend beyond named parties. The specialist must identify the relevant law and evidence; students must not select whichever foreign threshold yields a convenient result.

## Debrief · Escalation is not a permission slip

After the reveal, refer the ownership issue to B and retain the order hold. If A has already completed its two hours, that time is spent; the S$400 specialist fee is still needed. Starting directly with B would cost S$400 without A’s diversion. No evidence quantifies which route normally resolves more cases cheaply.

Neither the commercial deadline nor an adviser’s appointment authorises release. The compliance lead must arrange any mandatory action within its lawful timeframe, rather than waiting until tomorrow if that would be too late. Maintain secure records and follow the authorised escalation procedure; do not contact outside parties or transmit identity documents as part of this classroom exercise.

## Debrief · Use a status note with separate conclusions

Example: “Contact alert L1 closed as a verified different person. Buyer ownership review remains open: O1 matches L2 and holds 60 of 100 votes. Internal escalation condition met. Order remains held; specialist review assigned. No legal clearance or finding of a violation has been made.”

The record should identify the list and version, applicable-rule question, evidence provenance, ownership date, reviewer, unresolved issues and next responsible person. Restrict access to necessary authorised information. Preserve corrections rather than erasing the original alert, and use a clear review deadline without implying that expiry automatically releases the order.

## Instructor note

GVR-13 concerns a customer’s meaningful challenge to a refund flag. IS-24 concerns matching customer records to calculate retention. Here identity can be resolved correctly while a separate ownership relationship still requires compliance escalation. Ask students to state both conclusions rather than voting only “match” or “no match”.

All names, keys, votes, routes and costs are invented. The rule is an internal teaching stipulation, not a description of Singapore sanctions law.
