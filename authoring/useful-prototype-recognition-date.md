## A project’s promise is not its recognition date

Accounting guidance distinguishes research from development and requires evidence before development expenditure becomes an intangible asset. A useful prototype does not by itself settle every condition. The project and figures below are original fiction, using an explicitly supplied classroom policy rather than asserting which reporting rules every Shenzhen company must follow.

## Opening decision

You are controller of fictional Pearl Signal, a Shenzhen design business developing an original sensor-control design for ordinary warehouse equipment. The asset being assessed is the identifiable design and associated software, which the company controls and can license separately, not the physical prototype or employees’ knowledge.

The team has incurred and paid CNY260,000 by the 30 June reporting date. The project manager proposes capitalising all of it because the prototype now works and customers are interested. Capitalising means recording cost as an asset, not raising money or recording sales. No external financial statements have been issued.

Use this classroom policy: research expenditure is expensed when incurred. Development expenditure becomes an asset only from the date all six conditions can be demonstrated together. Before that date it is expensed, even if managers expect success. Expenditure properly expensed before qualification is not later restored to the asset.

The six conditions are: a feasible technical path to completion; an intention to complete and use or sell the design; the ability to use or sell it; probable future economic benefits; adequate technical, financial and other completion resources; and reliable measurement of the project’s attributable development spending.

For this exercise, the asset definition and control requirements are satisfied. From 1 March, documented intention, ability, resources and reliable project costing are established. A supported market-and-margin assessment also demonstrates probable economic benefits then. The unresolved issue is when technical feasibility was first demonstrated, not whether a hopeful sales conversation counts as proof.

January–February work searched alternative approaches and is classified as research. From March the team developed one chosen design. All listed later costs are directly attributable work of a type eligible once recognition conditions are met. There are no marketing, training, general administration, financing, tax or separately saleable prototype costs hidden in the totals.

**Student question:** Would you accept the proposed CNY260,000 asset? Separate research from development and identify the dated evidence needed before deciding which development costs qualify.

## Round two

The March demonstration operated only under narrow laboratory conditions. Test logs showed unresolved failures across the intended operating range. Engineers could not then establish a feasible path to a completed design. The manager’s description of this as technically ready was optimism, not a supported feasibility conclusion.

At the start of 15 May, the engineering team completed and documented the necessary feasibility work. The design had passed the tests required to demonstrate that completion was technically feasible. This is stipulated to be the first date that criterion was met; no earlier qualifying evidence was overlooked or merely filed late.

The other five conditions remained satisfied from 1 March onward. Consequently, all six first coincide at the start of 15 May. The ledger separately identifies CNY30,000 incurred before that point and CNY90,000 afterward. Do not spread May spending evenly by days or backdate the milestone to the start of the month.

At 30 June, routine integration and final preparation are still underway, so the design is not yet available for its intended use. This does not reverse the demonstrated feasibility of completing it. Under the supplied policy amortisation begins only when available for use. The required recoverability review has found no impairment charge for this exercise.

Costs before the qualifying date were initially recorded as expenses in the internal ledger. The manager asks to transfer those earlier costs into the asset now that the project looks more promising. There is no acquisition, change of accounting framework or prior-period error in the stipulated facts.

**Student question:** What asset cost and project expense belong at 30 June? State the recognition date, explain whether earlier expenses can be reinstated, and distinguish technical feasibility from readiness for use.

## Debrief · Find the first complete evidence set

The qualifying date is 15 May, not the March market assessment. Probable economic benefits and technical feasibility are separate conditions: meeting one cannot substitute for the other. An approved budget, named development phase or working demonstration also cannot eliminate the remaining conditions.

This is a judgment based on the facts supplied, not a universal rule that a particular prototype test creates an asset. In real work, review what the evidence demonstrated when spending occurred. A report filed later could raise a different evidence question; here the case explicitly rules that out.

## Debrief · Divide the cost at that date

Capitalise the CNY90,000 incurred from 15 May through 30 June. Expense CNY60,000 of research plus CNY80,000 of March–April development plus CNY30,000 of early-May development: CNY170,000. The reconciliation is CNY90,000 asset + CNY170,000 expense = CNY260,000 total incurred and paid.

The CNY110,000 of development spending before 15 May is not research merely because it fails recognition. It remains development expenditure expensed before all conditions were demonstrated. Conversely, calling the later work development does not alone justify capitalisation; the complete evidence set is what changes recognition.

## Debrief · Do not restore a successful past

Keep the CNY170,000 previously expensed out of the asset. Later success does not retrospectively qualify those costs under the supplied policy. Starting capitalisation on 1 March would produce CNY200,000 of asset cost, overstating the proper CNY90,000 by CNY110,000. Capitalising all CNY260,000 would overstate it by CNY170,000.

With no amortisation or impairment charge in this exercise, capitalising all costs would also understate project expense and overstate pre-tax profit by CNY170,000 relative to the correct treatment. Cash paid remains CNY260,000 either way. Recognition does not create cash, certify market value or guarantee future sales.

## Debrief · Feasible to finish is not finished

Technical feasibility concerns whether completion can be achieved; availability for use concerns whether preparation has reached the required operating condition. The 15 May milestone can permit recognition while routine completion work continues. The asset’s CNY90,000 carrying amount at 30 June is not proof it is ready for customer use.

Once available for use, the applicable amortisation policy must be applied; subsequent recoverability still needs review. No useful life or future expense amount is supplied here. Do not invent a five-year life, wait automatically for the first sale or describe the recognised cost as permanently protected from expense.

## Debrief · Preserve the evidence, not the target profit

Retain the technical test record, commercial assessment, completion plan, committed resources and dated cost records. Document why all conditions first coincide on 15 May. Have finance and engineering reconcile their descriptions before reporting; a project manager’s confidence is relevant context, not authority to choose a preferred recognition date.

Expensing early work does not mean it was wasteful or should be abandoned. Commercial investment decisions and accounting recognition answer different questions. Likewise, this policy requires eligible post-qualification costs to be capitalised; expensing everything merely to appear cautious would not apply the supplied policy correctly.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: reject or defend the whole-project proposal and request evidence. Minutes 4–9: locate the first date all conditions hold. Minutes 9–14: calculate CNY90,000 and CNY170,000, then diagnose a 1 March start. Minutes 14–18: explain why feasibility, readiness, cash and accounting value are different.

ACC-07 separates components of an acquired machine package, including training. Here cost type is stipulated eligible after qualification, and no machine is being acquired. The issue is the evidence-based start date for an internally developed intangible and the prohibition on reinstating properly expensed earlier expenditure.
