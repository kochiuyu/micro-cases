# The board has information. Does it have time to challenge?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 369 -->

GVR-31 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Board information · Constructive challenge · Decision timing

![Directors in a fictional Hong Kong boardroom discuss a thick paper pack and a concise briefing sheet beside a wall clock, with no legible writing.](../information-without-challenge-scene.jpg)

## Receiving papers is not the same as deliberating

HKEX’s Corporate Governance Guide for Boards and Directors identifies the quality and timing of board papers, effective meeting practices and time devoted to risk as matters for reviewing board effectiveness. These are connected questions, not simply a count of documents delivered.

The organisation and proposal below are fictional. Published governance guidance supplies context, not a legal conclusion about this unlisted company or a mandatory number of minutes for a decision.

## Opening decision

You chair the board of fictional unlisted Hong Kong company Harbour Workspaces. Management wants approval for a major fit-out project. All seven directors received the same 140-page pack 18 hours before this meeting. A two-page executive summary recommends approval; supporting analysis is in appendices. There has been no earlier board discussion of this proposal.

The agenda allows 12 minutes for the item. Management says the pack is complete and the project has been discussed extensively inside the executive team. The chair can rearrange today’s agenda or convene another meeting. Choose whether to put approval to a vote today or require a focused discussion before doing so.

Under the company’s fictional internal policy, project approval would authorise management to sign the fit-out contract. Calling it approval in principle would have the same effect unless the board explicitly withholds that authority. The exercise concerns how to prepare a decision, not whether the project is commercially best.

## Evidence

| Item | Information available |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed fit-out spending | HK$12 million |
| Supplier quote | Held unchanged for another 10 calendar days |
| Next available focused board meeting | In 5 calendar days; all directors can attend |
| Present operations | Can continue safely and normally during that interval |
| Alternatives | Full fit-out, phased fit-out or continue present operations |

No deposit is required to keep the quote open. Waiting five days creates no stated price penalty or lost sales in this fictional situation. No law, safety emergency or external deadline requires approval today. Directors have not yet tested the main assumptions together.

**Student question:** Put approval to a vote today, or require a focused discussion first? Identify what receipt of the complete pack establishes and what it does not.

## Round two

A director asks about the funding assumptions. Page 118 contains a scenario in which delayed tenant payments would require **HK$4 million of additional cash** during the fit-out. The executive summary does not mention this scenario. The available uncommitted cash buffer after the planned project payments is **HK$3 million**. No additional facility is committed.

The scenario has no assigned probability. Its timing and assumptions have not been independently checked. The finance director can explain it at the meeting in five days. Management has not compared the phased fit-out and current-operation alternatives on the same cash-timing basis.

All three alternatives remain available. There is not enough time left today to examine the scenario and alternatives properly. The chair can request a revised brief tomorrow and directors’ questions two days before the focused meeting. Ordinary operations may continue under existing budgets, but no fit-out contract or deposit is authorised without a fresh board decision.

**Student question:** Proceed with approval, reject the project, or defer the decision for five days? Specify the briefing and discussion needed, and what management may do before the board returns.

## Debrief · Delivery is only the starting point

A complete pack gives directors access to material. It does not show that the crucial issues are visible, understood or tested. Executive-team discussion does not substitute for board deliberation. Page count, hours of notice and attendance are inputs, not proof of an effective decision process.

In round one, require a focused discussion before approving. Rearranging today’s agenda could be defensible if directors genuinely have enough preparation and time. Do not treat 18 hours or 12 minutes as a universal legal threshold: adequacy depends on the proposal and the work already done.

## Debrief · A buried scenario changes the discussion

The disclosed downside would require HK$4 million against a HK$3 million available buffer: a **HK$1 million gap** under that scenario. This is not an expected loss and does not establish that the downside will occur. It does show a question the summary should have made easy to find.

The board needs to challenge cash timing, the evidence behind payment assumptions, possible responses and how the alternatives compare. The presence of the scenario in an appendix is not a substitute for discussing it. Its omission from the summary alone does not prove deception or a legal breach.

## Debrief · Defer the decision, not the responsibility

**Defer approval for five days** on the revealed facts. There is an unresolved material question, insufficient discussion time today and a feasible return date before the quote expires. Immediate rejection is not compelled either: the case does not establish that the project is unattractive.

Ask for a short decision brief stating the requested decision, alternatives, key assumptions, base and downside cash timing, funding gaps, uncertainties and management’s recommendation. Link it clearly to the supporting appendices; a short summary should organise evidence, not replace it. Circulate the revision tomorrow and collect directors’ questions two days before the meeting.

## Debrief · Make the next meeting useful

Reserve a substantial agenda block for questions rather than another long presentation. Have the finance director explain the scenario and ask directors to test the alternatives and unresolved assumptions. End with an explicit decision, or named outstanding questions and a further timetable if the evidence is still inadequate. Neither a revised paper nor a fixed number of minutes guarantees approval readiness.

Meanwhile, ordinary operations continue within existing budgets. Withhold authority for the fit-out contract and deposit. This prevents an administrative label such as approval in principle from turning unfinished deliberation into a commitment. The interim boundary supports the timing choice; it is not the main learning objective.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: show the initial setting and take the first vote. Ask whether the number of pages demonstrates readiness. Minutes 4–8: reveal the appendix scenario and ask what belongs in the decision brief. Minutes 8–13: choose a next step and design the focused meeting. Minutes 13–18: challenge whether postponement has a purpose, owner and return date.

Ask what would change if a genuine deadline made delay costly; do not invent such urgency for this case. Accept same-day discussion in round one only with a credible preparation-and-time argument. In round two, the stated lack of time removes that option. More time helps only if the board uses it to test decision-relevant evidence.

Do not ask students to determine a legal violation or calculate an expected project return. No probability or complete valuation is provided. Unlike GVR-19’s post-decision conditions and PPL-17’s group voice, the central mechanism here is making material information usable before board approval.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All company facts, amounts, timing and internal authority rules are fictional teaching assumptions. HKEX issuer guidance, HKEX’s own practices, UK corporate-governance guidance, UK public-body review guidance and OECD principles have different scopes; none is represented as universally binding on this unlisted company. No legal breach, director liability or deliberate concealment is inferred. The cash scenario has no assigned likelihood and is not a project valuation. The lesson is information architecture and time for board challenge before approval, not drafting an actual resolution or giving transaction advice.

Review criteria include timing of papers, quality of discussion and constructive challenge. Its public-body requirements do not apply to the fictional Hong Kong company.
