# Fill the vacancy. Explain the difficult part?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 137 -->

PPL-12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Realistic job previews · Expectation alignment · Recruitment communication

![An event coordinator explains the role to two adult candidates while a colleague demonstrates a closing handover with badge trays and lanyards.](../explain-the-difficult-part-scene.jpg)

## In the news

On 15 July 2026, Hong Kong’s Labour Department announced a two-day job fair offering more than 2,100 vacancies, with applications and possible interviews on site. Recruitment creates an opportunity to exchange information, not just collect applications.

The announcement does not evaluate job previews or explain employee departures. Harbour Events and all circumstances below are fictional.

## Opening decision

**Fictional classroom situation.** Harbour Events needs two event coordinators. Six shortlisted adults meet its entry requirements. Before offers, the manager must choose the principal job-preview format for all six. Selection uses separate, unchanged job-related criteria; the preview is for candidates to judge the role, not a secret test.

Both options disclose the same accurate pay, duties, locations, hours, rest arrangements and scheduling terms, including two rostered evening finishes in a typical week and occasional consecutive evening finishes. A representative four-week roster is supplied in advance. Evening work replaces daytime hours; it is not unpaid extra work. No terms are being concealed or changed.

The role combines satisfying live-event coordination with quieter checking, packing records and closing handovers after guests leave. The firm can afford either package, but one coordinator must lead it while preparing next month’s events. Both can finish before the same offer date; there is no expiring-offer pressure. The extra staff time for B displaces internal planning, not safety work or client commitments.

## Evidence

| Package | What candidates receive | Total for six candidates |
|---|---|---|
| A: structured briefing | 90-minute discussion of duties, representative roster, busy/quiet periods and questions with a current coordinator | HK$1,500; 2 staff-hours |
| B: paid observation | Three-hour guided observation of an evening event’s final stage and closing handover, roster discussion and questions | HK$6,000; 8 staff-hours |

Totals include preparation and applicable payments/expenses. No productive work. B includes paid time/travel, safety arrangements and a paid accessible equivalent with the same demonstration/information and no selection penalty. Both disclose identical accurate terms in advance, allow questions and withdrawal, and require no private reasons.

**Student question:** Choose A or B for this intake. What extra understanding would justify B’s additional cost and staff time, and what evidence would make you reconsider your choice?

## Round two

The manager reviews the three most recent voluntary departures from the same role. All three recruits had met the entry requirements. Two reported that they had understood the number of evening finishes but not the rhythm: consecutive evenings, followed by detailed closing work after the visible event ended. The third left for an unrelated opportunity. These are three accounts, not proof of what caused every departure.

In a separate, voluntary clarification discussion, two of the six current candidates say they had pictured evening work ending when guests left. Another says seeing how the closing handover works would be useful; the remaining three raise no question. Silence does not establish understanding. No candidate is ranked or rejected for asking, and no private explanation is requested.

Both previews can now explicitly walk through the same representative consecutive-evening sequence and distinguish guest departure from staff finish. A can do this through the roster and questions; B adds the observed closing routine. No retention or acceptance results from either package exist. The extra cost and staff time remain unchanged.

**Student question:** Keep or change your preview format? Specify how it will make the working pattern clear, and name one measure beyond offer acceptance that would show whether expectations were better aligned.

## Disclosure is not the same as understanding

The written terms matter, but a candidate can read them accurately and picture the job differently. Here the gap concerns the sequence and character of work, not a missing contractual fact. A useful preview connects the roster to an ordinary working day, including its satisfying and less attractive parts.

## Choose the communication method, not the candidate

B makes the closing routine visible and creates a shared reference for questions. Its additional resources are HK$4,500 and six staff-hours. That is an investment in clearer expectations, not a demonstrated saving from lower turnover.

A remains credible: a current coordinator can explain a representative sequence clearly and invite questions without requiring a three-hour visit. B is stronger if observation communicates something discussion cannot readily convey. Neither route may disguise working conditions or penalise someone for choosing not to proceed.

## Do not turn the preview into a hidden test

Ask candidates whether the explanation is clear, not whether they can impress a supervisor during observation. Assess entry skills separately. Do not score enthusiasm, willingness to reveal private circumstances or choice of the accessible equivalent. A preview is also not permission to normalise unsafe work or leave avoidable workplace problems unaddressed.

## Measure the match, not just signatures

Track whether candidates can describe the scheduling pattern in their own words, using feedback to correct the explanation rather than rank them. After hiring, ask whether the actual pattern matched what was presented. Record early departures and voluntary reasons alongside acceptances and resource cost.

Fewer acceptances could reflect informed withdrawal, but do not automatically mean success. More acceptances do not establish a better match. Small cohorts, different applicants and other job conditions prevent a simple before-and-after count from proving that the preview caused retention.

## Instructor note

Allow 3 minutes for the initial choice, 3 for the clarification reveal and 4–6 for revised designs and measures. Ask what A can communicate well, what B adds and what would justify six extra staff-hours. Do not frame truthful disclosure as optional. Distinguish candidate self-assessment from employer selection and later onboarding. The three departures and six candidates are fictional, nonexperimental evidence; there is no retention forecast or financial break-even calculation.
