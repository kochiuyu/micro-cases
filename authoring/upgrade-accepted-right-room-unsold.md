# An upgrade accepted. The right room left unsold?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 396 -->

HSP-33 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Paid upgrades · Room-type opportunity cost · Guest commitments

![Two fictional adult hotel colleagues compare a standard-room and two-bedroom-suite floor plan at a quiet reception desk, with two distinct room keys and no guest personal data.](../upgrade-accepted-right-room-unsold-scene.jpg)

## An empty room of which type?

A vacant premium room is not necessarily surplus inventory. An upgrade changes which room type remains available for the next request. Hotel revenue-management teaching and operating systems therefore distinguish room categories, live availability and demand. The hotel, room configuration, guest enquiries, probabilities and money below are fictional.

## Opening decision

You manage Lantern Quay, a fictional Hong Kong hotel, at 3 p.m. All stays in this exercise cover tonight only. All rooms are safe, inspected and ready. Existing bookings are fully protected; no overbooking, cancellation, room failure or guest relocation is permitted.

One arriving adult guest has a confirmed standard room S. The only uncommitted room is premium suite P. S has one sleeping room; P has two separate sleeping rooms within one suite. P is one saleable accommodation unit, not two separately saleable rooms. All other accommodation is committed.

The guest has indicated they would voluntarily accept P for an additional HK$500, including all charges, if offered now. P adds HK$100 in avoidable servicing costs compared with S. The guest’s original payment and standard servicing costs are common to both choices. No upgrade has been offered, accepted or paid.

A offers and confirms the upgrade now, then releases S for sale. B checks the guest into their booked S and keeps P available. Either room meets this guest’s stated requirements. Declining or not receiving an upgrade changes no original right, service or price. No later upgrade offer is available in this simplified comparison.

There is just one possible later booking for tonight. Initially the forecast treats this party as flexible between S and P: if it books, it buys whichever is available. The supplied booking probability is 50% under either allocation. Otherwise neither room receives further demand. This is an assumption, not a measured forecast.

A later sale contributes HK$600 in S or HK$1,000 in P, after all attributable costs, fees and servicing. The amounts include the complete later booking, not supplements. No extra restaurant income, repeat business or resale is assumed. Fixed hotel costs and the existing guest’s base contribution do not change.

For this exercise, management compares expected additional contribution while preserving all promises. The possible later booking is unconfirmed, not an entitlement to remove another guest. Both plans are operationally funded. The receptionist can inspect the enquiry details before making any offer; your first choice is provisional.

## Evidence

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: upgrade now | P occupied; S available | HK$400 | HK$600 |
| B: retain booking | S occupied; P available | HK$0 | HK$1,000 |

**Student question:** Would you offer the upgrade under the initial flexible-party forecast? Compare expected additional contribution for A and B. What room-type assumption must you check before confirming the offer?

## Round two

Before any offer is made, the reservations team reads the later enquiry. Two adult colleagues require two separate sleeping rooms within one private suite. Only P meets their stated configuration. They will not book S, two unrelated rooms, or a different arrangement. This is a product requirement, not an inference from their identities.

The party still has a supplied 50% chance of completing a booking if P remains available, contributing HK$1,000. If P is unavailable, it does not book at this hotel. Its enquiry reserves nothing. No other later demand for S or P appears; all prices, servicing costs and existing commitments remain unchanged.

This detail corrects the forecast’s room-substitution assumption, not the party’s booking probability. The arriving guest is still waiting for their booked S and has received no upgrade promise. You can now choose either route. Once an upgrade is confirmed, honour it even if a higher-contribution enquiry subsequently arrives.

**Student question:** Would you change the offer decision now? Calculate both expected contributions and the premium-only booking probability at which they tie. Explain why the room released by an upgrade need not replace the opportunity lost.

## Debrief · Count the upgrade once

The upgrade adds HK$500 less HK$100 extra servicing: HK$400. Do not count the original room payment again or treat HK$500 as contribution. Moving the existing guest from S to P does not add an occupied unit by itself; it exchanges the type of inventory still available.

## Debrief · Initial flexible demand

Initially A yields HK$400 + 0.50 × HK$600 = HK$700 expected additional contribution. B yields 0.50 × HK$1,000 = HK$500. I would provisionally offer A: it leads by HK$200 under the stated flexible-party assumption.

The premium room’s initial opportunity cost is not its entire HK$1,000 selling contribution. A can still sell S to the flexible party. Expected forgone contribution is 0.50 × (HK$1,000 − HK$600) = HK$200, less than the HK$400 upgrade contribution.

## Debrief · The room mix changes the answer

After the reveal, A yields HK$400: the released S cannot serve the only remaining party. B still yields 0.50 × HK$1,000 = HK$500. I would keep the guest in S and withhold the unpromised upgrade. B leads by HK$100 in expectation, not with certainty.

If the later party books, B adds HK$1,000 versus A’s HK$400. If it does not, B adds zero versus A’s HK$400. The expected comparison accepts that downside. Two available configurations are not interchangeable merely because each counts as one accommodation unit.

## Debrief · A useful threshold

Let p be the chance of a later P-only booking, holding every other assumption fixed. B yields HK$1,000 × p; A yields HK$400. They tie at p = 0.40, or 40%. Above 40%, B has higher expected contribution; below 40%, A does. At 50%, the supplied model favours B.

This is a sensitivity threshold, not an estimated probability or a universal upgrade rule. Real decisions require evidence about room-type demand, timing, alternative buyers and costs. Another buyer for S could change the comparison, but none exists in this exercise.

## Debrief · Do not undo the guest promise

The reversal is possible because no upgrade has yet been offered or accepted. If A had already been confirmed, the hotel would deliver P as promised rather than downgrade the guest to chase the later sale. Review inventory and requirements before committing; an economic opportunity is not permission to break a booking.
