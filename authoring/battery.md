# The phone with a fresh battery

Case 12 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Asymmetric information · Verification

![A buyer and seller inspect a used smartphone.](../battery-scene.jpg)

## Hong Kong in the news · 18 October 2026

A pavilion for second-hand smartphones debuted at a Hong Kong trade show, bringing together refurbishers and recycling platforms. If two used handsets look alike, what evidence makes one worth more?

[South China Morning Post, 18 October 2026](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/hong-kong-economy/article/3371309/second-hand-smartphone-traders-eye-hong-kong-springboard-global-exports?pgtype=live)

## Opening decision

You are choosing between otherwise identical used phones. One seller asks HK$300 extra, saying the battery was replaced. A genuinely fresh battery is worth HK$500 extra to you. An old one adds nothing. You put the chance the claim is true at 40%.

## Evidence

| Buyer’s estimates, HK$ | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Premium requested | 300 |
| Value of fresh battery | 500 |
| Value of old battery above baseline | 0 |

**Your decision** If you compare expected values, would you pay the premium on the seller’s word? What probability would justify it?

## Round two

Before payment an independent technician can identify a genuinely fresh battery perfectly, free of charge. The seller agrees in writing to refund the premium if the battery fails the test. The base phone remains worth its price either way.

**Your decision** What would you pay after a pass? After a fail? Who bears the cost of a false claim?

## Debrief

The unsupported claim is worth HK$200 in expectation, below HK$300. Break-even confidence is 60%. A pass makes the premium worthwhile by HK$200. A fail warrants zero premium, or its refund if already paid.

## Instructor note

Minutes 0–3: vote and compare reasons in pairs. Minutes 3–7: reveal the changed fact and vote again. Minutes 7–12: reconcile the answers. A test verifies a particular attribute. Ask students whether a screen showing an authorised part also proves remaining capacity. If the test can be fooled, ask for the probability of a false pass before accepting the same premium.
