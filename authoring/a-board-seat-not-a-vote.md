# An 18% holding. Does it belong on the associate line?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 549 -->

ACC-46 · Hong Kong and Greater Bay Area · 15 minutes · Significant influence · Equity method · Associate distributions · Acquisition date

![Two colleagues examine a board packet and papers on an electronics assembly workbench.](../a-board-seat-not-a-vote-scene.jpg)

## A percentage alone does not settle whether an investor has significant influence

Alibaba Health’s 2025 annual report describes assessing significant influence using board composition and contractual arrangements as well as ownership. The exercise uses a different group and an invented investment.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong-listed control-systems group paid HK$1.8m for 18% of both the ordinary equity and votes of a fictional Foshan assembly company on 1 January. Review the investment in the investor’s consolidated accounts, not separate company accounts.

On purchase the investor can appoint a voting director who participates substantively in the annual budget, operating plan and distribution policy. An unrelated shareholder controls the investee. The investor cannot direct policy on its own, and no arrangement requires joint consent with it. The rest of the investor’s rights are ordinary investor rights.

There is no exemption from the equity method if it applies. Ignore tax, impairment, other comprehensive income and adjustments to acquisition-date fair values. The investee has no preferred shares. Its year-end cash dividend is paid pro rata after acquisition.

## Evidence

| Initial accounting input | HK$ |
| --- | --- |
| Investment cost, 1 January | 1,800,000 |
| Investee after-tax profit during year | 600,000 |
| Investee total year-end cash dividend | 200,000 |

**Student question:** Would you classify the 18% holding as an associate on these facts? What profit, cash dividend received and closing investment balance should the investor report?

## Round two

In a revised file, the same purchase and substantive rights actually began on 1 July, not 1 January. The investee still earned HK$600,000 for the whole year; its reviewed split shows HK$250,000 before and HK$350,000 after acquisition. The HK$200,000 dividend was fully declared and paid after the July purchase and all ordinary shareholders, including this investor, share it pro rata.

**Student question:** Using the revised acquisition date, what profit, dividend and closing investment balance belong in the investor’s group accounts? Is it enough simply to halve the annual profit?

## Debrief · Influence is the policy right

Below 20% of votes there is a presumption against significant influence, not a ban. The voting board seat and substantive participation in budget, operating and distribution policy rebut that presumption on these facts. The group has neither control nor joint control. Apply the equity method for the associate; do not consolidate it line by line.

## Debrief · Separate income from cash

Initially record the investment at HK$1.8m. Include 18% × HK$600,000 = HK$108,000 in profit or loss. The dividend brings cash of 18% × HK$200,000 = HK$36,000. Under the equity method that distribution reduces the investment balance, not a second line of income.

## Debrief · Make the balance reconcile

For the January acquisition the closing investment is HK$1,800,000 + HK$108,000 − HK$36,000 = HK$1,872,000. Share of profit and investment carrying amount need not move by the same amount.

## Debrief · Start from when influence begins

The July acquisition uses the documented post-acquisition profit of HK$350,000, not half of HK$600,000. The equity-method profit is HK$63,000. Cash received remains HK$36,000; the closing balance is HK$1,827,000. Recognising a share of the prior profit would overstate the investor’s current-year result.

## Instructor note

Allow four minutes for classification, five to reconcile both balances and six to defend the timing. Ask which evidence would be needed if the investor had only an observer seat and no policy participation. No alternative measurement is assumed, and the director’s title alone should not settle the influence test.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

The group, investment terms, dates, arithmetic and judgement are fictional. The approved accounts and minutes referred to are stipulated teaching evidence only.

- [HKICPA, HKAS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures](https://www.hkicpa.org.hk/hksaebk/HKSA_Members_Handbook_Master/volumeII/hkas28_2011.pdf). Defines significant influence, presumption, examples of indicators and use of post-acquisition results under the equity method.
- [IFRS Foundation, IAS 28 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-28-investments-in-associates-and-joint-ventures/). Explains influence, initial cost, profit share and reduction for distributions.
- [New Zealand XRB, NZ IAS 28](https://standards.xrb.govt.nz/standards-navigator/nz-ias-28/). International equivalent sets out the rebuttable 20% test and the start of equity accounting on becoming an associate.
- [Alibaba Health, FY2025 annual report](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0710/2025071000289.pdf). Issuer describes evaluating lower and higher holdings using several indicators, not only the ownership percentage.
- [Angelalign, 2025 annual report](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0423/2026042302460.pdf). Reports a policy of equity accounting after acquisition for ordinary share investments with significant influence.
