# The rate is right. The cohort isn’t.

<!-- legacy-case-id: 546 -->

GVR-45 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Performance disclosure · Metric scope · Governance escalation

![Two colleagues compare a public scorecard with two open service ledgers.](../the-rate-is-right-the-cohort-isnt-scene.jpg)

## Performance figures need a clear scope

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing publishes guidance on how it reviews certain announcements after publication. Separately, the IFRS Foundation’s Management Commentary guidance explains why material information is assessed in relation to decisions. These sources provide reporting context; the service operator and its own policy below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Ming runs a fictional service operator’s quality reporting. Its public scorecard says “96% of all completed requests met the five-day target.” The analyst verifies that the numerator, denominator and 96% figure are correct for standard requests only. Phone-assistance requests were tracked separately and excluded from the figure. The report has already been circulated.

A funding panel’s fictional pilot requires at least 95% of all completed requests to have been completed within five days. The service committee separately orders an accessibility review if the phone-assistance cohort falls below 85%. Both readers have the scorecard and have not yet voted. The same five-day rule and period apply to both nonoverlapping cohorts.

A verified ledger establishes the standard and phone cohort size and the standard on-time count. The audit team has found no reliable on-time count for the phone cohort yet. No other requests enter either decision; do not exclude late completed requests from the denominator.

## Evidence

| Fictional audited data | Standard | Phone assistance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All completed requests | 800 | 200 |
| Completed within five days | 768 | Not yet verified |
| Published headline labelled all completed requests | 96% | Not separately published |

**Student question:** Should Ming correct or leave the headline? State what can be confirmed now, how many phone requests would need to be on time for each test, and what both decision-makers should be told pending verification.

## Round two

The audit has verified that 160 phone-assistance requests were completed within five days. The standard result and both readers’ rules are unchanged. Neither reader has acted yet; the original wording remains in the copy distributed to them.

**Student question:** What rates should the corrected scorecard show? Determine each reader’s decision and explain why silently changing the original heading would not be enough.

## Debrief · Correct known scope now

768 ÷ 800 = 96% for standard requests, but the combined rate for all completed requests is unknown. Correct the misleading label openly and mark the combined and phone rates pending. Notify both readers, and ask them to hold their decisions until they have verified evidence.

## Debrief · Derive the two cutoffs separately

The panel requires 0.95 × 1,000 = 950 on-time requests; phone assistance must supply 950 − 768 = 182. To avoid the committee review, phone assistance must reach 85% × 200 = 170. A result of 170 through 181 would avoid the review without qualifying for the pilot.

## Debrief · Monitor decisions, not just decimals

The phone rate is 160 ÷ 200 = 80%. The rate for all completed requests is (768 + 160) ÷ 1,000 = 92.8%. The pilot threshold is not met and the accessibility review is triggered. Keep the 96% correctly labelled and send a dated correction to the same audience and both decision-makers. Log the old wording and clarify that the initial standard rate itself did not change.

## Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 for the two rules, 4 for correction maths, and 5 for draft communications. Ask how the response differs if a reader had already voted. The exercise concerns governance of a fictional scorecard, not a listing-rule or financial-statement determination.
