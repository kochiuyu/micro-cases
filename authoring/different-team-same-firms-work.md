# A different team. Reviewing whose work?

## When expertise creates a self-review question

On 11 July 2022, the International Ethics Standards Board for Accountants published staff questions and answers about changes to its non-assurance-services provisions. These included a prohibition on services that might create a self-review threat for public-interest-entity audit clients. The concern is whether a firm can objectively evaluate work it helped create.

That audit rule does not automatically govern the fictional assignment below. This is an internal advisory review, not a statutory audit or an engagement claimed to meet a formal assurance standard. The board has supplied its own explicit appointment policy.

## Opening decision

You sit on the board of a fictional Hong Kong community-service organisation. It wants an external advisory evaluation of the design and operation of its grant-application process, including scoring criteria and approval checkpoints. Today is working day 0. The report must reach the board by the end of working day 20, and the total budget is HK$150,000. Neither provider has been appointed; both offers remain open for two working days.

Both firms have passed the board’s competence and confidentiality screening. The specialist, S, has completed six relevant sector reviews. The broader firm, B, has completed two sector reviews and eight other process reviews. Neither has an identified ownership, family or success-fee link. The named review staff report no previous design involvement, but firm-wide prior-work checks are still pending.

The board’s fictional policy requires attention to the firm’s prior work, not just the proposed team. A firm that designed a material part of the process cannot be the sole judge of that design. An unrelated, competent reviewer must have direct access to evidence, independently test the affected design judgments, and have authority to require corrections or report unresolved disagreement directly to the board. Separate internal teams or declarations alone are insufficient. If an adequate arrangement is not established before appointment, choose another provider.

**Student question:** Which provider would you provisionally prefer, S or B, and what must the firm-wide prior-work check establish before appointment?

## Round two

It is still working day 0. The check finds that a separate S team designed the scoring criteria and approval checkpoints eight months ago. The work was fully paid for. S disclosed it when asked; there is no evidence of dishonesty or defective work. B has no prior design involvement or other identified link. The new review must evaluate the design S helped create as well as its operation.

S proposes a different review team with a separate internal reporting line at no extra cost. It also obtains a quote from an available, unrelated, competent outside firm: HK$35,000 and six working days after S’s twelve-day review. The quoted outside work only reads S’s draft report. It does not include direct evidence access, fresh testing of affected design judgments, or authority to require corrections or report unresolved disagreement directly to the board.

Those missing rights and tasks could be negotiated, but their cost and timing have not been agreed. The quoted package is HK$125,000 and eighteen working days after appointment; it is not yet a compliant solution under the supplied board policy. B’s HK$135,000, eighteen-day offer remains available. You may appoint B now or use the two-day offer window to seek a fully specified, compliant arrangement for S while keeping B available. If agreement is not secured by the end of day 2, B must be appointed then to meet day 20. There is no signed contract or sunk cost.

**Student question:** Would you appoint B now or use the two-day window to seek a compliant arrangement for S while keeping B available? State the conditions and fallback that make your choice defensible.

## The firm, not only the team

Different staff do not erase firm-level self-review. S would be evaluating criteria and checkpoints its own firm helped design. This is not a claim that S is dishonest, incompetent or necessarily biased in every judgment. It is a structural threat that the board’s stated policy requires it to address.

## A safeguard needs actual authority

The proposed separate internal reporting line does not meet the supplied policy. Nor does an outside reader who sees only S’s draft. The independent reviewer needs evidence access, independent testing of affected design judgments, and correction or direct board-escalation rights. Naming an outside firm is not enough. Its agreed scope and authority matter.

## Compare complete, admissible offers

S’s original fee advantage is HK$135,000 − HK$90,000 = HK$45,000. Adding the quoted outside reading gives HK$90,000 + HK$35,000 = HK$125,000 and 12 + 6 = 18 working days. That would be HK$10,000 below B, but it buys an inadequate arrangement. Do not rank it as if it already met the policy. A compliant S proposal has an unknown final price and schedule.

## Two defensible routes, one firm boundary

Appointing B now is defensible: it is competent, has no identified prior-work link, costs HK$135,000 and delivers within eighteen working days. Alternatively, keep B available while giving S no more than two working days to agree adequate independent scrutiny, a total fee no greater than HK$150,000 and a delivery date no later than day 20. At day 2, B’s eighteen-day timetable reaches day 20 exactly. This fallback has no timing slack. If adequate S terms are not secured, choose B rather than extending negotiations past the deadline.

Neither route permits appointing S under its current separate-team or draft-reading proposals. The choice is between a specified compliant offer and a tightly bounded attempt to improve another offer, not between independence and a cheaper price.

## Scope of the lesson

The appointment policy and all organisations, proposals, dates and amounts in the exercise are invented. Real professional independence rules depend on the engagement, jurisdiction and applicable standards. Some services are prohibited regardless of proposed safeguards. This classroom policy is not a legal opinion or a statement that additional review always cures self-review. An advisory evaluation should not be represented as formal assurance merely because an outside provider performs it.

## Instructor note

Allow three minutes for the provisional choice and identify the pending firm-wide check. Reveal the prior design work and proposals, then allow four minutes for a revised decision. Ask students to separate expertise from independence and a quoted price from a complete admissible offer. Use three minutes to verify the arithmetic and the day-2 fallback. Finish by asking what evidence and authority a reviewer must have.

Accept either B now or the bounded negotiation route if students retain B, specify the missing independent-review rights and enforce the cost and deadline limits. Do not accept S’s present proposal. Do not reward allegations of corruption unsupported by the case. Keep discussion within the fictional policy; historical audit guidance is background rather than an automatic rule for this advisory engagement.

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

- [IESBA, Staff releases Q&As to spotlight key changes to non-assurance services provisions (11 July 2022)](https://www.ethicsboard.org/news-events/2022-07/iesba-staff-releases-qas-spotlight-key-changes-non-assurance-services-provisions-iesba-code). It does not establish the rules for this fictional advisory engagement.

Background on judging ethical threats from an informed outside perspective and the limits of consultation. Not a legal ruling on the fictional appointment.
- [AUASB, ASAE 3000 (January 2025)](https://standards.auasb.gov.au/asae-3000-jan-2025). Introductory scope distinguishes assurance from consulting and related engagements. Used to bound the exercise, not to impose Australian assurance requirements on a Hong Kong advisory assignment.
