# No name. No conversation?

## In the news

HKEX’s own whistleblowing page, marked updated 17 September 2024, describes an online route through which anonymous reporters can communicate with investigators and check progress.

The page concerns external parties reporting conduct involving HKEX. That update date is not a claimed launch date, and the documented practice does not establish how our fictional employer’s system will perform.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong exhibition-services company with 60 employees wants to make it easier to raise possible misconduct or serious departures from internal rules. It can fund one additional three-month access package now. Both cost HK$12,000 including launch training; either is feasible.

Existing policy already accepts named and unsigned concerns and prohibits retaliation. A trained reviewer outside the relevant line-management chain assesses them, with an alternate recipient if that reviewer is implicated. Assessment and follow-up staffing are funded separately.

Basic confidential phone or in-person contact and an unsigned paper box remain under either option. An unsigned note can be assessed on its contents even when no reply is possible. Personal grievances and urgent issues have appropriate separate or parallel routes; no emergency concern is awaiting this decision.

This choice adds access, not limits on investigations or protections. The other package can be reconsidered after the three-month review.

## Evidence

| Additional package · HK$12,000 each | Strength and limit |
|---|---|
| A · Private external-adviser appointments, including evenings | Conversation helps explain complex concerns. The adviser knows the person’s identity and shares it with designated handlers only where necessary under the disclosed process; no absolute secrecy promise. |
| B · Anonymous two-way online mailbox, accessible outside office hours | No name or email required. A case code allows questions and replies. It requires written communication and keeping the code; details may still identify someone. |

A’s adviser handles intake, not automatic independent adjudication. B still needs a responsible reviewer. The vendor describes B’s reply function, but the company has not yet checked whether its workers understand that flow.

**Student question:** Which additional package should the company introduce first, A or B? Name the unmet need it serves and one specific piece of evidence that could change your recommendation.

## Round two

An independent facilitator has now run a small usability exercise with six volunteer employees, using an entirely invented, non-personal example about an inaccurate training-attendance register. All six submit it without names or emails, retrieve a question using their individual case codes, and reply with the requested session date.

The designated reviewer sees the messages but no supplied name or email. This verifies the exercised return-message flow, not network anonymity or protection against identity clues in a real report. Four volunteers say they would consider it when reluctant to identify themselves; two still prefer a confidential conversation.

These are six volunteers’ task results and stated preferences, not the views of all 60 employees, actual reporting behaviour or evidence of misconduct. The exercise reduces one usability uncertainty about B; it does not establish trust. Existing confidential human access remains. A would add evening capacity and specialist intake conversation, not create the only human route.

**Student question:** Would you keep or change the first investment? Draft two sentences explaining what a reporter can expect, and name a responsible owner and one review criterion.

## Distinguish identity from access to information

An anonymous route can let someone communicate without supplying their name. That does not stop the account’s details from identifying them. Confidential handling means controlling access to information and any identity already known; it is not a promise that information can never be shared.

A offers a conversation with an adviser who knows the person’s identity. B can support follow-up without requiring that disclosure to the reviewer. Neither feature alone establishes trust, truth or absolute secrecy.

## Use the positive result without turning it into a vote

The exercise shows that these six people could return, read a question and reply using the case code. That makes B a more credible first investment if unnamed follow-up is the priority, while preserving existing human access.

A remains defensible if conversational access, evening appointments or help explaining complex concerns is the greater unmet need. Four stated preferences do not settle a 60-person workforce decision. Look for evidence of access needs, not a fictional majority mandate.

## Make a realistic promise with an accountable owner

For a B launch, two possible sentences are: “You may report without giving your name and use your case code to read questions and reply; details you include could still identify you. A designated reviewer outside the matter’s management chain will assess your concern and explain available updates and limits on confidentiality.”

Keep the existing prohibition on retaliation visible, without guaranteeing that retaliation is impossible. Assign the trained reviewer to acknowledgement, assessment and follow-up, with the alternate route for conflicts. The mailbox is a channel, not the person responsible for acting.

## Follow the concern fairly, not just the message count

A report is a lead to assess, not a verdict about the person mentioned. Listen, check evidence, restrict access appropriately and explain what updates can be given. A concern that cannot be substantiated is not automatically malicious.

Review whether staff can find the routes and explain their limits, whether questions can be exchanged, and whether cases have owners and timely action under the company’s stated process. Check conflicts and access controls. Zero reports or a high raw total would not, by itself, establish success.

## Instructor note

Take a provisional A-or-B vote on the additional access need. Baseline confidential contact, unsigned reporting, nonretaliation policy and independent assessment are already present; students are not choosing whether those protections deserve funding.

The second round is a six-person volunteer usability exercise, not a trust survey, penetration test or estimate of future reporting. Do not turn four out of six into an adoption forecast. The fabricated attendance example concerns no real person or actual misconduct.

Ask students to draft realistic expectations and assign ownership for response. Internal procedures do not replace appropriate urgent or external routes. Actual confidentiality exceptions and legal protections require applicable local guidance; comparative UK sources do not establish Hong Kong statutory rights.

## Sources and case assumptions

All information needed for classroom decisions is in the case. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The employer, workforce, packages, prices, system behaviour, six volunteers and preferences are invented. No actual report was filed, personal data transmitted or real HKEX service tested. The exercise uses entirely fictional non-personal attendance information. All six could complete the specified message exchange, but no network-anonymity audit, population inference, causal reporting improvement or evidence of wrongdoing is supplied. Four stated preferences are not a workforce vote or behavioural forecast. A’s adviser knows identity and provides intake, not automatic adjudication; disclosures follow the described limits. B does not require a name or email but report details can identify someone. Both retain basic confidential human contact, an unsigned box, funded trained assessment, an alternate recipient for conflicts, nonretaliation policy and appropriate grievance/urgent routes. The budget is for additional access, not investigation limits. Sources checked 14 November 2026. Relevant substantive sections of five documents from four institutional publishers were reviewed; HKEX’s two documents serve different purposes and are not independent experiments. The local page’s date is an update, not a launch, and its audience is external parties. Listed-issuer guidance is not treated as a universal small-business rule. UK guidance supplies comparative handling practice, not Hong Kong statutory protections, remedies or confidentiality law. No allegation is made against any real organisation.
