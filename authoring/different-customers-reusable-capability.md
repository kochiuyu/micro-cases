# Different customers. The same reusable capability?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 474 -->

STR-40 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Related diversification · Economies of scope · Capability transfer

![Two adult managers in a Hong Kong materials-testing office discuss plain packaging samples and a small unlabelled property model beside laboratory equipment, without displaying a preferred investment.](../different-customers-reusable-capability-scene.jpg)

## Relatedness is a working connection

Strategy teaching distinguishes entering an attractive business from making businesses better together. Laboratory accreditation also applies to specified activities, not every service a laboratory might imagine. The fictional choice below tests a concrete resource connection without treating an existing accreditation or a broad industry label as transferable permission.

## Opening decision

Fictional Harbour Materials Lab in Hong Kong tests industrial materials. Its board can devote one management team to one organic expansion this year: packaging testing for new customers, or operating a small commercial property. Neither involves acquiring a company. The property would not house the laboratory.

Both ventures have passed the same standalone financial and risk screen, including their own competent staff, entry costs and required approvals. Either fits the investment limit. The board has no evidence that one provides greater portfolio risk reduction. These assumptions prevent a return forecast or diversification slogan from settling the classroom choice.

The packaging customers do not overlap with existing clients. The property proposal includes several familiar local business tenants. A director prefers property because familiar customers feel related; another prefers packaging because both activities involve testing. The team has not yet checked which existing resources either proposal can actually use.

**Student question:** Which proposal would you investigate first for a firm-specific advantage? Name the evidence needed to distinguish reusable capability from familiar customers or a similar business label.

## Round two

Technical checks establish that packaging work can use the lab’s existing calibration-management routines and specified testing equipment for twelve hours each week. A checked schedule has twenty genuinely spare usable hours after existing orders, maintenance and contingency. Qualified operators are funded; sharing does not interrupt current service or reduce required controls.

Packaging sample preparation is different. Its standalone budget already funds the new preparation equipment, training, method validation and required scope-extension work. Existing accreditation does not automatically cover the new tests. No accredited commercial service may launch until the relevant competence and approval conditions are verified.

Comparable annual cost schedules use the same service outputs and accounting boundary. Operating existing and packaging services separately costs HK$3.00 million; operating them together costs HK$2.82 million. The latter includes all new preparation, sharing coordination and maintenance costs. These fictional estimates exclude no displaced work and do not count existing sunk equipment cost twice.

Property operations cannot use the laboratory’s equipment, calibration routines or technical quality system. Its competent operating team is separately funded, but the review finds no additional saving or customer advantage from laboratory ownership. Familiar tenants are not guaranteed contracts. Both ventures remain viable alone; only packaging has demonstrated this particular scope benefit.

**Student question:** Which expansion would you prioritise on this evidence? Calculate the proposed annual scope saving and remaining equipment headroom. State the conditions for launch and a fact that could reverse your recommendation.

## Debrief · Make the connection explicit

Prioritise packaging, subject to the stated launch gates. The link is specific equipment availability and repeatable technical routines, not identical customers. Relatedness can reside in capabilities used behind the service even when buyers differ. Customer familiarity alone does not establish a resource advantage in operating property.

The evidence supports this direction, not a claim that property is a bad business. It passed the same standalone screen. With only one management slot, the board favours the venture with a verified additional benefit from this firm’s resources, while keeping both ventures’ underlying market assumptions under review.

## Debrief · Calculate scope, not scale

The annual cost difference is HK$3.00 million minus HK$2.82 million = HK$180,000, or 6% of the separate-operation cost. It is an estimated economy of scope: delivering the specified different services together costs less than delivering the same outputs separately. No increased volume or lower quality is assumed.

Twelve of twenty spare equipment hours are needed, leaving eight hours weekly. That arithmetic is not permission to sell all eight. The original contingency remains protected, and demand, maintenance or job mix can change. Recheck the schedule rather than treating nominal equipment ownership as unlimited capacity.

## Debrief · Do not transfer a certificate by analogy

A calibration-management routine can be reusable while preparation skills and accredited scope still need work. The budget funds those gaps; it does not eliminate them. Name an accountable technical owner and require documented readiness before offering the new accredited service. Do not imply that all laboratory services carry identical recognition.

The case assumes that necessary scope and competence conditions are verified before launch, not that approval is guaranteed or automatic. If approval or capability development fails, delay or stop the launch. Avoid promising a calendar date based only on the existence of an experienced quality team.

## Debrief · Challenge the saving

Test cost schedules against actual sharing effort, equipment compatibility, calibration needs, scheduling conflicts and current-customer service. Record incremental costs consistently. Do not count a benefit already included in the standalone forecast again when appraising the combined venture, or label an accounting allocation change a cash saving.

The recommendation could reverse if required work displaces valuable existing orders, new methods need incompatible equipment, approval costs grow, or property offers a verified advantage omitted here. A reusable resource is useful only where it delivers a benefit after adaptation and coordination costs, within the firm’s real constraints.

## Instructor note

Ask students to complete a three-column map: existing resource, new use, cost or constraint. Require at least one resource that transfers and one that does not. The financial screens are stipulated, not investment advice or an invitation to invent superior property returns.

STR-05 examines customer overlap in an acquisition; STR-23 asks whether the current parent adds value to an existing division. Here the choice is organic scope expansion through operational resource sharing. All businesses, capacities and financial schedules are fictional; no source validates the estimated six-percent saving.
