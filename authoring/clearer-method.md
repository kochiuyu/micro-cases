## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. ClearFrame, a Hong Kong product-photography studio, is preparing a glossy-ceramics service. Two experienced photographers usually photograph matte stationery. They know the cameras, lighting equipment and safety procedures; the unfamiliar issue is adapting their photographic method.

Each has six funded, paid practice hours this week, with equipment access and scheduled feedback from a knowledgeable studio lead. Existing work is covered without overtime or changes to pay or breaks. Objects belong to the studio; no live customer order depends on this week’s practice.

Both goal options use the same checklist: accurate colour, visible surface detail and no glare hiding the product. Usable images are recorded and release checks remain. The manager must choose one shared team goal for the same block and Friday review, not a separate target for each photographer.

## Evidence

| Primary goal | Friday achievement sought | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| A · Outcome-led | Together produce 12 distinct sample images meeting the checklist. | A provisional stretch goal, not a guaranteed feasible quota or disciplinary threshold. |
| B · Learning-led | Complete one shared comparison of three safe lighting arrangements, explain which suits which surface or shape, and demonstrate an approach on a fresh object. | May leave fewer portfolio images ready this week; learning is not guaranteed. |

A allows method changes and feedback too. Two acceptable trial images of one straight-sided matte-finish reference object suggest some existing skills may transfer, but do not validate the glossy range.

B keeps useful images while judging the block mainly by evidence of a repeatable method, not merely completing three experiments. Neither option removes quality requirements or adds practice time.

**Student question:** Which primary goal would you agree for this week, A or B? Give a provisional reason and the evidence about the task that could change your choice.

## Round two

The lead reviews a preliminary practice record with the photographers. Their familiar arrangement gives acceptable images of a flat satin-finish tile, but hides detail on a curved glossy cup and a ridged glossy vase. Repeating that arrangement has not solved the reflections.

A brief demonstration shows that changing light position and diffusion can improve one difficult object. Neither photographer can yet explain which arrangement will transfer to the other shape. They have not systematically compared alternatives.

This fictional record reveals a method question, not low effort or shipped defects. The demonstration provides a starting point, not a universal recipe. Equipment, lead access, paid time and the quality checklist remain identical under either goal.

**Student question:** Would you keep the outcome-led goal or make learning the primary goal for this block? Write the Friday evidence you expect and one condition for moving to a suitable output target later.

## Change the immediate focus, not the quality standard

The initial reference images made A a reasonable provisional starting point. The new record shows why their success was narrow: a familiar arrangement does not yet reliably handle the different glossy shapes.

B now has a stronger rationale for this block. The immediate gap is knowing how to choose and adapt a method, not simply accumulating more attempts. That does not establish that output goals are always harmful or that experienced staff lack motivation.

## Make learning an observable deliverable

Friday’s evidence could include images from the compared arrangements, an explanation of when each approach worked or failed, and a demonstration on a fresh object against the same checklist.

A failed arrangement can be informative if the team explains what it learned. Three unexplained setups are not automatically success. The lead’s feedback should test the reasoning behind adjustments, not reward random switching.

## Keep business output in view

Usable images still contribute to the portfolio. B accepts that fewer may be ready this week in exchange for investigating a repeatable method; it does not exempt later work from customer-quality requirements.

Once representative tasks show repeatable acceptable results, agree an attainable output goal using observed work time and workload. If the method remains unreliable, revise support, time or service scope rather than release unchecked work.

A defence of retaining A must explain how its primary count will leave room for the needed comparison, not merely tell staff to persist. Both options require an honest review if the funded time proves insufficient.

## Do not turn a practice plan into a performance diagnosis

Six hours per person is 12 person-hours in total, not a throughput estimate. Neither the hours nor three arrangements guarantee mastery.

The qualitative record does not estimate the effect of goal choice, establish a personality trait or justify a sanction. The manager is selecting what to emphasise in a development block with agreed resources and feedback.

## Instructor note

Keep A credible: it includes method discretion, feedback and an unchanged quality checklist. Keep B specific: students must state evidence of a method, not “learn more”. After the reveal, require both a Friday deliverable and a later transition condition.

The scenario is about goal focus during unfamiliar method development, not equipment-safety onboarding, hiring readiness or allocation of coaching hours. Both photographers are experienced; support and paid time are the same.

The research sources overlap and include a student business simulation. They inform a bounded rationale, not a prediction of photographers’ results. No technical lighting prescription, worker diagnosis or real performance decision is being supplied.
