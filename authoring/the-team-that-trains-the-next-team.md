# The team develops talent. Who gets credit when it moves?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 594 -->

PPL-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Internal mobility · Manager incentives · Central support

![Three managers discuss a set of folders across a meeting table.](../the-team-that-trains-the-next-team-scene.jpg)

## A good move for the firm can look bad for a team

An original field study on talent hoarding found that internal job applications can be discouraged when managers are accountable for their own team’s results. Hong Kong’s Civil Service has also described career development through postings. This fictional design firm compares ways to recognise cross-team development without giving managers control over anyone’s choices.

## Opening decision

A Hong Kong design company is planning next year’s voluntary internal development programme, not deciding the outcome of an actual transfer. It models one representative case of a willing colleague moving from team A to an independently selected vacancy in team B, versus the baseline of no move. Job terms, free choice, selection and existing coverage protections are unchanged.

The exhibit shows hypothetical six-month changes in thousands of Hong Kong dollars, after local transition costs. Head office pays for coaching separately. Managers’ scorecard points initially equal their team’s contribution change in thousands, excluding the head-office budget. The firm can transfer c whole points from B to A for verified support of a voluntary move. This bookkeeping does not itself transfer cash or increase contribution and must not control individual selections.

## Evidence

| Impact relative to no move | HK$ thousands |
| --- | --- |
| A: lost contribution after local backfill | −9 |
| B: added contribution after local onboarding | +18 |
| Central paid coaching | −3 |

**Student question:** How could A and the firm evaluate the same move differently? Calculate the firm total and the whole-point range that would leave neither manager scoring below the no-move baseline.

## Round two

Before the programme is approved, the representative model is updated: B’s contribution gain is now only HK$10,000. A’s cost and central coaching remain unchanged. The receiving post is still legitimate; the exercise asks whether to run the extra development programme at this funding level, not whether staff may apply or move.

Managers cannot compel a move, veto an application, or receive credit for attendance alone. Any programme would require a documented handover and training completion, approved independently of the managers. No target number of moves or change to base pay is permitted.

**Student question:** Recalculate the feasible point range and the company total. Would that point range justify funding the programme on its own? What would you change or verify before testing incentives?

## Debrief · One team’s loss can coexist with a firm gain

Initially the firm gains −9 + 18 − 3 = 6, or HK$6,000. A has −9 without a transfer despite the firm gain. With c, A scores −9 + c and B scores 18 − c. Both are at least zero for whole points 9 through 18. The points net to zero; the head-office cost still counts.

## Debrief · A feasible scorecard split does not create value

Updated scores are −9 + c and 10 − c, so 9 or 10 makes both nonnegative. Firm contribution is now −9 + 10 − 3 = −2, or a HK$2,000 loss. Don’t approve the funded version on this example solely because the managers can be aligned. Seek an evidenced gain or reduce avoidable cost without weakening protections.

## Debrief · Reward useful support, not movement itself

Keep fair applications independent. If a programme is viable, define what verified support means, ensure credit is transferred once, and track workload, backfill, both teams’ results, and staff perceptions without ranking individuals. Do not deny employee mobility because one representative scenario shows a loss.

## Instructor note

Have students draw the firm and scorecard ledgers side by side. Reward a design that can make local incentives less obstructive without treating fictitious points as company income or turning voluntary moves into quotas.
