## Returning cash alongside future commitments

On 12 March 2025, Cathay’s annual-results announcement discussed ordinary dividends alongside investment commitments and other uses of funds. It supplies a regional example of several claims on corporate cash, not a model payout ratio for a small business. The seasonal company and figures below are original fiction.

## Opening decision

The owners of a fictional Hong Kong seasonal merchandise business are considering a discretionary cash distribution after a profitable year. Reported annual profit was HK$600,000; unrestricted bank cash today is HK$700,000. The proposal is HK$300,000 now, but nothing has been declared, promised or paid. Owners have no urgent personal funding need. Their alternative is to retain the money through the coming operating cycle and revisit a distribution later.

The documented cash plan reaches its lowest balance in month three: cumulative cash payments exceed receipts by HK$500,000 at that point. This includes all wages, taxes, suppliers, debt service and other required costs, including any modelled facility charges. Every earlier and later point has a higher balance before discretionary distributions. Contracted collections after the peak restore liquidity; do not use those later receipts before they arrive. The owners require at least HK$150,000 cash at every point, over and above the payments already in the plan.

A dashboard shows a currently undrawn HK$300,000 bank line. The provisional proposal assumes it remains available through month three; the contractual expiry and repayment dates must be checked before approval. If that assumption holds, drawing HK$250,000 at the peak would preserve the cash floor after a HK$300,000 distribution. Qualified advisers have confirmed adequate distributable reserves and the required approval route; this is not a lesson in jurisdiction-specific legal tests. Actual declaration remains conditional on a supported liquidity plan and compliance with all applicable duties and covenants. No owner entitlement or employee obligation may be cancelled to fund a payout.

## Round two

The bank confirms that the facility expires at the end of month two and any amount drawn must be repaid then. Renewal is under review, with no commitment. Drawing early and holding the money cannot finance the month-three peak because it must first be repaid. No other new credit or owner injection is committed; the operating plan and cash floor are unchanged.

The owners may now recommend any smaller affordable amount, including zero, without breaking a promise. With no new financing, retaining all cash leaves HK$200,000 at the month-three low point. A distribution up to HK$50,000 can preserve the stated HK$150,000 floor in this model. The forecasts are planning assumptions, not guaranteed collections or a legal safe harbour; actual approval still requires updated advice and the relevant checks. There is no supplied investment return or owner utility that identifies a uniquely best amount between zero and the model ceiling.

**Student question:** With no facility committed through the peak, would you recommend a smaller distribution now or retain all cash until collections arrive? Calculate the maximum under the stated cash-floor model and explain why it is a ceiling rather than an obligation to pay.

## Profit is not the payout budget

The HK$600,000 accounting profit is not an additional deposit to add to the HK$700,000 bank balance. At the peak, cash before distributions and borrowing is HK$700,000 − HK$500,000 = HK$200,000. A HK$300,000 payout would make that HK$100,000 negative without financing. To retain the HK$150,000 floor, it would require HK$250,000 available at that date, not merely an unused facility shown today.

## Match finance to the date of need

The line expires before the low point. Borrowing early adds cash and a repayment obligation that cancels that funding by the end of month two. An application for renewal is not a committed replacement. Do not pay first and assume the bank, staff or suppliers will absorb the gap later. If renewal is eventually committed, reassess its duration, conditions and repayment schedule rather than treating the stated limit as permanent cash.

## A maximum is not a target

Without peak-period finance, the model ceiling is HK$700,000 − HK$500,000 − HK$150,000 = HK$50,000. Paying HK$50,000 delivers some cash to owners but uses the entire margin above their minimum. Paying less, or zero until collections arrive, preserves extra flexibility. Explain the chosen buffer and next review date; do not imply that retaining cash forever or always paying the maximum is automatically best.
