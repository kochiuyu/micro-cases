# Decide before guests travel. Or wait?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 180 -->

HSP-15 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Hospitality · Service commitment timing · Guest preparation

![Two activity organisers discuss plans beside indoor still-life materials, with an empty courtyard sketching area beyond.](../before-guests-travel-scene.jpg)

## A service-communication context

On 15 November 2022, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council urged activity-booking providers to explain restrictions and communicate changed arrangements clearly. It discussed actual complaints, not the fictional decision below.

Our art operator, guests, forecast timetable and protections are original classroom assumptions, not a real weather forecast or legal cancellation rule.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong operator has 30 adult guests booked for a Saturday 10:00 courtyard sketching session. A fully staffed indoor still-life workshop at the same address is already reserved as an alternative. It has the same duration, materials, accessibility and fee, but is a different experience. Guests have been told about both formats and may decline either.

The operator must now choose its format-decision deadline. A commits to the indoor workshop at Friday 10:00. B waits for a scheduled Friday 18:00 forecast review, retaining the courtyard option if the professional safety plan and conditions permit, otherwise choosing indoors. Both packages are fully funded, including the backup room, paid staff and notification work. No extra revenue or cost saving from either is established.

The weather adviser expects the later review to contain more useful local detail, not certainty. At present neither format has been announced as final. Under either plan the operator sends a Friday 10:00 message and offers a full activity-fee refund on request until Friday 20:00. Statutory and existing contractual rights remain unchanged. No unsafe travel or activity is encouraged: the same qualified safety lead monitors conditions, may stop or cancel either format and promptly communicates urgent changes. An indoor announcement is not a guarantee that travel or the event will remain safe.

## Round two

The operator checks the guests’ actual preparation plans. Fourteen are travelling specifically for this session. They can cancel their pre-booked transport and accommodation without charge until Friday 14:00; after that, HK$400 per person becomes non-refundable under those suppliers’ terms. All 14 say they would cancel that trip if told in time that only the indoor format will run. This is a stated preference, not proof they will act.

The other 16 are local, have no comparable advance travel commitment and say they prefer to wait for the chance of the courtyard session. Neither group is a vote that automatically settles the decision. The operator has not promised the outdoor format to either group.

A confirmed indoor message at 10:00 gives the travelling guests four hours before their 14:00 cancellation deadline. Under B, the final format message at 18:00 arrives four hours after it. The activity-fee refund remains available until 20:00 but does not itself reverse those separate travel charges. No additional travel reimbursement budget or change to suppliers’ terms has been arranged; this does not waive any applicable legal rights.

B must therefore send an explicit 10:00 interim notice: format unresolved, final update at 18:00, indoor fallback possible, and the existing fee-refund route. Travelling guests can choose to cancel the activity and their travel before 14:00 rather than accept uncertainty. A will not later switch back outdoors after guests rely on its indoor commitment. Both retain the same safety controls. No probability of a safe courtyard session, forecast accuracy gain or guest satisfaction outcome is supplied.

## Two clocks, not just one forecast

The operator’s information clock points to 18:00; the travelling guests’ reversible-preparation window closes at 14:00. A message can be timely for staff setup yet late for customers. A values an earlier dependable format commitment. B preserves a possible outdoor experience but asks some guests to choose travel before knowing the final format.

## Keep fee refunds and travel reliance separate

Fourteen × HK$400 = HK$5,600 of potential separate travel charges after the deadline. This is not an expected loss, an automatic compensation bill or a saving caused by A. It depends on guests retaining bookings and the outcome they value. Refunding an activity fee is helpful but does not undo a third party’s non-refundable booking.

## Defend a deadline, not an imaginary certainty

A is defensible because the operator can offer a known alternative before the relevant preparation commitment. Its cost is losing the chance of a safe outdoor session even if the later forecast improves. B is also defensible if preserving that chance matters enough and the interim notice lets guests make a genuine, timely exit. Do not tell them to wait until 18:00 while concealing their 14:00 decision. Sixteen local preferences do not cancel the needs of 14 travelling guests.

## Make the notice usable

For A: confirm the indoor workshop, describe it accurately, give the fee-refund contact and 20:00 deadline, and remind guests that safety updates remain possible. For B: say the format is not confirmed, indoor is possible, give the 18:00 update and the same refund route, and directly flag the known 14:00 travel cancellation deadline to affected guests. Send through the agreed direct channel, check delivery and follow up failures. Urgent safety messages are immediate, not held for a scheduled update.

## Instructor note

Draw the timeline 10:00 → 14:00 → 18:00 → 20:00. Ask whose option disappears at each point. The class chooses a service commitment deadline, not a weather threshold, evacuation plan or legal refund entitlement. Both options preserve professional safety control and a funded indoor fallback. Require an honest account of the outdoor option forgone under A or preparation uncertainty under B. No expected-value winner follows without additional probabilities and preferences.
