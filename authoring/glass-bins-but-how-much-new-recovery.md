# More glass in the bins. How much is new recovery?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 536 -->

SUS-44 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Waste recovery · Collection network · Displacement

![Two staff compare a housing-estate route map while a resident drops a glass bottle into a green recycling bin.](../glass-bins-but-how-much-new-recovery-scene.jpg)

## Hong Kong collects waste glass through multiple channels

Hong Kong’s Environmental Protection Department describes a regional collection network for glass containers. In a 2025–26 estimates reply it reported that total glass container collection in 2024 came from channels including community recycling, catering premises and residential premises. The district, options and measurements below are fictional.

## Opening decision

Ka leads a trial in a fictional Hong Kong district. Add puts two new glass bins on a separate route. Move relocates two existing bins to better-used stops on the present route. All locations meet the district’s safety and access checks. Assume both options’ material is accepted for useful glass recovery. There is no processor rejection threshold in this exercise.

The exhibit gives each option’s estimated monthly uplift in glass delivered by the trial network versus keeping the current bins as they are. A separate survey estimates how much of that uplift comes from residents who would otherwise have used an existing off-network drop-off. Collection at all other sites is unchanged except for this displacement.

The district wants the most additional recovered glass across both channels, not just the most glass in its own bins. A route must fit the HK$3,000 monthly spending cap. Costs are incremental relative to keeping the current bins. No emissions factors are supplied.

## Evidence

| Fictional monthly forecast | Add | Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glass uplift in trial network | 540 kg | 380 kg |
| Already recovered elsewhere without the change | 150 kg | 70 kg |
| Extra collection spending | HK$2,800 | HK$1,900 |

**Student question:** Estimate additional recovery across both channels and spending per extra kilogram. Which option meets the district objective? What would you report alongside the quantity collected by the trial network?

## Round two

A follow-up with households near Add reveals that 260 kg, not 150 kg, of its forecast uplift would otherwise have been recovered at an existing drop-off. Move’s estimate is confirmed. The revised figure is a replacement for the earlier estimate, not a second amount to subtract.

**Student question:** Which option now provides more additional recovery within budget? What is the highest amount of Add’s uplift that could come from existing recovery before Add loses its strict lead by weight?

## Debrief · Separate local collection from new recovery

Add initially recovers 540 − 150 = 390 kg that would not otherwise be recovered; Move recovers 380 − 70 = 310 kg. Both fit the cap. Add leads on the district objective by 80 kg, even though it costs about HK$7.18 per extra kilogram compared with HK$6.13 for Move.

## Debrief · The view across both channels changes the choice

With the corrected survey, Add yields 540 − 260 = 280 kg of additional recovery, versus Move’s unchanged 310 kg. Choose Move, which also spends HK$900 less per month. Add’s 540 kg local uplift is still the larger figure, but some of it only moved from another collection channel.

## Debrief · Check the tie threshold

Add ties Move when 540 − displaced kilograms = 310, or at 230 kg displaced. It needs displacement below 230 kg to lead strictly (at most 229 kg if recorded in whole kilograms). Publish the local uplift, estimated displacement and cross-channel total separately.

## Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 to compare outcomes, 4 to revise the estimate, and 5 to discuss how tracking other channels could verify displacement. Move itself can reduce access at an old site; the case assumes trial-wide totals already account for changes within its own route.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All pilot forecasts are fabricated. A change in destination is not a new kilogram of recovered glass when both destinations recover it. The accounting is by mass, not lifecycle environmental impact.

- [Hong Kong EPD, Glass Container Recycling](https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/english/environmentinhk/waste/pro_responsibility/glass-container-recycling.html). Describes the regional collection and treatment arrangement, channels for collection and potential reuse routes.
- [Hong Kong EPD, 2025–26 expenditure estimates reply on glass](https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/sites/default/files/epd/english/news_events/legco/files/fc_25-26_eng.pdf). Breaks out original collection tonnage by source; 2024 collected tonnage is not the same as additional recovery.
- [Hong Kong EPD, Guidelines for Glass Container Recycling in Housing Estates](https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/sites/default/files/epd/glass/HousingEstate_Aug2025.pdf). Recommends reviewing bin location and frequency and coordinating estate and community collections.
