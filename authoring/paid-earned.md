# Paid today. Earned today?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 61 -->

ACC-06 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accounting · Revenue recognition · Advance payments

![A pottery workshop owner, bookkeeper and adult customer discuss paperwork beside shelves of handmade bowls.](../paid-earned-scene.jpg)

## In the news

Ocean Park Corporation’s financial statements for the year ended 30 June 2025, approved on 19 September 2025, describe different revenue timing for different promises. Future-use admission receipts are deferred until tickets are used or expire; annual and unlimited-entry passes are recognised over their validity periods, except during park closure.

The report illustrates why the service promise matters, not a claim that Ocean Park recorded these receipts incorrectly. Our pottery studio is fictional, and its separate sessions are not annual passes.

## Opening decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong pottery studio has launched prepaid workshop packages. During this month, 100 customers each paid HK$1,200 for four sessions, bringing in HK$120,000. The owner wants the monthly management report to celebrate the launch with the headline “HK$120,000 revenue”. You are checking that report before it closes.

Each package promises four distinct, identical sessions with equal standalone selling prices. The whole payment has been collected. There are no opening package balances, refunds, expiries, cancellations, other promised benefits or financing component. Costs have not been supplied. The bookkeeping uses accrual accounting, which separates receiving money from earning it by providing the promised service.

The bank receipts are reconciled, but the attendance records have not yet been checked. The owner says: “These are real paying customers, not expressions of interest. Surely we can report a strong launch.”

## Evidence

| This month’s package sales | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Customers paying | 100 |
| Price per four-session package | HK$1,200 |
| Cash received | HK$120,000 |
| Customer-sessions purchased | 400 |

**Student question:** Would you approve the proposed revenue headline now? What would you verify, and how could the report still recognise the genuine success of collecting HK$120,000?

## Round two

The attendance reconciliation shows that 100 of the 400 purchased customer-sessions were completed by month-end. A customer-session means one customer receiving one session, not a whole group class. None is partly completed at the reporting date.

The other 300 customer-sessions have not started and remain valid services owed to customers. The HK$120,000 cash receipt is unchanged, and the equal-session assumptions still hold.

**Student question:** What cash receipts, earned revenue and remaining service obligation should the report show? How would you explain the difference without calling the launch either a failure or completed profit?

## Allocate the package, then count delivery

Each package contains four equally priced sessions, so allocate HK$1,200 ÷ 4 = HK$300 per customer-session. Equal allocation is justified by these stated assumptions, not by a rule that every bundle must be divided equally.

With 100 customer-sessions completed and none partly delivered, earned revenue is 100 × HK$300 = HK$30,000. The 300 sessions still owed account for 300 × HK$300 = HK$90,000. The two amounts reconcile to the HK$120,000 collected.

## Keep three different measures visible

Cash receipts: HK$120,000. This is the money collected from these packages during the month. It does not become HK$30,000 because revenue is adjusted, and it is not necessarily the closing bank balance after other payments.

Earned revenue: HK$30,000. This reflects the sessions delivered. It is not profit: instructors, materials, rent and other costs have not been provided.

Unearned revenue or contract liability: HK$90,000. This represents the payment allocated to services still owed. Here it is a delivery obligation, not an automatically due HK$90,000 cash refund or an estimate of the cost of running those sessions.

## A good launch can include unfinished work

The studio has attracted paying customers and collected useful cash. Neither achievement disappears. But the report should also show what has been delivered and what staff must still provide.

A clear headline could say: “HK$120,000 collected from new packages; HK$30,000 earned through completed sessions; 300 customer-sessions remain to deliver.” Reserve staff time and capacity for those sessions rather than treating the entire receipt as completed performance.

If the initial records put every receipt into revenue, reclassify the unearned HK$90,000 to the service liability. As later sessions are provided, the appropriate amount moves from that liability to revenue without requiring another customer payment. Real packages with different services, expiry or refund terms need their own analysis.

## Instructor note

Ask students to separate what the bank proves from what attendance records prove. After the reveal, have them calculate HK$300 per customer-session and reconcile HK$30,000 earned plus HK$90,000 unearned to HK$120,000 received.

The decision is how to report a successful prepaid launch faithfully, not whether advance payments are bad. Do not turn the report into a profit calculation, a bonus-policy debate or a recommendation to refund customers. Distinguish customer-sessions from group-class counts. The simplified example is not a complete standards-compliance assessment.
