# They understand the benefit. Can they picture doing it?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 327 -->

MKT-28 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Customer self-efficacy · Product demonstrations · Physical product fit

![A home-organisation demonstrator and prospective buyer stand beside a modular desktop organiser in an ordinary room.](../understand-benefit-picture-setup-scene.jpg)

## A belief about benefits is not a belief about capability

Bandura’s original self-efficacy account distinguishes believing that an action produces a useful outcome from believing oneself capable of performing that action. Separate product-demonstration research examines how showing a process differs from displaying its result. Neither claim means a demonstration can make an unsuitable product fit a customer’s home.

The brand, organiser, customers and budget below are fictional. The decision concerns honest support for informed use, not a promise of sales or a diagnosis of customers’ general abilities.

## Opening decision

A fictional home-organisation brand in Hong Kong sells a modular desktop organiser. It is unfamiliar to the next twenty adult prospective buyers, who have volunteered for a product-information session. The product holds ordinary stationery; it is not wall-mounted, powered or safety-critical. Assembly uses three tool-free steps: align the base, slide in the dividers and secure the supplied clips. The full instructions, price, dimensions and usage limits are available under either communication option.

The organiser needs a clear, level space at least 40 cm wide and 30 cm deep, with 25 cm height clearance. These are the complete supplied space requirements including access for use. The front must face the user; rotating it to exchange width and depth is not a valid installation. The product cannot be cut, compressed or reconfigured to a smaller footprint. No alternative size or paid home-installation service is available this cycle.

One HK$4,000 communication allowance funds either A, a polished benefit advertisement showing a tidy desk and the finished organiser, or D, a five-minute one-to-one hands-on demonstration of the three steps with a chance to practise. Both reach the same twenty prospects within the same period, include the same truthful product information and fit the funded staff time, materials and space. A does not conceal instructions; D does not guarantee effortless success. Only one can be produced this cycle. The objective is informed, suitable adoption, not maximum purchases regardless of fit. A diagnostic conversation about the prospects’ remaining concerns will arrive before committing the allowance.

## Round two

The diagnostic conversations show that all twenty prospects can correctly explain the storage benefit and want that outcome. Twelve have verified spaces measuring 45 cm wide, 35 cm deep and at least 25 cm high. They say they are unsure they can align the base and secure the clips themselves. Their uncertainty is about carrying out setup, not understanding the benefit. No practice result yet proves whether they can complete it independently.

The other eight have only 35 cm of usable width in the intended location; depth and height are sufficient. Measurements and the required orientation are verified. They have no other usable location and cannot modify the furniture. A demonstration cannot create the missing width. The product specification, budget and options remain unchanged. No prospect is required to practise, purchase or disclose personal information beyond the voluntary task discussion.

**Student question:** How would you use the chosen communication for the twelve fitting prospects and respond to the eight with insufficient space? What evidence would distinguish greater setup confidence from successful use or a sale?

## First answer: diagnose the unresolved question

D is a sensible provisional choice for an unfamiliar setup, but unfamiliarity alone does not show that a demonstration is necessary. Ask the customer to describe the benefit, identify the setup steps they doubt they can perform and check the intended space. A could be useful if the outcome itself were unclear; showing more of a desired outcome is less directly responsive to a capability concern.

## The fitting group needs a process, not another promise

For the twelve fitting prospects, choose D: demonstrate each step at normal speed, explain alignment and clip checks, then offer voluntary practice with the same product and instructions. Let customers attempt the task themselves rather than complete it for them and call that independence. Provide help when requested and record what help was needed. Do not imply that everyone must find setup easy.

Self-efficacy here means confidence in completing these particular setup actions. It is not liking the brand, valuing a tidy desk or agreeing that the demonstrator is skilled. Successful supported practice may help, but confidence and actual independent performance must be checked separately.

## The non-fitting group needs an honest boundary

The eight narrower spaces are 40 − 35 = 5 cm short. A 40 cm-wide organiser cannot meet the stated conditions in 35 cm. Do not treat that mismatch as low confidence or ask customers to believe harder. Explain that this product is unsuitable for their verified space and do not press for a purchase. A different size is a future design possibility, not an available solution.

## Measure the action you intend to support

Before and after the demonstration, ask separately how confident the fitting prospects are that they can align the base and secure the clips using the instructions without staff assistance. Then offer a fresh setup attempt and record correct completion, errors, requested assistance and opt-outs. A staff-completed example is not evidence of independent use. Keep voluntary participation and ordinary support intact.

Track any later purchase and reported use separately; neither increased confidence nor an observed attempt equals a sale or sustained home use. The maximum identified fitting group here is twelve of twenty, or 60%, not a predicted conversion rate. A before-and-after change without a comparable control does not prove the demonstration caused it.

## Instructor note

Ask for a provisional medium and diagnostic questions before the reveal. Afterwards, require a different response to the two groups and a measure that does not confuse benefit comprehension, task confidence, actual completion and purchase. Students need no psychometric scale or significance test; their measures should be task-specific and transparently described.

This is buyer capability belief and actual product fit, not seller expertise signalling or checkout friction. Both choices provide full product information and paid staff time. Bandura’s theoretical and measurement work and Ringler and colleagues’ demonstration research address different mechanisms; do not claim the latter proves a self-efficacy effect or guaranteed sales for this organiser. Overseas advertising guidance is background, not Hong Kong legal advice.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All products, costs, participants and measurements are fictional. Participation is voluntary and both options provide full information. The 60% fitting share is not a conversion forecast. The two Bandura sources are related theoretical/measurement work; demonstration research concerns other mechanisms. Retailer guidance supplies a fit-check principle, not these product specifications. - [Albert Bandura (1977), Self-efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change](https://dradamvolungis.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/self-efficacy-unifying-theory-of-behavioral-change-bandura-1977.pdf). Original article distinguishes efficacy from outcome expectations and describes mastery and modelling. Its treatment contexts do not supply consumer conversion rates.
- [Albert Bandura, Guide for Constructing Self-Efficacy Scales](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/websites.uta.edu/dist/1/4994/files/2021/07/Guide-for-Constructing-Self-Efficacy-Scales-Bandura2006.pdf). Author’s measurement guidance distinguishes task-specific capability judgments from intention, outcome expectation and self-worth. No published scale is reproduced.
- [Ringler, Sirianni, Peck and Gustafsson (2024), Does your demonstration tell the whole story?](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Joann-Peck/publication/369962710_Does_your_demonstration_tell_the_whole_story_How_a_process_mindset_and_social_presence_impact_the_effectiveness_of_product_demonstrations/links/6466238cc9802f2f72e516a8/Does-your-demonstration-tell-the-whole-story-How-a-process-mindset-and-social-presence-impact-the-effectiveness-of-product-demonstrations.pdf). Original five-study article examines process versus outcome demonstrations, cognitive flow, narrative transportation and social presence. It is not evidence that this fictional hands-on session improves self-efficacy or sales.
- [IKEA, Step-by-step: find your perfect clothes storage system](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/rooms/bedroom/how-to/step-by-step-find-your-perfect-clothes-storage-system-puba0dfefd0/). Primary retailer guidance calls for checking width, depth, height, obstructions and opening space. The fictional organiser and measurements are not an IKEA product.
- [US Federal Trade Commission, Advertising FAQs: A Guide for Small Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business). Primary guidance discusses truthful express and implied claims, material omissions and evidence. Used as a communication safeguard, not a statement of Hong Kong law.
