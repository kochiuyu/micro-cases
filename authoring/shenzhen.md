# Would more stock listings bring Shenzhen homebuyers back?

Case 3 · Shenzhen · 10–15 minutes · Wealth effect, causal inference

![Two journalists weigh a housing headline](../shenzhen-scene.jpg)

## Opening decision

You edit a Shenzhen business newsletter. A reporter proposes the headline “More IPOs will bring homebuyers back.” You have one exhibit before deadline.

## Evidence

| Measure | 2021 | 2022 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Newly listed firms | 233 | 190 |
| Listed firms at year end | 2,578 | 2,743 |
| New-home price index, December 2021 = 100 | 100 | 99.8 |
| Resale-home price index, December 2021 = 100 | 100 | 96.3 |

**Student question** Publish the headline, qualify it, or hold it? Cite one observation and name one missing link.

## Round two

Suppose new listings rebound next year. Most shares sold belong to investors outside Shenzhen. Local households’ shareholdings, pay and mortgage terms stay the same.

**Student question** Which Shenzhen buyers now have more money for a down payment? What single change to the scenario would make a housing rebound more plausible?

## Debrief

A listing can turn a founder’s illiquid stake into spendable funds when shares can be sold. For that to lift Shenzhen housing demand, the proceeds must reach people who want to buy homes there. A household whose existing shares rise in value can also increase spending at the same salary. That is a wealth effect. A pay rise provides a separate income channel.

Weak business prospects could lead firms to postpone listings and households to postpone purchases. Both measures fell over this interval. These two observations cannot tell us which changed first or whether one caused the other. Ask for evidence on where selling shareholders live and whether their home purchases changed relative to comparable households.

## Instructor note

0–4 minutes. Take an editorial vote. Ask a student to distinguish a flow of new listings from the stock of listed firms.

4–8 minutes. Reveal who owns the shares. Ask two students with different votes to identify the household in their explanation.

8–12 minutes. Compare proposed headlines. A defensible version is “New listings slowed as resale housing weakened. Who received the proceeds?”

## Sources and case assumptions

All information needed for the classroom decisions is provided in the case. The sources below are optional background, not assigned reading.


- [SZSE Market Overview, December 2021](https://docs.static.szse.cn/www/market/periodical/month/W020220110583931660153.html). Annual new listings and year-end listed firms.
- [SZSE Market Overview, December 2022](https://docs.static.szse.cn/www/market/periodical/month/W020230103526615954513.html). Same measures one year later.
- [National Bureau of Statistics, December 2022 housing prices](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202301/t20230116_1891673.html). Tables 1 and 2, Shenzhen row, same month last year = 100. Subtract 100 to obtain the percentage change.
- [Cash to spend: IPO wealth and house prices](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/cash-to-spend-ipo-wealth-and-house-prices/). Compares house prices in areas exposed to IPOs with a synthetic comparison group. Use its design to ask what comparison Shenzhen would need.
- [Stock Market Wealth and the Real Economy](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25959). Uses regional differences in stock ownership to study how changes in equity wealth affect local spending. This motivates the second-round question about who owns the shares.

The newsroom, headline and shareholder scenario are fictional.
