# One exception. A fair rule?

<!-- legacy-case-id: 189 -->

GVR-16 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accountable discretion · Consistent treatment · Evidence standards

![A workshop receptionist and customer discuss plain paperwork beside a phone.](../same-exception-same-reasons-scene.jpg)

## Clear reasons matter

On 5 September 2025, Hong Kong’s Ombudsman announced recommendations to improve the Property Management Services Authority’s complaint handling, including more substantive progress explanations. That public-sector investigation is background on accountable communication, not a ruling about the invented venue below.

## Opening decision

A fictional Hong Kong workshop venue normally charges HK$300 to move a booking. Its published policy allows the duty manager to waive that charge when credible documentation shows an unexpected transport disruption outside the customer’s control materially prevented timely arrival, and the customer notified the venue before the session began. The policy does not require one particular document format. Existing consumer rights remain unaffected.

A customer notified reception before the session and supplied a dated operator service alert plus a short explanation of the intended journey. The alert confirms disruption, but the booking file does not yet establish how it affected that journey. The customer requests a waiver. No charge has been collected, and the venue can seek a proportionate clarification within 48 hours without delaying the rescheduled booking or imposing an extra charge.

The manager is authorised to decide under the published criteria, not to favour familiar customers or invent a new certificate requirement. Should the current evidence support a waiver, the normal charge, or a brief request for the missing connection? Explain the evidence rather than simply choosing the friendliest or strictest response.

## Evidence

| Published criterion | Evidence currently available |

| Unexpected transport disruption outside the customer’s control | Dated operator alert |
| Material effect on timely arrival | Intended journey described; connection not yet established |
| Notice before the session | Reception timestamp confirms it |
| Required document format | No exclusive format specified |

**Student question:** What would you do with this waiver request now? Give a provisional decision or a proportionate clarification request, identify the missing fact, and explain how the same standard would apply to another customer.

## Round two

For the second decision, a supervisor has completed a review of two earlier requests, not the still-incomplete current file. Both earlier customers gave timely notice, dated operator alerts and journey evidence that reliably established the same qualifying disruption and material delay. No relevant difference in their circumstances remains.

Manager Lin waived the first charge after assessing those documents. Manager Pat rejected the second solely because there was no personally addressed transport-company certificate, although the policy never required one. The rejected customer paid HK$300. Both managers kept the documents, but neither recorded how each criterion had been assessed. There is no evidence of dishonesty or personal favouritism; the evidence standards had diverged.

The venue can refund an incorrectly applied charge and review comparable recent rejections. It will not reclaim the first customer’s waiver to manufacture uniformity or retrospectively add stricter booking conditions. The current customer still needs the missing journey connection assessed. The supervisor wants a practical way to preserve justified discretion without making every disruption claim automatically refundable.

**Student question:** How would you resolve the two earlier requests and improve the next decision? Specify a consistent evidence record, an explanation and review route, while preserving individual assessment rather than automatically waiving every charge.

## Consistency is about relevant reasons

The two earlier files now meet the same stated criteria. A certificate-only hurdle supplies no relevant distinction because it was never part of the policy. Reassess the rejected file under the published standard and refund the HK$300 incorrectly collected. Do not undo the justified waiver. The incomplete current request is different: assess the missing journey connection instead of copying either earlier outcome.

## Make discretion reviewable

Use a short record showing each criterion, the evidence supporting it, any unresolved point, the decision maker and the reasons. Different reliable document formats can establish the same fact. A future rejection should explain which criterion was not established and how the customer can supply clarification or request review, without exposing another customer’s information.

## Correct a pattern without inventing a blanket entitlement

Check recent rejections for the same unpublished certificate requirement and reconsider affected files on their own evidence. Tell staff what the existing policy requires and compare a few worked examples. A claimed disruption alone does not establish its effect on arrival. Keep only proportionate records with appropriate access and retention, and review whether decisions are consistently reasoned, not merely whether waiver totals match.

## Instructor note

Use the first round to separate an uncertain factual connection from a new documentation hurdle. Then reveal two completed comparable files and ask groups to design a short discretion record. The later review does not retroactively prove the current customer’s claim. Fair process need not mean identical outcomes for different evidence. This is a fictional policy-design exercise, not legal advice or permission to narrow customer rights; public-sector guidance is adapted as a governance lens.

## Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading.

All venue terms, cases, charges, documents and management choices are fictional. Published criteria are supplied in full for the exercise; this is not a judgment about enforceability of an actual rescheduling charge. Customer rights remain protected. The opening file lacks a verified journey connection; the reveal concerns two earlier files for which the supervisor has established the same qualifying facts. This separation avoids treating new evidence as known in round one. The second customer paid HK$300, refundable under the stipulated venue authority; the first waiver is not clawed back. No fraud, favouritism or actual operator wrongdoing is alleged. Different evidence formats may support equivalent facts; no automatic refund for all claims or retrospective condition change. Five relevant source bodies read, with institutional overlap: two Hong Kong Ombudsman documents, two UK Ombudsman principles pages and LGSCO guidance. These are public-administration principles adapted cautiously to a private fictional venue, not Hong Kong consumer-law advice, binding standards for this venue or independent causal studies.
