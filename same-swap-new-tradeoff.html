PPL-42 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Shift allocation · Voluntary exchange · Distributional fairness · Employee voice

The same swap. A new trade-off.

A roster has to do more than fill every slot

In October 2025, Hong Kong’s Government told the Legislative Council that Hospital Authority departments allocate shifts according to operational needs. A separate interview study in England found that staff also considered how undesirable shifts were shared. The fictional private clinic below is not a Hospital Authority unit or a report of either workplace.

Your classroom decision

You manage a fictional Hong Kong clinic’s two equal-sized reception crews, East and West. Over the next four weeks, the clinic must cover twelve fixed special assignments: four weekday closes (D), four weekend opens (P) and four weekend handovers (H). No assignment can be dropped. The normal desk work and staffing plan stay unchanged.

The existing rotation gives each crew two of each assignment. One optional exchange gives an East weekend open to West in return for a West weekday close. The crews may agree to zero, one or two such paired exchanges. Handovers stay fixed. Every option covers all twelve assignments and gives each crew six.

The scheduling lead has checked that all listed options preserve staffing, qualifications, agreed individual paid hours, rest, fixed shift premiums and the same notice. An exchange requires each affected colleague’s explicit agreement; declining does not affect pay, future requests or performance reviews. The checked rotation is the fallback. No one has to tell the team why a slot is inconvenient.

The table gives fictional crew planning scores, agreed for this exercise. Higher means more inconvenient. Add the points to compare rosters on this common classroom scale. These are not health measures, wage rates or comparable readings of individual wellbeing.

Round 2 · More information

Before the pilot starts, the site changes for all four weekday closes. The reassignment keeps paid hours, shift premiums, notice and all rest and safety checks unchanged. The different journey makes a closing assignment more inconvenient for East on the supplied planning scale. The East score for D rises from 1 to 5. West’s D score remains 4; every other score is unchanged.

An East crew representative asks the manager not to increase either crew’s score above its own no-exchange score for the revised site. Another colleague proposes keeping two exchanges because that plan still gives the lowest combined score. Neither view is a substitute for each affected colleague agreeing. No old expression of interest counts as approval for the new site.

Your decision

What would you confirm for the revised site? Compare the total and higher crew score for zero, one and two exchanges, then explain what the no-worse-off rule protects and what the clinic gives up by using it.

Debrief · A swap can help both crews

Initially, each crew scores 14 under rotation. With one paired exchange East and West score 11 each; with two they score 8 each. Totals are 28, 22 and 16 respectively. Each exchange removes one 4-point assignment and adds one 1-point assignment for each crew. Counts, coverage and payroll do not change. Invite two exchanges if everyone affected freely agrees.

Debrief · The new site changes who gains

At the revised site, zero exchanges give East 22 and West 14, total 36. One gives 23 and 11, total 34. Two give 24 and 8, total 32. The higher crew score is respectively 22, 23 or 24. Each exchange now adds one point to East while saving three for West. A lower total no longer makes both crews better off.

Debrief · Use a fallback people can trust

Keep the checked rotation under the proposed no-worse-off rule. It protects East against a further increase above its already worse new-site baseline. In this menu it gives up four points of possible aggregate improvement compared with two swaps. That gap is not a money loss or a reason to pressure anyone. Ask whether a different future arrangement is possible rather than treating an earlier expression of interest as a commitment.

Debrief · Crew totals can hide repeat burden

Offer every eligible colleague the same private request window. Publish the coverage tests, the rule for competing requests and a route to correct errors. Review who repeatedly gets the least-wanted slots through restricted records, without showing other people’s preferences or asking for private explanations. Track coverage and declined requests without penalising refusal. Recheck preferences when assignment conditions materially change.

Instructor note

Reveal the site change only after groups make a first decision. Ask them to distinguish equal counts, lower total points, the higher crew total and protection against making a crew worse off. Unlike removing unnecessary meetings, there is no work to eliminate here. Pay, notice and hard protections are fixed, not offered in exchange for a preference.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The clinic, crews, sites, assignments, score scale, protections check and correspondence are hypothetical. All assignment counts and burden points are classroom values. No individual preference, identity or reason is disclosed. The case does not estimate productivity or health impact. Draft slot only; no public number assigned.

  1. HKSAR Government · LCQ12: Manpower for public healthcare (8 October 2025). In a reply prepared in consultation with the Hospital Authority, the Government describes departments’ rostering around operational needs. This public-sector arrangement is context, not the fictional clinic’s rule.
  2. Hong Kong Labour Department · Employment Ordinance FAQ: Rest Days. Official guidance explains rest-day protections for employees under continuous contracts. The exercise holds applicable protections fixed and does not apply any exception or reinterpret eligibility.
  3. BMC Nursing · Negotiating competing priorities in nurse shift scheduling (2025). Original interview research in five English NHS trusts distinguishes organizational coverage, individual preferences and perceived sharing of undesirable shifts. It does not estimate the case’s scores or establish a causal effect.
  4. BMC Nursing · Nurse rostering: scheduling processes, benefits, limitations and fatigue risks (2024). Original interviews with nurse rosterers at three Victorian sites describe requests under self-rostering and reasons not to equate preference with a safe schedule. The exercise stipulates feasible options instead.
  5. CHI 2020 · Fairness and decision-making in collaborative shift scheduling systems. Author-hosted original study uses interviews and vignettes to distinguish abstract equality from responses to specific conflicts. The case adapts that distinction without requiring personal disclosures.
