HSP-01 · Snow Town · Tourism · 10–15 minutes

Why keep a promise to a guest who will never return?

A couple arrives at your mountain guesthouse just after the last bus has left. You quoted them ¥400 for the room. They have travelled a long way to see the snow and expect this to be their only visit. They have little choice about where to sleep tonight.

You could honor the quote or ask for another ¥80 at check-in. For this exercise, assume the guests would pay, serving them costs the same either way, and your objective is to maximize profit. First consider a world in which future travelers have no way to learn what happened tonight.

Would you charge ¥400 or ¥480? Explain your choice.

Round 2 · Let future guests read reviews

Now suppose the couple can post a review. Adding the surcharge creates a 50% chance of losing one future booking. That booking would earn ¥200 after costs, valued in today’s money. Everything else stays the same.

Discuss

Would you change your price? How low would the chance of losing the booking have to fall before you would add the surcharge?

Debrief · Compare the two choices

With no future consequence, the surcharge earns an extra ¥80. With reviews, its expected cost is 0.50 × ¥200 = ¥100. Charging extra now reduces expected profit by ¥20, so the profit-maximizing owner honors the quote.

At a 40% chance of losing the booking, the owner breaks even because 0.40 × ¥200 = ¥80. Below 40%, the surcharge pays. Above 40%, keeping the promise pays. The calculation uses the profit on the lost booking because the owner also avoids the cost of serving that guest.

The couple never returns in either round. Reputation matters because one guest’s experience can influence a different guest’s decision. The assumed 50% is the chance of losing business, including the possibility that a review is never written or read.

Instructor note · Running the discussion

Objective. Explain how information reaching future customers can change a seller’s incentive today.

0–3 minutes. Show only the opening. Take a vote and ask one student on each side to explain. If someone says “a surprise fee is unfair,” acknowledge the objection and ask what a seller concerned only with profit would do under the stated assumptions.

3–8 minutes. Open round 2. Ask pairs to calculate the expected cost and vote again. If someone argues that the couple never returns, ask whose future booking is at risk.

8–12 minutes. Reveal the debrief. Ask for one real-world reason the chance of losing a booking might be below 40%, and one reason it might be above it. For a quick exit ticket, ask what changes if the future booking earns only ¥100 after costs.

Exit ticket answer. At 50%, the expected loss falls to ¥50. Charging extra now gains ¥30 in expectation.

Round 2 · After the first discussion

Adding the surcharge now creates a 50% chance of losing one future booking worth ¥200 in profit today. What would you charge? At what probability would you be indifferent?
