ECO-41 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Price discovery · Benchmarks · Expected cash flow

The shares jumped. What changed?

Reported context

In late September 2024, Hong Kong’s stock market rallied as Mainland authorities announced measures spanning monetary policy, property and capital markets. HKEX also reported trading records over that period. Trading turnover is the value exchanged, not a price change or a measure of what investors expect any one company to earn.

Your classroom decision

At a Hong Kong brokerage, junior analyst Iris is writing a client note. The fictional listed packaging firm Harbour Fibre announced a supply contract before the market opened. Its shares rose from HK$100 at yesterday’s close to HK$108 at 11 a.m.

Her editor asks for a sentence that separates the broad market move from what the contract might explain. Returns in the exhibit use the same closing and 11 a.m. times. There were no distributions in this window. Peer shares are those of three similar listed packagers, equally weighted; Harbour Fibre is not included.

Security or basketReturn through 11 a.m.
Harbour Fibre+8%
Broad Hong Kong index+5%
Three packaging peers+7%
Your decision

Would you publish “the contract raised Harbour Fibre’s value by 8%,” or would you write something narrower?

Round 2 · More information

The contract adds deliveries for one year. Iris learns it replaces an expiring contract at the same annual revenue, but the new contract requires more upfront spending. She finds no reliable estimate of net cash flow.

For a deliberately simplified check, consider a one-year claim: before the move its expected payment was HK$110 and the required return was 10%. Hold the expected payment at HK$110 and ask which required return would produce HK$108.

Your decision

How would you revise the note now, and what evidence would let you defend a change in expected cash flow?

Debrief

Against the broad index the simple difference is 3 percentage points; against packaging peers it is 1 point. This depends on benchmark choice, and neither residual identifies the contract’s effect.

Holding the expected payment at HK$110, the required return consistent with HK$108 is 110 ÷ 108 − 1, about 1.85%. Holding the rate at 10% instead would require an expected HK$118.80 payment. A real share is not a one-year claim.

The stock gained 8% in a rising market and rose about one point more than these peers. The move is consistent with firm-specific news, but the new contract’s contribution is not identified. Seek a forecast of incremental receipts, costs and investment against the expiring contract.

Instructor note

Give students two minutes to draft a headline and three to compare benchmarks. Reveal the contract replacement and ask them to make the one-year calculation in pairs.

What would make the peer gap larger or smaller even if the contract had no effect? Ask for a different exposure to market news or a simultaneous company announcement.

Boundary of the evidence

One trading window cannot separate cash-flow expectations, the required return and trading pressure. The simple peer difference is not a formal estimate of abnormal return or causality.
