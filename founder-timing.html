Equal partners. Earned when?

In the news · More than a handshake

The official programme for Cyberport’s Venture Capital Forum listed a startup clinic on 6 November 2025 covering business formation and founder arrangements, alongside other practical issues.

The scheduled topic highlights a choice that can arise before a young company sells anything: how should partners connect ownership and future work? The programme does not tell us what advice any attendee received. Our founders below are fictional, not clinic participants.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Two Hong Kong founders are developing a room-sensor service for small offices. They have made equal contributions to a basic prototype and want both to have the opportunity to earn an equal founder allocation. Substantial product development, installation and customer support still lie ahead.

One founder proposes making the whole agreed allocation unconditional from the outset: “We trust each other, and this recognises that we are equal partners.” The other wants to recognise the prototype work but attach agreed conditions to the part intended to reward future involvement.

They have not signed formation or share documents. Students are choosing what they should discuss before doing so, not changing anyone’s existing ownership. Salary, intellectual-property ownership and decision-making authority would also need separate agreement.

Round 2 · One fact changes

When they compare their plans, one founder can currently commit to working on the venture for only six months. The other expects to keep building it for several years.

The first founder has disclosed this honestly and may later be able to stay longer. There is no allegation of misconduct. Their equal prototype contributions and equal potential allocation remain unchanged, and nothing has been signed.

No vesting schedule, upfront credit, departure rule or percentage split has been specified. Time committed is one input to a discussion, not a complete measure of value.

Your decision

Does the six-month limit change your proposal? How could you recognise completed work, preserve equal potential opportunity and address future involvement without pretending they are the same question?

Separate the size from the timing

An equal potential allocation answers how much each founder could receive under the agreed arrangement. It does not settle when that allocation becomes unconditional or what happens if a role changes.

Vesting means making the right to retain some equity dependent on meeting agreed conditions over time. It can apply to both founders under the same principles while leaving their potential allocations equal. Actual mechanisms and rights depend on properly prepared documents and applicable law.

Recognise the past; discuss the future

The prototype work is already done. The future development and service work is not. Identify what portion of the proposed allocation rewards each before debating a schedule. No percentage can be deduced from the information supplied.

The six-month disclosure makes the mismatch in expectations visible. It need not justify punishment, unequal treatment or a claim that the shorter-term founder adds little value. They might agree a different role, recognise substantial completed work upfront, or use reciprocal conditions for future contribution.

Someone defending an unconditional allocation should explain why the value already contributed warrants it and how the company would resource later work. Someone defending conditions should explain fairness to both founders, including work already done.

A schedule does not settle the whole partnership

Clarify expected duties, availability, credit for past work and what happens if either person leaves or changes role. Discuss notice, transition and fair treatment of different circumstances. Neither founder should be able to rewrite the arrangement unilaterally after the fact.

Ownership timing does not automatically resolve board votes, deadlock, pay, intellectual property or future funding. Nor does staying a certain number of months prove high-quality contribution. Obtain appropriate Hong Kong legal and tax advice before implementing real arrangements; this case supplies no contract or recommended schedule.

Instructor note

Allow two minutes for the first vote, two for separating allocation from timing, two for the reveal and six for revised proposals. Ask whether equality requires unconditional ownership on day one. Then ask what recognises prior work and what rewards work still to come.

A strong answer explains a fair process for both founders, not a universal percentage or an automatic forfeiture rule. Compare professional perspectives without importing US tax procedures or assuming a Silicon Valley convention is Hong Kong law. The decision is about a proposed arrangement before signing, not taking shares from an existing owner.
