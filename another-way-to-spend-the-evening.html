STR-18 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Substitution · Competitive boundaries · Customer purpose

The rival across the road. Or a different way to meet the need?

Viewing choices extend beyond one category

On 20 February 2025, Hong Kong’s Communications Authority released findings from its 2024 broadcasting survey. The report describes viewing habits and reasons for using Internet-based media, including convenience. It is not a study of lost customers at cinemas. Our fictional screening venue supplies its own small interview exercise below; none of its figures comes from that official survey.

Your classroom decision

A fictional independent Hong Kong screening venue has seen fewer repeat visits. Its owner initially watches the cinema across the road: that cinema has a simpler booking journey and a lower pair-ticket offer. The owner has one funded HK$20,000 development envelope for a four-week optional trial in currently unused sessions. Existing bookings, ordinary screenings, accessibility, safety and staff hours remain protected.

A improves booking and funds a clearly priced pair offer for the venue’s normal screenings. It competes more directly with other cinemas on convenience and total price. B develops a small-group film-and-conversation session: a licensed short film followed by facilitated discussion, with bookable start slots at 6:00, 7:30 or 9:00 p.m. It tries to make a shared evening out distinctive from watching video at home. B is not unrestricted on-demand viewing and cannot start at any time.

Each trial fits the same all-in envelope, including staff, promotion, licences and any price support. Capacity and rights for the stated content and public use are already verified; no consumer streaming subscription is used for public exhibition. No copyrighted content needs to be copied for this exercise. The team can prepare only one trial properly. There are no comparable demand or margin forecasts, and neither option has already been sold.

Which trial would you fund first, and what customer purpose are you assuming when you define the venue’s competitors?

The venue invited 40 adult former customers who had agreed to research contact; 20 voluntarily answered. Each described one most recent occasion when they had considered this venue but did not attend. Interviewers coded exactly one outcome and one main reason per respondent. Four had no shared viewing occasion at all, so do not count them as captured by a competitor. A single person may use both home viewing and cinemas on other dates; this classification concerns the reported occasion, not a permanent customer identity.

No respondent has evaluated or committed to either proposed trial. B’s discussion may appeal to the four who valued conversation, but travel and scheduled slots remain; it cannot be assumed to win the six who wanted flexible starts. A addresses reported booking or pair-price concerns more directly, but even those six have not promised to return. Nothing is known about the other 20 invited people. No retention rate, willingness to pay or response probability is supplied.

Would you change your trial choice after these accounts? Distinguish direct rivalry, substitution and no occasion, then identify what you would need to learn before committing beyond the trial.

Debrief: name the job before the rival

If the job is “see this film in a cinema”, another cinema is the obvious rival. If it is “enjoy screen entertainment with friends”, home viewing may be a substitute. If people cannot arrange to meet, neither a better cinema nor a home-viewing service necessarily solves the problem. These boundaries depend on the occasion and purpose, not just the provider’s industry label.

A broader boundary does not make every person recoverable

Among the 20 respondents, 6/20 = 30% chose another cinema, 10/20 = 50% watched at home and 4/20 = 20% had no shared occasion. Those are descriptive shares of respondents only. Do not divide the ten home viewers by all forty invitees and call that a market switching rate, or assume the other twenty behaved similarly. The study broadens the competitive question but does not establish how much revenue either trial will generate.

Choose what to test, not an automatic winner

A has a narrower, more direct link to the six provider-switching accounts. B explores whether a hosted shared experience offers enough value over staying home, but does not reproduce home’s full flexibility. Either first trial is defensible if the manager states the customer purpose, the unresolved constraint and the evidence sought. Assess actual booking, attendance, paid contribution and reasons for refusal within the protected trial; expressions of interest and favourable conversation are not sales. A larger rollout needs a viable operating and financial case, not merely the largest interview category.

Instructor note

Ask students to draw a purpose-centred map rather than list every entertainment business as equally competitive. Contrast direct rivalry, a substitute route to a similar purpose and no occasion. Do not turn the 20 accounts into causal market-share losses. STR-09 examines activity-system fit; this case identifies the competitive boundary before choosing a response. No real outreach, ticket sale or film distribution is authorised by the classroom exercise.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All venue facts, trial resources, session slots, interview invitations and responses are original fiction. Forty adult former customers were invited with valid research-contact permission; twenty answered. Outcomes 6+10+4=20 and reasons 4+2=6 and 6+4=10 are mutually exclusive for the single recalled occasion. No claim of representativeness, causal substitution, lifetime value or trial conversion is warranted. All-in HK$20,000 is identical for either alternative and includes required content/exhibition rights, staffing and price support. Existing promises, accessibility and safety remain funded. Neither choice creates an unlimited flexible-start service or a right to publicly show consumer streaming content.
