FIN-29 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Liquidation preferences · Exit distributions · Conversion rights

The same ownership. The same exit proceeds?

The reporting question

Public model venture terms distinguish priority payments, participation in remaining proceeds and conversion to ordinary shares. Equal headline ownership therefore need not imply equal exit distributions. The contract’s definitions matter, including what triggers a distribution and which claims are paid first.

The figures and offers below are fictional. This is a simplified distribution exercise, not an investment recommendation, valuation, legal conclusion or transaction. Foreign model language is background only.

Your classroom decision

Two founders compare alternative funding offers for their Hong Kong startup. Each supplies HK$2 million now and gives its investor 20% ownership on an as-converted basis: if all shares became ordinary shares, the investor would own 20% and founders together 80%. Only one offer would be accepted. All other funding, control and assistance terms are identical for this exercise. There are no employees’ shares, options, other investors, later dilution, dividends or side agreements.

At a qualifying cash exit, V denotes the total HK dollars available to these shareholders after all debts, transaction costs and other prior claims have already been paid. V is not enterprise value. The same V applies to either offer. Examine scenarios of HK$1 million, HK$6 million and HK$15 million, with no probabilities supplied. The original HK$2 million investment is not added again to V.

The opening worksheet compares payouts while the investor remains preferred, not a final election. Under A, pay the investor up to HK$2 million first; founders receive all remaining proceeds and the investor receives no share of that remainder. Under B, pay up to HK$2 million first, then divide any remainder 20% to the investor and 80% to founders, with no participation cap. If proceeds are insufficient, investor receipts cannot exceed V and founders receive zero. The conversion clause will be checked before a final offer recommendation; nothing has been signed.

Round 2 · More information

The full clause confirms that A’s investor may instead convert to ordinary shares immediately before the exit and receive 20% of the entire V, giving up the priority payment completely. The investor elects whichever route gives more cash; at equality either route is allowed. Conversion does not create another payment, change V or dilute the founders further. B may also convert on the same 20% basis, but its uncapped preferred participation route remains available. All other stipulated terms are unchanged.

The founders want the offer that leaves them at least as much cash at every supplied exit scenario, holding all non-distribution factors equal. Recalculate the elected outcomes, find A’s conversion threshold and make that narrow recommendation. No scenario probabilities are available, so do not calculate an expected return or infer how likely an exit is. Actual enforceability, tax, investor suitability and negotiations are outside the exercise.

Your decision

When investors elect their better route, what changes at the three exits, where is A’s conversion threshold, and which offer better serves the founders’ stated comparison?

First answer: staying preferred

All amounts in HK$ million. At V of 1, both offers pay investor 1 and founders 0. At V of 6, A pays investor 2 and founders 4; B pays investor 2 + 20% × 4 = 2.8 and founders 3.2. At V of 15, A’s preferred route pays investor 2 and founders 13; B pays investor 2 + 20% × 13 = 4.6 and founders 10.4.

These are conditional preferred-route calculations, not A’s final election at the largest exit. At V of 6, the investor’s cash share is 33.33% under A and 46.67% under B, despite the same 20% as-converted ownership. At V of 1, both receive 100% of available shareholder cash, yet recover only half their original investment. Priority is not a guarantee of full repayment.

Second answer: electing conversion

A compares the available priority payment, min(V, HK$2m), with 20% of V. For positive proceeds below HK$10m, remaining preferred is better. At HK$10m, both routes give HK$2m; above that, conversion gives more. At zero, both give zero. At V of HK$15m, A converts and receives HK$3m; founders receive HK$12m, not HK$13m. The HK$1m and HK$6m outcomes do not change.

Participation is not conversion plus preference

For V above HK$2m, B’s preferred receipt is HK$2m + 0.20 × (V − HK$2m). This exceeds pure conversion, 0.20 × V, by HK$1.6m. For positive V up to HK$2m, B receives V, also exceeding 20% of V. B therefore stays preferred at all positive supplied exits. It shares the remainder, not 20% of the entire exit on top of HK$2m.

Choose within the stated scope

The elected founder outcomes at exits 1, 6 and 15 million are respectively 0, 4 and 12 million under A, versus 0, 3.2 and 10.4 million under B. A is no worse in any supplied scenario and strictly better at 6 and 15 million. Recommend A under the stipulated equal-other-terms comparison. At the low exit both offers exhaust available proceeds; neither protects founders there.

This is distribution arithmetic, not a claim that A is always the best financing in reality. Assistance, control, other securities, future rounds and negotiated terms may differ outside the exercise. Do not call the 20% figure a guaranteed exit share or add the investment to cash already defined as available at exit.

Instructor note

First require two recipients’ payouts to add to V for all three scenarios. Keep the initial comparison explicitly conditional on remaining preferred. After revealing conversion, ask which cell changes and why founders cannot force the investor to retain the lower payout. Derive the threshold from 0.20 × V = HK$2m, then check the insufficient-proceeds boundary.

This differs from a convertible instrument’s later financing conversion: ownership here is held fixed, while exit distribution rights differ. It is not a cap-table valuation or an actual securities transaction.
