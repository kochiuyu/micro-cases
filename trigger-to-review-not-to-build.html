SUS-30 · Fictional waterfront neighbourhood · 15–20 minutes · Adaptive pathways · Evidence triggers · Participatory governance

A new safeguard. Who helps decide when it changes?

A pathway needs both evidence and decision rules

The Thames Estuary 2100 approach combines monitoring with scheduled plan updates rather than treating an adaptation plan as a once-only decision. Its 2026 monitoring review illustrates why evidence quality, asset condition and changing community exposure can matter alongside water levels.

New Zealand’s 2024 coastal guidance distinguishes signals, decision triggers and the point when an option no longer meets objectives, allowing time to act. NIWA’s account stresses community engagement and uncertainty; the IPCC assessment highlights inclusive, flexible planning and the risks of locking in maladaptation. These sources inform a fictional governance exercise, not engineering thresholds or emergency instructions.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A waterfront neighbourhood group advises a local authority on a courtyard flood-adaptation plan. The authority retains statutory decisions, emergency response, maintenance and safety duties. A qualified engineer has approved existing protection for continued use under its inspection and emergency procedures. This is not a choice to ignore a safety warning.

The group compares Calendar, a full plan review every three years, with Trigger, the same three-year backstop plus an earlier review whenever the courtyard sensor reports water at or above 0.30 metres above the local reference mark for at least ten continuous minutes. Both retain routine inspections, official warnings and urgent responses to credible danger, regardless of review dates. The sensor threshold is invented for teaching and is a planning signal, not a real safe-depth rule.

Under either approach, a full review examines repair, a removable barrier trial, drainage improvements and potentially a permanent wall. No option is preselected. Construction needs engineering assessment, permissions, funding and authority approval. The wall would take eighteen months from an authorised decision to completion. The group has no forecast of when current protection might cease to meet objectives, so the eighteen-month lead time cannot yet prove that any trigger is early enough.

A HK$60,000 annual planning envelope covers monitoring, evidence checks and engagement, separately from protected emergency and maintenance budgets. Calendar’s fixed activities cost HK$20,000; Trigger’s enhanced checks and engagement cost HK$35,000. Each additional triggered full review costs HK$10,000. All figures are complete annual planning costs for this exercise. Neither option is justified solely by hotel or property owners’ preferences: tenants, shop workers, people with access needs and nearby communities must be able to participate.

Round 2 · More information

In the first year, the sensor reports 0.34 metres continuously for twelve minutes. It therefore meets the stated raw-data trigger once. Inspection later finds a loose mounting that makes readings unreliable. An independent reference gauge and site observations do not confirm this event’s depth. The available evidence neither verifies a threshold crossing nor proves there was no flood risk.

The authority immediately checks conditions under its existing safety procedures. For the classroom scenario, the engineer authorises a reversible temporary precaution while the instrument is repaired: a pre-designed removable barrier with supervised accessible routing, without blocking emergency exits or transferring water risk to neighbours. Qualified staff deploy it; residents are not asked to improvise a flood defence. Its separate operational funding is already secured.

The group has two new planning-cost quotes: HK$8,000 for independent sensor verification and repair, and HK$10,000 for one expedited full plan review. Both are additional to Trigger’s HK$35,000 fixed activities; there have been no other triggered reviews this year. The group can publish a provisional incident record, invite affected groups to review the evidence, and ask the authority to decide next steps with reasons. It cannot authorise an irreversible wall.

Future triggers need a pre-agreed verification route, named responsible people, a response deadline and a procedure for unreliable or missing data. Emergency action must not wait for statistical confirmation or community voting. Conversely, a single suspect observation must not automatically commit the neighbourhood to permanent construction. No calibrated flood probabilities or wall benefit estimates are provided.

Answer the review-design choice

Prefer Trigger with the three-year backstop, provided monitoring and response capacity are credible. Calendar is administratively simpler and costs HK$15,000 less in fixed annual activities, but a calendar alone can delay consideration of change. Neither approach replaces emergency duties. Trigger does not mean construct immediately; it initiates structured consideration.

Agree objectives with affected groups, then define observable evidence, reliable collection, verification, responsibilities and review consequences. Estimate the time before objectives might fail under plausible scenarios and compare it with option-specific design, consent and construction lead times. Eighteen months for a wall is an input to this analysis, not evidence that the 0.30-metre signal is sufficient.

Answer the suspect reading

The raw reading meets the proposed signal: 0.34 ≥ 0.30 metres and twelve ≥ ten minutes. Its validity is unresolved because the mounting is unreliable. Record both the observation and the defect; do not silently erase the alert or treat it as a verified trend. Keep the authorised reversible precaution and current emergency procedures while repairing and independently checking the instrument.

Proceed with one expedited evidence-and-options review, not an automatic wall. Trigger fixed activities HK$35,000 + verification/repair HK$8,000 + review HK$10,000 = HK$53,000, leaving HK$7,000 of the HK$60,000 planning envelope. A further HK$10,000 review would require HK$3,000 more than the remaining envelope; seek authorised funding rather than divert protected emergency resources. No avoided-damage benefit has been calculated.

A defensible verification and participation rule

A sensor alert opens an incident record and prompts the responsible officer’s immediate safety check. Independent technical verification examines mounting, calibration, reference data and observations. The officer reports findings promptly under a pre-agreed service deadline, and escalates uncertainty or missing data rather than waiting indefinitely. Actual deadlines and technical tolerances require qualified local design, not classroom invention.

A review panel includes authority engineers and accessible representation from tenants, workers, owners, people with access needs and neighbouring areas. Publish the evidence, uncertainties, distributional impacts and reasons for the authority’s decision. Offer non-digital participation and a route to challenge the evidence. Participation shapes objectives and trade-offs but cannot vote away safety duties or another community’s rights.

Keep options open without postponing everything

The authority can retain the current pathway with improved monitoring, trial a reversible measure, commission further design or change pathway when supported by evidence. Keep the three-year backstop even if no reliable signal appears; check demographic, ecological and access changes as well as water. Review trigger performance after incidents and agree revisions openly before the next one.

An unreliable sensor is a reason to repair the evidence system, not a reason to abandon adaptation. A permanent wall may eventually be justified, but its risk-transfer, access, ecology, cost and lead-time implications need assessment. Temporary measures also need monitoring and an explicit end or renewal decision; reversibility does not mean harmlessness.

Instructor note

Require students to separate four gates: immediate safety response, verification, deliberative plan review and authorised implementation. The case deliberately supplies no date of protection failure or flood probability. Students cannot derive an optimal trigger or a wall net present value.

SUS-14 selects heat assets; STR-08 compares scenarios. This case determines who revisits a plan and how uncertain evidence changes it. Accept Calendar only with a credible explanation of how mandatory urgent responses and emerging evidence will still prompt necessary reconsideration. Do not reward equating consultation with a referendum on engineering safety.
