IS-08 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Information systems · Supplier dependence · Planned migration

A better system. A harder exit?

In the news · Think about the exit at selection

On 8 January 2026, the HKMA issued its updated Practice Guide on Cloud Adoption, including portability and workable exit planning.

That guidance addresses banks. Our fictional rental company is not a bank, and this case does not apply banking requirements to small businesses.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong event-equipment rental company is choosing a system for quotes, bookings and return reminders. This is administrative work, not equipment safety or maintenance. Both shortlisted services meet its stated current functional, security and privacy requirements, with affordable, comparable initial setup effort.

A links the workflow in supplier-specific tools. B uses simpler steps that staff connect, with documented common formats and a simpler configuration. Both let the company keep copies of its records; neither transfers ownership of the supplier’s software.

Existing salaried staff stay employed. Time released from routine administration could go to overdue quotation follow-ups, but no extra sales are assumed. The manager needs a provisional recommendation for the coming year; nothing has been signed.

Use 48 working weeks. The time estimates have equal estimation quality. B’s migration estimate covers its replacement workflow, current records, limited parallel running and staff retraining, assuming today’s modest requirements; its common formats make a move easier, not free. External fees exclude the company’s own salaried staff time.

Which service would you recommend provisionally? What specific evidence about A’s exit cost or useful time saving could change your choice?

Round 2 · One fact changes

With permission and fictional test data, a prospective replacement supplier imports 20 representative booking records with their attachments and linked quote IDs. The team checks the records and opens the attachments successfully. This supports that tested data path, not every record or a working replacement service.

The supplier also provides a written estimate for rebuilding the currently documented quote-and-reminder workflow, migrating the current data volume, limited parallel running and staff retraining: HK$18,000 external fees plus five staff-days. A’s written terms allow export during the contract and a 30-day read-only/export window after ordinary termination.

The estimate assumes the current simple workflow, no new integrations, scheduled access and supplier cooperation. It is not a full migration rehearsal or a guarantee of later prices, availability or access during a sudden provider failure. Exported records do not run the reminders. External fees still exclude the company’s staff time.

The manager can reserve this level of cash and staff availability if a planned switch becomes worthwhile. A now has a more credible, but still costlier, exit route. Its current subscription and weekly time estimates are unchanged.

Put a value on useful time without inventing cash savings

A costs HK$6,000 more per year. Its estimated three-hour weekly advantage over 48 weeks releases 144 staff-hours. The quotation backlog gives those hours a useful possible destination.

Those hours are not a payroll saving or guaranteed sales. Salaried staff remain employed. A can be worthwhile if the manager values the useful capacity enough; B remains attractive if lower cash spending and simpler future switching matter more.

An export is only part of an exit

The 20-record check provides specific evidence about the tested records, attachments and links. The separately scoped work on reminders, configuration, parallel running and retraining addresses how a replacement service might actually operate.

The access window and available resources also matter. Together they make A’s planned exit more credible than an export button alone, without making it as simple or inexpensive as B’s stated route.

Compare dependence without pretending exit is certain

On the current estimates, A’s possible future exit needs HK$12,000 more external spending and three more staff-days than B’s. Both the money and the staff availability deserve attention.

No exit probability or timing is supplied. Do not add the full migration bill to next year’s subscription as if a switch will definitely happen. Equally, do not ignore it because the workflow is convenient today. This is a planned procurement choice, not a guarantee of emergency recovery.

Bound the commitment and agree review signals

For A, a sensible boundary might prohibit a new integration until its effect on the documented exit scope has been estimated. Review whether the service still frees useful time and whether fees remain worth paying. B also needs maintained records and a workable migration plan.

Set a review date and a measurable trigger: for example, a fee above an agreed value threshold, verified time savings below a chosen level, or proposed customisation outside the migration estimate. The team must choose those thresholds; none is uniquely established by this case.

Instructor note

Take a provisional A-or-B vote before the scoped exit evidence. A offers a genuine operating benefit; B offers lower spending and less dependence. Do not make either “always choose open” or “always choose cheapest” the answer.

The reveal includes workflow rebuilding, transition, retraining, access and resource planning as well as a small data test. Require students to name what is established and what remains conditional. Staff-days in migration estimates are not included in the external fees; no wage conversion or hours-per-day assumption is supplied.

The existing staff-time estimates are planning inputs, not experimental results. This differs from Case 35’s emergency restoration: a planned switch with continuing access is not protection against every abrupt supplier shutdown. Security and privacy are not dismissed; both services are expressly assumed to meet the stated screening requirements.
