HSP-03 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Hospitality · Revenue management · Length of stay

One busy night. Two nights of business.

In the news · More reasons to stay overnight

In a 23 July 2025 Legislative Council reply, the Hong Kong Government reported that its expanded exhibition incentive scheme had launched on 1 July, with an additional HK$500 million. The reply also reported about 1.42 million overnight visitors for meetings, incentive travel, conventions and exhibitions in 2024.

That citywide activity provides a setting for hotel room-block decisions, not a demand forecast for any particular hotel. Our hotel and all its booking figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You manage a small Hong Kong hotel with ten identical, uncommitted rooms available on both Friday and Saturday. Existing reservations are separate and will be honoured. This decision never involves moving a booked guest.

A visiting group wants all ten rooms for Saturday only. The group must receive an answer today; there is time to inspect the forecast before replying. Its offer contributes HK$700 per room after all directly attributable costs. For this simplified comparison, assume payment is secured on acceptance and the HK$7,000 total contribution is certain, with no cancellation or collection risk.

Your summary forecast shows eight individual room sales on Saturday, each contributing HK$600. Contribution means money remaining after the booking’s directly attributable costs, including commissions and servicing. Unchanged hotel overheads are excluded from both alternatives.

Saturday summaryContribution
Group: 10 rooms × HK$700HK$7,000
Individual forecast: 8 rooms × HK$600HK$4,800

This sheet compares Saturday only. It has not yet shown the individual guests’ arrival and departure dates.

Your decision

Accept the Saturday group block or keep the rooms available for individual guests? What else would you check before replying?

Round 2 · One fact changes

The reservations manager opens the stay details. The eight forecast individual bookings are each for both Friday and Saturday, at HK$600 contribution per room-night. If Saturday is unavailable, these guests would not book Friday alone.

No replacement Friday-only demand or other additional business is forecast for these ten rooms. There is no extra catering or meeting-space contribution in either option. The group requires all ten Saturday rooms and will not accept a smaller block.

The eight two-night stays are a forecast, not existing reservations. They might not all materialise. The group’s HK$7,000 remains the certain comparison under the classroom payment assumptions. All existing reservations remain protected.

Your decision

Now would you accept the group or hold out for the two-night stays? How much confidence in the forecast does your choice require?

Compare the complete stays

If all eight two-night bookings materialise, they contribute 8 × 2 × HK$600 = HK$9,600. That is HK$2,600 more than the group’s HK$7,000.

The Saturday-only comparison missed eight Friday room-nights. Accepting the group would make Saturday unavailable and therefore lose those entire two-night stays in this scenario.

This is called displacement analysis: comparing the business accepted with the business it prevents you from accepting. Here, displacement means forgone future bookings, not removing already-booked guests.

How much forecast risk will you take?

Six two-night stays contribute 6 × HK$1,200 = HK$7,200, just above the group. Five contribute HK$6,000, below it. Six is the smallest whole number of these stays that beats HK$7,000.

That threshold is not a probability. The case does not supply a demand distribution, so it does not establish a precise risk-adjusted optimum. Eight forecast stays should not be relabelled guaranteed business.

Check the hotel’s comparable-date booking history, current booking pace and cancellation assumptions. These can improve confidence; citywide visitor totals alone cannot.

A decision you can defend

Hold the rooms if the evidence supports enough two-night demand and the hotel accepts the downside risk. State the forecast assumptions rather than simply pointing to HK$9,600.

Accept the group if the hotel values the certain HK$7,000 and the individual forecast is too weak. Recognise the full-stay opportunity being given up, not just Saturday’s HK$4,800.

Do not invent a smaller group, guaranteed repeat business or extra Friday-only sales to make both options fit. Those could matter in another case, but they are not available here.

Key idea: rooms are counted by the night, but guests may buy several nights together.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes for the Saturday comparison, 3 for the stay-length reveal and 5–7 for the risk discussion. Ask students to draw Friday and Saturday as two columns before recalculating. Ask group advocates what certainty is worth; ask individual-booking advocates what supports their forecast. Keep contribution distinct from gross room revenue and whole-hotel profit. No existing booking is cancelled or displaced physically.
