ECO-47 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Online travel agents · Price parity · Platform competition · Diversion

A cheaper booking channel. Can it offer a lower price?

When a hotel cannot undercut one booking site on another

In 2020 Hong Kong’s Competition Commission accepted commitments from three online travel agents to address concerns including wide price parity. The register now lists all three commitments as expired in 2025. Wide price parity can stop a hotel from using a rival booking site to offer a lower price. The dated case is context only; the contract and demand below are invented and do not describe any platform’s current policy.

Your classroom decision

Yan manages a fictional Hong Kong hotel. The same refundable room is listed by HarbourGuide and the newer SideDoor. Both retain a percentage of the advertised room price on each completed stay, 20% and 10% respectively. Serving each occupied room-night costs HK$450 regardless of where it was booked.

The hotel can commit to one pricing plan for the next selling period. With wide parity, the HarbourGuide price cannot exceed the SideDoor price, so the last row is unavailable. If the clause is dropped, all three plans are possible. There are 140 available room-nights. The volumes are fictional forecasts of completed nights, not a quota or a guarantee. In the entrant-only plan, assume the only customer flows are shifts from HarbourGuide to SideDoor and newly attracted SideDoor bookings. Ignore taxes, other channels and fixed costs.

Your decision

What does the hotel earn per completed night on each channel under each plan? Which plan maximises its total contribution with and without wide parity? How many SideDoor nights under the entrant-only discount are new to the hotel, rather than diverted from HarbourGuide?

Round 2 · More information

HarbourGuide offers a sponsored placement only if the hotel retains wide parity and keeps both listings at HK$900 for this period. It charges no extra fee; its regular commission still applies. The hotel forecasts 18 additional completed HarbourGuide nights, none diverted from SideDoor. If the hotel declines the offer it can choose any original plan without wide parity. No other estimate changes.

Your decision

Does the offer change Yan’s recommendation at 18 extra nights? What is the smallest number of genuinely additional HarbourGuide nights that would make the offer at least as attractive? Would a clause covering only the hotel’s own website prevent the SideDoor-only discount in this model?

Debrief · Separate price from margin

Contribution per occupied room-night is price × (1 − commission rate) − HK$450. At HK$900 the hotel keeps HK$270 on HarbourGuide and HK$360 on SideDoor; at HK$850 it keeps HK$230 and HK$315 respectively. Moving more bookings to SideDoor can help even at a lower posted price, but matching forces a lower margin on HarbourGuide as well.

Debrief · Distinguish new bookings from diversion

Keeping both prices at HK$900 earns 80 × HK$270 + 20 × HK$360 = HK$28,800. Matching the discount earns 92 × HK$230 + 24 × HK$315 = HK$28,720. SideDoor-only earns 68 × HK$270 + 52 × HK$315 = HK$34,740 without wide parity. Of the 32 additional SideDoor nights, 12 are diverted and 20 are new to the hotel, assuming no other customer flows.

Debrief · Value actual discovery

Taking the offer earns HK$28,800 + 18 × HK$270 = HK$33,660, short by HK$1,080. The break-even gain is HK$5,940 ÷ HK$270 = 22 extra completed nights, within the 140-night capacity. A clause covering only the hotel’s own website does not restrict SideDoor in the stipulated comparison.

Instructor note

Ask whether the placement causes 18 additional completed nights or merely redirects guests who would book anyway. The hotel’s profit calculation does not establish traveller welfare, market entry or whether any clause is lawful. To discuss the platform’s rationale, distinguish discovery that creates a match from a restriction that prevents a rival from sharing its lower fees with travellers.
