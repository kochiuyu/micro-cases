MKT-01 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Price discrimination · Self-selection

Would you share a ten-trip ferry pass?

Hong Kong in the news · 11 March 2026

During the outlying-island ferry fare review, the Transport Department said multi-ride tickets and monthly passes would see smaller increases than single fares. Why offer frequent travellers a different price per trip?

Your classroom decision

You set fares for a fictional harbour crossing. A single costs HK$12. Consider a ten-trip pass valid for one week, initially restricted to one passenger. Travellers take their stated trips either way and can buy singles.

Weekly demand and candidate prices · classroom assumptions
One commuter10 crossings
Ten occasional riders1 crossing each
Possible pass pricesHK$90 or HK$110
Your decision

Which pass price earns more while giving the commuter a discount? What will occasional riders buy?

Round 2 · One fact changes

The pass can now be shared without cost. The ten occasional riders travel together and can split one pass equally. Trip demand stays fixed.

Your decision

At your chosen price, how much weekly revenue disappears? Would you keep sharing, limit it or withdraw the pass?

Debrief · Compare the choices

With a personal pass, HK$110 yields HK$230: one pass plus ten singles. HK$90 yields HK$210. With sharing at HK$110, both groups buy passes and revenue falls to HK$220. Singles alone would yield HK$240.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–3: vote and compare reasons in pairs. Minutes 3–7: reveal the changed fact and vote again. Minutes 7–12: reconcile the answers. The menu sorts customers only while the restrictions separate their buying patterns. Ask discount advocates how many additional journeys would justify it. Ask supporters of identity checks what enforcement would cost. No real ferry operator’s fare or sharing rule is assumed.
