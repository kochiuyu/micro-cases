HSP-04 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Hospitality · Reservations · Service process

They did not come. Did they try to cancel?

In the news · Deposits or a better conversation?

In July 2019, MICHELIN Guide interviewed Hong Kong restaurateurs about reservation deposits. Some described advance payment as protection for scarce seats and preparation costs; others emphasised trust and personal contact.

These are historical operator views, not a controlled estimate of what deposits achieve or a statement of current policies. Our restaurant below is fictional and unrelated to those operators.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You manage a small Hong Kong restaurant. Across the last four Saturday dinners, five reserved two-person tables stayed empty. While those tables remained booked, staff had turned away enquiries.

The booking sheet labels all five “no-shows”. You have not yet checked messages or spoken with the parties, so the label describes the attendance record, not why the tables were empty. There is no evidence that every table could have been resold.

The owner proposes a HK$200 deposit per table for future Saturday reservations, credited against the meal. Under the proposal, cancellations received at least 24 hours before the booking receive a full deposit refund; later cancellations and non-arrivals forfeit it. These are proposed classroom terms, not an existing contract or a legal recommendation.

No deposit has been charged. Existing bookings keep their current terms. You can instead strengthen reminders or redesign how bookings are changed.

Your decision

Would you introduce the proposed deposit, use stronger reminders without a deposit, or change the reservation process? What do you need to learn about the five empty tables?

Round 2 · One fact changes

The restaurant checks its own records. Three of the five parties sent cancellation messages more than 24 hours before their bookings. They used the reply address printed on their confirmations, and the messages arrived in that inbox.

Nobody monitored the inbox during those periods, so the booking sheet was never updated. The other two empty bookings remain unexplained. These are verified records in the fictional exercise, not assumptions that every guest behaved the same way.

There were waiting-list enquiries, but no evidence that all three released tables would have been refilled. The proposal has not been implemented, so there are no deposit penalties to apply to these past bookings.

Your decision

Would you keep the deposit proposal, redesign cancellations, or do both? Specify how a timely cancellation reaches a responsible person and releases the table.

Diagnose before increasing the penalty

At least three records reflect a failed cancellation process, not simply customers withholding notice. Collecting a deposit would not itself read the inbox or release a table.

Under the proposed terms, timely messages received in the advertised channel would qualify for a refund even if staff read them late. Do not silently redefine “received” as “noticed by staff”.

The two unexplained bookings do not prove that deposits are useless or necessary. More evidence is needed about late cancellations and non-arrivals.

Design the complete service process

Make cancellation usable: provide a working channel on the confirmation, with a clear deadline and an acknowledgement that tells the guest whether the change has been recorded.

Assign ownership: name the shift role that monitors requests and a backup when that person is absent. Update the booking record promptly and handle any refund consistently with the stated terms.

Release and refill: contact the waiting list or reopen availability once cancellation is recorded. Confirm any replacement booking; a waiting-list enquiry is not a sale.

Decide what to test next

A deposit may still be appropriate for some services or costly late cancellations. If testing it, explain the amount, meal credit, deadline, cancellation route and refund conditions before future customers book. Consider booking friction and administration as well as attendance.

Track requests received, time to acknowledgement, correct booking updates, tables released, actual refills and remaining empty tables. Distinguish timely cancellations, late cancellations and unexplained non-arrivals. Four Saturdays are not a stable causal estimate.

Do not automatically cancel a confirmed booking merely because a guest ignores a reminder. Nor should you retroactively charge prior guests.

Key idea: a commitment policy needs a working way to change that commitment.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the policy vote, 3 for the inbox reveal and 5–7 for drawing the cancellation-to-refill chain. Ask who does each step, how a guest knows the cancellation succeeded and which outcome would show improvement. Accept a monitored process with or without a carefully scoped deposit trial. Do not promise that all released seats will sell or turn deposit collections into estimated profit.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for the classroom decisions is provided above. These sources document the background and are optional reading.
