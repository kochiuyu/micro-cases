SUS-48 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Second use · Condition clearance · Capacity matching

A battery still has capacity. Can it be reused?

Retired vehicle batteries require a viable downstream route

In 2026, the Hong Kong Government reported how retired electric-vehicle batteries were being collected and handled at licensed facilities and discussed future arrangements. Reuse is a separate question from continuing to drive the original vehicles. All batch and project figures below are hypothetical.

Your classroom decision

Lok manages a fictional Hong Kong commercial fleet. A qualified contractor has removed all six retired packs. For this exercise assign only one of two routes: an authorised recycler or a qualified second-use service. No student or fleet staff handle, inspect or alter a pack. The second-use service can deploy only clearance-approved packs, with final system compatibility already confirmed for this specific client.

The client wants at least 65 kWh of usable supplied capacity in one project. Pick the lowest direct routing cost that meets it. The project can use at most three of these packs, and all packs not selected go to the recycler. If none can meet 65 kWh, route the entire batch to the recycler and do not promise the project. There is no other source in this procurement.

For each selected pack, take the contractor’s project-level usable capacity as given and add it across units. A selected pack costs HK$3,000 for the professional second-use route instead of HK$1,000 for recycling. These costs exclude every later environmental or operating effect. Only affirmative clearance, not an estimated capacity alone, allows a pack to count.

Can the project be promised on the verified evidence? Find a lowest-cost feasible routing and its full batch cost. Why do the 30 kWh figures not make Type C eligible?

Round 2 · More information

Before commitment, the contractor withdraws one Type A pack’s clearance because its condition file does not establish suitability. That pack’s capacity estimate is unchanged. The two Type C packs remain pending. The remaining clearances and all costs stay unchanged.

Your decision

Can the project still reach 65 kWh with three approved packs? What if one Type C pack later obtains the necessary independent clearance while all other conditions stay unchanged?

Debrief · Use the cleared set

Two A and one B yield 25 + 25 + 18 = 68 kWh. One A and two B yield only 61 kWh. No two cleared packs reach 65 kWh, so three must be selected. Three second-use and three recycling routes cost 3 × HK$3,000 + 3 × HK$1,000 = HK$12,000.

Debrief · An unchanged capacity is not unchanged clearance

The remaining A and two B reach only 25 + 18 + 18 = 61 kWh. Do not promise the project; recycle all six at HK$6,000 unless new verified evidence changes the eligible set before routing. If one C is later cleared, the two strongest eligible packs are C plus A at 55 kWh, so three are still needed. A + B + C yields 73 kWh at a total of HK$12,000, if the new clearance arrives before routing.

Debrief · Audit the full routing ledger

Pending or withdrawn status never qualifies. Ensure the second-use partner receives the approved identifier for each selected pack, and the rest are accounted for through the authorised recycling route. Do not infer avoided emissions from the capacity or route alone.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 to find feasible combinations, 4 to apply the withdrawn clearance, and 5 to separate environmental effects from admissibility. This is a logistics choice using professional assessments, not a technical or safety standard.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

No technical testing, handling, safety threshold or actual battery valuation is being prescribed. All routing costs, client capacity and clearance states are invented.
