GVR-41 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Procurement fairness · Tender notice accessibility · Document control · Bid handling

The notice was live. Was the tender pack?

A tender reminder and the formal notice serve different purposes

Hong Kong’s Digital Policy Office offers an email alert for government IT tenders. It distinguishes that alert from a formal invitation and directs suppliers to official notice channels. The fictional case asks what changes when the problem affects the tender documents themselves, not just the reminder.

Your classroom decision

Wai manages procurement for a fictional Hong Kong community service organisation. It is tendering for an IT help-desk contract. Its portal carries an open notice; anyone can view the notice and eligible suppliers can obtain the tender pack. An optional mailing list sends reminders with links. A routing mistake delayed one group’s reminder.

It is Day 18; closing is set for the same hour on Day 20, and all bids remain sealed. The initial audit confirms the complete current pack was available to every eligible supplier from Day 0. The committee must choose whether to keep the closing date or extend it for all suppliers.

Under this organisation’s fictional rule, every eligible supplier must be able to access the same final material documents for at least six full days before closing. Optional reminders do not start that clock. Each day of extension incurs HK$6,000 of bridging service costs. The committee can approve at most HK$20,000 for extensions. A separate authority must approve cancellation or any spending above that budget.

Round 2 · More information

A second audit finds that a Day 12 revision to the required weekend staffing schedule affects bid costs. A portal permission error let the eight suppliers obtain it on Day 12 but withheld it from the other six until Day 17. The notice stayed visible, and the optional reminder issue is unchanged. Test accounts and version logs confirm the revised pack has been accessible to everyone since Day 17.

The committee may extend before closing without opening offers, republish the same revision and deadline to everyone, and allow all bidders to replace submissions before the new deadline. Local fictional procedures permit this response; no one may accept only one group’s late bids. Other preparation conditions are unchanged.

Debrief · A late reminder is not late access

The initial records show 20 full days of document access for both groups. The first group had 19 days after its reminder; the second had five. The latter difference matters to outreach, but it does not violate this fictional six-day access rule. Keep Day 20 on the initial evidence, fix the reminder route, and retain delivery and portal-access checks.

Debrief · Ask about the document that changes the offer

The Day 12 revision changes staffing requirements and what suppliers need to price. By Day 20, the groups have eight and three days, respectively, with the same final pack. A visible listing and a delivered link do not establish that an eligible supplier can actually retrieve the complete pack.

Debrief · Restore opportunity before bids are opened

Day 17 plus six full days gives Day 23 at the same hour. That is three extra days, costing 3 × HK$6,000 = HK$18,000, within the budget by HK$2,000. An extension to Day 22 would cost HK$12,000 but still leave the affected group only five days. Extend to Day 23 rather than accept selective late submissions.

Debrief · Use a common, documented procedure

Publish the corrected pack and common deadline on the official portal, then resend the reminder to everyone. Test retrieval across eligibility groups, record the version and availability timestamps, keep unopened offers sealed and allow all bidders to replace their bids by the same deadline. If the test finds a further delay or cost overrun, escalate rather than waive the rule for one group.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes to read, 4 to distinguish access from attention, 4 to decide after the audit, and 5 to defend a process. All time intervals use the same hour each day. This is an internal governance exercise, not a claim about Hong Kong notice periods, the applicability of trade agreements, or lawful remedies for an actual tender.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The opening describes a real government email service. All organisation details, deadlines, process permissions, internal rules, costs, counts, defects and material changes in the exercise are fictional. Nothing describes an actual failure or gives legal advice.

  1. Digital Policy Office, E-mail Notification of Government IT Open Tenders. Distinguishes its optional email alert from the formal invitation and points suppliers to official notice channels. Source of the opening.
  2. Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, Guide to Procurement. Separates publication, submission deadlines and designated opening. Its real timelines and rules are not the fictional committee’s rule.
  3. Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, Stores and Procurement Regulations, Chapter III. Discusses tender notices, handling tender documents and allowing adequate preparation time. Supports the distinction between a listing and document access.
  4. HKSAR Government, LCQ11: Tendering arrangements for public works projects. Explains how sufficient preparation time lets bidders understand project requirements and assess risk. Public works timelines do not apply to this IT classroom scenario.
  5. World Trade Organization, Agreement on Government Procurement, revised text. Articles VII and X separate the notice from tender documentation and address distribution of amendments for covered procurements. No coverage or remedy is inferred for this case.
