STR-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · market access · product architecture · qualification economics

The part that opens the market

Production location is not enough to prove origin

Hong Kong’s Trade and Industry Department distinguishes product-specific rules from a general origin rule under the CEPA trade-in-goods agreement. Its documentation also distinguishes materials by the origin evidence needed to support a claim. The distributor and commercial gate below are fictional, not a test under CEPA or advice about any real product.

Your classroom decision

Inkstone makes a small desk organiser in Hong Kong. A distributor offers an ordinary order for 3,000 units under either design. It will increase the total order to 7,000 units only when its fictional sourcing audit verifies at least HK$40 of qualifying input value per unit at the fixed HK$100 transfer price.

This is the buyer’s own commercial sourcing gate, not a tariff rate or government certificate. It applies to the entire order: all units must use one design and be backed by the same evidence. The buyer is indifferent to the design where the evidence passes, and offers no intermediate quantity. Neither deal prevents later sales elsewhere, but those sales are not in this exercise.

Design A stays with the current assembly. Design B has a different component architecture and needs a tooling charge. The exhibit gives net contribution per unit before that charge and only changes listed elsewhere apply. Neither design raises quality or retail price.

Round 2 · More information

Before commitment, the distributor’s audit team can verify only HK$38 of inputs on B. A verified replacement component on B increases the qualifying amount by HK$5, but costs HK$6 extra per finished unit. It does not affect tooling, price or performance. The workshop can choose A, unmodified B at the ordinary order, or B with the replacement on all units.

Your decision

Which choice now wins? What is the most Inkstone should pay per unit above B’s original part cost for a verified replacement that restores the larger order?

First decision: price the larger order, not only the part

A falls below the HK$40 gate and earns 3,000 × HK$32 = HK$96,000. B’s initially evidenced HK$44 clears the gate, and its 7,000 units earn 7,000 × HK$24 − HK$35,000 = HK$133,000. Provisionally choose B, ahead by HK$37,000, subject to verification.

Second decision: the larger order can cost too much

Unmodified B now misses the gate and earns only 3,000 × HK$24 − HK$35,000 = HK$37,000. With the replacement, evidenced value reaches HK$43; the larger order earns 7,000 × (HK$24 − HK$6) − HK$35,000 = HK$91,000. Choose A at HK$96,000.

Break-even: charge the premium to every unit

The extra component can take at most HK$37,000 ÷ 7,000, HK$37/7 per unit exactly (HK$5.28 if quoted to the cent), if the larger order is verified and comparison costs stay unchanged. The premium is not paid on just the 4,000 units the gate adds.

What to take away

Designing for market access means deciding which components to use, what qualification evidence suppliers can support and what volume that unlocks. A statement about where final assembly occurs does not settle any of those choices.

Instructor note

Use the private gate exactly as stated. The exercise gives no actual product classification, input calculation, tariff, origin or certification decision.
