PPL-07 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · People management · Assessment design · Entry competencies

A realistic test. The right skill?

In the news · Match requirements to the work

In a 4 June 2025 reply to a Legislative Council question, the Hong Kong Government explained that civil-service entry requirements are set against job requirements and reviewed for good performance.

This is a local example of matching requirements to work, not a private-sector hiring rule or endorsement of a test method. The company and assessment design below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A small event-services company is recruiting a junior booking coordinator. On entry, the person must spot conflicting booking information, explain uncertainty clearly to customers and escalate when needed. The company will teach its proprietary booking software after hiring; prior shortcut knowledge is not required.

Four shortlisted applicants meet the basic advertised requirements. Nobody has been assessed or eliminated. The manager has one fixed final-assessment slot per applicant and can use either of two methods for this round.

Both fit the available time. All tasks use dummy messages and records, not useful unpaid production work. Instructions and materials will be standardised, with reasonable access needs addressed. The exercise asks you to design an assessment, not select a person.

A familiar-looking screen can measure the wrong thing

The simulation resembles the workplace, but 60 of its 100 points reward shortcut speed that the company plans to teach. A high total could therefore reflect prior interface familiarity more than the required booking judgement.

This is a mismatch in the proposed design, not evidence about any applicant. Nobody has yet been scored, so the team can correct the assessment before using it rather than changing candidates’ goalposts after seeing results.

Keep a credible assessment option

A structured interview remains defensible for this intake: use the same job-related dilemmas and consistent standards to assess explanation, uncertainty and escalation. It is not a free conversation scored for charm.

A revised simulation is also possible. Use a simplified mock-up or reference sheet and score conflict detection, response clarity and appropriate escalation. Check that the format actually isolates these skills; giving a short orientation alone does not establish equal familiarity or fix an inappropriate weighting.

Make the scoring behaviour visible

Consider this dummy mismatch: the customer asks for Tuesday, while the venue record says Wednesday. A weak response confirms one date without checking. An adequate response notices the mismatch and asks which date is intended. A strong response identifies the conflict, avoids making a promise and clearly explains the next verification step.

These are illustrative scoring anchors, not a validated selection instrument. Assess the booking judgement and communication required by the role, not accent, charisma or unannounced speed. Apply the chosen standards consistently and train assessors to use them.

Separate entry requirements from teachable skills

Software speed is not inherently irrelevant. It could belong in an assessment if demonstrably needed on entry and consistently disclosed. In this case, however, the organisation explicitly plans to teach proprietary navigation.

Start with the job’s tasks, identify what must be present at entry, choose an assessment that elicits those skills and define observable scoring criteria. Keep access arrangements appropriate; identical format alone does not guarantee fairness.

Instructor note

Invite a provisional choice between two legitimate methods before showing the rubric. Afterwards, focus on the 60-point criterion, not an invented ranking of the four applicants. The decision is how to assess, not whom to employ.

Neither method guarantees good hires or eliminates all bias. Review assessor consistency and outcomes after use without treating four people as causal proof. Combining methods may be useful in another process, but the current constraint is one final-assessment slot.
