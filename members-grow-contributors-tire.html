MKT-40 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Brand communities · Member contribution · Sustainable participation

More members. Who answers next?

A community is more than an audience

Brand-community research describes relationships among members, not only connections to a seller. Community guidance also warns that participation can be concentrated and that newcomers need support. The fictional figures below do not follow a universal participation ratio or prove that community growth increases sales.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Paperloop, a Hong Kong craft-tool brand, runs an optional adult community for sharing decorative paper-layout ideas. It has 1,000 registered members. Technical safety, warranty requests and contractual customer support go to separately funded company staff, not unpaid members. Ordinary moderation and accessibility support also remain fully funded.

For next month, choose how to use twenty-four additional paid community-staff hours. A uses sixteen hours recruiting members and eight answering creative discussion threads. B uses four hours improving optional newcomer guidance and reusable examples, and twenty hours answering threads. Pay rates and all other incremental costs are equal.

A estimates 200 additional registrations. B does not run that recruitment campaign. The brand wants useful, welcoming exchanges that participants can sustain, not just a larger mailing list. Existing members may read, ask, share or stop participating without losing product access, service or status.

Last month 120 eligible creative-question threads each received a helpful first answer within seven days. Each counted answer addressed a different thread and was checked against the same relevance standard. Later replies may also be valuable, but they are outside this deliberately limited measure. The dashboard shows neither who answered nor their future availability.

Your decision

Would you choose A or B provisionally? What would you need to know about who supplies helpful responses and what they freely want to contribute before treating 200 new registrations as community success?

Round 2 · More information

Four members supplied 96 of last month’s 120 counted answers; sixteen other members supplied 24. The four describe the current pace as too demanding and freely indicate capacity for only 36 answers next month. The other sixteen indicate 24. These are intentions, not work commitments or diagnoses of burnout.

Use sixty distinct member answers as a planning scenario under either plan, not a guarantee. Staff will take different unanswered threads, avoiding double counting. For this exercise, each staff answer requires twenty minutes, including checking and follow-up, and meets the same helpfulness standard. Recruitment and guidance hours cannot also count as answering time.

The comparable baseline is 120 new eligible threads next month. Under A, recruitment is estimated to add sixty more, giving 180; no extra member-answer contribution from new registrants is assumed. Under B, use 120 threads. These are supplied workload assumptions, not measured effects of either plan.

B’s guidance work offers low-pressure ways to participate, permission-based reuse of examples and a clear route to paid staff. It does not promise to restore the four members’ previous output. No answer quota, ranking contest or compulsory availability is proposed. Required moderation and customer service are protected even if no member answers anything.

Your decision

Compare A and B’s distinct threads covered within the supplied scenario. How concentrated was last month’s contribution? Recommend a plan and a fallback that respects members’ freedom, including what changes if none of their intended answers materialise.

Count contribution without ranking people

The four supplied 96/120 = 80% of counted answers. They are only four of twenty answer contributors and four of 1,000 registered members. Those denominators describe concentration, not the worth of other members. Quiet readers, thoughtful questions and occasional contributions can create value outside the chosen measure.

The sixty intended member answers are half last month’s 120. Do not require the four to fill the difference or treat their past generosity as a service contract. The data support a capacity concern, not a universal claim that a small core always answers everything.

Translate staff time into response capacity

A provides 8 × 60/20 = 24 staff-answer slots. With sixty distinct member answers, 84 of 180 threads receive a counted answer, leaving 96 without one in the seven-day window. That is 46.7% covered, rounded, under the supplied scenario.

B provides 20 × 60/20 = 60 staff-answer slots. With sixty member answers, 120 of 120 threads are covered in the scenario. B reallocates twelve paid hours from recruitment to answering relative to A; it does not create free extra staff or guarantee future member output.

Choose support before accelerating demand

Choose B here: the current contribution base is strained, and the recruitment scenario adds questions without demonstrated additional answering capacity. B addresses this bottleneck while keeping participation voluntary. More registrations could be valuable later, but their contribution, needs and welcome experience must be assessed rather than assumed.

The guidance allocation may help newcomers find suitable roles, but the calculation credits it with no automatic extra answers. Recognise contributions with permission, offer different ways to engage and ask what support people want. Do not make exclusive product benefits depend on maintaining an unpaid answering quota.

Keep a genuine fallback

If no intended member answers appear, A covers only 24 of 180 threads and B covers 60 of 120. The remaining counts become 156 and sixty. Communicate honest waiting expectations for these optional discussions, defer recruitment or seek separately approved paid capacity. Do not imply the brand has guaranteed seven-day peer responses.

Preserve ordinary paid customer-service routes and mandatory safeguards regardless of community activity. Track unique helpful first responses, unanswered threads and waiting times alongside contribution concentration and voluntary feedback. Do not infer profit, loyalty, member wellbeing or causal improvement from registrations or answer counts alone.

Instructor note

Ask students to distinguish registered members, answer contributors, distinct threads and staff hours. B is stronger under the supplied scenario, not a universal rejection of growth. MKT-09 concerns additional advertising reach; MKT-18 concerns permission and contact cadence. Here members help create community value, but that contribution cannot be conscripted.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

Original fictional craft-community design. Last month's 120 eligible creative threads each received one counted helpful first answer: four core members supplied 96 and sixteen others supplied 24. No thread is counted twice, and later replies may also have value. The 1,000 registered members include these twenty contributors; other members are not presumed valueless. Next month's baseline is 120 threads. A estimates 200 extra registrations and sixty extra threads; B assumes no extra recruitment or threads. Both allocate twenty-four paid staff-hours: A uses sixteen for recruitment and eight for answers; B uses four for optional guidance and twenty for answers. Each staff answer takes twenty minutes including checking and follow-up, yielding 24 or sixty slots. Both scenarios assume sixty voluntary member answers, comprising 36 from the core and 24 from others, with no staff overlap. A covers 84 of 180 threads, leaving 96 unanswered; B covers 120 of 120, leaving none under this scenario only. These are not guarantees or causal estimates. With no member answers, A covers 24 of 180 and B sixty of 120, leaving 156 and sixty unanswered respectively. Routine moderation, safety, warranty and contractual support remain separately funded. The brand has no right to compel unpaid contributions. No actual membership records are used. Foreign and open-source guidance supplies concepts, not applicable employment law.
