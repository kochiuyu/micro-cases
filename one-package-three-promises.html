ACC-29 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Revenue allocation · Standalone selling prices · Performance obligations

One package price. Three promises?

The reporting question

The IFRS Foundation separates allocating a contract price from recognising revenue when promises are fulfilled. Its five-step overview uses relative standalone selling prices for distinct promises. Public reporting also shows why identifying those promises and estimating their prices can require judgement.

This fictional exercise supplies those judgements and a simplified policy. It is not a complete IFRS, Hong Kong reporting or tax analysis. No actual company or customer is depicted.

Your classroom decision

A Hong Kong supplier signs a HK$12,000 package for a compact label printer, basic installation and twelve months of optional support. The customer has selected and committed to the support within this contract: it is not an unexercised future purchase option. All HK$12,000 is collected at signing. No goods or services have transferred yet. The controller must finalise the initial allocation before recording any revenue.

For this exercise, all three promises are distinct. The printer works with readily available setup resources; basic installation does not modify it, and support is separately usable. There are no returns, variable amounts, financing adjustments, tax deductions, additional promises or contract changes. Allocate the fixed price in proportion to validated standalone selling prices at contract inception. Spread the bundle discount across all three promises; no exception assigning it to only one applies.

The draft worksheet gives standalone prices of HK$10,000 for equipment, HK$2,000 for installation and HK$3,000 for twelve-month support. These figures need final evidence checking. For timing, recognise the equipment allocation when control transfers on delivery, installation when that distinct task is complete, and support evenly over twelve months from its start. Neither cash collection nor price allocation fulfils a promise.

Round 2 · More information

Before the initial allocation is finalised or any revenue is recorded, the controller finds that the HK$2,000 installation figure included an unrelated training service that this contract does not promise. Verified comparable standalone sales at contract inception support HK$1,000 for the basic installation actually promised. Equipment remains HK$10,000. Support was also entered from the wrong service tier: the purchased twelve-month tier has a validated inception standalone price of HK$4,000, not HK$3,000. Total standalone prices still sum to HK$15,000; the contract price stays HK$12,000.

Use the corrected evidence to complete the initial allocation, not to reprice a completed allocation for later market movements. At the later reporting snapshot, control of the printer has transferred, installation is complete and exactly three of twelve support months have been provided evenly. All cash was already collected. No further cash, services, contract amendments or other adjustments have occurred. Calculate cumulative revenue and the remaining contract liability, meaning the collected amount still associated with unfulfilled promises.

Your decision

What corrected allocation and cumulative revenue follow at the three-month snapshot, and why does the unchanged cash total not settle the revenue amount?

The provisional allocation

The draft standalone total is HK$15,000. The HK$12,000 price is 80% of that total. Allocate HK$8,000 to equipment, HK$1,600 to installation and HK$2,400 to support. The HK$3,000 bundle discount is shared proportionately. At signing no promise has transferred: revenue is zero and the full HK$12,000 received is a contract liability.

Correct the inputs, not the contract price

Corrected standalone amounts are HK$10,000, HK$1,000 and HK$4,000, still totalling HK$15,000. Apply 80% to obtain HK$8,000, HK$800 and HK$3,200. Equipment is unchanged; HK$800 shifts from installation to support. Both allocations sum to HK$12,000. The correction changes the distribution of a fixed price, not the amount collected.

Allocation is not recognition

At the stated snapshot, equipment revenue is HK$8,000 and installation revenue HK$800. Three support months earn 3/12 × HK$3,200 = HK$800. Cumulative revenue is HK$9,600; the remaining nine support months account for the HK$2,400 contract liability. Revenue plus that liability reconciles to the HK$12,000 collected.

Had the draft inputs wrongly remained, cumulative revenue would have been 8,000 + 1,600 + 600 = HK$10,200, overstating this snapshot by HK$600 and understating the remaining liability by the same amount. The corrected allocation moves more consideration to the still-partly-unfulfilled support promise. It does not defer the printer after its stipulated transfer.

Keep three questions separate

Ask what is promised, how much of the fixed consideration belongs to each promise, and when each promise is fulfilled. A single invoice does not make a single obligation; an optional service already selected is different from an option to buy later. Distinctness and recognition patterns are supplied facts here, not conclusions that any equipment bundle automatically qualifies for.

Instructor note

Give three minutes for the first allocation and signing entry, then reveal both input corrections. Require a reconciliation of cumulative revenue plus remaining liability to cash, and an explanation of why the standalone total can stay unchanged while timing changes. The quantitative answer is fixed by the supplied policy; the judgement task is keeping allocation and fulfilment distinct.

This differs from identical prepaid-session timing and principal-versus-agent cases. It concerns multiple distinct promises and corrected inception evidence before initial allocation is finalised. It does not teach reallocation for later standalone market-price changes, contract modification, error correction in issued financial statements or full compliance. The IFRS/AASB materials overlap; US guidance and Microsoft’s own disclosures illustrate related concepts, not governing rules for this fictional supplier.
