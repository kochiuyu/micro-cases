STR-02 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Distribution strategy · Channel control

A bigger market. Whose customers?

Hong Kong in the news · October 2025

On 27 October 2025, Hong Kong-listed Eternal Beauty announced exclusive distribution rights for Swiss Perfection in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau. A next-day company release described responsibility for brand operations, marketing and sales. A partner can bring market access and execution capacity. What should a smaller brand keep control of?

Your classroom decision

You founded a fictional Hong Kong canvas-bag brand. A mainland distributor offers a two-year exclusive right to sell your products throughout mainland China, online and offline. It will fund local marketing, staff, warehousing and storefront setup. You retain the brand and product designs; your Hong Kong business is outside the exclusive territory.

The distributor commits to buying HK$600,000 of stock each year, paid before delivery. Producing and delivering that stock costs you HK$360,000 each year. Assume these commitments are honoured and the costs remain fixed for this exercise. Additional orders are possible but not forecast. The offer expires tomorrow. You can accept, seek revised terms at the risk of losing it, or decline and consider a smaller self-funded launch. You have no competing firm offer.

Your decision

Accept the exclusive offer, negotiate or decline? Which benefit is worth the two-year commitment, and what would you check before signing?

Round 2 · One fact changes

The detailed schedule says the distributor will register and control the mainland online storefront accounts. You receive monthly wholesale-order totals, but no product-level retail sales, returns or customer-retention reports. At the end of two years, the distributor must stop using your brand, but the contract provides no account transition or customer-relationship handover.

The distributor says it needs protection for its launch investment and may withdraw if you demand too much. The annual purchases, costs, duration and other terms are unchanged. These restrictions are invented for the case, not terms reported for Eternal Beauty or Swiss Perfection.

Your decision

Would you still sign unchanged? Name your two most important revisions and one concession you could offer in return.

Debrief · Revenue, learning and control

Guaranteed purchases buy revenue, not complete market knowledge. The minimum order contributes HK$240,000 a year, or HK$480,000 over two years before unchanged overheads. It reduces uncertainty about your wholesale income under the assumed payment terms. It does not tell you which bags customers like, whether stock is moving or what it would cost to replace the distributor later.

Separate the rights. Owning your brand is different from operating a storefront, accessing useful sales reports or moving a trading relationship when a partnership ends. You can regain exclusive use of your brand while the channel and accumulated learning remain with someone else. The reveal matters because dependence can reduce your future options, not because outsourcing is automatically bad.

Trade protections rather than demand everything. You might seek product-level sales and returns reporting, useful anonymized retention measures, agreed account governance and a workable transition plan. Or propose a narrower territory, selected channels or a shorter initial term with milestones. The partner may reasonably want time to recover investment, minimum supply commitments or a narrower reporting burden in exchange.

Prioritize what changes your decision. For example: keep two-year exclusivity if there is meaningful reporting and a defined exit process; or accept less guaranteed volume for a shorter trial. These are negotiation proposals, not guaranteed alternatives. No financial value for lost learning or transition costs is supplied, so the case has no single calculated best offer.

Back to the news · A partnership, not an allegation

The real announcements describe a partnership’s scope and intended contribution. They do not disclose the fictional reporting restrictions, storefront terms or exit problem. No dispute or poor outcome is alleged. General export guidance describes the local knowledge and services distributors can provide while encouraging attention to territory, performance and duration.

Practitioner guidance on the distributor model also flags information access and storefront transition. In practice, platform rules, privacy requirements and contractual details constrain what can be shared or transferred. Ask for compliant business reporting and a feasible transition process, not unrestricted ownership of people’s personal data. This classroom exercise explores strategy, not legal advice.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–3: vote on the initial offer and identify the distributor’s contribution. Minutes 3–7: reveal the schedule and ask for two proposed changes, not a wish list. Minutes 7–12: pair a brand team with a distributor team and negotiate one exchange. Ask whether the resulting package still makes economic sense for both sides.

Challenge “the guaranteed order solves everything” with missing retail information. Challenge “keep all control” with the partner’s investment and the brand’s limited resources. Ask what students would do if the partner refuses their essential term. Do not let students invent a funded direct-sales alternative or assume the proposed account transfer is legally or technically possible without checking.
