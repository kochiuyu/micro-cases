A profitable day. One more hour?

In the news

In a Legislative Council reply on 24 January 2024 about overnight attractions, Hong Kong’s government said that extending merchants’ operating hours was a commercial decision.

That historical discussion does not show that longer hours are profitable for every shop. The print shop, prices and forecasts below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Your Hong Kong print shop normally opens for eight hours on Saturday. Its planning forecast shows HK$1,600 of operating cash surplus after all costs of those eight hours: an average of HK$200 per opening hour. This is not an estimate of the next hour’s result.

You are deciding whether to close at the usual time or offer one additional hour next Saturday. The working forecast for that hour is 24 small print jobs at HK$25 each. These would be additional orders, not orders shifted from the earlier hours or another day. Demand is uncertain; no customer has been promised extended hours.

Both schedules are feasible. A willing employee can cover the extension under an agreed paid arrangement, with rest, safety and existing employment protections retained. The owner is not supplying unpaid replacement labour. You have not yet received the final extra-hour cost breakdown. Decide provisionally before announcing the hours.

Your decision

Would you provisionally extend opening or keep the usual closing time? Is the HK$200 average enough to decide, and what extra-hour information would change your recommendation?

Round 2 · More information

The cost breakdown arrives before any hours are announced. Each additional small job uses HK$10 of consumables and per-job charges. This differs from the mix sold earlier in the day.

Any extension up to one opening hour triggers a minimum two-hour paid block under this shop’s agreed arrangement: HK$320 in total employer cost. This is a fictional contractual package, not a statutory pay rule. It costs HK$320 even if few customers arrive; paying for the block does not make a second opening hour feasible under today’s staffing plan.

The extension also adds HK$60 for power, cleanup and other incremental operating costs. Together these figures include all additional operating costs in this one-Saturday model, including closing tasks. Existing rent and the original shift’s pay do not change. There is no displaced work, later sales effect or uncounted owner time in the comparison.

The same cost package can handle up to 30 additional small jobs at the stated price and per-job cost. The 24-job forecast has not become a booking. No evidence of long-run customer benefits or neighbourhood effects has been supplied.

Your decision

At the 24-job forecast, should the shop add the hour on the supplied operating-cash criterion? Calculate the change, then find the smallest whole number of additional jobs that would make the extension add a positive amount.

The next hour is not the average hour

The usual eight hours generate HK$1,600, or HK$200 per hour on average. That summary combines their customer mix and costs. It does not show the receipts or costs caused by extending the day.

For the extra hour, forecast receipts are 24 × HK$25 = HK$600. Per-job costs are 24 × HK$10 = HK$240, leaving HK$360 before the extra-hour staffing and operating package.

Count the whole step

The extension triggers HK$320 + HK$60 = HK$380 even though customers can enter for only one more hour. Do not divide the paid block in half or treat its second paid hour as free capacity.

Incremental operating cash surplus is HK$600 − HK$240 − HK$320 − HK$60 = −HK$20. The day would still show HK$1,580, but that is HK$20 less than closing normally. A profitable day can contain an unprofitable extension.

Find the boundary, not a universal break-even point

Each additional job leaves HK$25 − HK$10 = HK$15 toward the HK$380 triggered package. Matching the usual-closing result requires 380 ÷ 15 = 25⅓ jobs. With whole jobs, 25 leaves −HK$5 and 26 leaves +HK$10.

Twenty-six is the smallest number that improves this comparison, within the stated 30-job capacity. It is not the shop’s total break-even volume, an optimal opening schedule or proof that 26 customers will arrive.

Recommend within the evidence

At the 24-job forecast, keep the usual closing time on the supplied cash criterion. An extension becomes financially preferable in this model if credible additional demand reaches at least 26 comparable jobs, with the same costs and no displaced sales.

Forecast error matters: choosing a trial for learning would need a stated learning purpose and accepted downside, not the claim that the daily average guarantees HK$200. A private cash calculation also does not measure customers’ convenience, workers’ overall welfare or wider social benefits. Employment protections are constraints, not costs to evade.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the opening, 3 for a provisional vote, 2 for the cost reveal, 4 for the incremental calculation and threshold, and 2 for limits. Ask which costs change when the shop adds this hour.

Contrast the original HK$200 average hourly surplus with the extension’s HK$20 forecast loss. At 25 jobs the increment is negative HK$5; at 26 it is positive HK$10. The stated package covers at most 30 jobs, so do not extrapolate the threshold beyond its scope.

The minimum two-hour paid block is a fictional arrangement, not a legal rule or permission to open for two extra hours. Closing is the cash-preferred choice at the supplied forecast; do not force false balance by inventing future customer benefits. Forecast demand remains uncertain.
