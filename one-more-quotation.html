ECO-16 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Costly search · Value of information · Stopping rule

One more quotation. Worth the search?

Buying has a process as well as a price

Hong Kong’s procurement guide records a change to electronic supplier payments for procurement exercises invited from 1 June 2025. It also describes delivery and quality considerations in evaluation. That is public-procurement context, not permission for the fictional private buyer below to bypass any rule.

Your classroom decision

A fictional private print workshop needs a standard finishing attachment. It has a satisfactory, firm, all-in quote of HK$10,000. One additional, already screened supplier can provide a comparable quote after the buyer completes a specification check. Both suppliers meet the same quality, safety, service and delivery specification; only the price of that additional quote is uncertain.

The check costs HK$300 in additional paid staff time and administration. For this classroom model, the new quote is HK$8,000 with probability 40%, or HK$10,500 with probability 60%. These are supplied assumptions, not market estimates. The current HK$10,000 offer remains valid and available, so the buyer can reject an expensive new quote.

For the first decision only, assume the check and quote can be completed before the purchase deadline without delay cost. No money has been spent and no order placed. The owner’s stated objective is to minimise expected total procurement cost, with no separate risk-aversion premium; all possible costs are affordable. Only this one extra search is available. No law, funding condition or company policy requires another quote, and all required checks and approvals are already satisfied.

Round 2 · More information

Before anyone acts, the supplier confirms the new quote cannot arrive until Friday. The workshop must have usable equipment by Thursday. If it commits to the current supplier by Thursday, the existing price includes timely supply. If it waits to choose on Friday, either supplier can supply only on Monday; the current HK$10,000 offer remains valid for that later delivery.

A verified temporary attachment can cover Thursday through Monday safely and meet all customer commitments. Its complete incremental hire, fitting and return cost is HK$600. That cost is incurred whenever the buyer waits for the new quote, even if the buyer then returns to the original supplier. The HK$300 search cost is still additional. Buying now and cancelling later is not a free option: the order would be a non-refundable commitment, so no such route is offered.

The probabilities, comparable specifications, affordability and expected-cost objective remain unchanged. The choice is now between buying on time for HK$10,000 or paying both search and temporary-cover costs for the extra option. No customer deadline, safety requirement or procurement obligation may be ignored. Nothing is sunk yet.

Value the option, not an unwanted high price

The buyer keeps the HK$10,000 fallback. In the low-price outcome, searching saves HK$2,000; in the high-price outcome, the buyer declines HK$10,500 and saves zero. Expected gross saving is 0.40 × HK$2,000 = HK$800. Deducting HK$300 gives HK$500 expected net benefit under the first-round no-delay assumption. Expected procurement cost is HK$9,500, not the average of both new quotes plus the search fee.

Waiting changes the cost of learning

With temporary cover, the low-quote outcome costs HK$8,000 + HK$300 + HK$600 = HK$8,900. The fallback outcome costs HK$10,000 + HK$300 + HK$600 = HK$10,900. Their weighted average is HK$10,100, HK$100 above buying now. Under the supplied expected-cost objective, stop searching. This does not prove the lower quote cannot occur; it says the option costs more than its expected saving.

A stopping rule with a boundary

Let temporary-cover cost be d. The extra option’s expected net benefit is HK$800 − HK$300 − d. The routes tie at d = HK$500. Below that, the one additional search lowers expected cost; above it, stopping does. At the tie, financial expectation alone does not select a route. This is a one-step model with a preserved fallback, not a universal optimal search policy.

Instructor note

Require students to distinguish the price revealed from the price chosen. Then reveal the delivery timing before any expenditure and ask which costs are incremental. Do not let the high quote replace an available cheaper fallback, or let a return to the old supplier erase cover already required by waiting. Keep mandatory checks outside the stopping choice. Discuss how risk preferences, quote expiry or quality uncertainty would change the model rather than silently assuming them away.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The workshop, equipment, quotes, probabilities, timing and costs are original fiction. The owner minimises expected total procurement cost without a separate risk penalty; worst-case HK$10,900 is affordable. Supplier comparability, compliance and required approvals are stipulated; this is not guidance to evade procurement requirements. Only one extra search is available, not an infinite sequence or unrestricted negotiation. The current quote is preserved but later delivery follows waiting. Search and temporary cover are paid in both searched outcomes, including fallback; no sunk cost exists when the second decision is made. Initially expected chosen price is 0.4 × 8,000 + 0.6 × 10,000 = 9,200; plus 300 gives 9,500. Adding 600 cover gives 10,100. Gross expected option value 800; net before delay 500; break-even cover 500. No guarantee of saving or empirical estimate of the stated distribution.
