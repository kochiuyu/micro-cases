SUS-50 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Light nuisance · Operating schedules · Neighbour impact

The shops are closed. Why are their signs still lit?

External lighting can serve a business and affect its neighbours

Hong Kong’s Charter on External Lighting is a voluntary scheme, and its guidance distinguishes installations used for decoration, promotion and advertising from some other types of lighting. The fictional shopping lane, operating policy and time measurements below do not represent Charter thresholds.

Your classroom decision

Yan manages six shopfronts below flats on a fictional Hong Kong lane. Their decorative display signs can be scheduled. Required entrance, accessibility, emergency and task lighting are separately controlled and not touched. Each of three shop groups has two equally powered signs. All six would otherwise remain on until 23:00.

Shared switches all signs off at the latest shop’s closing time. Individual switches each shop’s sign off at its own closing time. For this exercise, the signs must remain on until their shop closes and can switch off immediately then. All timers are vetted. Ignore energy tariffs and intensity; count time for signs that stay lit after their shop closes.

The property committee requires a recurring-equivalent timer cost no higher than HK$500 each month and no more than eight after-close sign-hours on a representative day. Among feasible designs choose the lower monthly cost. Any hours shown are local times for the same day.

Round 2 · More information

Group C gets approval to trade until 22:00 on the representative day. Group A and B hours do not change. All six signs must still remain on until their shops close; both timer options and their costs remain available.

Your decision

Which plan now meets the committee’s rule? If the committee wants to keep the cheaper Shared system, what is the latest closing time Group C could have on the representative day and still meet the eight-hour cap?

Debrief · Apply the shop hours, not a single sign count

No change produces 2 × (4 + 3 + 2) = 18 after-close sign-hours. Shared switches off at 21:00 and yields 2 × (2 + 1 + 0) = 6. Individual yields zero. Both fit the cost and hours limits, so choose Shared at HK$180.

Debrief · Show how one closing time affects other shops

With Group C closing at 22:00, Shared produces 2 × (3 + 2 + 0) = 10, failing the eight-hour limit. Individual yields zero and fits the HK$500 budget at HK$450, so choose Individual. No path or emergency lighting is switched off.

Debrief · Find the exact boundary

At 21:30, Shared leaves A lit 2.5 hours and B lit 1.5 hours after closing. Two signs in each group produce 2 × (2.5 + 1.5) = 8. Shared qualifies up to 21:30 under the model. Sign-hours are a simple exposure proxy, not a measurement of nuisance or a reason to restrict lawful trading.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 for the timeline, 4 for the second choice and 5 to discuss brightness, line of sight and residents’ feedback. The fictional contract rule applies only to decorative displays.
