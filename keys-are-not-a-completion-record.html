Keys handed over. Ready to move in?

An office offer includes more than floor space

Capital Business Center’s company announcement dated 6 August 2026 described a new Wan Chai serviced-office location, including workspaces, connectivity and meeting-room services. This is the provider’s description of its offer, not an independent quality assessment. There is no claim that it had handover defects. Our operator, customer and unfinished items below are entirely fictional.

Your classroom decision

You manage Harbour Rooms, a fictional serviced-office operator. An eight-person customer is due to move into its permanent office on Monday. Safety, accessibility, fire approvals, lawful occupation, access control, core connectivity and essential facilities have all been independently checked and are ready. Two promised, non-safety-critical service features remain to be checked at Friday’s joint walkthrough. Do not assume that “non-safety-critical” means optional or that a key receipt proves full completion.

Prepare a recommendation between A: Monday access to the permanent office with any unfinished items explicitly retained on an outstanding-work record; and B: an agreed temporary office on Monday and Tuesday, followed by a second move when the permanent office is ready. B has all promised features, adequate capacity and equivalent accessibility and security. The operator will fund temporary space, both necessary moves and support; neither route increases the customer’s agreed fee. A avoids a second move, while B separates work from any incomplete service features.

Neither route is imposed. The customer can decline either, and a funded escalation route preserves accommodation and all contractual remedies. Actual legal and contract review is stipulated to permit voluntary interim access without waiving completion obligations or remedies. The agreed original service date remains Monday unless a separate valid change is made; this exercise makes no such change. Do not issue a certificate of full completion just to record possession.

Round 2 · More information

Friday’s joint walkthrough confirms two outstanding promises: the permanent reception name panel is not installed, and the meeting-room wireless presentation function is not working. A correctly identified temporary name panel and a tested wired presentation connection are available. They preserve security and accessibility but do not satisfy the promised permanent features. All previously stated safety and essential-system checks remain valid; no hazardous work will occur during occupation.

The customer says it prefers one move and can work with these disclosed temporary arrangements on Monday and Tuesday. It needs the permanent name panel and wireless presentation for a Wednesday 10 a.m. demonstration. It will choose A only with a named contact, a tested completion update and a workable fallback. Contractors have booked Tuesday after-hours work with consent, targeting completion at 6 p.m.; this is a target, not proof of completion or an extension of Monday’s original promise.

You can reserve an equivalent accessible demonstration room at the same site for Wednesday 10 a.m., with tested wireless presentation and an approved temporary identity panel, at no customer charge. The customer confirms that room would meet its demonstration need if necessary. Your facilities manager can inspect both permanent features with the customer’s nominated representative at 8 a.m. Wednesday and confirm the outcome by 8:30 a.m. If either fails, the held room remains available and the repair obligation stays open. Option B remains available; no choice has yet been imposed or recorded as full completion.

Your decision

What route would you now agree with the customer, and what completion-and-fallback record must stay open after the keys are handed over?

Debrief: separate access, readiness and closure

A fits this customer’s stated wish to avoid a second move, provided it freely accepts the temporary working arrangements and the promised fallback. B remains legitimate if the customer changes its preference or interim use becomes unsuitable. Neither route makes Monday’s unfinished promises disappear. Record key/access transfer separately from completion of each service feature; do not ask the customer to sign an inaccurate “all complete” statement.

Give each outstanding item a path to closure

Record the missing name panel and failed wireless presentation separately against the original specification. For each, name the operator’s accountable facilities manager, the contractor task, the consented Tuesday access slot and the 6 p.m. target. The Wednesday 8 a.m. check tests the installed panel against the agreed identity specification and the wireless function with the intended presentation equipment. Keep evidence of the result, the customer representative’s observations and the 8:30 a.m. status communication. A contractor saying “finished” is not the same as a passed test.

Own the transition after possession

Keep one named customer contact and the reserved Wednesday room until the agreed checks succeed. If either feature fails, confirm the fallback before the 10 a.m. demonstration and give a revised repair plan without declaring the obligation closed. Retain the original promise, open-item status, access consent and all remedies; do not silently convert a temporary workaround into the purchased service. The timing allows 90 minutes between the 8:30 update and the demonstration, but the fallback is already reserved, not something to search for then.

Instructor note

Ask students to produce a short handover record, not simply choose the nicest room. It should separate access delivered, two services outstanding, temporary arrangements accepted, accountable owner, target, acceptance test, communication and fallback. Acknowledge the customer’s one-move preference while keeping its right to decline or pursue remedies. Do not treat construction “practical completion” rules from overseas guidance as a decision on this private service contract. No actual legal acceptance, waiver or property transaction is performed.
