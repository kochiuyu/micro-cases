FIN-17 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Loan covenants · Financial headroom · Financing flexibility

More credit. A tighter promise?

Ratios in a real borrowing note

FIT Hon Teng’s 2025 interim report, filed on Hong Kong’s exchange on 30 September 2025, disclosed financial covenants on certain borrowings and reported compliance during the period. This illustrates that financing terms can include financial ratios. It does not suggest a breach at that company. The distributor, offers, forecasts and remedy below are entirely fictional; their ratio is not FIT Hon Teng’s covenant.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong distributor needs HK$600,000 for an approved working-capital plan. It is choosing between two committed facilities before signing either. Each permits that draw for twelve months, with interest paid quarterly and the remaining principal due at maturity. Both have the same acceptable security, reporting obligations and HK$600,000 limit. There are no fees or variable-rate changes. Operating cash forecasts cover interest under either offer and maturity repayment separately; no automatic renewal is assumed.

A fixes interest at 6% annually but requires gross facility principal divided by the contract-defined trailing-twelve-month EBITDA to be no greater than 3.0 at each quarter-end. EBITDA is the supplied earnings measure before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with no extra add-backs. Cash is not subtracted from debt. B fixes interest at 8% and has no EBITDA-ratio covenant, but ordinary payment and other contractual duties still apply. Both are otherwise comparable solely for this exercise.

The base forecast at the first test is principal HK$600,000 and EBITDA HK$240,000. A’s written procedure is supplied in advance: forecast deterioration prompts discussion, not automatic default. If an actual quarter-end test fails, the borrower must notify the lender and has a contractual 30-day remedy window. Principal reduction during that window can restore the ratio using the tested EBITDA; further drawings are suspended until restored. The lender may accelerate repayment if the ratio remains unremedied after that window, not immediately on a failed test. No discretionary waiver is promised. These are stipulated teaching terms, not a statement of standard banking law.

Round 2 · More information

Before either offer is signed, a downside forecast reduces the first test’s contract-defined EBITDA to HK$180,000, while gross principal remains HK$600,000. This is a scenario, not an actual breach or an assigned probability. The cash plan still funds operating obligations, interest under either offer and maturity repayment. A further HK$80,000 unrestricted cash reserve is already held, separate from all those commitments. It can be used for a remedy without delaying wages, suppliers or taxes; no external rescue or new borrowing is assumed.

If the forecast becomes the actual test, A permits the owner to use that reserve for an immediate principal reduction during the 30-day window, without fee. The permitted debt then equals three times the tested EBITDA. Any principal repaid cannot be redrawn during the rest of the term, so the reserve used genuinely reduces financing flexibility. Interest subsequently applies to the lower outstanding principal. B leaves the full reserve available but retains its higher rate. No benefit value or probability is supplied for keeping the reserve, and no later-quarter EBITDA recovery is guaranteed.

Your decision

Would you keep your facility choice after the downside forecast? Calculate A’s required principal reduction and remaining reserve, and distinguish a forecast warning, a failed test with an agreed remedy, and possible acceleration after an unremedied failure.

Measure the boundary, not only the rate

The base ratio is 600,000 / 240,000 = 2.5. With principal unchanged, EBITDA must be at least 600,000 / 3 = HK$200,000. The base forecast is HK$40,000 above that boundary. At a constant HK$600,000 draw, annual interest is HK$36,000 versus HK$48,000: a HK$12,000 advantage for A. Neither this saving nor the base ratio gives a probability of staying compliant.

Calculate the remedy using the contract

At EBITDA HK$180,000, the ratio is 600,000 / 180,000 = 3.33, above 3.0. Permitted principal is 3 × 180,000 = HK$540,000. A HK$60,000 reduction restores exactly 3.0 and leaves HK$20,000 of the HK$80,000 reserve. There is no extra headroom at that tested earnings level. Paying down with cash works here because the covenant uses gross principal, not a net-debt measure.

Avoid turning a warning into an immediate call

A weaker forecast is not itself the failed quarter-end test. An actual failed test activates notification, the written remedy window and the drawing suspension. Successful timely reduction restores the specified ratio; an unremedied failure permits acceleration after the window. None of this promises a waiver or prevents other contractual duties from applying. Real clauses, definitions and dates must be read rather than inferred from a generic ratio label.

Choose the price of flexibility

A is defensible if the owner accepts the tighter promise and can use the funded remedy while monitoring later tests. B is defensible if keeping the full reserve and avoiding this earnings constraint is worth its higher rate. B is not risk-free and A is not automatically unaffordable. HK$12,000 is the constant-draw comparison only: after a remedy, actual interest depends on the lower balance and timing. No exact post-remedy annual saving is calculated without that schedule.

Instructor note

Ask students to calculate the threshold before considering the downside. Require separate answers for cash affordability and contractual compliance. Accept either choice with a clear value placed on retained flexibility and no invented probability, waiver or refinancing. The exercise is a fictional financing comparison, not an instruction to sign a loan, a legal opinion, or an accounting classification exercise. Keep wages, taxes, suppliers and approval rights protected.
