MKT-26 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · B2B value propositions · Cash and capacity benefits · Tender communication

A lower price. A clearer customer business case?

Time released is not necessarily cash released

The UK Government Efficiency Framework distinguishes a reduction in spending from staff time released for other work. Customer-value research likewise asks suppliers to understand the buyer’s actual use, rather than only list product features. These are background ideas, not rules imposed on the fictional private tender below.

Your classroom decision

You lead the bid team of a fictional Hong Kong document-workflow service. A private trade association is evaluating a one-year contract. Your fixed all-in price is HK$60,000, including implementation, training, support and exit assistance. The service meets every mandatory security, privacy, accessibility and delivery requirement. Neither presentation changes the product, price or contractual terms.

You may submit one of two fully compliant presentation versions. Feature-led uses its permitted two-page executive summary for a clear comparison with the association’s current process: searchable records, routing and reusable templates. Use-case-led uses that space to connect the same functions to the purchaser’s documented workload and costs. Both retain the complete required technical and compliance schedules. The tender expressly allows either summary format and assesses fit, credible benefits and total one-year cost; its criteria will not change.

A jointly checked task sample, scaled to the association’s agreed annual workload, forecasts 1,200 staff hours released over the year at unchanged quality. Its planning cost rate is HK$200 per hour. A separate budget review confirms HK$36,000 of external printing and courier spend would disappear. There is no overlap between those expenses and staff time. The payroll and redeployment implications have not yet been confirmed. No figures are guarantees of realised benefits.

Would you lead with the feature comparison or the quantified customer-use case, and how would you label the 1,200 hours before payroll and redeployment are confirmed?

Round 2 · More information

The purchaser confirms it must retain its salaried staffing and roster for the full contract year. There is no overtime, agency spend, recruitment or paid vacancy that this service would avoid. Payroll will therefore not fall. The nominal HK$240,000 obtained from 1,200 hours × HK$200 cannot be presented as a cash saving.

Managers can consolidate 800 of the released hours into already authorised, non-mandatory member-record enrichment work. Each record takes 0.4 staff hour; the available backlog exceeds 2,000 records. The same staff have the required skills, and supervision and systems need no extra spending. The other 400 hours are fragmented across the roster and have no identified productive use this year. Essential checks and service standards stay intact under both versions.

The HK$36,000 external-spend reduction remains supported and excludes the enrichment work. There is no agreed monetary value or new revenue for enriched records. The purchaser has enough budget to meet the service fee, but has not decided whether this capacity benefit justifies the net extra spend. You must submit the final bid summary now, with the same price, scope and evidence. You cannot invent a future redundancy or claim the same time benefit twice.

Your decision

What would your final lead message say about cash, usable capacity and cost, and would you keep a customer-use summary or switch to the feature-led version?

A useful first-round preference is conditional

A feature comparison is easy to inspect and does not depend on unverified financial assumptions. A customer-use summary can be more relevant to the buyer, but its time forecast must initially be labelled as capacity potentially released, not a payroll saving. The HK$200 planning rate does not establish that a payment disappears.

Build a cash bridge, not an inflated return

Annual external cash spending avoided is HK$36,000. Against the HK$60,000 fee, the buyer spends HK$24,000 more over the year on the supplied facts. Labour cash saving is zero. Claiming HK$240,000 plus HK$36,000 as cash benefits, or describing HK$216,000 as net cash savings, would be wrong.

Make the capacity benefit concrete

The usable 800 hours can support 800 ÷ 0.4 = 2,000 enriched records. The other 400 hours have no demonstrated productive use in the year. Neither 2,000 records nor 800 hours should be converted to cash without an agreed mechanism. They describe the same benefit, so do not add their values together.

A defensible lead message

For an all-in annual fee of HK$60,000, the proposed workflow is forecast to remove HK$36,000 of external printing and courier costs and provide usable capacity for 2,000 additional record enrichments, without reducing payroll. Net extra cash spend is HK$24,000. A further 400 released hours have no identified use this year. Benefits depend on the stated workload and redeployment plan.

The use-case summary remains strong if it leads with this transparent trade-off, not a false saving. Feature-led is also defensible if the buyer needs technical clarity, but the same cash limits must remain explicit. The evidence supports a customer decision, not a guaranteed tender win or a claim that the buyer must purchase.

Instructor note

Allow three minutes for the opening choice and four minutes for the revised message. Ask students to distinguish a cost-rate valuation from an actual avoided payment, and total hours released from usable hours. Require the net extra cash spend and the 2,000-record calculation.

Accept either presentation if it preserves mandatory schedules and accurately states costs, benefits and limitations. Prefer a clear customer-use case when it makes the purchaser’s trade-off easier to evaluate. This is B2B economic-value communication, not bundle preference elicitation, capability signalling or a proposal to reduce required staffing. UK and New Zealand guidance supplies concepts, not local tender law.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All suppliers, buyers, tender conditions, costs, time estimates and effects are fictional. Forecasts are not realised outcomes or guaranteed savings. Public procurement guidance provides conceptual background, not legal rules for this private tender. The two New Zealand sources and two UK sources are related institutional guidance.

  1. UK Government, Government Efficiency Framework. Sections4.3–4.5 and6.2 distinguish budget reductions from non-cash benefits and require evidence, net costs and no double counting.
  2. James Anderson and James Narus, Business Marketing: Understand What Customers Value (1998), author-university summary. Public research summary explains customer-specific value models and first-hand assessments. It is a substantive summary, not access to the full journal article.
  3. New Zealand Government Procurement, Decide your evaluation criteria. Distinguishes mandatory submission conditions from qualitative value and capability criteria. Used as background, not law governing this private Hong Kong tender.
  4. New Zealand Government Procurement, Writing the business case. Public guidance connects costs, benefits, risks, objectives and options in a buyer’s decision. No restricted contract material is used.
  5. UK Cabinet Office, Assessing competitive tenders (updated 17 August 2026). Guidance links evidence to stated award criteria and whole-life value. Its legal rules are not asserted to apply to this fictional private buyer.
