ENT-46 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Venture exit · Asset bundles · Net recovery · Transfer scope

The workshop closes. Which assets sell together?

Clearance lists what can be sold

Hong Kong’s Government Logistics Department publishes auction information for stores, including descriptions and quantities. This exercise treats that as an example of identifying what is offered for sale. A fictional solvent workshop is comparing private written offers rather than running a public auction.

Your classroom decision

The owners of a closing Hong Kong craft workshop must clear their unit before its agreed hand-back date. All customer, staff and creditor commitments have already been dealt with and there are no outstanding claims in this exercise. The remaining choice is which private offer produces the most cash net of clearance costs.

One buyer offers HK$110,000 for a bundle of the three physical lots in the exhibit, conditional on also receiving a copy of the design software and its licence. The owners initially believe the licence is transferable and expect bundle clearance costs of HK$6,000. Three other buyers offer for the physical lots separately and require no software. All buyers can collect and pay by the same fixed deadline.

Listed clearance costs cover removal and hand-back work attributable to each offer. The original purchase cost is sunk. There are no other differing costs, taxes or waiting options in this exercise.

Your decision

Which offer currently appears to recover more cash? Calculate both amounts and state what the owners must check before agreeing.

Round 2 · More information

The software provider confirms in writing that the workshop cannot transfer its software licence. The bundle buyer offers HK$98,000 for the physical equipment only. Clearance cost stays HK$6,000. The three separate buyers’ terms and the deadline are unchanged. Software is removed from the handover by the workshop at a cost common to both plans.

Your decision

Which offer now gives the greater recovery, and what physical-only bundle offer would make the owners indifferent?

Debrief · Compare cash after clearance

Separate lots recover HK$58,000 + HK$26,000 + HK$11,000 = HK$95,000. The original bundle would recover HK$110,000 − HK$6,000 = HK$104,000 if it could include the licence, a HK$9,000 advantage. That figure is conditional, not cash already available.

Debrief · Physical custody does not give transfer rights

The software provider’s decision removes that component from either handover. Under the physical-only quote, the bundle recovers HK$98,000 − HK$6,000 = HK$92,000. Choose the separate lots, which now lead by HK$3,000.

Debrief · Find the threshold for the same scope

With unchanged clearance cost, a physical-only bundle needs HK$95,000 + HK$6,000 = HK$101,000 to tie. Compare future cash for the same eligible assets rather than applying the old premium to a smaller bundle.

Debrief · Keep a handover list

For each lot record what is included, the collection deadline and who verifies removal. Write explicit exclusions for software and other items not sold. A headline bid is incomplete if scope or clearance costs are unclear.

Instructor note

Allow 15 minutes: 2 to read, 4 to net the offers, 4 to value the revised scope, and 5 to draft a handover list. Ask why an owner should not count purchase cost or advertise rights not held.
