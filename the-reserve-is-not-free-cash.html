GVR-46 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Emergency governance · Designated reserves · Replenishment controls

The reserve is not free cash

Response funds have a defined purpose

Hong Kong’s statutory Emergency Relief Fund provides urgent relief following events such as fires and floods. Separately, charity regulators in Singapore and England and Wales publish guidance about maintaining reserves for defined purposes and unexpected cost. The charity, internal designation and exception below are fictional; they are not terms of Hong Kong’s public fund.

Your classroom decision

Yan manages a fictional Hong Kong outreach charity. The board has set aside part of its own unrestricted cash for emergency deployment. This reserve is internally designated: no donor restriction applies, but management cannot use it for routine spending without the board’s written exception. All other essential obligations are separately covered.

The charity has an ordinary supplier payment due today. Available ordinary cash falls short. The emergency reserve also has a verified payment for response equipment due this week; count that commitment now, before releasing any cash for routine use. The exhibit gives amounts before either payment.

A reserve advance for ordinary spending needs signatures from the board chair and treasurer, a verified ordinary grant receipt within 30 days sufficient to repay it, and a dated replenishment instruction. The advance may not take the uncommitted emergency reserve below HK$70,000 and is capped at HK$20,000. The verified receipt is legally available for ordinary expenses. Paying the emergency equipment bill itself serves the reserve’s designated purpose.

Round 2 · More information

Before any signatures or payment, the verified emergency equipment commitment rises to HK$32,000. The confirmed future receipt and both budgets otherwise stay unchanged. The ordinary supplier has not agreed to a split or a delay. The board permits seeking revised written terms but not missing the due date unilaterally.

Your decision

How much of the gap can the advance cover now? If the board wants to preserve the emergency floor and pay under the stated controls, what must it arrange before treating the ordinary invoice as funded?

Debrief · Deduct the emergency commitment first

The ordinary gap is HK$35,000 − HK$22,000 = HK$13,000. The reserve has HK$110,000 − HK$26,000 = HK$84,000 after its own commitment, or HK$14,000 headroom above the floor. The maximum advance is the smallest of HK$14,000 headroom, HK$20,000 policy cap and HK$18,000 assured repayment: HK$14,000.

Debrief · Release only what solves the problem

The advance needed is HK$13,000, leaving HK$71,000 uncommitted in the emergency reserve. Authorise it only after both approvals and a dated restoration plan using the confirmed receipt. Reconcile each release and credit the replenishment back to the designated reserve when it is received.

Debrief · A receipt later does not remove the floor today

The revised commitment leaves HK$110,000 − HK$32,000 = HK$78,000, or HK$8,000 headroom. The available advance now covers HK$8,000 of the HK$13,000 gap, leaving HK$5,000 unfunded. Obtain verified ordinary funding or an agreed written change covering that timing gap. Do not treat a proposed split as consent or spend through the reserve floor.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 for the cash and reserve bridge, 4 for the stress, and 5 for the authorization log. Ask how the answer would change if the reserve instead came from an externally restricted gift. The board exception here is invented and does not determine real charity powers.
