MKT-39 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Numeric distribution · Category-weighted distribution · Market access

More stockists. More of the category market?

Count outlets, then ask what the weights mean

Retail distribution can describe how many outlets carry a brand or how much category spending those outlets represent. These are different measures of access. Neither tells us what shoppers will buy from the brand. The Hong Kong sauce business, store universe, costs and historical category figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional Harbour Spoon sells one bottled cooking sauce. Its defined market contains exactly one hundred physical outlets that sell the relevant sauce category. Ten currently stock its product. The next-quarter plan retains those ten and adds either package M, thirty small stockists, or package L, ten larger stockists.

The two candidate groups do not overlap with each other or with existing stockists. The remaining fifty outlets are unavailable this quarter. These four groups form the entire teaching universe; online outlets and restaurants are excluded consistently. The case does not estimate coverage of all Hong Kong retail.

Each indivisible package costs HK$60,000, the entire incremental listing and service budget. All differing listing, delivery and support charges for the quarter are included. Existing-store costs are unchanged. Neither package may be mixed, split or combined, and no retailer agreement has been signed.

For the comparison, every chosen outlet carries this same product for the whole quarter, with equal shelf support and no out-of-stocks. Supply capacity and food-safety requirements are satisfied under either plan. Store size may matter for category sales, but does not establish customers’ preference for this sauce.

The commercial objective is to maximise access to category spending using a consistent historical sales benchmark within the fixed budget. A colleague proposes M because it adds three times as many outlets. Store-level category sales will arrive before the decision. Actual brand sales, margins and conversion in the proposed stores are not known.

Round 2 · More information

The previous quarter’s complete category-sales ledger uses the same one hundred outlets and one consistent sauce-category definition. The ten existing stockists generated HK$100,000 in category sales; the thirty M candidates generated HK$150,000; the ten L candidates generated HK$300,000; the fifty remaining outlets generated HK$450,000. These are disjoint group totals, not per-store figures.

The market total is therefore HK$1,000,000. All amounts include every brand in the category, not just Harbour Spoon. The ledger is a historical benchmark, held fixed when comparing proposed coverage. It does not forecast next quarter’s category spending or claim that listing Harbour Spoon creates these sales.

Numeric distribution divides chosen outlet count by all one hundred outlets. Category-weighted distribution divides the historical category sales of chosen outlets by the category sales of all one hundred. Include the retained ten stores under both plans. Do not weight by total grocery turnover or use only the new stores as the market denominator.

Your decision

Calculate total and incremental numeric and category-weighted distribution for M and L. Which meets the stated access objective? Write a claim that distinguishes covered category spending from Harbour Spoon’s future sales or market share.

Debrief · Both measures have a consistent universe

The existing network has numeric distribution of 10/100 = 10%. Its category-weighted distribution is 100,000/1,000,000 = 10%. That numerical equality is accidental. Each metric has its own numerator and denominator; both use the same outlet universe, product scope and historical sales period.

M reaches forty outlets, so numeric distribution is 40%. L reaches twenty, so it is 20%. Relative to the existing 10%, the gains are thirty and ten percentage points. M wins the store-count comparison. This is not a count of distinct shoppers: the same person may visit several outlets.

Debrief · Weight by the specified category

M covers category sales of 100,000 + 150,000 = HK$250,000, or 25% of the market benchmark. L covers 100,000 + 300,000 = HK$400,000, or 40%. Relative to the existing 10%, the gains are fifteen and thirty percentage points. L wins category-spending coverage despite having fewer stockists.

The thirty M candidates average HK$5,000 of category sales each; the ten L candidates average HK$30,000. These averages explain the reversal but are not needed to calculate the group totals. Weighting by all grocery sales would answer a different question. Do not call category-weighted distribution an all-commodity-volume measure.

Debrief · Recommend access, not promised capture

Choose L for this stated historical category-access objective at the same HK$60,000 cost. It adds access to HK$300,000 of benchmark category spending rather than M’s HK$150,000. That is twice the additional category spending, not three times and not a claim that Harbour Spoon earns either amount.

A precise statement is: “L would place our sauce in twenty of the hundred defined outlets, representing 40% of their previous-quarter sauce-category sales.” Do not say the brand gains 40% market share, reaches 40% of people or will sell HK$400,000. Availability and consumer purchase are separate events.

Debrief · Keep the next decision open

To rank expected profit, obtain credible brand-level demand, margin, competitive conditions and sustained availability evidence, and include all relevant future costs. Equal listing budgets do not make conversion or contribution identical. Distribution can both influence and respond to brand demand, so a historical association alone does not establish the effect of these proposed listings.

After implementation, monitor actual store coverage and out-of-stocks separately from sell-through and contribution. Recalculate weights when the agreed benchmark changes and show that change explicitly. If the objective becomes neighbourhood convenience, unique customer reach or profit, reconsider the evidence rather than reusing the category-coverage ranking as a universal answer.

Instructor note

MKT-09 deduplicates advertising audiences; MKT-21 assigns credit for recorded cross-channel purchases. This case compares prospective distribution breadth using store counts and historical category weights. There are no duplicated stores, no display redesign and no measured sales uplift. Ask students to preserve both valid coverage answers rather than declaring one percentage false.
