HSP-11 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Booking limits · Cancellation dependence · Guest commitments

A cancellation forecast. Another promise?

In the news

On 15 June 2026, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council described accommodation complaints, including a guest moved from a full guesthouse to a substitute with different facilities. It called for clearer responsibilities and prompt explanations when booked accommodation cannot be provided.

That complaint does not establish deliberate overbooking. Our fictional hotel instead faces a decision before accepting another reservation.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Harbour Window has 20 usable rooms for one future night and 20 reservations. Eighteen bookings will definitely arrive in this simplified model. The remaining two each have a 50% chance of cancelling before the arrival-day allocation cutoff. Cancellation terms are unchanged. There are no other arrivals, extensions, cancellations or room failures.

A new guest will definitely stay if accepted. Choose A: stop at the existing 20 reservations; or B: accept this one extra reservation with a funded, expressly agreed alternative-accommodation arrangement.

Before buying, the new guest has seen and accepts either Harbour Window or a named nearby partner hotel, with the room, access needs, facilities and location checked against the guest’s stated requirements. A partner room can be held exclusively through the allocation cutoff. If all original guests arrive, the new guest uses that agreed alternative, with paid transport and the stated service credit. The existing 20 guests retain their original rooms and rights; nobody is pressured to move.

The arrangement, communication, cancellation/refund rights and funding have been professionally checked for this fictional exercise. No statutory right is waived. Do not infer that any real hotel may override a specific-hotel reservation by paying compensation. B is a transparent two-property promise, not a concealed guarantee of Harbour Window.

Do not add the guest’s payment again. A has zero incremental contribution. Common existing-booking receipts, cancellation refunds and costs cancel out in the comparison. Both plans are fully funded.

Initial model: the two uncertain bookings cancel independently. Neither cancels with probability 25%; at least one cancels with probability 75%. These supplied probabilities are classroom assumptions, not hotel-industry estimates. No later room sales or forecast reputation benefit is included.

Your decision

Stop at 20 or accept the extra booking under the stated protections? Compare the expected incremental contribution, then name a guest-service reason that could still justify stopping.

Round 2 · More information

A booking review establishes that the two uncertain reservations belong to the same event delegation. Their trip depends on one event decision: with 50% probability both cancel, and with 50% probability both arrive. There is no one-cancellation outcome.

Each booking still has a 50% cancellation chance. The expected number of cancellations is still one. What changes is whether a room becomes available: under the linked model that chance is only 50%, not 75%.

The other 18 arrivals, the new guest’s accepted alternatives, partner-room availability, cash amounts and guest protections stay exactly the same. The event decision becomes known before allocation. This is the supplied fictional dependency, not evidence that all group bookings cancel together.

Your decision

Would you now accept the extra booking? Recalculate its expected incremental contribution and explain why an unchanged average cancellation count does not preserve the original decision.

An average is not an available room

Initially the four equally likely combinations are: neither cancels, first only, second only, or both. Three provide at least one room. Expected cancellations are 0 × 25% + 1 × 50% + 2 × 25% = 1.

After the reveal, only two outcomes remain: zero cancellations or two, equally likely. Expected cancellations remain 0 × 50% + 2 × 50% = 1, but the chance of finding at least one room falls from 75% to 50%. A second empty room cannot be used twice by the one extra guest.

Compare complete incremental outcomes

Initially B adds 0.75 × 600 − 0.25 × 800 − 100 = HK$150 in expected contribution. Actual incremental outcomes are +HK$500 or −HK$900 after the hold fee, not a certain HK$150.

With the linked reservations, B adds 0.50 × 600 − 0.50 × 800 − 100 = −HK$200. A remains zero. On the supplied cash criterion, stopping now is preferable. No invented demand or reputation bonus rescues B.

Keep the guest promise in the decision

A can be chosen initially despite B’s positive expected contribution: it avoids another guest facing location uncertainty and avoids reliance on the partner arrangement. B initially has a defensible cash benefit only within the stated, funded and accepted protections.

The hold does not add a twenty-first room at Harbour Window. Disclosure and compensation do not make the two experiences identical. If the alternative becomes unavailable or unsuitable, the exercise’s premise fails; a larger expected margin cannot substitute for honouring rights.

Check joint behaviour before raising limits

A reservation-level cancellation percentage is not a complete booking model. Ask which reservations depend on a common event or shared travel decision, when uncertainty resolves and which capacity can actually serve each promise.

This two-outcome exercise illustrates the mechanism, not an optimal booking rule for all hotels. It omits price segments, multiple-night stays, further demand and uncertain accommodation costs.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the setting, 3 for an initial vote, 2 for the reveal, 3 for calculation and revote, and 2 for synthesis. Have students draw the possible cancellation outcomes before comparing the alternatives.

Expected cancellations remain one, but the probability of at least one vacant room falls from 75% to 50%. Count the separate HK$100 hold once: B’s expected incremental contribution changes from positive HK$150 to negative HK$200, against A’s zero.

Keep the guest promise central: B offers an expressly accepted, suitable two-property arrangement with a held partner room, not a concealed specific-hotel guarantee. No existing guest is displaced. Do not invent reputational benefits or generalise the supplied dependency to every group booking.
