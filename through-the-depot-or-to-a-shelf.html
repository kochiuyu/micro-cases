OPS-19 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Cross-docking · Flow eligibility · Warehouse exceptions

Straight through the depot. Or onto a shelf?

A hub connects incoming and outgoing flows

DHL announced the opening of its expanded Central Asia Hub in Hong Kong on 14 November 2023, describing increased handling capacity and connections across its network. This illustrates coordinated logistics infrastructure, not a claim about the warehouse method or savings in our case. The small distributor and all figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong distributor expects 120 identical sealed cartons of non-perishable stationery at 8 a.m. tomorrow. Four confirmed customer loads each require 30 cartons and depart at noon. Inbound identification, quantity, condition and required clearance checks can finish by 9 a.m. A trained team has sufficient safe staging and loading capacity, with three hours available before departure. No product requires repacking or special treatment.

Cross-docking sends eligible goods from receipt to their assigned outbound load without routine rack storage. The alternative puts cartons into reserved racks and picks them for dispatch. Both routes can meet the existing noon commitments. Storage capacity is reserved for all 120 if required; neither method bypasses receipt checks, safe vehicle separation or release authorisation.

The contractor’s complete relevant quote is HK$2 per cross-docked carton plus a single HK$80 coordination charge if any of this batch is cross-docked, or HK$5 per carton for storage and later picking. The storage quote includes up to seven days of holding and its eventual outbound pick; final transport and mandatory checks cost the same under both routes and are outside this comparison. A split is permitted without another fixed charge. No route is booked or paid yet. Confirm assignments and appointments again before arrival.

Your decision

Would you plan to cross-dock the 120 cartons or put them into storage? Compare the quoted costs, then specify the checks that must still pass before goods can bypass the racks.

Round 2 · More information

Before the inbound truck arrives, one customer validly cancels its entire 30-carton order and noon collection under the agreed terms. No fee, replacement order or remaining delivery promise arises from that cancellation. The other three 30-carton loads remain confirmed, with unchanged departure times and capacity. The distributor owns the 30 released cartons, but they have no assigned recipient or departure time.

The inbound quantity remains 120 and cannot be reduced at this point. Racks, staff, checks and all quoted rates remain available. The 30 unassigned cartons can be stored safely under the seven-day quote while the team finds a new allocation; no sale or dispatch within seven days is guaranteed. Longer holding would require a fresh cost review. For today’s comparison, use the quoted handling commitment, not an invented inventory-sale forecast.

The supervisor can split the receipt into 90 cross-dock cartons and 30 put-away cartons before moving either group. Receipt and location records must show both destinations, and cancelled allocation labels must be removed from the released cartons. If any remaining outbound appointment or check fails, affected cartons also go to the authorised storage route; they must not block a staging lane while waiting indefinitely.

Your decision

How would you route the shipment after the cancellation? Calculate the revised handling cost and explain what happens to the cartons without an outbound assignment, rather than treating cross-docking as an all-or-nothing label.

Match the flow before counting savings

Initially all 120 cartons have confirmed matching outbound demand and sufficient processing time. The cross-dock quote is 120 × HK$2 + HK$80 = HK$320, compared with 120 × HK$5 = HK$600 for storage and picking. The HK$280 difference is quoted handling cost, not total business profit. Quantity, identity, condition, clearance, appointments and safe capacity still have to match.

Reclassify only the exception

After cancellation, 90 cartons remain eligible. Cross-docking them costs 90 × HK$2 + HK$80 = HK$260. Storing the other 30 costs 30 × HK$5 = HK$150. The split totals HK$410, HK$190 below storing all 120 at HK$600. The fixed HK$80 is charged once, not per load or carton. Sending all 120 straight through is no longer a feasible option because 30 have no destination.

Storage is a planned path, not failure

Keep the 30 cartons in recorded reserved locations and review reassignment and holding time. Cross-docking is not permission to ship unwanted goods, skip inspection or leave freight on a traffic route. The remaining 90 can still flow without being forced onto shelves just because part of the batch changed. Any additional exception needs its own eligibility and cost review.

Instructor note

OPS-02 splits an urgent shipment around missing components; this case assumes the complete inbound shipment and asks which warehouse flow each portion qualifies for. It is not an inventory-demand forecast or a claim that fewer touches always lowers total costs. The rates are original contract assumptions, including a batch coordination charge and bounded storage period. Separate today’s routing commitment from uncertain future sales of unassigned stock.
