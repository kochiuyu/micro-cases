IS-23 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Regression to the mean · Evaluation design · Noisy performance measures

The worst branches improved. Did coaching work?

An improvement is not yet an impact

Evaluation researchers distinguish a change after a programme from a change caused by it. Selecting unusually low observations can select temporary bad luck as well as persistent difficulty. The branch chain, scores, coaching and costs below are original fiction; no real business or employee is being assessed.

Your classroom decision

You lead branch support for a Hong Kong stationery chain with 24 shops. A short, nonpunitive coaching session helps teams organise routine product-information handovers. It changes no safety procedure, customer rights or staffing. Existing training and support remain available everywhere. Results will evaluate the programme, not rank people or determine pay, promotion or dismissal.

Six branches received the optional session last month. Their average monthly handover-completeness score rose from 60 to 72 out of 100. The same scoring rubric was used in both months. The score reflects a small authorised sample of ordinary handovers, aggregated to branch level; it is not sales, profit or a complete measure of service quality. The briefing does not yet say why these branches were selected or what happened elsewhere.

You can A expand the session to the other 18 branches now for HK$18,000, including paid attendance time, or B spend HK$6,000 on a six-week evaluation and wait before a broad rollout. Under B, any subsequent rollout still costs HK$18,000. Both complete paths fit the HK$24,000 discretionary budget. The existing service continues normally. Waiting could postpone useful support; expanding could consume staff time on a programme with little added value. Keeping existing support without new spending is also available. No monetary value for a score point has been estimated.

The HK$6,000 evaluation quote includes delivery, measurement, paid participant time and the later-group coaching session even if broad rollout is cancelled. The subsequent HK$18,000 is a fixed quoted chain-wide scale-up and support package, regardless of how many branches joined the evaluation; it is not a second per-person charge for the same session.

Separate recovery from added improvement

The coached mean rose by 72 − 60 = 12 score points. The not-yet-coached mean rose by 72 − 61 = 11 points. The difference between these changes is 1 point. This is a descriptive contrast, not an identified causal effect: coaching timing was not random, and equal earlier averages do not prove equivalent future trends.

The comparison weakens the claim that coaching explains the entire 12-point rise. It does not prove coaching had zero effect, nor does it establish that exactly 11 points were regression to the mean. Shared changes, sampling variation and different branch circumstances may also matter. Without branch-level uncertainty, a significance test or precise interval cannot be calculated from these means alone.

Why choosing the lowest month changes the story

Imagine an unchanged branch whose recorded score combines its underlying performance with temporary variation. Selecting a month precisely because its score is unusually low tends to select negative temporary variation. A fresh measurement need not repeat that unusually negative component. Across such selections, less extreme readings can follow even without coaching. This is regression to the mean, not a corrective force that makes every branch recover.

It is an average tendency under an appropriate repeated-measurement setting, not a prediction that every low score rises next month or returns to 74. Genuine persistent problems can remain, and a useful intervention can coexist with regression to the mean. The earlier records make the selection issue visible; they do not reveal each branch’s true long-run mean.

Choose spending and wording separately

A can be defended as a bounded practical choice if management values timely support despite uncertain effectiveness, accepts its HK$18,000 cost and monitors burden and quality. It cannot be justified by claiming a proven 12-point coaching effect. B buys a more credible comparison but costs HK$6,000, delays possible benefits and may still be inconclusive. Keeping existing support preserves the budget but forgoes both possible benefit and additional learning.

A responsible report says: both selected low-scoring groups improved; the coached group improved one point more in this descriptive comparison; the programme’s added effect remains uncertain. No score-to-profit conversion or cost-effectiveness calculation is justified.

Improve the design before collecting another success story

Define eligibility in advance, informed by repeated baseline measurements rather than a single extreme month. Randomly assign early and later coaching among comparable consenting eligible branches, preserve routine support, and measure both groups over the same period before later coaching begins. Random assignment makes systematic baseline differences less likely; it does not guarantee exact balance in a small sample.

Predefine the branch-level outcome and analysis, document participation, monitor spillover and workload differences, keep the sampling rubric consistent, and report uncertainty. Repeated baselines help describe variability but are not a substitute for a credible contemporaneous comparison. Merely finding other branches with the same one-month score does not automatically solve the problem. No employee-level consequential decision is permitted.

Instructor note

0–4 minutes: show the initial briefing and vote. Ask students to distinguish a 12-point change from a programme effect. 4–8 minutes: reveal how branches were selected and the comparison history; calculate both changes and revote. 8–12 minutes: sketch underlying performance plus temporary variation and explain why selecting a low observation matters. 12–15 minutes: compare the spending choice with the claim students would write.

Do not declare the programme ineffective, call the 1-point contrast causal, infer significance without dispersion, or equate the comparison recovery entirely with regression to the mean. This case concerns selection on an extreme noisy baseline, not searching twenty treatments for a winning p-value, aggregate correlation, or contamination between users. Accept different spending choices when their evidence claims and protections are honest.
