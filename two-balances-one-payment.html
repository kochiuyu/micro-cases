ACC-09 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Accounting · Bank reconciliation · Payment controls

Two balances. One payment.

In the news · Issued is not yet settled

On 11 December 2015, HKMA and the Hong Kong Association of Banks marked the e-Cheque service launched four days earlier.

A digital cheque still needs presentation and processing. That historical context introduces our fictional workshop’s accounting question; no real bank or customer records are involved.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. Harbour Repair has approved a HK$40,000 equipment purchase and checked the supplier and invoice. Paying today secures agreed next-day dispatch; waiting means the next dispatch opportunity. No discount, extra income or financial penalty is assumed.

The manager requires at least HK$25,000 to remain after the new payment and after allowing for payments already issued but not yet processed. That reserve covers all other known needs over this short decision horizon. It is the workshop’s supplied rule, not a legal minimum.

Two unreconciled records show different amounts at the same cutoff. The cash book is the business’s own bank-account transaction record, not cash in a drawer. The bank statement shows posted transactions, not a certified amount free to spend. The discrepancy must be explained before release.

Unreconciled record · same cutoffBalanceApparent amount after HK$40,000
Cash bookHK$58,000HK$18,000: below reserve
Bank statementHK$70,000HK$30,000: above reserve

For this bounded exercise, there are no other unknown flows, bank holds, restrictions, overdraft or foreign-currency issues. The two screens are not from different dates. Neither apparent remainder is yet a verified payment basis.

Your decision

Would you provisionally hold or release this approved payment? Which balance supports your view, and what must be verified before any release?

Round 2 · One fact changes

An independent authorised reviewer matches three items to supporting records:

Customer receipt: HK$9,000. The transfer has settled, is unrestricted and available, and matches an existing receivable. The bank includes it; the cash book does not.

Bank service charges: HK$1,000. This confirmed aggregate charge has been debited by the bank but is missing from the cash book.

Issued e-Cheque: HK$4,000. It is correctly recorded as a payment in the cash book, but the supplier has not presented it, so the statement has not deducted it. The cheque remains valid and due.

These explain all differences at the cutoff, with no further movement before the decision. The reviewer confirms the reconciliation and existing payment approvals. The task is to apply the reserve rule to these verified classroom facts, not to execute a real transfer.

Your decision

Can the approved payment now meet the reserve rule? Which items change the cash book, and which must not be recorded there a second time?

Correct the business record where entries are missing

Add the settled HK$9,000 receipt and deduct the HK$1,000 confirmed charges: HK$58,000 + HK$9,000 − HK$1,000 = HK$66,000.

The receipt collects an already-recorded receivable; it is not new sales revenue. The bank charge needs its missing entry. The HK$4,000 cheque is already recorded correctly in the cash book, so deducting it there again would count the same payment twice.

Explain the bank side without changing settled facts

The statement’s HK$70,000 includes money already committed to the unpresented cheque. Allowing for that HK$4,000 gives HK$66,000, matching the corrected cash book.

Do not add the HK$9,000 to the statement again: it is already included. The original HK$12,000 gap consists of HK$8,000 net missing cash-book entries and HK$4,000 of payment timing. Reconciliation explains the difference; it creates no money.

Use the agreed amount for the conditional decision

HK$66,000 − HK$40,000 leaves HK$26,000, which meets the supplied HK$25,000 reserve with HK$1,000 to spare. On the verified facts and approvals, the workshop can release the approved payment.

The bank might initially display HK$30,000 after the new payment, but HK$4,000 is still committed to the issued cheque. The usable remainder under this rule is HK$26,000, not HK$30,000.

This conclusion depends on the stated availability and complete reconciliation. It does not establish overall profitability, solvency or the absence of every possible control risk.

Keep correction and timing separate next time

Update omitted ledger transactions, retain evidence and reconcile already-recorded outstanding payments until they clear. Keep the payment approval and reserve check separate from the act of matching balances.

Holding while the discrepancy was unexplained was a prudent response. Once the evidence resolves it and the approved rule is met, the answer can change. The lesson is neither “trust the bigger balance” nor “never pay until every issued cheque clears”.

Instructor note

Before the reveal, ask students to state what their preferred balance is missing. A provisional release hypothesis is conditional; do not reward ignoring an unexplained gap merely because the bank screen looks sufficient.

Optional journal detail: debit bank-account cash HK$9,000 and credit receivables HK$9,000; debit bank charges HK$1,000 and credit cash HK$1,000. No new entry is needed merely because the correctly recorded HK$4,000 remains unpresented.

Use two parallel bridges to HK$66,000: cash book plus missing receipt less charges; statement less outstanding payment. For an exit question, ask why the HK$4,000 is not deducted again from the corrected cash book. The equipment’s merit is already approved; no investment appraisal or new revenue recognition is required.
