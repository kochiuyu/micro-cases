Five per cent more. More of what?

In the news · A headline price measure

On 22 January 2026, Hong Kong’s Census and Statistics Department reported December 2025 Composite CPI 1.4% above December 2024.

This historical household-price measure is not a forecast or the cost of the fictional retailer’s materials below.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong art-supply retailer is planning its next free introductory workshop programme. The last comparable programme used 100 identical participant kits at HK$100 each: HK$10,000 in materials.

The new materials allocation is HK$10,500, a 5% increase. Management also authorises HK$2,000 of discretionary contingency for either plan. Unused contingency remains available for later unplanned commercial needs; it is not protecting wages, safety, debt or other obligations.

The owner must provisionally choose 100 or 105 places before confirming current kit prices. No places have been promised. Staffing, room and scheduling capacity for either plan are already funded separately. The contents and quality of each kit will stay the same.

The current same-kit quote is not yet available. A bigger allocation alone does not establish how many kits it buys. Neither plan is assumed to generate extra shop sales.

Your decision

Which plan would you provisionally choose, 100 or 105 places? What same-kit price comparison could change your choice?

Round 2 · More information

The supplier confirms complete delivered costs at either order size for exactly the same kit quantities and quality. The paper pack rises from HK$40 to HK$50 per kit. All other kit materials remain HK$60.

The kit therefore costs HK$110 instead of HK$100. There is no order-size discount, minimum-order issue or omitted delivery, tax or setup charge.

Both plans fit the HK$12,500 total authorised allocation plus contingency. A requires HK$11,000 for 100 kits; B requires HK$11,550 for 105. No other resources or customer commitments change.

These are fictional supplier figures for this specific kit basket, not a claim about Hong Kong paper prices or general inflation.

Your decision

After the quote, choose 100 or 105 places. State your contingency draw and what you gain or give up. What would justify your choice?

Name the buffer you would use

A costs 100 × HK$110 = HK$11,000. It draws HK$500 above the HK$10,500 ordinary allocation, leaving HK$1,500 contingency.

B costs 105 × HK$110 = HK$11,550. It draws HK$1,050, leaving HK$950 contingency. Five more places use HK$550 more of the buffer.

Both are authorised and feasible. A preserves flexibility for later needs; B provides five additional workshop places. Keeping 100 still needs a draw. Neither budget calculation establishes shop revenue, profit or learning outcomes.

More dollars need not mean more kits

The ordinary allocation rises 5%, but the unchanged kit costs 10% more. Its purchasing power for this basket falls, even though its money amount rises.

The headline household CPI describes a different basket. Its lower historical rate does not make it wrong; it simply cannot replace the same-kit quote for this decision. A narrow kit comparison is not an index of the retailer’s whole business.

Weight the basket, not just the percentages

Paper rises 25%, from HK$40 to HK$50. Other materials are unchanged. Previously paper accounted for 40% of kit spending and other materials 60%.

The basket change is 0.40 × 25% + 0.60 × 0% = 10%, matching HK$110 ÷ HK$100 − 1. An unweighted average of the two percentage changes would give 12.5% and answer the wrong question.

The comparison holds quantities and quality fixed, so the change is about prices rather than a larger or better kit.

Optional depth · Express the allocation in the old basket’s prices

With the kit price index rising from 100 to 110, HK$10,500 ÷ 1.10 = about HK$9,545.45 in previous-kit-price dollars. Relative purchasing power changes by 1.05 ÷ 1.10 − 1 = about −4.5%, not exactly −5%.

Without contingency, the allocation buys 95 whole HK$110 kits, spending HK$10,450 and leaving HK$50. The divisible result of about 95.45 is not a usable kit count. That calculation explains purchasing power; it is not a third offered plan.

Instructor note

Keep the first vote provisional: students do not yet know current kit prices. Ask which comparable quote they need, not for a precise cost guessed from the CPI headline.

After the reveal, require a choice and its buffer consequence. A is not automatically prudent and B is not automatically growth: the owner has authorised either tradeoff. No extra sales value or probability of future contingency needs is supplied.

The main lesson is nominal allocation versus purchasing power and relevance of the chosen basket. The optional deflation calculation can follow the practical decision. Do not turn the case into a margin exercise, inflation forecast or argument that official household measures are defective.
