Payment promised. Documents accepted?

Trade payment protection has documentary conditions

A documentary credit is a bank undertaking tied to a complying presentation of the specified documents. It is distinct from the underlying sale. Open-account trade instead relies on the buyer’s payment obligation after shipment. Fees, financing and the ability to prepare documents matter alongside the commercial relationship.

The exporter, banks, buyer and terms below are invented. These are simplified classroom conditions, not a quotation, complete statement of documentary-credit rules or advice to enter a transaction.

Your classroom decision

An exporter has a HK$200,000 order from an established overseas business buyer. Previous orders were paid on time, but no default probability or guarantee is supplied. Both sides accept either open-account payment or a documentary credit. Currency, order size, shipping obligations and payment date are identical: payment is due on day 30 after shipment. Necessary liquidity and production are funded; this is not an emergency cash-gap decision.

Open-account financing and administration cost HK$1,000. The documentary-credit route costs HK$3,000 for financing, bank charges and administration, with no other initial exporter charge. The fictional issuing bank’s irrevocable undertaking is payable on day 30 only after a complying presentation by day 10. Required documents are the commercial invoice, packing list and carrier transport document, with the exact required information supplied to the exporter before shipment. Document preparation is feasible; specialised staff are available. Bank and country risk have been assessed but are not claimed to disappear.

The bank examines documents, not whether the physical shipment makes the buyer happy. The underlying sales rights remain separate. No later switch of payment route is assumed. All amounts are HK dollars; there is no exchange-rate exposure in this exercise.

Round 2 · More information

For round two, suppose the exporter chose the documentary credit. The goods were shipped as agreed. On day 8, the bank identifies one discrepancy: the exporter’s commercial invoice mistakenly states USD200,000 instead of the required HK$200,000. The actual sales price is HK$200,000. All other documents and conditions are checked and compliant. The bank has not accepted the discrepant presentation.

The stipulated options are: A, spend HK$600 on an authorised truthful correction and expedited resubmission, assured to reach the bank on day 9; or B, pay HK$200 to request a waiver through the bank. Under these fictional agreed terms, a waiver works only if both buyer and issuing bank accept it by day 10. Their response is unknown and may arrive too late. If no waiver arrives, the next correction can reach the bank only on day 11, after the deadline. No extension or late acceptance is promised.

The original HK$3,000 is already incurred under either choice; the new charges are additional. Both are affordable and include all relevant incremental handling and financing cost in the stipulated scenario. Correcting the invoice means replacing an error with the true agreed currency through the authorised process, never altering a third-party document or inventing shipment facts. If the discrepancy remains unresolved at the deadline, this bank undertaking is not payable under the supplied terms; the underlying HK$200,000 buyer obligation remains, but collection is uncertain.

Your decision

Would you pay for timely correction or request a waiver? Compare the additional costs and deadline consequences, and explain what happens to the bank undertaking if the discrepancy is not resolved.

Separate the promises

The buyer’s obligation arises from the sale. The bank’s separate undertaking depends on the stipulated complying presentation. An on-time, correct shipment does not automatically cure a currency discrepancy in the invoice. Conversely, documentary compliance is not a bank inspection or warranty of the goods.

Price the initial choice

The documentary route costs HK$2,000 more than open account. It adds a conditional bank undertaking, not unconditional insurance against every loss. Open account remains defensible for a firm accepting the established buyer’s credit exposure and wanting lower cost. The credit route remains defensible for a firm valuing the bank undertaking and able to manage its requirements. No default probability is supplied, so an expected-value ranking would invent evidence.

Compare the two repair routes

Correction adds HK$600, giving HK$3,600 total charges, and reaches the bank before the deadline with the sole discrepancy truthfully corrected. Waiver adds HK$200, giving HK$3,200 incurred charges, but requires two acceptances in time. The extra HK$400 buys the stipulated timely correction rather than reliance on that uncertain waiver. The original HK$3,000 is sunk for this second decision.

A failed condition is not an automatic write-off

Recommend correction if preserving the complying presentation is worth the extra HK$400. A student choosing waiver must explicitly accept the possibility that the bank undertaking becomes unavailable, not assume that the buyer’s goodwill binds the bank. If the deadline is missed, do not equate the unavailable bank undertaking with a certain HK$200,000 loss: the buyer still owes payment, with recovery unknown. Real cases require the actual credit, applicable rules, bank advice and any refusal or amendment process.

Instructor note

0–4 minutes: choose the payment route and describe its conditions. 4–8 minutes: reveal the currency discrepancy and map day 8, day 9, day 10 and day 11. 8–12 minutes: compare incremental costs and identify which approvals a waiver needs. 12–15 minutes: distinguish physical delivery, bank payment conditions and the buyer’s continuing obligation.

Do not invite forged or backdated documents, invent a waiver probability, or call the credit risk-free. The timing and cure routes are stipulated teaching terms, not universal UCP deadlines. Unlike FIN-13, there is no invoice sale or recourse calculation. This is the documentary condition for a bank undertaking.
