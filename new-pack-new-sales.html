A new pack. New sales?

A historical Hong Kong pack-size example

Swire Coca-Cola’s 2019 Sustainable Development Report describes introducing mini cans in Hong Kong after investing in a production line. This illustrates offering another pack size, not evidence that its launch displaced existing sales or reduced profit.

Our biscuit maker, costs, demand and capacity are original fiction, not Swire Coca-Cola data or a nutritional comparison.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong biscuit maker is planning one month’s uncommitted retail production. Without a launch it expects 1,000 standard-pack purchases at HK$30 each. Complete variable cost is HK$18 per pack, leaving HK$12 contribution before unchanged fixed costs. Existing accepted customer orders are separately protected and already allowed for in available capacity.

A proposed smaller pack sells for HK$20 with HK$12 variable cost, or HK$8 contribution. Its full launch forecasts 400 small-pack purchases and needs HK$600 of avoidable design and promotion cost, not yet spent. Both sizes meet quality and labelling requirements. The small pack contains fewer biscuits: pack counts are not equal weights of food.

The first worksheet adds 400 × HK$8 minus HK$600 to existing contribution. It has not checked where small-pack purchases come from or whether production fits. The manager can launch the full 400-pack proposal or retain the standard-only range this month. Nothing is announced or promised. Funding is available and there is time to review the forecast. No additional future benefit has been valued.

Round 2 · More information

A revised estimate splits the 400 small-pack purchases: 150 replace purchases that would otherwise have been one standard pack each, while 250 are genuinely additional purchases from people who would buy neither of this maker’s packs without the launch. This one-for-one substitution is a supplied forecast assumption, not a causal result proved by interviews. Standard-pack demand becomes 850 before any capacity constraint.

Production confirms 1,150 pack-sealing slots after protecting existing commitments, maintenance and safe working arrangements. Each size uses one slot: sealing and checking, not biscuit weight, is the constraint. Other stages have enough capacity. The full launch supplies all 400 small packs, leaving 750 slots for standard packs. The remaining 100 standard purchases are lost that month, not backlogged or converted into more small-pack sales.

Compare the two prepared plans: standard-only, or the complete 400-small-pack launch with remaining slots used for standard packs. A smaller launch, targeted channel, extra shift or outsourcing needs a new costed proposal and demand estimate; none is supplied. No accepted order is broken. All incremental production, selling and fulfilment costs are included, with no additional setup or inventory cost omitted. The figures are conditional plans, not guaranteed sales.

Your decision

Would you approve the prepared launch or retain the standard-only plan? Reconcile small-pack contribution, displaced standard purchases and capacity-displaced purchases, then identify one assumption worth testing before redesigning the offer.

New labels are not necessarily new business

The opening worksheet predicts 400 × HK$8 − HK$600 = HK$2,600 extra contribution. It assumes no lost standard purchases and enough capacity. Standard-only contribution is 1,000 × HK$12 = HK$12,000.

Separate switching from additions

Switching removes 150 × HK$12 = HK$1,800 of standard contribution. Before capacity, the range yields 850 × HK$12 + 400 × HK$8 − HK$600 = HK$12,800, a gain of HK$800. Cannibalisation reduces the gain but does not by itself make this unconstrained proposal unattractive.

Capacity creates another loss

Demand needs 850 + 400 = 1,250 slots, exceeding 1,150 by 100. Supplying all 400 small packs leaves 750 standard. These 100 lost standard purchases are additional to the 150 that switch; confusing them misses a second effect.

Reconcile the prepared launch

750 × HK$12 + 400 × HK$8 − HK$600 = HK$11,600, HK$400 below standard-only. The bridge is +HK$3,200 from small packs −HK$1,800 from switching −HK$1,200 from capacity displacement −HK$600 launch cost. This is contribution before unchanged fixed costs, not total company profit.

Redesign is not a free correction

Retain standard-only under the supplied figures and contribution objective. A later offer could change quantity, positioning or channel, but cannot assume it reaches only new buyers at the same cost. Test what purchasers would otherwise buy and what output is displaced. Some switching can coexist with a profitable extension; this month’s comparison is not a permanent ban on smaller packs.

Instructor note

Use three columns: new-pack purchases, old purchases replaced by customer choice, and old purchases displaced by capacity. Require the HK$800 unconstrained gain as well as the HK$400 constrained decline. The full launch is the proposal being assessed, not a free optimisation variable. Pack counts are not grams or unique customers. No outside reading, assumed lifetime value or advertising uplift is needed. Unlike removing a slow item with linked baskets, this case adds a variant competing for purchases and constrained output.
