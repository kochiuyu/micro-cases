HSP-29 · Fictional hotel · 15–20 minutes · Service recovery justice · Guest choice · Consistent entitlement

A fair recovery. Must it be identical?

Fair procedure and useful recovery are different questions

Service-recovery research distinguishes the outcome offered, the process used and the way the guest is treated. A 2026 study by Kenesei and Kolos examines context-dependent compensation expectations through scenarios, while PolyU’s account of its researchers’ work describes why an apparently valuable offer can be unsuitable for a traveller. Neither supplies a universal recovery tariff.

Public Ombudsman principles call for consistent reasons without rigidly identical responses, alongside appropriate financial or non-financial remedies. ACCC guidance separately illustrates that a business policy cannot erase applicable consumer remedies. These are conceptual references from their own jurisdictions, not legal advice about the fictional hotel.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A hotel’s advertised optional audio-guide for its lobby art display was unavailable for one hour during twelve guests’ stays. The failure is verified, non-safety-related and resolved. All twelve fall within the same published goodwill category. The hotel has apologised and explained the problem, and has separately assessed and funded every contractual or statutory remedy owed. Nothing in the goodwill choice replaces, caps or delays those rights.

The manager is designing a transparent additional recovery policy, not bargaining with the loudest guest. Cash gives each affected guest HK$100. Choice offers each guest one of three options: the same HK$100 cash; HK$200 dining credit usable during this stay; or two additional hours of checkout time. Each option has a verified complete incremental operator cost of HK$100 in this exercise, including fulfilment, administration and any displaced contribution. These are stipulated costs, not face-value equivalence or an industry rule.

All twelve can be accommodated even if everyone selects the same option: room-turnover staffing and dining capacity are secured without overtime pressure, safety shortcuts or harm to later guests. The dining credit needs no extra purchase, covers tax/service charges and can buy suitable available items. It is not transferable, has no cash change and expires at the end of the stay; those restrictions are prominent. The hotel makes no claim that a credit equals cash to a guest.

Checkout extension is exactly two hours beyond the booked time, with no new fee. The cash option is equally visible and accessible, not reserved for guests who insist. Neither policy requires a release, favourable review, loyalty enrolment or silence about the failure. Everyone receives the same explanation, response speed, private choice route and escalation access. The total additional goodwill budget is HK$1,200. The same twelve guests are covered under either policy.

Round 2 · More information

Before the policy is finalised, three illustrative guests describe their circumstances and preferences. Guest A leaves before breakfast and wants the cash, with no time to use dining or late checkout. Guest B has a late departure and says two extra checkout hours would be more useful than cash or dining. Guest C already plans to dine before leaving and prefers the credit after seeing its restrictions. These are individual statements, not demographic segments or evidence that everyone shares their rankings.

An illustrative selection exercise across all twelve guests produces four cash choices, four dining choices and four checkout choices. All have understood the alternatives; all selected non-cash options remain available. Treat these selections as the delivery plan for the cost calculation, not a representative satisfaction survey or proof of future loyalty. Each guest selects only one additional instrument.

The manager worries that different-looking outcomes could appear inconsistent. The same published eligibility category and menu can be explained to everyone without revealing another guest’s plans or preferences. Guests may change their selection before fulfilment; unused non-cash selections can be changed to cash if unusable, so expiry or availability cannot silently remove the goodwill entitlement. There is no double redemption.

A concern that the original failure caused a different material loss must be assessed separately under the retained rights and escalation route. It is not dismissed because a goodwill card was accepted. The present twelve-guest goodwill budget remains an operational planning assumption, not a ceiling on the hotel’s obligations.

Your decision

Which policy would you now adopt? Calculate the cost of the stated twelve selections, explain how different instruments can preserve a consistent entitlement, and write a brief guest-facing offer that protects cash choice and existing rights.

Answer the policy comparison

Cash costs 12 × HK$100 = HK$1,200. Choice also costs HK$1,200 if all twelve redeem one instrument, regardless of the mix, because each complete operator cost is stipulated at HK$100. Neither policy wins this exercise through lower cost. Cash is simple, unrestricted and requires little deliberation; Choice adds a small decision but preserves cash while offering potentially useful alternatives.

Prefer Choice within the supplied capacity and clear-terms safeguards. Its reason is guest fit, not a claim that HK$200 dining credit is twice as generous as HK$100 cash. A guest may place little or no value on a benefit they cannot use. If a real hotel cannot secure the promised alternatives or present them accessibly, a clear cash policy can be better than a misleading menu.

Answer the revealed guest differences

A chooses cash, B checkout and C dining because their stated circumstances differ. These are voluntary selections from the same entitlement, not manager-assigned favours. Do not infer a choice from age, nationality, loyalty status or complaint intensity. The four-four-four plan costs HK$400 cash + HK$400 dining fulfilment + HK$400 checkout fulfilment = HK$1,200.

Dining credits total HK$800 in face value, but their stipulated operator cost is HK$400. Four checkout extensions total eight guest-room hours, not eight staff-hours or a cash amount. No sum of face value, room-hours and cash establishes total guest welfare. The case supplies neither satisfaction scores nor future booking effects.

Consistent entitlement, individually useful instrument

Consistency means the same eligibility standard, equal access to the complete menu, clear terms, timely delivery and review route. It need not mean identical instruments when each guest freely chooses. Record category, offered options, selected option and fulfilment; avoid collecting unnecessary travel or personal information. Explain relevant reasons without exposing another guest’s details.

Maintain cash as a genuine fallback. Let a guest switch an unused non-cash selection when it proves unusable; prevent double redemption without turning the process into a dispute. Do not promise late checkout until capacity is secured, or count dining credit breakage as a success. Look for recurring failures and prevent them rather than relying on an attractive recovery menu.

A clear offer

“I’m sorry the audio-guide was unavailable. In addition to any remedies you are entitled to, please choose HK$100 cash, HK$200 dining credit for this stay, or two extra checkout hours. Cash is equally available. I can explain the terms; accepting a goodwill option does not waive your rights.”

Give the complete dining restrictions and confirmed checkout time in the accompanying explanation, with an accessible way to ask questions or select cash. The short offer is not the entire policy. Resolve the underlying service failure and explain prevention; respectful treatment supports the remedy but cannot substitute for an obligation.

Instructor note

Have students keep three columns mentally separate: operator cost, stated instrument and guest usefulness. Require both the HK$1,200 budget and the non-equivalence of cash, face value and time. Accept Cash as a defensible simpler policy only if its limitations are acknowledged, rather than assuming identical money guarantees identical satisfaction.

HSP-02 restores one traveller’s disrupted trip; GVR-16 tests consistent grounds for a waiver. Here the task is a general rights-preserving menu before individual requests. Equal goodwill eligibility does not establish equal total harm, and later material-loss claims need separate assessment. The case is not a compensation calculator, a legal ruling or a reason to withhold a remedy until a guest complains loudly.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All hotel, failure, guests, costs and capacities are invented. Verified one-hour non-safety lobby audio-guide failure is fixed; twelve guests in one goodwill category. All owed contractual/statutory remedies separately assessed and funded, never capped by HK$1,200 goodwill budget. Every instrument complete incremental operator cost HK$100; dining face value HK$200 is not cost or guest utility. Capacity covers all twelve choosing any one option without displaced guest rights or unpaid labour. Choice retains equally accessible HK$100 cash, clear restrictions, no release/review/loyalty condition. Four each selections cost HK$1,200, not HK$1,600. Unused non-cash selection may switch to cash if unusable, no double redemption. No loyalty, causal satisfaction or representative preference estimate.
