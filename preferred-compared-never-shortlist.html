MKT-24 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Consideration sets · Purchase-occasion recall · Conditional preference

Preferred when compared. Never on the shortlist?

Being liked is not the same as being considered

Consumer-choice research distinguishes the alternatives people seriously consider from the option they prefer within that set. Brand tracking also separates having heard of a brand, considering it and intending to buy it. Those measures answer different questions; none is automatically a completed purchase.

Our specialist umbrella retailer and all results below are fictional. The case compares two communication priorities, not two ways to pressure customers into buying.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong specialist retailer sells an own-label folding umbrella suitable for everyday commuting. In a paid prototype comparison, 40 adult volunteers were shown this model and two alternatives, with all names, accurate prices and features visible. Thirty chose the retailer’s model as their favourite. No one purchased, and participants were recruited for convenience, not as a representative sample of all commuters. Being shown the model was part of the task.

The retailer has HK$12,000 for one complete four-week communication pilot. Features emphasises verified folded-size and opening-mechanism comparisons. Occasion connects the model and shop identity with packing a bag for a rainy commute, showing the same truthful product and price. Both include production, equal planned placements to the same broad adult commuter audience and a small voluntary evaluation. Neither supplier promises extra purchases or a recall increase. There is no funded combined package this round.

Stock, prices, access, delivery and existing product information stay unchanged. Both pilots keep the shop identity visible and all comparison claims substantiated; neither invents weather urgency or collects sensitive profiles. The objective is more appropriate, wanted purchases over time. Today’s choice is which communication problem to investigate first, not a forecast that 75% of exposed people will buy. The retailer has not yet checked how recent category shoppers formed their own shortlists.

Round 2 · More information

A separate diagnostic interview recruits 100 adult volunteers who recently bought a folding umbrella for commuting. They are not the 40 comparison participants. Before showing any brand or model names, the interviewer asks about that purchase occasion, the models that came to mind and those seriously considered. Only afterwards are names shown to clarify whether respondents already knew this retailer’s model before the trip. The following mutually exclusive categories summarise their accounts; each person appears once.

Reported past shopping experiencePeople
Had not heard of the model before that trip20
Knew it, but it did not come to mind for that trip60
Considered it and bought it8
Considered it but chose another12

The 12 who considered but did not buy report that another model suited their preferred folded size or opening feel better. The 60 who knew but did not think of it report no comparison with it on that occasion. Their later recognition does not establish that they would have preferred or bought it if reminded. These are retrospective self-reports from a convenience sample, not verified thought histories, population shares or an experiment testing either pilot.

Both pilot offers remain feasible at HK$12,000. The diagnostic does not supply their causal effects or relative profit. Use it to choose a priority and a falsifiable evaluation, not to promise to recover all 60 shoppers. Features may help explain relevant differences but need not reverse genuine preferences; Occasion may help retrieval but cannot guarantee consideration or purchase. All audience, privacy and truthful-message safeguards remain.

Your decision

After the interviews, which message would you prioritise? Separate people who knew but did not consider the model from those who considered and rejected it, and specify a measure that tests your diagnosis without forecasting sales from these counts.

Keep the denominator attached

Thirty of 40 is 75% in a task where everyone was shown all three models. It describes preference conditional on that prompted comparison, not spontaneous recall, market share or purchase conversion. The separate interview sample cannot be joined to these 40 to construct a sales funnel or multiplied by 75% to predict additional buyers.

Read the diagnostic categories

In the interviews, 80 of 100 say they already knew the model: 60 did not think of it for the trip and 20 considered it. Those 20 comprise eight reported buyers and 12 who chose another. Twenty others report prior unfamiliarity. Do not add the 80 aware and 20 considering as different people. The observed 8/20 = 40% among reported considerers is not a causal conversion rate or a stable forecast.

Match the message to the missing step

Occasion is a defensible first priority because the largest reported group knew the model without retrieving it for the relevant trip. A clear connection between the model, shop identity and that use occasion addresses this hypothesis. Features addresses understanding and relative appeal during comparison; it may be sensible if that is the chosen priority, but it does not by itself demonstrate that absent options will enter shortlists.

Neither result means people forgot because the brand was bad or that all non-buyers should be persuaded. Some genuinely prefer another umbrella. The 20 unfamiliar respondents may need basic awareness; the 60 are not simply unaware. A useful strategy respects those different states without treating respondents as permanent customer types.

Measure without supplying the answer

For an occasion-focused evaluation, ask a fresh consenting sample the same unprompted purchase-occasion question before displaying model names. Record spontaneous model recall and serious consideration separately, then aided recognition. For Features, assess accurate understanding of the verified differences and comparative preference without calling either a sale. Keep audience recruitment, questions and order consistent across comparisons; prompts can change what people mention.

Test the hypothesis, not just the headline

Within the funded pilot, use its voluntary evaluation to check the nominated intermediate outcome and record limitations. A comparison group and suitable assignment would strengthen causal interpretation if feasible within the package; a before-and-after improvement alone cannot isolate the message from other changes. Track actual wanted purchases and costs separately before claiming commercial return. Ask whether any effect persists beyond a prompted session. No new tracking, compulsory survey or free extra experiment is assumed.

Instructor note

Start with the 30-of-40 result and ask what the task supplied that ordinary shopping may not. Reveal the four non-overlapping interview groups; have pairs reconcile 80 aware, 20 considering and 8 buyers within that sample. An occasion-first choice is well supported diagnostically, not proven financially superior. Accept Features only with an explicit priority and acknowledgment of the larger retrieval question. Do not infer that reminding 60 people yields45 sales, that recognition equals consideration or that every rejection is an information failure. This differs from partner-brand attribution, catalogue navigation and checkout friction.
