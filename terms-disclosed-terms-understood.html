GVR-33 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Contract comprehension · Meaningful disclosure · Consistent representations

The terms are disclosed. Are they understood?

Disclosure is not the same as understanding

Clear contractual wording and actual customer understanding are different tests. Plain-language and disclosure research supports testing what people can explain, not merely whether they clicked or signed. A summary can also divert attention from omitted terms. The service, contracts and test results below are fictional, not legal advice.

Your classroom decision

You manage fictional Hong Kong home-cleaning provider Clear Harbour. Before launching a new single-visit offer, choose A: one dense contract, or B: a short question-and-answer summary followed by the full contract. Neither has been offered or signed. The goal is informed agreement and a service the company can actually deliver.

For this exercise, qualified advisers have reviewed both complete packages and confirmed identical substantive rights and obligations. B accurately highlights key terms and points to the complete terms, available before agreement and as a retained copy. This stipulated review is not proof that customers understand either format or that sales representations are accurate.

The offer costs HK$800, paid on booking, for one scheduled visit. Under the supplied cancellation term, cancellation received at least 48 hours before the scheduled start receives an HK$800 refund. Later cancellation receives HK$600 after a fixed HK$200 cancellation charge. These are fictional offer terms, not a statutory cooling-off rule.

Receipt occurs when the cancellation reaches the designated online form or email address, both available continuously. The system records a timestamp. Staff acknowledgement comes within two working days and does not determine when cancellation takes effect. Hong Kong local time applies. No renewal, extra cancellation fee or changed statutory right is proposed.

A contains the same receipt rule within its full text. B foregrounds the question: Does cancellation count when my message arrives or when staff reply? Its answer states the receipt rule and links directly to the full clause. Both provide readable print, customer-selected Chinese or English, accessible assistance and time for questions.

Both format packages and their maintenance fit the same approved budget. Sales scripts and operational procedures still require a joint launch check. Management favours A because every term appears in one place; the service lead favours B because important consequences are easier to locate. Neither team has yet seen the comprehension results.

Round 2 · More information

A small pre-launch diagnostic uses two separately recruited groups of 20 consenting adult volunteers from the intended audience. One sees A and one B in their preferred supported language, with equal reading time and the same scenario question. The groups were not randomly assigned; these are descriptive checks, not a causal trial.

The question concerns a Friday 14:00 visit and a cancellation received on Wednesday at 13:59, with acknowledgement on Thursday. Eight of 20 A readers and 16 of 20 B readers correctly identify the full HK$800 refund. Every incorrect response treats staff acknowledgement, rather than receipt, as the relevant trigger.

Four B readers still misunderstand. The diagnostic tests only this scenario, not understanding of every term, actual cancellation behaviour or agreement quality. No volunteer signed, paid or received a real service. The test summaries contain no identities or private personal details.

The joint check then finds a proposed sales script promising that staff confirm every cancellation within one hour, at any time. Operations has no overnight or weekend response team and cannot support that promise. Both draft contracts instead allow two working days for acknowledgement; their continuous receipt systems are operational.

The one-hour statement has not been used with customers. Managers can correct the script and rehearse the explanation within the existing launch budget. B makes the receipt-versus-reply distinction easier to discuss, but changing layout alone cannot make an unsupported response promise true. Launch can wait for this correction without breaching existing commitments.

Your decision

What must change before launch, and which format would you take into a further check? Apply the receipt rule to the Wednesday example, separate cancellation effectiveness from acknowledgement, and name a comprehension check that the existing test missed.

Debrief · Read the clock, not the reply

The 48-hour boundary for Friday 14:00 is Wednesday 14:00. Receipt at Wednesday 13:59 is 48 hours and one minute ahead, so the supplied term gives the full HK$800 refund. Thursday acknowledgement does not turn that timely cancellation into a late one.

A useful contrasting question uses receipt at Wednesday 14:01: 47 hours and 59 minutes before the visit, so this fictional term gives HK$600 after the HK$200 charge. Exactly Wednesday 14:00 qualifies for the full refund because the rule says at least 48 hours. Apply only the supplied terms, not a presumed general law.

Debrief · Improve understanding without overstating the test

The correct-response shares are 8/20 = 40% for A and 16/20 = 80% for B, a descriptive 40-percentage-point difference. B is a sensible candidate for further checking, not a proven universal winner. Separate recruitment, small groups and one question limit what this comparison establishes.

A legally reviewed text can still be misunderstood. Equally, a signature, reading-time score or positive design rating does not demonstrate comprehension. Ask customers to apply the rule in their own words to a new example; do not merely ask them to repeat a sentence or tick that they understand.

Debrief · Correct the representation

Do not launch either package with the unsupported one-hour promise. Correct the unused script before customer contact and align staff explanations with the reviewed terms: receipt fixes the cancellation time; staff acknowledgement follows within two working days. Do not hide the contradiction in smaller print or call the summary the problem.

Keep the continuous form and email receipt mechanism working, with reliable timestamps and a clear help route for delivery failures. Test the funded acknowledgement process before launch. If the business wants faster replies, first fund and verify that capability and obtain review of the revised offer; do not announce an unfunded service improvement.

Debrief · Preserve the full agreement

Prefer B for the next check while retaining easy access to all terms and a durable copy. Highlight the trigger, timing, charges and contact channels without deleting qualifications or substantive protections. Let customers ask questions without sales pressure. Any later change to rights needs its own proper review; this exercise does not authorise one.

Test comprehension of important terms outside the summary too, such as the included cleaning scope and appointment access arrangements in the full draft. Those details are not supplied here for calculation; the manager must include them in the next test. A summary is a navigation aid, not evidence that untested provisions are understood.

Debrief · A launch gate, not a signature target

Assign a joint owner for the contract, summary, sales explanation and operating process. Before launch, verify their consistency and rehearse receipt and reply scenarios, including the exact deadline. Retest the misunderstanding with fresh volunteers and check accessible formats. Record unresolved issues rather than treating a higher signing rate as success.

If customers still confuse receipt with acknowledgement, revise the explanation and test again. Do not deny access to the service merely for failing a quiz or use the test to waive rights. For any real contractual problem, obtain advice on the actual documents, representations and applicable law.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: choose a format and write a comprehension question. Minutes 4–8: calculate the refund and compare the diagnostic shares. Minutes 8–13: repair the script and draw separate receipt and acknowledgement timelines. Minutes 13–18: propose a broader check without changing customer rights or declaring B legally safer.

GVR-02 communicates an urgent changing safety warning; GVR-11 resolves authority and versions after a change request. Here there is no emergency, competing contract version or signed agreement to amend. The task is comprehension and truthful service representation before agreement formation, with legal review and operational review serving different purposes.

  2. OFCA · Enhanced Clarity in Contracts and Transparency and User-friendliness of Contract Documents. Primary explanation of a voluntary telecommunications industry code stresses accessible language and prominent termination information. Its sector-specific provisions are not asserted to govern this cleaning service.
  3. Martínez, Mollica, Liu, Podrug and Gibson · What did I sign? · Cognitive Science Society proceedings · 2021. Original English-language experiment compares comprehension and recall across legal and simpler registers. Participant screening and the specific excerpts limit transfer; no source effect size or universal comprehension guarantee is used.
  4. Behavioural Insights Team for the UK government · Improving consumer understanding of contractual terms and privacy policies · 2019. Original online testing supports measuring understanding directly and reports that summary formats can help selected points while reducing attention elsewhere. The case’s small non-random diagnostic is not presented as equivalent evidence.
  5. UK CAP Code · Section 3: Misleading advertising · substantiation and qualification. Primary comparative advertising guidance distinguishes supported claims from misleading qualifications and exaggerated performance. Used as a representation-design principle, not a ruling or statement that UK advertising rules govern Hong Kong contracts.
