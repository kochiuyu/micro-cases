FIN-32 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Financial leverage · Equity returns · Financing risk

More return on equity. More risk to equity?

The financing question

Finance education separates the return generated by a business from the return left for its owners after financing. Borrowing changes the claims on the same operating result; it does not itself improve that result.

Harbour Display Hire and all amounts below are fictional. This exercise compares project equity outcomes, not real loan offers or investment recommendations.

Your classroom decision

You advise fictional Hong Kong business Harbour Display Hire. A one-year display-equipment project needs HK$1,000,000 today. The owner can fund it entirely with equity (E), or contribute HK$400,000 and borrow HK$600,000 (D). The equipment, customers, costs and operating outcomes are identical under both plans.

The loan has a fixed annual rate of 5%. All HK$600,000 principal plus one year’s interest are due at year-end. There are no earlier debt payments. Both plans are available before commitment; neither has been accepted. No taxes, fees, personal guarantees, early calls, other liabilities or additional contributions apply.

At year-end, the project ends and its equipment is sold. Assume net asset recovery of exactly HK$1,000,000 in each supplied scenario, available before debt repayment. This is a classroom assumption, not a real guarantee. Operating profit is additional cash after every operating cost; it excludes asset recovery and financing payments.

The opening operating scenario produces HK$120,000 profit. Compare one-year return on the owner’s initial project equity: (cash returned to owner minus initial equity) ÷ initial equity. All residual cash is distributed after paying debt. Do not subtract principal again when calculating profit.

The owner has enough capital for either plan. Under D, the HK$600,000 not invested remains outside this project; its use and return are unspecified. No scenario probabilities or risk preference are supplied. You cannot infer a whole-wealth winner or an expected return from the project percentages alone.

Round 2 · More information

Before choosing, assess a downturn scenario: operating profit is only HK$20,000, still positive before financing. All other assumptions remain unchanged, including HK$1,000,000 net asset recovery before year-end debt payment and the fixed 5% borrowing rate.

This is an alternative outcome for the same year, not a second year or an event that has already occurred. Both funding choices remain available. No probabilities are added.

Your decision

Under the downturn, calculate both equity returns and verify that D repays its debt. Does positive operating profit protect the owner from an equity loss? Explain how the comparison affects your recommendation.

Debrief · The opening percentage

E returns HK$1,120,000 to its owner: HK$1,000,000 asset recovery plus HK$120,000 operating profit. The equity gain is HK$120,000, giving 12%. D owes HK$600,000 × 5% = HK$30,000 interest, plus HK$600,000 principal.

Debrief · A smaller equity base

D returns HK$1,120,000 − HK$630,000 = HK$490,000. Against HK$400,000 initial equity, its gain is HK$90,000 and its return is 22.5%. The higher percentage accompanies a smaller project-equity investment and a smaller absolute equity gain. It does not establish greater total owner wealth.

Debrief · Positive operations, negative equity return

In the downturn, total project cash is HK$1,020,000. E returns that amount: HK$20,000 gain ÷ HK$1,000,000 = 2%. D pays HK$630,000 and returns HK$390,000. Its HK$10,000 equity loss divided by HK$400,000 is −2.5%. The operating profit is positive but below the fixed interest charge.

Debrief · Loss is not default

D still repays principal and interest in full: HK$1,020,000 exceeds HK$630,000. The owner’s loss is a reduction in returned equity, not an unpaid lender balance or an extra cash call. Do not invent refinancing or count the recovered capital twice. Full asset recovery is stipulated, not generally safe to assume.

Debrief · What leverage changes

Between these two scenarios, operating profit falls HK$100,000 under either plan. Equity gain also falls HK$100,000 under either plan. But the equity-return decline is 10 percentage points for E and 25 for D. The same operating change is larger relative to D’s smaller equity base. This is financial leverage, not changing operational performance.

Debrief · Conditional choice

D offers the higher project-equity return in the opening scenario but a loss in the downturn; E remains positive in both. A recommendation needs the owner’s tolerance for loss, credible outcome probabilities and the alternative use of outside capital. These two illustrations establish sensitivity, not expected returns, optimal borrowing or every possible downside.

Instructor note

Allow four minutes for opening cash reconciliation, four for equity returns, four for the downturn and five for the recommendation. Require students to distinguish cash returned from profit. Ask whether a 22.5% project return answers a whole-wealth question, and why a fixed borrowing rate does not fix the equity return.
