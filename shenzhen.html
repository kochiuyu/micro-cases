IS-01 · Shenzhen · Housing and household wealth · 10–15 minutes

Would more stock listings bring Shenzhen homebuyers back?

You edit a Shenzhen business newsletter. A reporter proposes the headline “More IPOs will bring homebuyers back.” You have one exhibit before deadline.

Your editorial decision

Publish the headline, qualify it, or hold it? Cite one observation and name one missing link.

Round 2 · Follow the proceeds

Suppose new listings rebound next year. Most shares sold belong to investors outside Shenzhen. Local households’ shareholdings, pay and mortgage terms stay the same.

Revise your forecast

Which Shenzhen buyers now have more money for a down payment? What single change to the scenario would make a housing rebound more plausible?

Debrief · Trace the wealth effect

A listing can turn a founder’s illiquid stake into spendable funds when shares can be sold. For that to lift Shenzhen housing demand, the proceeds must reach people who want to buy homes there. A household whose existing shares rise in value can also increase spending at the same salary. That is a wealth effect. A pay rise provides a separate income channel.

Weak business prospects could lead firms to postpone listings and households to postpone purchases. Both measures fell over this interval. These two observations cannot tell us which changed first or whether one caused the other. Ask for evidence on where selling shareholders live and whether their home purchases changed relative to comparable households.

Instructor note · Running the discussion

0–4 minutes. Take an editorial vote. Ask a student to distinguish a flow of new listings from the stock of listed firms.

4–8 minutes. Reveal who owns the shares. Ask two students with different votes to identify the household in their explanation.

8–12 minutes. Compare proposed headlines. A defensible version is “New listings slowed as resale housing weakened. Who received the proceeds?”
