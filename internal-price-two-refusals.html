An internal price. Two sensible refusals?

Integrated production still needs coordination

In its results announcement of 14 May 2025, Hong Kong-listed VTech described vertical integration and its global manufacturing footprint. That company statement provides manufacturing context, not evidence about any internal pricing dispute. Our two-unit manufacturer and every price, capacity and order below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

A fictional manufacturer has a component unit and an assembly unit inside one company. Managers are evaluated on their own unit contribution and may negotiate internal transfers or use qualified outside alternatives. The component unit can make 1,000 units this month; 600 are already committed and must be honoured. Assembly needs 300 additional components. The initially available spare capacity is therefore 400 units, with no other productive use or external sales opportunity currently identified.

An internally supplied component costs the company HK$40 in additional production resources. A qualified external supplier offers assembly any required quantity up to 300 at HK$70 per component, delivered and inspected. Both sources meet identical specifications and timing. Assembly’s downstream receipts less its own additional assembly costs exceed HK$70 per component, so the outside purchase price is its relevant upper limit. All 300 are needed for the same downstream output under either source.

Fixed costs, staffing, safety, quality checks and existing commitments remain unchanged. There are no extra internal transport costs, batch minimums, transaction fees or taxes in this classroom comparison. Units can be sourced separately, not only as an all-or-nothing package. At a boundary price a manager is indifferent; a price strictly inside a feasible range gives both an incremental gain. These are management-accounting charges within one company, not tax transfer-pricing advice. No transfer or new external sale has yet been agreed.

Your decision

What internal price range would make both unit managers willing to consider the 300-component transfer? Recommend a price within that range and explain how the group benefits compared with buying all 300 outside.

Round 2 · More information

Before either unit commits, a new external customer offers to buy up to 200 components this month at HK$80 each. Each external unit uses the same capacity and HK$40 production cost as an internal unit, plus HK$5 external-only packing and delivery. The offer is firm for any quantity up to 200, requires no minimum and expires at the decision deadline. There is no contract breach if fewer units are accepted. Existing 600-unit commitments remain protected.

Accepting all 200 external units leaves 200 units of spare capacity for assembly. Assembly still needs 300 and can buy the remainder outside at HK$70 each. No overtime, expansion or later delivery is available this month. External net contribution is HK$80 − HK$40 − HK$5 = HK$35 per unit. Internal transfers beyond the remaining 200 would therefore displace that contribution. Managers may negotiate prices by tranche; do not require a single averaged price across all 300.

Your decision

With the new external opportunity, how many components should transfer internally and at what acceptable prices? Separate spare-capacity units from units that displace external sales, and reconcile the managers’ choices with the group’s interest.

Start with the outside options

Initially, the supplier’s relevant minimum is HK$40 because all 300 internal units fit unused capacity. Assembly will not pay more than HK$70 when an equivalent outside supply is available. The weakly acceptable range is HK$40–70; strictly between those limits both improve. At HK$55, each unit manager gains HK$15 per internal unit relative to its stated alternative. The group saves 300 × (70 − 40) = HK$9,000, regardless of how that saving is divided.

Price the constrained units differently

After accepting the external opportunity, the first 200 internal units still have a HK$40–70 range. For the next 100, the component unit needs HK$40 + HK$35 foregone contribution, giving HK$75 to be indifferent, while assembly’s maximum remains HK$70. There is no mutually beneficial per-unit range for that constrained tranche. At HK$70 the component manager loses HK$5 against the external alternative; at HK$75 assembly pays HK$5 more than outside.

Reconcile the company result

Use 600 units for existing commitments, 200 for the new external customer and 200 for assembly: total capacity 1,000. Buy assembly’s remaining 100 outside. Relative to accepting the external opportunity and buying all 300 assembly components outside, internal sourcing of 200 saves HK$6,000. Forcing all 300 inside would sacrifice 100 × HK$35 = HK$3,500 of external contribution while saving HK$9,000 on purchases: HK$5,500 net, which is HK$500 worse than the split. A blended price might make the whole package acceptable but cannot remove that lost group value.
