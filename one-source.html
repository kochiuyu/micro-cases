IS-04 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Information systems · Authentication · Independent verification

Two approvals. One source.

In the news · A convincing message is not authority

In a 26 June 2024 Legislative Council reply, the Hong Kong Government described a fraud reported that January involving a fabricated, prerecorded video conference and a loss of about HK$200 million. The reply explicitly said the meeting involved no interaction.

The lesson is not to judge a message solely by familiar-looking people or details. The fictional workshop below faces an ordinary bank-detail change, not a confirmed deepfake or a known fraud.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You manage a Hong Kong exhibition-display business. An established print supplier has completed a genuine job. The approved invoice is HK$80,000, and the goods are ready for collection today.

An email in a familiar project thread asks you to pay into a different bank account. It says payment must arrive before today’s collection. Missing collection could disrupt installation, but the cost and likelihood of delay are not yet known.

Your operations manager and accounts colleague have both approved the paperwork. They checked the goods, invoice amount and approved order, and recognised the project details. The payment has not been sent and the saved supplier bank record has not been changed.

Check completedWhat it supports
Goods match the orderThe underlying purchase is genuine
Invoice matches HK$80,000 approved amountThe amount is consistent with that purchase
Two colleagues approve the paperworkTwo people reviewed it; how they authenticated the change is not yet clear
Your decision

Would you approve the new payment destination on the strength of these two checks, or pause for another check? What exactly still needs to be established?

Round 2 · One fact changes

Both colleagues relied on the same incoming email and its attached “confirmation”. Neither contacted the supplier through the previously verified number in your company’s supplier records.

The email supplies a replacement telephone number and asks you not to call the old number. You have not independently established who authorised either change.

There is no evidence yet proving fraud. Equally, there is no independent confirmation that the supplier authorised the new destination. The goods and debt remain genuine. No payment has been made.

Your decision

What would you do before authorising the bank-detail change, and how would you handle the collection deadline if confirmation is unavailable?

Separate the two questions

Do we owe the supplier? The order, goods and invoice support that conclusion.

Has an authorised supplier representative requested this payment destination? The current checks do not establish that. Repeating the same source does not remove its weakness.

Two-person approval can still be useful. It is not a substitute for independently authenticating a changed instruction.

Verify through an established route

Hold the bank-detail change while contacting an authorised supplier representative through contact information previously verified and held separately from this message. Confirm the requested destination and authority, and record the confirmation before applying the normal approval process.

Do not use only the new number supplied by the unverified request to authenticate that same request. A familiar thread, correct spelling or realistic voice is not conclusive evidence on its own.

If the established contact cannot be reached, escalate to the responsible manager and use an independently established alternative contact route. An unanswered call does not prove fraud.

Manage delay without bypassing the check

Tell the installation team that verification may affect collection and explore a confirmed collection extension with the supplier through the established channel. The case provides no quantified delay cost or fraud probability, so do not invent an expected-loss calculation.

Do not automatically pay the old account either: its continued suitability has not been confirmed. A genuine bank change can proceed after reliable verification and normal approval; the rule is not to reject every change forever.

Key idea: two reviewers are not two independent sources. Verify authority and destination, not just the legitimacy of the purchase.

Instructor note

Allow 2 minutes for the first vote, 3 for the source reveal and 5–7 for designing a practical verification step. Ask students to name the evidence each check establishes. Keep a genuine debt separate from an unverified payment instruction. Do not ask students to detect a deepfake from visual artefacts, accuse a colleague or supplier, or simulate a real transfer. The intended response is controlled verification, not a claim that fraud is certain.
