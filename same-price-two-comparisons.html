MKT-14 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Reference-price framing · Value positioning · Customer expectations

The same price. Two comparisons.

Industry context

On 14 September 2026, Hong Kong’s Consumer Council reported that promotional formats and pack sizes do not always reveal the best value, and encouraged checking historical and unit prices.

Our fictional homewares shop starts with substantiated prices and clearly disclosed differences. The question is which truthful comparison best supports its positioning, not whether an invented discount can be justified. The Council’s supermarket findings are not evidence about these kettles or their buyers.

Your classroom decision

A fictional Hong Kong homewares shop sells its Everyday kettle for HK$300 during a planned promotion. The same kettle, checkout price, stock, service, return terms and one-year commercial warranty apply whichever message it chooses. Both advertising formats cost the same. The shop wants to be known for dependable everyday essentials at a sensible price, not luxury imitation.

A leads with “Everyday kettle: now HK$300, our usual selling price HK$400. Save HK$100.” The shop has complete transaction records substantiating that HK$400 is its genuine usual price for this exact model over a reasonable period, not an inflated one-day reference or a manufacturer’s suggested price. The promotion’s dates and terms are clear.

B leads with “Choose the features you need: Everyday HK$300; Premium HK$500.” Premium is a different, genuinely available kettle sold by the same shop at its documented current price. Both serve the basic purpose of boiling water, but they are not identical products. The adjacent, equally prominent comparison shows the features and warranty below. Both complete layouts have passed the supplied truthfulness checks; neither claims equal overall quality, equal lifespan or a discount from Premium’s price. This is a classroom assumption, not a legal safe harbour for real advertisements.

Both models meet applicable safety requirements. Each has 1.5-litre capacity and automatic shut-off. Everyday boils water only and has a one-year commercial warranty. Premium also has selectable temperatures and keep-warm, with a three-year commercial warranty. These warranty periods do not remove statutory rights and are not measured product lifespans. No durability comparison is available. No sales or preference test has yet established which message works better.

Your decision

Would you lead with A’s genuine saving against the usual price or B’s clearly bounded comparison with the premium model? Explain what value you want the target customer to notice while keeping the product and HK$300 price unchanged.

Round 2 · More information

The shop conducts four exploratory interviews with convenience-recruited adult shoppers. Each sees both complete layouts and the product information; the order is not controlled. Two sample comments about A are: “The HK$100 saving gets my attention,” and “I would want to know whether that price will come round again.” About B, one says: “I only boil water, so I may not need the extra settings.” Another says: “Seeing them together makes me wonder whether the cheaper one will last as long.” These are individual reactions, not representative segment sizes, purchase decisions or a causal test of the layouts.

The product team confirms the stated capacity, shut-off, functions and warranty differences. It has no comparative failure-rate or service-life study and cannot answer the last shopper’s lifespan question from matching capacity or a price gap. Neither longer life for Premium nor equal life for Everyday has been established. The shop can improve prominence or wording of the already disclosed differences, but may not invent a quality equivalence or hide Premium’s extra features. Both original price bases remain substantiated; no deceptive former price or broken safety requirement has been discovered.

Your decision

Keep or change your preferred comparison? Propose one truthful headline or clarification, and explain which customer need it addresses without treating the interviews as proof of higher sales or equal durability.

Name the reference before calculating

A compares the same model with its own genuine usual selling price: HK$400 − HK$300 = HK$100, or 25% of HK$400. B compares two different models: Premium costs HK$200 more at their current prices. That gap is not a HK$200 discount on Everyday, whose own usual price is HK$400. A larger numerical comparison does not make the saving claim interchangeable.

A deal and a product trade-off

A makes the temporary bargain salient for shoppers who already know they want an ordinary kettle. It may also make the usual or future deal price more prominent in their thinking. B encourages a customer to compare the features they need against a more expensive option. Shared basic functions can support that bounded comparison; they cannot establish equal durability or overall quality. These are possible interpretations, not measured effects for the shop.

Neither frame replaces the evidence

An A revision could be: “Boil-only Everyday kettle, HK$300: HK$100 below our genuine usual HK$400 price.” Retain the clear promotion terms and product details. A B revision could be: “Boil only or choose temperature settings? Everyday HK$300; Premium HK$500.” Keep the one-year versus three-year commercial warranties and other differences prominent. If asked about lifespan, say that comparative lifespan evidence is unavailable. Do not use “same quality for less”.

Choose the audience, then check understanding

Prefer A if the priority is an understandable genuine offer for customers already seeking basic functionality. Prefer B if the priority is helping buyers decide whether the extra settings and warranty suit their needs. Neither is shown to maximise sales. In a subsequent test, ask what customers think is being compared, what differences they recall and what durability claims they infer. If measuring purchases, use a credible controlled design and the same product/price, and predefine the result of interest. Four interviews seeing both layouts do not identify a conversion uplift.

Instructor note

Ask students to separate a former-price saving, a cross-model price gap and a perceived quality inference. Keep truthfulness fixed in the opening so the discussion is about positioning rather than correcting an obviously false discount. Interviews reveal questions worth answering, not proof that the layout makes a legally misleading overall impression or that most shoppers share those beliefs. In practice the overall presentation and local requirements need review before publication. The supported decision is a transparent positioning rationale with a bounded next check; do not manufacture a sales winner, a lifespan guarantee, a legal opinion or a claim that premium prices always mean better quality.
