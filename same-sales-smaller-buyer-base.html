MKT-31 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Buyer penetration · Purchase frequency · Sales decomposition

The same sales. A smaller buyer base?

Sales totals do not count distinct buyers

In a 12 May 2026 article, Worldpanel by Numerator described its China consumer-goods research as finding that buyer growth accompanied growth for most growing brands. That is a reported market pattern, not proof that any particular acquisition campaign will work.

Our pantry brand and all figures below are fictional. The case asks what a sales total conceals before anyone promises a marketing result.

Your classroom decision

You manage fictional Hong Kong pantry brand Bay Jar. Its marketing brief must identify the next diagnostic priority: R, understand why category-buying households do not buy Bay Jar; or F, understand how to create another useful purchase occasion for current Bay Jar buyers. This is a research priority, not approval of a campaign budget.

The dashboard shows the same sales in two comparable 12-week periods: the same calendar weeks one year apart. The monitored universe is the same 10,000 households, with complete purchase records and no panel changes. It is a fictional observation universe, not an estimate for all Hong Kong.

Management argues that stable sales mean buyer reach is stable, so F is the obvious priority. You must decide whether the aggregate dashboard supports that claim or whether a buyer breakdown is needed first.

Debrief · One total, different components

Require the buyer breakdown in round one. The dashboard establishes stable recorded volume and value, not stable distinct buyer reach. Several combinations of buyers, occasions and quantities can produce the same total.

Earlier volume is 2,000 × 4 × 1 = 8,000 packs. Later volume is 1,600 × 5 × 1 = 8,000 packs. The buyer count falls 20% while frequency rises 25%: 0.80 × 1.25 = 1.00. These percentage changes cannot be added. Pack quantity per purchase and price are unchanged.

Debrief · Name the denominator

Brand penetration among all monitored households falls from 2,000 ÷ 10,000 = 20% to 1,600 ÷ 10,000 = 16%: a fall of four percentage points, or 20% relative to the original level.

Among category buyers, the brand’s buyer reach falls from 2,000 ÷ 8,000 = 25% to 1,600 ÷ 8,000 = 20%. That is five percentage points. Neither percentage is a volume market share: the case does not provide total category packs.

Debrief · Diagnose before promising growth

R is the better-supported diagnostic priority because the records expose narrower buyer reach while average purchase frequency has already increased. Ask which category buyers do not buy Bay Jar and why: availability, relevance, recognition and preferences are competing explanations, not established findings.

F remains a possible growth route, but these totals do not establish that more frequency is achievable, cheaper or more profitable than wider reach. Nor do they prove an acquisition campaign will succeed. A narrower buyer base is a diagnostic signal, not by itself a complete marketing investment case.

There are 400 fewer buyers in net terms. We cannot say exactly 400 households left: entrants and departures may offset. Nor can we say each continuing buyer increased purchases. Group averages can change because the buyer mix changes. This is not a retention-rate or lifetime-value calculation.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–4: show only the aggregate table and take the first vote. Ask whether the same total can come from fewer people. Minutes 4–9: reveal the buyer table and have pairs reconstruct both totals. Minutes 9–13: calculate the two reach measures and vote on the diagnostic priority. Minutes 13–18: separate evidence about the pattern from evidence about a remedy.

If students favour R, ask what the exhibit actually says about non-buyers’ reasons or campaign response: nothing. If they favour F, ask why more purchases per buyer should outrank the newly observed reach problem without cost or response evidence. Accept a carefully argued alternative priority, but not a claim that unchanged sales prove unchanged reach.

Do not call 25% minus 20% a five-percent fall; it is five percentage points. Do not label group frequency as individual loyalty or equate purchase with consumption. MKT-13 concerns cohort value; MKT-09 concerns advertising audiences. Here the unit is a unique household that bought the brand during a defined period.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

All brand, household, volume, price and frequency figures are original fictional teaching data. The same 10,000-household universe is observed completely over two matched 12-week periods with no weighting, attrition, coverage or pack-size change. Brand buyers are subsets of category buyers within each period, but cross-period buyer overlap is unknown. Aggregate purchases, not consumption, are measured. Brand penetration denominator is all monitored households; category-buyer reach is reported separately. Campaign cost, profit margin and causal response are not supplied. Source research does not establish those missing effects for Bay Jar. The decision concerns diagnostic focus, not a proven profit-maximising campaign.
