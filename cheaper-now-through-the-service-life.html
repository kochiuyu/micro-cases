SUS-25 · Hong Kong · fictional community centre · 15–20 minutes · Life-cycle costing · Public procurement · Comparable service

The cheapest green purchase. Over which lifetime?

A price tag is not a service-life comparison

European Commission and German Environment Agency guidance describe life-cycle costing as a comparison extending beyond acquisition to operation and end-of-life costs or receipts. NIST’s 2025 manual explains why alternatives need a common study period and consistent treatment of replacements and residual value.

UNEP’s sustainable procurement guidance considers environmental, social and economic value. Its lighting guidance connects performance, service life and maintenance planning. None of these sources establishes which fictional offer below is greener, supplies its prices or prescribes the discount rate used here.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. You advise a publicly funded community centre choosing lighting for a confirmed eight-year service period. Two packages deliver the same required illumination, operating hours, colour quality and safety throughout their stated service lives. Both meet all mandatory procurement and environmental requirements. Replacement can maintain equivalent service without disruption. The facility closes after year eight, so no further lighting service is needed.

Before quotations were requested, the centre specified an eight-year financial life-cycle comparison among compliant offers. It uses a 4% real discount rate for this exercise, not an official Hong Kong rate. All amounts are constant today’s HK dollars, including tax and installation where applicable. Assume no real price escalation. Initial spending occurs now; operating payments occur at each year-end. Exclude financing charges and unpriced environmental effects; do not add them silently to the financial ranking.

The initial budget is HK$170,000, enough for either package. Future operating and replacement funding is available if properly planned. Your colleague has entered purchase, electricity and planned maintenance but has not checked the service-life and recovery schedules. The table is therefore an incomplete working comparison, not an award recommendation. All figures are invented.

Round 2 · More information

The technical review confirms that A lasts four years at the required service level. It needs one complete replacement at the end of year four for HK$110,000, including removal, installation and net disposal costs. The replacement lasts another four years and has the same annual energy and maintenance costs. Do not add another replacement at year eight, when the service requirement ends. B lasts all eight years without replacement. Planned annual maintenance excludes the stated replacement, so do not count it twice.

At the end of year eight A’s gross scrap proceeds exactly cover its removal and recovery costs: its net terminal value is zero. A brochure’s HK$20,000 scrap figure is gross, not an extra net receipt. B has a documented net recovery offer of HK$10,000 after removal and recovery expenses, conditional on the specified maintenance and complete return. For the base calculation assume those conditions are met and the buyer pays. Then test the result if B’s receipt is zero. Recovery of materials does not mean the equipment can deliver further compliant lighting service.

Use these rounded present-value factors: 6.7327 for HK$1 paid at each of the eight year-ends; 0.8548 for a year-four payment; 0.7307 for a year-eight receipt. Add costs and subtract receipts. The initial incomplete totals were HK$234,654 for A and HK$294,654 for B. No further financial cash flows are omitted under these assumptions. No comparable manufacturing, transport, replacement or end-of-life environmental assessment has been supplied.

Your decision

Which package would you recommend under the specified financial method? Rebuild both eight-year present costs, test B with no recovery receipt, and state what your result does and does not establish about environmental performance.

Buy the same service

A purchase is not an eight-year lighting service if the equipment lasts only four years. Compare both packages over the same service horizon, performance requirement, price basis and discount rate. Include A’s replacement rather than pretending it lasts eight years or comparing four years of A with eight years of B. The original HK$60,000 purchase advantage is a useful first observation, not a complete cost saving.

Rebuild the financial comparison

Annual costs are equal: A uses HK$6,000 of electricity plus HK$14,000 maintenance; B uses HK$7,500 plus HK$12,500. Each has HK$134,654 in discounted operating costs using the supplied factor.

A: 100,000 + 20,000 × 6.7327 + 110,000 × 0.8548 = HK$328,682. B: 160,000 + 20,000 × 6.7327 − 10,000 × 0.7307 = HK$287,347. B costs HK$41,335 less in present-value terms. These amounts use rounded factors; using unrounded factors changes the totals by only a few dollars, not the ranking.

Test the receipt and fund the cash

If B’s recovery receipt disappears, its present cost becomes HK$294,654, still HK$34,028 below A. B therefore does not win only because of optimistic salvage. A’s brochure cannot justify subtracting its gross scrap proceeds again after net terminal costs have already been set to zero.

Present value is not a payment schedule. B needs HK$160,000 now and HK$20,000 in each operating year. A needs HK$100,000 now, HK$20,000 annually and an additional HK$110,000 at year four. Neither option violates the stated initial budget. The buyer must still reserve the actual future cash, not merely its discounted equivalent.

Cheaper financially is not necessarily greener

With the supplied costs and verified service lives, recommend B under the predeclared financial method. A remains more electricity-efficient: 32,000 kWh over eight years versus B’s 40,000 kWh, a difference of 8,000 kWh. A’s lower use does not itself prove lower total environmental impact, because it also needs replacement equipment. Equally, B’s lower present cost does not prove lower emissions or material impacts.

A whole-life environmental comparison would need comparable evidence on production, replacement, transport, electricity supply and recovery within the same service boundary. Check the coverage, assumptions and verification of any environmental declarations rather than treating a label as a complete ranking. Do not invent monetary environmental benefits to overturn or reinforce the financial result. If evidence changes verified lives, costs or compliance, rebuild the comparison using the disclosed method before award.

Instructor note

Allow 15–20 minutes. Pause for a provisional first choice, then reveal the lifetime and terminal-value audit. Ask students to draw the cash-flow timeline before calculating. Supply the factors rather than requiring annuity derivation. Accept an initial preference for A only if explicitly provisional; a student who withholds judgment until life and recovery are checked has identified the central problem.

Require the replacement, net rather than gross recovery, equal eight-year service and a separate environmental conclusion. Discuss why a present-value saving cannot pay this year’s bill. This case is about lifetime-consistent procurement costing, not component repairability (SUS-10), funding community upkeep (SUS-20), rebound in demand (SUS-21), or accounting for external site impacts (SUS-24). All prices, lives, requirements, funding and offers are invented; public sources support the method, not these estimates. Foreign guidance is not presented as Hong Kong procurement law.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

Original fictional classroom design. All packages, prices, energy use, maintenance costs, service lives, recovery offers, budgets and the 4% real rate are invented. The fixed service horizon is eight years, with equal mandatory lighting performance and no service needed after year eight. Constant-dollar cash flows use a real rate. Both packages have HK$20,000 annual operating costs; A needs HK$110,000 replacement at year four, not at year eight. A terminal value is net zero; B is net HK$10,000 at year eight in the base case. Rounded-factor present costs: A HK$328,682, B HK$287,347; advantage B HK$41,335. With zero B receipt, B costs HK$294,654, still HK$34,028 less. Electricity totals are A 32,000 kWh and B 40,000 kWh. Financial cost is not a whole-life environmental impact measure. Public sources supply concepts, not tender terms or model parameters. No source dataset or long quotation is reproduced.

  2. NIST · Handbook 135, Life Cycle Costing Manual for the Federal Energy Management Program · August 2025. Primary manual: common study period (printed pages 17–19), constant and current dollars (pages 32–33), and residual value (pages 48–50). Supports consistent treatment of time, replacement and terminal values, not the fictional 4% rate.
  3. UNEP · Sustainable Public Procurement. Official overview considers environmental, social and economic value, rather than purchase price alone. Used as context, not a claim that the financial winner is environmentally best.
  4. UNEP United for Efficiency · Green Public Procurement Technical Guidelines and Specifications for Energy-Efficient Lighting · 2023. Public technical guide, especially indoor lighting maintenance and lifetime discussion on printed pages 34–35. Supports checking service life against required performance and planning replacement; no technical threshold is imposed on this invented scenario.
  5. German Environment Agency · Life cycle costing · 22 September 2017. Official methodological overview includes operation, disposal and possible environmental costs. Its discussion of wider emissions informs the limits of a financial-only comparison; historical legal details are not asserted as current law.
