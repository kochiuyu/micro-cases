MKT-09 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Marketing · Audience overlap · Campaign reach

A smaller audience. More new people?

In the news · Combining advertising channels

On 31 July 2025, JCDecaux Transport announced Hong Kong’s “5G Summer Thrill” promotion, combining MTR station displays and interactive livestreaming.

This company announcement supplies a local mixed-channel example, not independent evidence of reach or sales. Our kitchenware shop, budget and audience figures are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. An independent Hong Kong kitchenware shop is launching a compact steaming basket. Its factual product message and creative are approved. A base online video campaign is already booked for a specified two-week window, with projected reach of 30,000 distinct adults in the defined local target audience.

The base booking cannot change in this exercise. The shop has a final HK$6,000 for exactly one additional fixed package during the same window. Its objective is to reach as many additional target people beyond the base campaign as possible.

“New people” here means newly reached by this campaign, not new customers or people unfamiliar with the brand. There is no evidence about which placement produces better buyers or more sales.

Both carry the same message and use comparable exposure definitions, target population, geography, period and measurement approach. Creative production and delivery costs are included. The packages cannot be split; the base campaign’s cost is common to both choices.

These are planning estimates of distinct people, not ad loads or device counts. Neither standalone number says how many people would also see the base campaign.

Your decision

Which placement would you provisionally book? Is A’s larger standalone audience enough, and what information could change your choice?

Round 2 · One fact changes

The planning model now supplies each package’s overlap with the base campaign. Of A’s 20,000 target people, 16,000 are also projected to see the base campaign. Of B’s 15,000, 5,000 are also projected to see it.

Use these mutually compatible estimates as exact inputs for this classroom calculation. Both describe the same two-week window and measurement approach; neither additional placement changes the base campaign’s 30,000-person reach in the model.

No identities or tracking implementation are needed. Real forecasts remain uncertain; the exercise supplies a planning comparison, not observed results.

Your decision

Which package now adds more distinct target people, and what is the combined campaign reach? Does that result show which option creates more sales?

Subtract the people already counted

A adds 20,000 − 16,000 = 4,000 target people beyond the base. Its combined campaign reach is 30,000 + 4,000 = 34,000.

B adds 15,000 − 5,000 = 10,000. Its combined reach is 30,000 + 10,000 = 40,000.

Adding the headline audiences directly would give 50,000 or 45,000 and count some people twice. Only one additional package is bought, so its overlap with the base matters; an A-versus-B overlap is not needed for this choice.

The smaller placement better meets this objective

B reaches 5,000 fewer people on its own, yet adds 6,000 more people beyond the existing campaign than A. At the same HK$6,000 cost, choose B for the stated additional-reach objective under this model.

The reveal identifies useful audience complementarity, not a new third package or proof that cooking content always outperforms lifestyle video. Both standalone audience figures can be accurate and still be insufficient for the combined-campaign decision.

Reach is not response, and repetition is not automatically waste

An ad exposure does not establish attention, recall or a purchase. The 10,000 newly reached people are not 10,000 new customers, and this table cannot rank incremental sales.

Repeated exposure can serve a different objective, such as reinforcing a message. This allocation explicitly prioritises reaching additional people. If the objective changes, frequency and response evidence would be needed; A would not automatically become the better choice.

Keep the comparison consistent as plans change

Review whole-window distinct reach and overlap using compatible definitions and methods. Do not add each day’s distinct audience and call the result a two-week distinct total.

Check whether revised estimates still support the choice. With B’s overlap fixed at 5,000, A would tie B’s 10,000 additional people if A’s overlap with the base were 10,000, rather than 16,000. This is a model threshold, not a promised future outcome.

Instructor note

Take a provisional A-or-B vote before revealing overlap. A’s headline advantage supports an initial lean, not an assumption that duplication is zero. B’s apparent relevance to cooking also does not establish superior conversion.

Use two addition bridges: base 30,000 plus A’s 4,000 new people; base 30,000 plus B’s 10,000. Avoid a not-to-scale Venn drawing that suggests unsupported population proportions. No named individual or cross-platform tracking task is part of the exercise.

Optional cost comparison: HK$6,000/4,000 = HK$1.50 per additional modelled target person for A; HK$6,000/10,000 = HK$0.60 for B. Neither is customer acquisition cost, cost per sale or whole-campaign return. Keep the main discussion on the objective and people counted once.
