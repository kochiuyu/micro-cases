GVR-43 · Hong Kong · 15 minutes · Safety governance · Exposure rates · Reporting incentives

Safety reports rose. Did harm rise too?

Workplace safety counts need context

Hong Kong’s Labour Department publishes counts and rates of work-related injuries. The US Occupational Safety and Health Administration separately encourages near-miss reports, including an anonymous reporting option. The venue operator, dashboard and decision rules below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

Ying is safety lead for a fictional venue operator in Hong Kong. At the start of Quarter 2 it opened an anonymous channel for near misses: events that could have caused harm but did not. Both quarters count each verified near miss once, including reports from any channel. Injury definitions and verification did not change.

One director wants to reverse the anonymous reporting rollout when near-miss counts rise. Another wants to keep the channel and assess the risk with injury rates, investigation findings and time to respond to reports. Do not attribute any change in injury rates to a particular cause from this comparison alone.

The fictional board orders a targeted review when a verified injury rate reaches at least 3 per 10,000 work hours. It normally applies the rule to any work group with reliable denominators, as well as to the operator as a whole. At the first vote only the operator-wide figures are available.

Round 2 · More information

Before the next meeting, the safety lead verifies the Quarter 2 split. The overnight set-up crew accounted for 10,000 of the 40,000 hours and 4 of the 6 injuries. All other crews together worked the remaining hours and had the remaining injuries. The complete set of overnight near-miss reports has not yet been classified by crew.

Compute the two Quarter 2 injury rates. What targeted review is now required under the same rule, and what can the board say about the overnight near-miss trend?

Debrief · Separate reports from harm

Near-miss report rates increased from 4 to 8 per 10,000 hours. The injury count rose from 4 to 6, but hours doubled, so injury rates fell from 2 to 1.5 per 10,000 hours. Improved report capture could explain some of the first increase, but a lower injury rate cannot establish what caused either trend.

Debrief · Retain the channel

The operator-wide Quarter 2 rate does not meet the review trigger. Keep anonymous reporting, avoid rewards for few reports, and show injuries and near misses separately with common exposure denominators. Include how quickly reports are investigated or acted on and what injuries or high-risk incidents reveal. The rule is a trigger, not a reason to ignore other evidence.

Debrief · A pooled rate can conceal a crew

The overnight rate is 4 ÷ 10,000 × 10,000 = 4. All other crews together have 2 ÷ 30,000 × 10,000, or about 0.67. The overnight crew meets the threshold and needs a targeted review. The data do not establish an overnight near-miss trend, so classify those records before commenting on it.

Instructor note

Use 2 minutes to read, 4 to calculate, 4 to disaggregate and 5 to test the incentive facing a supervisor penalised for reports. These simple rates ignore sampling noise and severity; no legal reporting or operational safety recommendation for a real site is implied.

Sources and case assumptions · optional reference

All information needed for classroom decisions is provided above. Sources are optional reading for attribution and background.

The reported metrics, exposure hours, dashboard rule and venues are invented. Guidance from other jurisdictions is cited for concepts, not as Hong Kong legal requirements.

  1. Hong Kong Labour Department, OSH Statistics Bulletin No. 25. The introduction distinguishes injury and industrial accident counts and reports their respective exposure-based rates.
  2. Hong Kong Labour Department, 2024 annual report, Safety and Health at Work. Work Safety Performance compares absolute counts and rates per thousand employees or workers.
  3. US OSHA, Worker Participation. Action item 2 supports anonymous near-miss reporting; action item 5 warns that reporting can be suppressed by incentive design.
  4. UK HSE, Investigating accidents and incidents. The workbook’s adverse-event form distinguishes near misses from accidents involving injury or ill health.
  5. US OSHA, Voluntary Protection Programs policy and procedures. The section on programme evaluation lists near misses and response timing as leading indicators, with injuries and exposures among lagging indicators.
