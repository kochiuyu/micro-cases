HSP-18 · Hong Kong · 10–15 minutes · Tenant mix · Operating compatibility · Whole-site management

The highest rent. The right neighbour?

A place contains more than separate leases

URA’s 2022–23 annual report describes 618 Shanghai Street, opened in November 2019, as a revitalised cluster combining restaurants, retail and community activity. This is institutional context, not proof that a tenant raises neighbouring sales. Our courtyard, tenants and figures below are fictional.

Your classroom decision

A private Hong Kong mixed-use courtyard has homes above ground-floor shops and one vacant unit. Its site promise combines daytime convenience with a quiet residential entrance from 8 p.m. to 8 a.m. That condition concerns commercial servicing and avoidable disturbance; residents and emergency services retain access at all times. It is a site rule, not a statement of statutory noise limits.

For an illustrative one-year letting, A is an indoor evening workshop-and-refreshment venue, open 4 p.m. to 10 p.m., offering HK$40,000 monthly rent. B is a clothing-repair and alterations service, open 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., offering HK$32,000 monthly rent. Both pass the stipulated financial, permitted-use, safety, access and other required checks. A’s indoor and visitor arrangements can meet applicable noise requirements; evening opening itself is not forbidden. Delivery details still need review.

The owner can afford either. Compare twelve months with identical occupancy, rent collection, ordinary management, fit-out support and other owner costs; no vacancy, incentive or tax difference is assumed. No lease has been signed. Existing occupants’ rights, services and access remain protected. The decision is a recommendation subject to documented operating terms, not permission to ignore legal duties.

Your decision

Which tenant would you provisionally recommend, A or B? Identify what you must check about shared access and the site promise before treating the rent difference as the whole decision.

Round 2 · More information

A’s original supply plan requires a 5 a.m. delivery through the residential entrance. That conflicts with the site promise and is unacceptable. A checked alternative uses a separate authorised service gate at 10 a.m., with sufficient approved storage inside the unit. A and its supplier agree to that schedule. An owner-funded receiving service costs HK$3,000 monthly all-in, with no setup cost or extra charge to residents. Emergency access and other tenants’ servicing remain unaffected.

A keeps its HK$40,000 rent. Annual rent less the additional receiving cost is HK$444,000, or twelve times HK$37,000. B needs no additional receiving service and contributes HK$384,000 annual rent on the same basis. A’s remaining annual advantage is HK$60,000, not the original HK$96,000. These are comparable owner cash flows before identical other costs, not whole-property profit or tenant viability.

Thirty people answered a voluntary courtyard feedback exercise; eighteen requested a convenient alterations service, which the site currently lacks. They are not a representative sample. No spending commitment or effect on neighbouring shops’ sales is established. B addresses a documented service gap, but that benefit has no supplied monetary value. Both candidates remain feasible under checked terms. The owner must weigh adjusted income and evening activity against daytime convenience, without assuming a free hybrid tenancy.

Your decision

Would you retain or change your recommendation after the delivery check and feedback? Compare the adjusted owner income with the site contribution, and name the operating terms and follow-up evidence you would require before and after letting.

Make compatibility an operating condition

A’s original delivery route is rejected, not excused by higher rent. The revised route preserves a feasible evening option through an agreed time, separate gate, approved storage and paid receiving. Record responsibility for delivery compliance, access and incident handling. Do not make residents absorb an unagreed burden.

Compare income on the same basis

A initially offers HK$480,000 annually against B’s HK$384,000, a HK$96,000 difference. Receiving costs HK$36,000, leaving HK$444,000 and a HK$60,000 advantage. Mitigation does not erase the premium. Nor does the premium measure every effect on the site or guarantee tenant success.

A service gap is not a sales forecast

Eighteen of thirty is 60% of respondents, not 60% of residents or a forecast of purchases. B offers a concrete service-mix reason to consider foregoing HK$60,000 annually. A offers income and evening activity. Either recommendation needs explicit priorities and enforceable operating terms. No evidence establishes a rise in neighbouring turnover.

Instructor note

First reject the incompatible route, then compare feasible offers rather than a disruptive straw option. Require a written servicing schedule, accountable contact, monitoring and lawful response process, preserving rights. HSP-16 chooses ambience within a restaurant; this case manages separate property uses and shared access. Do not draft or sign an actual lease or turn the fictional site hours into legal thresholds.
