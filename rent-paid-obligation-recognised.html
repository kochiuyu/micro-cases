ACC-38 · Hong Kong · 15–20 minutes · Lease accounting · Right-of-use assets · Renewal assessment

Rent paid. No obligation on the statement?

Cash rent is only one part of the account

Paying rent is a cash event. Reporting a lease also requires identifying the right to use an asset and the associated payment obligation. The fictional schedule below applies supplied IFRS-style lessee rules; it is not a choice between leasing and buying or a legal interpretation of a real contract.

Your classroom decision

Fictional Harbour Store has signed a lease for a small Hong Kong warehouse. The space becomes available at the start of year one. The enforceable contract gives the tenant two non-cancellable years and a sole option for one further year. Each occupied year costs HK$10,000, paid at that year’s end. No purchase or ownership transfer is possible.

The bookkeeper proposes recording only HK$10,000 rent expense when the first annual payment is made. The controller instead proposes recognising a right-of-use asset and lease liability at commencement, then depreciation and interest. The lease has already been chosen; neither proposal changes the landlord’s contract, the cash due or the building’s ownership.

Apply the ordinary lessee model: no short-term or low-value exemption applies. The accounting term includes the two compulsory years plus the optional year if exercise is reasonably certain. Assess relevant economic incentives, not merely whether the contract calls the year optional. The first working paper provisionally excludes renewal, pending the complete commencement evidence.

The implicit lease rate cannot readily be determined. Use the supplied appropriate incremental borrowing rate of 10% annually for either possible term. All payments are in arrears. There are no prepayments, incentives, direct costs, restoration obligations, service components, variable payments or guarantees. Taxes are outside this exercise. The warehouse’s useful life exceeds either lease term.

Initial liability equals the sum of each included payment divided by 1.10 raised to its payment year. Initial right-of-use cost equals that liability here. Depreciate it evenly over the accounting term to zero. Each year, interest equals opening liability × 10%; closing liability equals opening liability plus interest minus cash paid. Assume no impairment or later changes.

Round 2 · More information

Before the opening entries are finalised, the complete file reaches the controller. It contains an existing three-year customer storage commitment and a documented assessment that relocating after year two would impose significant avoidable cost and disruption. The assessor concludes that exercising the tenant’s renewal option is reasonably certain. Accept that conclusion for this exercise.

These facts existed when the warehouse became available. The first paper was incomplete, not an established two-year measurement subsequently changed by a new event. Finalise the initial accounting using the complete evidence. The legal option remains an option, and the landlord receives no extra payment at commencement.

Use three annual HK$10,000 payments, the same supplied 10% rate and three years of straight-line depreciation. Carry unrounded values through calculations, then report to the nearest cent. Assume the first payment occurs exactly at year-end and the warehouse is used throughout that year. All depreciation and interest are expensed, not capitalised into another asset.

Debrief · Recognise the right and obligation

Choose the controller’s proposal. At commencement the tenant receives a right to use the warehouse and recognises its associated lease liability. Neither balance means the tenant owns the underlying building. Annual cash rent alone does not represent the complete reporting required by the supplied ordinary lessee model.

Under the provisional two-year assumption, liability is 10,000/1.10 + 10,000/1.10² = HK$17,355.37. This is a conditional working figure, not permission to omit renewal evidence. The two payments total HK$20,000 undiscounted; present value is lower because the payments occur in the future.

Debrief · Optional is not automatically excluded

The completed assessment makes renewal reasonably certain, so include year three. The initial liability is 10,000/1.10 + 10,000/1.10² + 10,000/1.10³ = HK$24,868.52. The added discounted third payment is HK$7,513.15. Initial right-of-use cost is also HK$24,868.52 because the specified adjustments are all zero.

The contract has not become a compulsory three-year contract merely because accounting includes renewal. The measurement reflects the supplied assessment of exercise. Do not infer reasonable certainty from optional wording, management preference alone or a numerical probability cutoff. Real assessments require the relevant enforceable rights and economic circumstances.

Debrief · Interest and cash are different

Year-one interest is HK$24,868.5199098 × 10% = HK$2,486.85. After the HK$10,000 payment, liability is HK$24,868.5199098 + HK$2,486.8519910 − HK$10,000 = HK$17,355.37. The payment reduces principal by HK$7,513.15; it is not all interest and does not extinguish the liability.

The opening asset and liability are non-cash recognition here. The first-year cash outflow is still exactly HK$10,000. Do not add the initial liability to that payment as another cash outflow or treat the interest component as a second payment to the landlord.

Debrief · Depreciate the use right

Annual depreciation is HK$24,868.5199098 divided by three = HK$8,289.51. After one year, the right-of-use carrying amount is HK$16,579.01. It differs from the HK$17,355.37 closing liability because straight-line depreciation and the effective-interest liability schedule follow different patterns.

First-year lease-related expense is depreciation plus interest: HK$8,289.5066366 + HK$2,486.8519910 = HK$10,776.36. It exceeds the HK$10,000 cash payment by HK$776.36. Do not also charge the full cash payment as rent expense; that would double count under this model.

Debrief · Check the whole commitment

Across three years, payments total HK$30,000. Depreciation totals the initial HK$24,868.52 asset, and interest totals approximately HK$5,131.48, the difference between payments and opening liability. Both closing balances reach zero after year three under the fixed assumptions. The model changes timing and presentation, not the contractual cash total.

The reveal completes initial measurement at the same commencement date. A genuine later reassessment can involve different rules and a revised discount rate; this exercise does not calculate one. Keep the evidence date, option judgment, payment timing and rate visible rather than treating every new working paper as a lease modification.

Instructor note

Minutes 0–5: choose the reporting model and calculate the provisional liability. Minutes 5–10: reveal the commencement evidence and calculate the third-year effect. Minutes 10–15: reconcile the first-year asset, liability, expenses and cash. Ask which amounts represent rights, obligations, consumption, financing and actual payment.

FIN-12 compares buying and leasing before commitment. This case concerns reporting an already chosen lease and a supplied renewal assessment. All business facts, amounts and judgments are fictional. No student must determine actual legal enforceability, choose a real discount rate or provide financial reporting advice to a real tenant.
