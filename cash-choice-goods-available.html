SUS-22 · Fictional regional community · Hong Kong-based charity · 15–20 minutes · Cash and in-kind support · Recipient choice · Market availability

Cash support. Or the goods people need?

Support is useful when people can use it

Humanitarian practice distinguishes the flexibility of cash from the ability to deliver specific goods. Market assessment and recipient preferences matter to both. These ideas motivate a modest fictional support round, not a disaster-response prescription or a claim about an actual community.

Your classroom decision

Fictional classroom situation. A Hong Kong-based charity is planning one supplementary household-support round for 100 consenting adult recipients in a fictional regional island community. Eligibility is already settled through the same non-coercive rules under every option. Your role is to recommend the transfer format, not decide who deserves help. Immediate emergency needs and existing entitlements are separately protected; the exercise must not delay urgent assistance.

The aim is to help recipients meet their own priority everyday needs over the next two weeks. C offers HK$200 per recipient as an unrestricted cash transfer, not a store voucher, reimbursement or spending condition. G offers each recipient one specified package: laundry detergent costing HK$40, household paper goods costing HK$60 and shelf-stable staples costing HK$100. These are procurement costs, not a claim about what every person values. Goods are suitable only after recipient confirmation; no dietary or health assumptions are made.

Both quotes schedule the transfer or delivery for day three. Either complete option costs HK$22,000: HK$20,000 in transfers or goods plus HK$2,000 for delivery and administration. Safe cash access, accessible goods delivery, privacy and feedback support are funded in the quotes. An assisted non-digital cash route is available at no recipient fee. Nobody must publicise personal hardship, accept unsuitable goods or lose later support for declining an offer. Prices and quotes are fixed for this round. No transfer or purchase has been made.

Initial conversations suggest varied needs. Local shops are open, but the team has not yet checked stock quantities for this round or asked all recipients to choose between fully described offers. It can complete that check today without delaying distribution. The same budget could support a mixed design if a supplier confirms its terms. Cash permits choice; packages may secure particular goods. Neither label alone establishes which will help more.

Round 2 · More information

The same-day market check finds a narrow, temporary constraint. After allowing for other residents’ ordinary demand, accessible retailers together could supply this cohort at most twenty detergent units by day seven. Individual purchase access is not guaranteed. Their next replenishment is on day ten; no affordable, accessible alternative supply or acceptable substitute has been identified for that first-week need. Paper goods, staples and other usual purchases remain available. This is a fictional supply constraint, not a claim about any actual island or a forecast of price inflation.

A separate regional supplier confirms that it can deliver additional stock by day three, without taking the twenty retail units or diverting other committed supplies. Its full-package quote remains available. It also confirms a mixed quote at the same HK$22,000 total: forty detergent units at HK$40 each, HK$160 unrestricted cash for each of those forty recipients, and HK$200 unrestricted cash for each of the other sixty. The HK$2,000 covers all shipping, payment and administration for this design; no unpriced extra trip is required.

In private, voluntary conversations after the exact offers and supply limit are explained, forty recipients say they want one of these suitable detergent units by day seven plus the remaining cash. The other sixty already have enough detergent for this period and prefer cash for differing priorities. These are stated preferences for this round, not permanent categories or measured welfare gains. Recipients can change their choice before ordering without penalty. The team must reconfirm delivery and informed acceptance, rather than assign packages based on demographic assumptions.

Cash changes purchasing power, not the number of units

Cash lets recipients decide among available options, but it does not put extra detergent on shelves by day seven. If all forty stated requests remain, a maximum of twenty units through the assessed retail route leaves at least twenty requests unfilled through that route. Actual access could be lower. This is a capacity bound, not a prediction that particular people will go without or that no need can change.

The constraint is specific to one item, place and time. It does not establish that all local markets have failed or that cash is generally inappropriate. A later delivery may miss this round’s stated timing even when it eventually arrives.

In-kind support helps only if the supply route is real

Delivering forty additional suitable units from the separate supplier addresses the identified stock constraint. Buying the same scarce retail units centrally would merely reallocate them; calling that in-kind assistance would not create more local supply. Verify sourcing, arrival, quality and accessible handover, not only a purchase order.

Research distinguishes adding external goods from adding purchasing power, but price effects depend on context. Do not assume cash causes inflation or goods lower prices here. No demand curve, price response or broader market effect has been estimated for this fictional round.

Check the mixed budget without equating cost and value

Forty detergent units cost 40 × HK$40 = HK$1,600. Cash for their forty recipients costs 40 × HK$160 = HK$6,400. Cash for the other sixty costs 60 × HK$200 = HK$12,000. Transfer and procurement spending is HK$20,000; adding HK$2,000 delivery and administration gives HK$22,000.

Each recipient receives HK$200 in cash or cash plus goods measured at programme cost. This is not proof of identical utility or equivalent market resale value. No one receives both a full HK$200 transfer and an extra unit under this quote. A 100-package order would procure sixty detergent units that recipients say they do not presently want, while also fixing other contents rather than letting them prioritise.

Recommend a format, preserve a choice

The mixed offer is a strong provisional recommendation under the supplied preferences and confirmed additional supply: meet the forty timely detergent requests while leaving the remaining resources flexible. Reconfirm the offer and order only accepted goods. The aim is responsiveness, not forcing a preferred organisational format on everyone.

All-cash support remains useful for other needs, but cannot promise every stated first-week detergent request through the constrained retail route. Full packages secure goods but do not fit the stated preferences as well. If delivery becomes unreliable, preferences change or a suitable retail supply returns, reassess the mix with recipients. Do not withdraw cash, impose spending surveillance or punish resale simply to protect the original plan.

Ask what happened, not whether the parcels look impressive

Check privately and proportionately whether accepted goods arrived when needed, transfers were usable without fees or access barriers, and recipients could address the priority they identified. Offer an accessible complaint and correction route. Record stock and price changes without claiming that this small programme caused them.

Stated preferences guide design, not a causal impact estimate. Delivery counts show transfers made; they do not prove improved nutrition, health, income or long-term wellbeing. Collect only information necessary for service improvement, with appropriate consent and protection.

Instructor note

Suggested 15–20 minutes: three minutes for the first choice, four for the item-specific stock constraint, four to cost the mixed offer, and five for the revised recommendation and recipient safeguards. Ask why an unrestricted transfer is different from a restricted shop voucher, and why a centrally purchased local unit is different from an additional delivered unit.

Both initial options are legitimate pending assessment. The reveal supports a mixed format rather than declaring one instrument universally superior. The same cohort, budget and protected urgent support remove an eligibility or life-saving rationing decision. The twenty-unit bound is not an exact forecast of unmet wellbeing. Equal assumed delivery costs make this a transfer-design case, not evidence that cash and goods normally cost the same to administer. All scenarios and numbers are fiction; humanitarian sources provide concepts, not facts about this community.
